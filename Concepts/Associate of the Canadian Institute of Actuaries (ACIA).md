## Associate of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries (ACIA)

**ACIA** is the associate designation of the **[[Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)]]**. Since its board approved new qualification pathways in June 2021, the CIA examines toward it itself, with modules and an exam of its own, beside the routes that recognise associate-level credentials from the SOA, the CAS and other bodies. An Associate who goes on to complete an FCIA specialty track becomes a [[Fellow of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries (FCIA)|Fellow]].

## Requirements

> [!answer]- Pathway 1 — the CIA's own route
> - **A degree from a CIA-accredited university program**, with the courses the CIA mandates, and Student membership of the CIA from the first year, with its orientation module.
> - **[[Exam ACIA-M1 (CIA)|ACIA Module 1]]** — after the first year of the degree
>     - Introduction and actuarial communications; the actuarial environment; actuarial work.
> - **[[Exam ACIA-M2 (CIA)|ACIA Module 2]]** — in the final year
>     - Traditional actuarial solutions; advanced actuarial models and predictive analytics applications.
> - **[[Exam ACIA-CAPSTONE (CIA)|ACIA Capstone Exam]]** — after the degree
>     - An open-book, six-hour exam: a four-hour common section and a two-hour long- or short-term specialized section.
> - **The CIA Professionalism Workshop**, then an application for ACIA membership.

> [!answer]- Pathway 2 — from another body's associate-level education
> - For candidates with full or partial associate-level qualifications from another actuarial organization — an associate designation of the ASSA, CAS, IAAust, IFoA, SAI or SOA; CAS credit for the VEEs, Exams 1, 2, MAS-I, MAS-II and the DISCs; SOA credit for the VEEs, P, FM, FAM, ALTAM or ASTAM and SRM — or fully qualified actuaries from other IAA member organizations.
> - **ACIA Module 1** where not already covered (and for the fully qualified actuaries), **ACIA Module 2** for all, the **Capstone Exam** for the fully qualified actuaries, and the **CIA Professionalism Workshop**.

A holder of a recognised fellow-level designation (Pathway 3) may also take the ACIA, but goes to the FCIA directly.
