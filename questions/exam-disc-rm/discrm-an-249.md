A small mutual insurer places its reinsurance directly with a reinsurer whose own salaried employees negotiate with the insurer, with no broker involved. Which description fits that reinsurer?

- A) A broker-market reinsurer, which relies on intermediaries it compensates
- B) A direct-writing reinsurer
- C) An involuntary reinsurance pool, such as a FAIR plan
- D) A Lloyd's syndicate, which can be reached only through its own members

## Explanation

Reinsurance is marketed through brokers or intermediaries, who produce business for reinsurers and are paid by them, or by direct writers, whose salaried employees produce the business and negotiate directly with ceding companies. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — The stem says no broker is involved.
- **C** — An involuntary pool is a different kind of reinsurance source, set up for hard-to-place risks, not a reinsurer marketing through its own staff.
- **D** — Lloyd's syndicates are a source of reinsurance, but nothing in the stem describes one.
