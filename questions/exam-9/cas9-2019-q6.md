Given the following \$1,000 par value zero-coupon bonds with annual compounding:

| Bond | Years to Maturity | Yield to Maturity | Price of Bond |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1 | 5.00% | \$952.38 |
| B | 2 | 6.50% | \$881.66 |
| C | 3 | $y_C$ | \$810.60 |
| D | 4 | $y_D$ | \$735.03 |

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the price of a \$1,000 par value bond with 5% annual coupons maturing in four years.

### Explanation
$$810.60 = \frac{1000}{(1 + y_c)^3}, \qquad y_c = 7.25\%$$

$$735.03 = \frac{1000}{(1 + y_d)^4}, \qquad y_d = 8\%$$

$$\text{Price} = \frac{50}{1.05} + \frac{50}{1.065^2} + \frac{50}{1.0725^3} + \frac{1050}{1.08^4} = 904.01$$

Alternatively:

$$\text{Price} = 952.38 \times \frac{50}{1000} + 881.66 \times \frac{50}{1000} + 810.60 \times \frac{50}{1000} + 735.03 \times \frac{1050}{1000} = 904.01$$

Alternatively:

$$810.60 = \frac{1000}{1.065^2 (1 + r_3)}, \qquad r_3 = 8.77\%$$

$$735.03 = \frac{1000}{1.065^2 \times 1.0877 \times (1 + r_4)}, \qquad r_4 = 10.28\%$$

$$\text{Price} = \frac{50}{1.05} + \frac{50}{1.065^2} + \frac{50}{1.065^2 \times 1.0877} + \frac{1050}{1.065^2 \times 1.0877 \times 1.1028} = 904.01$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use bond prices to calculate yields to maturity, use those yields in calculating the price of another bond, calculate forward rates, and understand the expectations hypothesis and the liquidity preference theory.

Candidates were expected to calculate the price of the bond, which included the amount and timing of payments, and calculating the discount factor for each time-period (which could be accomplished by using yield to maturities, ratios of the zero-coupon bond prices, or forward rates).

Common mistakes included:

- Calculating the price of the bond using a fixed 8% yield for each of the payments, rather than the corresponding yield for that year's zero-coupon bond.
- Calculation errors in interim steps.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the expected one-year short rate one year from now. Assume the expectations hypothesis is valid.

### Explanation
Under expectations hypothesis, expected short rate = forward rate

$$1 + f_2 = \frac{(1 + s_2)^2}{1 + s_1} = \frac{1.065^2}{1.05}, \qquad f_2 = 8.02\%$$

Alternatively:

$$\text{Expected}_{1,2} = \frac{952.38}{881.66} - 1 = 8\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the forward rate for the second year and apply the expectations hypothesis to arrive at the expected short rate.

A common mistake was calculating the expected one-year short rate two years from now, rather than one year from now, i.e. $1.0725^3 / 1.065^2 - 1 = 8.77\%$

## Part c (0.25 points)

Calculate the expected one-year short rate one year from now. Assume the liquidity preference theory is valid with a constant liquidity premium of 2%.

### Explanation
Under liquidity preference theory, expected short rate = forward rate - liquidity premium

$$8.02\% - 2\% = 6.02\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to apply liquidity preference theory by adjusting the forward rate calculated in part b by the liquidity premium to arrive at the expected short rate.

A common mistake was adding the liquidity premium to the forward rate rather than subtracting it, i.e. 8% + 2% = 10%
