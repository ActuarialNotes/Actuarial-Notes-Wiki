One blanket limit of \$900,000 covers two buildings, valued at \$600,000 and \$500,000 at the time of loss, with a 90% coinsurance percentage and a \$2,000 deductible. A fire causes a \$220,000 loss to the second building. How much does the insurer pay?

- A) \$178,000
- B) \$198,000
- C) \$218,000
- D) \$220,000

## Explanation

When one limit applies to two or more items, the coinsurance condition applies to the total of all the property the limit covers:

$$
\begin{align*}
R &= \frac{900{,}000}{0.90 \times 1{,}100{,}000} \\
&= \frac{10}{11} \\
\text{Paid} &= \frac{10}{11} \times 220{,}000 - 2{,}000 \\
&= 198{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Divides the limit by the full total value instead of 90% of it.
- **C** — Tests the blanket limit against 90% of the damaged building's value alone, which it easily meets, so no penalty appears.
- **D** — Ignores both the coinsurance penalty and the deductible.
