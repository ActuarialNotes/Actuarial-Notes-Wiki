# VEE Mathematical Statistics

**VEE Mathematical Statistics** is one of the three *Validation by Educational Experience* (VEE) requirements of the [[Associate of the Society of Actuaries (ASA)|ASA]]. A VEE is not an exam. It is credit for university coursework, or for an approved alternative, that the SOA's VEE Administration Committee has approved for the topic. A candidate applies for it once they have passed two SOA, CAS or CIA actuarial exams. The CAS does not require this VEE for the [[Associate of the Casualty Actuarial Society (ACAS)|ACAS]].

> [!question]- About the requirement
>
> Typically, the VEE requirement for mathematical statistics will be covered by a one semester course that follows the probability course used to cover the learning objectives for Exam P. This course should be calculus-based. All of the topics listed below should be covered.
>
> The topics are those of the SOA's *Guidelines for Approval of VEE Courses and Educational Experiences*, effective November 1, 2024. A course is approved against them and then listed in the SOA's Directory of Approved VEE Courses/Experiences, or its Alternate VEE Options list. The SOA's own pages describe how a candidate claims the credit.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Mathematical Statistics
> 1. Explain the concepts of [[Random Sample|random sampling]], [[Statistical Inference|statistical inference]] and [[Sampling Distribution|sampling distribution]], and state and use basic [[Sampling Distribution|sampling distributions]].
> 2. Describe the main methods of estimation and the [[Properties of Estimators|main properties]] of [[Estimator|estimators]], and apply them. The only required method is [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation|maximum likelihood]] and properties include [[Bias|bias]], [[Variance|variance]], [[Mean Square Error|mean squared error]], [[Consistency|consistency]], [[Efficiency|efficiency]], and [[Minimum Variance|UMVUE]].
> 3. Construct [[Confidence Interval|confidence intervals]] for [[Parameter Estimation|unknown parameters]], including the mean, differences of two means, [[Variance|variances]], and proportions.
> 4. Test [[Hypothesis Testing|hypotheses]]. Concepts to be covered include [[Neyman-Pearson Lemma]], significance and [[Power of a Test|power]], [[Likelihood Ratio Test|likelihood ratio test]], and [[Information Criteria|information criteria]]. Tests should include for mean, [[Variance|variance]], [[Contingency Table|contingency tables]], and [[Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test|goodness-of-fit]].
