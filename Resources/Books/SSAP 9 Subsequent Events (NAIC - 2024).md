Statement of Statutory Accounting Principles No. 9 of the NAIC's Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual. It defines subsequent events and establishes the criteria for recording them in the financial statements and/or disclosing them in the notes; its conclusions apply to both quarterly and annual statement filings.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objective C5; NAIC Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual, 2025, SSAP No. 9, paragraphs 1-8, available in Appendix IV of the COPLFR P&C Practice Note (as the content outline states it)

## Scope of Statement
- The statement defines [[Subsequent Events|subsequent events]] and sets the criteria for recording them in the financial statements and/or disclosing them in the notes, for both quarterly and annual statement filings.

## Summary Conclusion
- Key Terms
    - Subsequent events are events or transactions that occur after the balance sheet date but before the statutory financial statements are issued and before the audited financial statements are issued, or available to be issued.
    - Material subsequent events are Type I (recognized), which provide additional evidence about conditions that existed at the balance sheet date, or Type II (nonrecognized), which provide evidence about conditions that did not exist at that date but arose after it.
    - Financial statements are "issued" when widely distributed for general use and "available to be issued" when complete in a form that complies with SAP and all necessary approvals have been obtained.
- Recognition Guidance
    - The effects of all material Type I subsequent events are recognized in the financial statements; their nature and amount are disclosed only if necessary to keep the statements from being misleading.
    - Material Type II subsequent events are not recorded but are disclosed, with the nature of the event and an estimate of its financial effect or a statement that no estimate can be made.
    - An entity considers supplementing the statements with pro forma financial data when a nonrecognized event is significant.
    - Examples of Type I events include the settlement of litigation whose cause preceded the balance sheet date; examples of Type II events include the sale of a bond or capital stock, a business combination and loss of plant or inventories by fire or natural disaster after the balance sheet date.
- Disclosures
    - Reporting entities disclose the dates through which subsequent events were evaluated and the dates the statements were issued, or available to be issued.
