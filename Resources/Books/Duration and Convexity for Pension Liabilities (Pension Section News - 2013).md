A short article by Martin McCaulay in the September 2013 (Issue 81) Pension Section News of the Society of Actuaries. It presents duration and convexity as a risk metric for pension plan sponsors: the formula for effective duration can be used to estimate the value of pension liabilities at different interest rates, and a convexity adjustment reflects that the liability increase for a rate decrease is greater than the decrease for a rate increase.

## Estimating Changes in Liabilities
- Pension liabilities have an inverse relationship to interest rates, and the change can be estimated using the duration of the liabilities.
- For every 100 basis point change in interest rates, the liability changes by duration divided by 100 in the opposite direction.
- The typical pension plan has a duration of about 15.

## Macaulay Duration
- The original formula for [[Macaulay Duration|duration]], developed in 1938 by Frederick Robertson Macaulay, measures a bond's weighted average cash flows.

## Modified Duration
- [[Modified Duration]] measures the sensitivity of a bond's price to interest rate movements and is related to Macaulay Duration with annual coupons.

## Effective Duration
- Effective Duration, used to price bonds with options, approximates the slope of a bond's value as a function of interest rate movements.
- Pension liability duration is measured using the formula for Effective Duration, substituting the liabilities for the bond's value.

## Illustrative Examples of Pension Duration with the Convexity Adjustment

## Convexity
- The traditional formula for pension duration does not consider [[Convexity|convexity]].
- Duration with a convexity adjustment can provide a better estimate of the change in liability when there is significant volatility.
