# Exam ALTAM
The ALTAM Exam is a three-hour exam consisting of six questions, worth a total of 60 points.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The exam will be taken at Prometric testing centers. The questions will be displayed on the computer. Candidates will also be provided with an Excel Workbook. The Excel Workbook will contain one question. It will also contain tables and formulas that may be used for any of the six questions.
>
> Five of the questions are to be answered in pen in exam answer booklets provided by Prometric. For these questions candidates may use the Excel Tables, and may use Excel for calculations, but only the answers provided in the exam answer booklet will be graded.
>
> One of the questions will be answered in the Excel Workbook. For this question, only the information provided in the Excel Workbook will be graded. Candidates may use the scratch paper booklet for rough notes, but nothing written on the scratch paper will be graded.
>
> At the end of the exam candidates will upload their Excel Workbook for grading the Excel question and will submit their exam answer booklets to Prometric to forward to the SOA for grading the questions answered in the answer booklets.
>
> Knowledge of the FM, P, and FAM exams, and the Mathematical Statistics VEE material is assumed for the ALTAM Exam.
>
> Exam Registration Candidates may register online or with an application.
>
> Introductory Study Note The Introductory Study Note has a complete listing of all study notes as well as errata and other important information.
>
> Past Exams Past LTAM Exams from October 2018 through 2022 and past ALTAM Exams from Spring 2023 through present are available on the SOA website.
>
> Updates Candidates should be sure to check the Updates page on the exam home page periodically for additional corrections or notices.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Survival Models for Contingent Cash Flows {10–20%}
> The Candidate will understand key concepts concerning [[Multiple|multiple]] state [[Mortality-morbidity|mortality/morbidity]] and [[Individual-joint Life Mortality|individual/joint life mortality]] models for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Understand and explain [[Feature|features]] of typical [[Mortality Curve|mortality curves]] and [[Heterogeneity|heterogeneities]] in [[Mortality|mortality]].
> 2. Apply [[Markov Multiple|Markov multiple]] state models to [[State-Contingent Life|state-contingent life]] and [[Long-Term Health Insurance Benefit|long-term health insurance benefits]], and [[Continuing Care Retirement Community|Continuing Care Retirement Communities]] ([[CCRC|CCRCs]]).
> 3. Understand and critique the assumptions underlying [[Markov Multiple|Markov multiple]] state models for [[Long-Term Insurance Benefit|long-term insurance benefits]].
> 4. Derive and apply [[Kolmogorov's Forward Equation|Kolmogorov’s forward equations]] for [[Continuous Time Markov Multiple|continuous time Markov multiple]] state models.
> 5. Calculate [[State-Dependent Probability|state-dependent probabilities]] for [[Continuous Time Markov|continuous time Markov]] models.
> 6. Apply the [[Chapman-Kolmogorov Equations|Chapman-Kolmogorov equations]] to calculate [[Discrete Time Transition Probability|discrete time transition probabilities]] in the [[Markov]] model.
> 7. Construct and [[Deconstruct Multiple Decrement Table|deconstruct multiple decrement tables]] using the associated [[Single Decrement|single decrement]] models and appropriate [[Fractional Age Assumption|fractional age]] assumptions.
> 8. Calculate [[Maximum Likelihood Estimate|maximum likelihood estimates]] of [[Transition Intensity|transition intensities]] and [[Probability|probabilities]] for [[Multiple|multiple]] state and [[Multiple Decrement|multiple decrement]] models, assuming [[Piecewise Constant Transition Intensity|piecewise constant transition intensities]].
> 9. Calculate [[Approximate Confidence Interval|approximate confidence intervals]] for the [[Estimator|estimators]] in Topic 1(h), using [[Asymptotic Property|asymptotic properties]] of the [[Maximum Likelihood Estimator|maximum likelihood estimators]].

> [!example]- Premium and Policy Valuation for Long-Term State-Dependent Coverages {12–20%}
> The Candidate will be able to perform calculations on the present value [[Random Variable|random variables]] associated with [[Benefit|benefits]] and [[Expenses|expenses]] for [[Single Life|single life]], [[Joint Life|joint life]], [[CCRC|CCRCs]], or other [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Define and interpret [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent insurance]] and [[Annuity|annuity]] present value [[Random Variable|random variables]] and identify and calculate their expected [[Value|values]].
> 2. Derive and apply [[Two-Term|two-term]] and [[Three-Term Woolhouse Approximation|three-term Woolhouse approximations]] for calculating expected present [[Value|values]] of [[State-Dependent Cash Flow|state-dependent cash flows]].
> 3. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[State-Dependent Life Insurance|state-dependent life insurance]], [[Long-Term Health Insurance|long-term health insurance]], and [[CCRC|CCRCs]] using the [[Equivalence Principle|equivalence principle]].
> 4. Calculate [[Policy Value|policy values]] for [[State-Dependent Life Insurance|state-dependent life insurance]], [[Long-Term Health Insurance|long-term health insurance]], and [[CCRC|CCRCs]].
> 5. Identify and apply [[Thiele's Differential Equation|Thiele’s differential equation]] in a [[Single Life|single life]] or [[Multiple|multiple]] state setting.

> [!example]- Joint Life Insurance and Annuities {8–16%}
> The Candidate will understand key concepts concerning [[Estimation|estimation]] and construction of [[Multiple|multiple]] state and [[Joint Life|joint life]] models for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Understand how [[Joint-Life Mortality|joint-life mortality]] can be [[Modelled|modelled]] using (i) a [[Time-To-Status-Failure Random Variable|time-to-status-failure random variable]], and ([[Ii|ii]]) a [[Multiple|multiple]] state model.
> 2. Understand the implications of [[Independence|independence]] or [[Dependence|dependence]] of [[Future Lifetime|future lifetimes]] in both [[Version|versions]] of the [[Joint Life|joint life]] model from 3(a). Identify sources of [[Dependence|dependence]] and understand how they are accommodated in the models.
> 3. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuities|annuities]] on [[Joint Live|joint lives]] using the [[Equivalence Principle|equivalence principle]].
> 4. Calculate [[Policy Value|policy values]] for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuities|annuities]] on [[Joint Live|joint lives]].

> [!example]- Profit Analysis {10–20%}
> The Candidate will be able to analyze [[Emerging Surplus|emerging surplus]], and to set [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] and [[Reserve|reserves]], using [[Profit Testing|profit testing]].
>
> 1. Calculate and interpret expected [[Profit|profit]] and [[Actual Profit|actual profit]],
> 2. Analyze and interpret [[Gain|gains]] by [[Source|source]].
> 3. Calculate and interpret [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], [[Net|net]] present value, [[Internal Rate|internal rate]] of [[Return|return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback period]] for [[Long-Term Life|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]], and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
> 4. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[Long-Term Life|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] based on a specified [[Profit|profit]] objective.
> 5. Calculate [[Reserve|reserves]] for [[Long-Term Life|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] using [[Profit Testing|profit testing]].

> [!example]- Pension Plans and Retirement Benefits {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand how [[Retirement Benefit|retirement benefits]] are [[Accrued|accrued]], valued, and [[Funded|funded]].
>
> 1. Calculate [[Replacement|replacement]] ratios for Defined [[Contribution]] ([[Defined Contribution Plan|DC]]), and Defined [[Benefit]] ([[Defined Benefit Plan|DB]]) [[Plan|plans]], including [[Final Average Salary|final average salary]] ([[FAS]]), [[Career Average Earning|career average earnings]] ([[CAE]]), and [[Career Average Revalued Earning|career average revalued earnings]] ([[CARE]]) [[Plan|plans]].
> 2. Calculate the [[Required Contribution Rate|required contribution rate]] to [[Meet|meet]] a [[Target Replacement|target replacement]] ratio for a [[DC Plan Participant|DC plan participant]], using a [[Deterministic|deterministic]] approach.
> 3. Identify, interpret, and apply [[Service Table|service table]] and [[Salary Scale|salary scale]] functions for [[Pension Plan Valuation|pension plan valuation]].
> 4. For a [[DB Plan|DB plan]], calculate and interpret [[Accrued Benefit|accrued benefits]], including [[Benefit|benefits]] on [[Early Exit|early exit]] from the [[Plan|plan]].
> 5. For a [[DB Plan|DB plan]], calculate and interpret the actuarial [[Actuarial Accrued Liability|accrued liability]] and the [[Normal Cost|normal cost]] for [[Benefits Payable|benefits payable]] on [[Age Retirement|age retirement]] or [[Early Exit|early exit]] using the [[Projected Unit Credit|projected unit credit]] ([[PUC]]) and [[Traditional Unit Credit|traditional unit credit]] ([[TUC]]) [[Valuation|valuation]] methods.

> [!example]- Universal Life Insurance {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand the [[Policy|policy]] design and [[Benefits Payable|benefits payable]] under Type A and Type B [[Universal Life Contract|Universal Life contracts]] and be able to assess and quantify [[Account Value|account values]], [[Insurance Premium|premiums]], and [[Reserve|reserves]].
>
> 1. Understand the [[Cashflow|cashflows]] and calculate [[Account Value|account values]] and [[Benefit|benefits]] under Type A and Type B [[Universal Life Policy|Universal Life policies]].
> 2. Calculate [[Reserve|reserves]] for [[No-Lapse Guarantee|no-lapse guarantees]].
> 3. Use [[Deterministic Profit Testing|deterministic profit testing]] to calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] or [[Reserve|reserves]], and assess [[Emerging Surplus|emerging surplus]] for [[Universal Life Insurance|Universal Life insurance]], including [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], [[Net|net]] present value, [[Internal Rate|internal rate]] of [[Return|return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback periods]], under [[Best|best]] estimate or [[Stress|stress]] test assumptions.

> [!example]- Embedded Options in Life Insurance and Annuity Products {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand various types of [[Equity-Linked Life Insurance Guarantee|equity-linked life insurance guarantees]], [[Option|options]] that are [[Embedded|embedded]] in [[Life Insurance|life insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] and be able to price, [[Reserve|reserve]], and [[Hedging|hedge]] the [[Risk|risk]] inherent in these [[Option|options]].
>
> 1. Define and calculate [[Payoff|payoffs]] under each of the following [[Options Embedded|options embedded]] in [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]]:
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit|Guaranteed minimum death benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Maturity Benefit|Guaranteed minimum maturity benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Income Benefit|Guaranteed minimum income benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Withdrawal Benefit|Guaranteed minimum withdrawal benefit]]
> 2. Value the following [[Options Embedded|options embedded]] in [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]], using the [[Black-Scholes Model|Black-Scholes]] model:
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit|Guaranteed minimum death benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Accumulation-maturity Benefit|Guaranteed minimum accumulation/maturity benefit]]
> 3. Construct a [[Replicating Portfolio|replicating portfolio]] for the [[Option|options]] in 7(b) using [[Delta Hedging|delta-hedging]]
> 4. Understand and evaluate the [[Cost|costs]] associated with [[Discrete-Time Rebalancing|discrete-time rebalancing]].
> 5. Use [[Deterministic Profit Testing|deterministic profit testing]] to assess [[Emerging Surplus|emerging surplus]] for [[Equity-Linked Life Insurance Cash Flow|equity-linked life insurance cash flows]], including [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], [[Net|net]] present value, [[Internal Rate|internal rate]] of [[Return|return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback periods]], under [[Best|best]] estimate or [[Stress|stress]] test assumptions.
