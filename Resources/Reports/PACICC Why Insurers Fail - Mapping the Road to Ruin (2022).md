The 2022 instalment of PACICC's Why Insurers Fail research series, a case study of four recent P&C insurer failures outside Canada. It examines Gefion Insurance A/S (Denmark, 2021), CBL Insurance Limited (New Zealand, 2018), Anbang Insurance Group Co., Ltd. (China, 2020) and Merced Property & Casualty Company (California, 2018) against the three common causes of failure the series identified in earlier case studies — internal operations, organizational structure and adequacy of regulatory oversight — and concludes that natural disasters should be added as a fourth.

## Executive summary
- The series' earlier case studies — Advocate General (1989), Maplex General (1995), Canadian Millers' Mutual (2001), HIH (2001), Reliance (2001) and Markham General (2002) — identified three common causes of P&C insurer failure:
    - 1 Internal operations — poor underwriting, insufficient loss reserving, rapid growth, inappropriate diversification, risky acquisitions and joint ventures, [[Corporate Governance|corporate governance]] deficiencies, bad investments, poor capital management, and [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] misuse or mismanagement
    - 2 Organizational structure — holding company arrangements and other complex corporate structures
    - 3 Adequacy of regulatory oversight — gaps in regulatory oversight
- The paper adds a fourth: 4 Natural disasters — failure to understand exposure aggregation, and inadequate [[Stress Testing|stress-testing]] of capital and reinsurance requirements against such aggregations, can lead directly to sudden insurer failure; many of these perils appear to be accelerating with [[Climate Change|climate change]].

## Insolvency case studies
- 1 Internal operations — the case of Gefion Insurance A/S (Denmark, 2021)
    - Causes of the Gefion failure: corporate governance deficiencies (insufficient control of outsourced activities; ineffective compliance, risk management and internal audit) and rapid growth in new markets across Europe in six years.
    - Lessons learned: internal controls; caution entering new markets and lines of business; capital management; reinsurance is not a solution for chronic underwriting losses.
- 2 Organizational structure — the case of CBL Insurance Limited (New Zealand, 2018)
    - Causes of the CBLI failure: a holding company structure that let CBLI write most of its business outside New Zealand without appropriate local supervisory scrutiny; failure to estimate reserves for long-tailed claims; a cautious regulatory approach; outsourcing of fundamental insurance operations.
    - Lessons learned: holding company supervision on a "whole company" basis; [[Reserve Adequacy|adequate provisions]] for outstanding claims; regulators acting quickly when in doubt; regulatory capacity in host jurisdictions.
- 3 Adequacy of regulatory oversight — the case of Anbang Insurance Group Co., Ltd. (China, 2020)
    - Causes of the Anbang failure: inconsistent financial reporting standards, corporate governance deficiencies, rapid growth and fraud through a complex and opaque equity structure.
    - Lessons learned: harmonized financial reporting and regular review by the prudential regulator; internal controls; caution in new markets and lines; sound risk management.
- 4 Natural disasters — the case of Merced Property and Casualty Company (U.S., 2018)
    - Causes of the Merced failure: catastrophic loss from the 2018 Camp Fire, with poor underwriting — an over-concentration of homeowners business in high-severity fire zones (60 percent of policies and 81 percent of direct written premium).
    - Lessons learned: avoid geographical concentration of risk; climate change is increasing solvency risk, so stress-test property portfolios against changing natural catastrophe models and unmodelled risks; diversify across lines of coverage.

## Conclusion
- 1 Despite improvements in the business environment, P&C insurers can still fail.
- 2 Common causes of P&C insurer failures have not changed over time.
- 3 By fueling increases in natural disasters, climate change adds a new source of potential insurance failure.
- 4 The causes of P&C insurer failure are not unique to one country or a single business model.
