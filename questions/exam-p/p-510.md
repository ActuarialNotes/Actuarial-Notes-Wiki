A company insures two buildings for one year. Its insurer will pay the value of a loss, rounded down to the nearest million. Let $X$ and $Y$ represent the insurer's payments in millions on each of the buildings. The joint probability function is:

| $Y$ \ $X$ | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.38 | 0.10 | 0.02 |
| 1 | 0.16 | 0.11 | 0.03 |
| 2 | 0.06 | 0.09 | 0.05 |

Calculate the expected value of the maximum of $X$ and $Y$.

- A) $0.33$
- B) $0.70$
- C) $0.87$
- D) $1.20$
- E) $2.00$

## Explanation

Build the distribution of $M = \max(X,Y)$ by collecting the cells that give each value.

**$M = 2$** whenever either variable equals 2 — the bottom row plus the right column, less the double-counted corner:

$$
0.02 + 0.03 + 0.05 + 0.06 + 0.09 = 0.25
$$

**$M = 0$** only when both are 0:

$$
P[M = 0] = 0.38
$$

**$M = 1$** is everything left over:

$$
P[M = 1] = 1 - 0.25 - 0.38 = 0.37
$$

Now take the expectation:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[M] &= 0(0.38) + 1(0.37) + 2(0.25) \\
     &= \mathbf{0.87}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 0.70, is $E[Y]$. Since $\max(X,Y) \geq Y$, with strict inequality whenever $X > Y$ (probability $0.10 + 0.02 + 0.03 = 0.15$), the answer must exceed $E[Y] = 0.70$. Since $\max(X,Y) = X + Y - \min(X,Y)$ and $\min(X,Y) \geq 1$ with probability $0.11 + 0.03 + 0.09 + 0.05 = 0.28$, it must fall short of $E[X] + E[Y] = 0.50 + 0.70 = 1.20$. Those two strict bounds leave only choice (C).
