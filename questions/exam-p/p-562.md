The damage from an accident at a certain intersection follows a uniform distribution on the interval $[a, b]$, where $0 < a < 3$. The probability that the damage is greater than 4, given that it is greater than 3, is 0.90. The probability that the damage is greater than 4, given that it is less than 11, is 0.70.

Calculate the unconditional probability that the damage is greater than 4.

- A) $0.70$
- B) $0.75$
- C) $0.80$
- D) $0.85$
- E) $0.90$

## Explanation

For a uniform distribution every probability is a length ratio, and the common denominator $b - a$ cancels inside each conditional probability.

**First condition** — note $\{X > 4\}$ sits inside $\{X > 3\}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.90 &= P[X > 4 \mid X > 3] = \frac{b-4}{b-3} \\
b - 4 &= 0.9(b-3) \\
0.1b &= 1.3 \implies b = 13
\end{align*}
$$

**Second condition** — here $\{X > 4\} \cap \{X < 11\}$ is the interval $(4, 11)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.70 &= P[X > 4 \mid X < 11] = \frac{11 - 4}{11 - a} = \frac{7}{11-a} \\
0.7(11-a) &= 7 \implies a = 1
\end{align*}
$$

The stated range $0 < a < 3$ confirms $a = 1$ is admissible. Now the unconditional probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 4] &= \frac{13 - 4}{13 - 1} = \frac{9}{12} \\
         &= \mathbf{0.75}
\end{align*}
$$

The two conditioning events are different in kind — one is a *superset* of $\{X>4\}$ and the other *overlaps* it — so their numerators differ. Getting the intersection right in each case is what determines $b$ and $a$ separately.
