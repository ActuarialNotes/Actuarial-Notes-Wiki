A boat insurance company's annual profit is normally distributed with mean 100. The mean and standard deviation remain constant from year to year, and the annual profits are independent.

The probability that the company's annual profit is negative in at least one of the next two years is 0.36.

Calculate the standard deviation of the insurance company's annual profit.

- A) $84$
- B) $109$
- C) $119$
- D) $125$
- E) $127$

## Explanation

Let $p$ be the probability of a nonnegative profit in a given year. The two years are independent, so

$$
0.36 = P[\text{negative in at least one of two years}] = 1 - p^2
$$

giving $p^2 = 0.64$ and $p = 0.80$.

So a single year is nonnegative with probability 0.80. Since the mean is 100, symmetry places 200 at the same distance above the mean that 0 sits below it, which means $P[X < 200] = 0.80$ as well. A cumulative probability of 0.80 corresponds to $z = 0.84162$, so profits of 100 and 200 sit at $z$-scores of 0 and 0.84162.

A profit difference of $200 - 100 = 100$ therefore spans 0.84162 standard deviations:

$$
\sigma = \frac{100}{0.84162} = \mathbf{118.82}
$$

Choice (A), 84, multiplies by the $z$-score instead of dividing: $100(0.84162) = 84.16$. The check is that a $z$ below 1 means the 100-unit gap is *less* than one standard deviation, so $\sigma$ must exceed 100.
