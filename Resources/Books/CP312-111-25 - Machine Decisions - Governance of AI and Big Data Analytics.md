A CRO Forum paper on how insurers can adapt existing governance and model risk frameworks to cover Big Data tools, including machine learning algorithms. It examines what reasonable steps can be taken to know what is happening inside the black box, ensure reliability and avoid unfair discrimination. It focuses solely on the (re)insurance industry and is meant as a practical overview for risk managers.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam CP-312 (SOA)|Exam CP-312]] — Outcomes 3a.

## 1 Executive summary
- 1.1 Introduction
    - Big Data and AI present particular challenges due to complexity, self-calibration, autonomy and the potential for unexpected results and unforeseen impacts.
- 1.2 A number of key insights emerged
    - It is vital to invest sufficient effort upfront to define the problem the tool is expected to solve in pursuit of a business objective.
    - Human intervention is and will remain critical; a key challenge is identifying when and how humans need to intervene and what triggers prompt their intervention.
    - Training needs apply at all levels in an organization.
    - Model governance techniques and frameworks that exist today do not need to be fundamentally altered, but can be enhanced and adjusted to meet the needs of complex tools and machine learning.
