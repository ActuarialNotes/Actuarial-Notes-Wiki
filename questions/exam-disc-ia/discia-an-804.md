A retailer leases its store on a three-year lease. Its CGL has a \$1,000,000 each occurrence limit and a \$100,000 Damage to Premises Rented to You limit. An employee's carelessness starts a fire that does \$250,000 of damage to the landlord's building. Later in the year, a pipe the retailer's staff left open floods the store, causing \$20,000 of water damage to the building. What does Coverage A pay the landlord in total for the two losses?

- A) \$100,000
- B) \$120,000
- C) \$250,000
- D) \$270,000

## Explanation

Exclusion j(1) removes property damage to property the named insured rents. After exclusion q the form says exclusions c to n do not apply to damage *by fire* to premises while rented to the named insured, subject to the Damage to Premises Rented to You limit. For other perils, exclusion j(1) is lifted only for premises rented for seven or fewer consecutive days.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid}_{\text{fire}} &= \min(250{,}000,\ 100{,}000) \\
&= 100{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The water damage is not fire and the rental is three years, so exclusion j(1) removes it. The total is **\$100,000**, answer **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** adds the water damage, applying the fire give-back to every peril.
- **C** pays the fire loss in full under the each occurrence limit, ignoring the separate premises-rented limit.
- **D** makes both mistakes.
