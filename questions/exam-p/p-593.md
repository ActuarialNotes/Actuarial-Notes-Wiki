An insurance company's annual profit is normally distributed with mean 39. The 28th percentile of the annual profit is 21.

Calculate the percentile associated with an annual profit of 3 for this insurance company.

- A) 4th
- B) 6th
- C) 8th
- D) 10th
- E) 12th

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the annual profit, so $X \sim N(39, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma$ unknown. The 28th percentile pins it down:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.28 &= P[X < 21] = P\!\left[Z < \frac{21 - 39}{\sigma}\right] \\
\frac{-18}{\sigma} &= -0.5828 \\
\sigma &= 30.885
\end{align*}
$$

Now standardize a profit of 3:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X < 3] &= P\!\left[Z < \frac{3 - 39}{30.885}\right] \\
         &= P[Z < -1.1656] \\
         &= 0.122
\end{align*}
$$

So a profit of 3 sits at the **12th percentile**.

Choice (A), 4th, comes from scaling the percentile in proportion to the profit: $0.28 \times (3/21) = 0.04$. Percentiles are not proportional to the values they mark — the normal cdf is nonlinear, and here the two profits are 0.58 and 1.17 standard deviations below the mean rather than in a 1-to-7 ratio.
