A commercial umbrella with a \$5,000,000 limit schedules business auto liability with a \$1,000,000 limit as required underlying insurance. To save premium, the insured reduces its auto liability limit to \$500,000 without telling the umbrella insurer. A covered auto accident then produces a \$1,800,000 judgment. How much does the umbrella pay?

- A) \$0
- B) \$500,000
- C) \$800,000
- D) \$1,300,000

## Explanation

If an underlying policy's limits are reduced, the umbrella still pays only what it would have paid had the scheduled insurance been in force. It attaches at the required \$1,000,000:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Umbrella} &= \min(1{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}000{,}000,\ 5{,}000{,}000) \\
&= 800{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The auto policy pays \$500,000 and the insured bears the \$500,000 gap. The answer is **C**. This is why an umbrella underwriter reviews the underlying policies and limits, and requires them to be maintained.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Reducing the underlying limit doesn't void the umbrella; it only leaves a gap below it.
- **B** — Is the gap the insured bears, not the umbrella's payment.
- **D** — Drops the umbrella down to the reduced \$500,000 limit. Drop-down applies when an aggregate is exhausted by claims, not when the insured buys less than required.
