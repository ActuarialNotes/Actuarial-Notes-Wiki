A company insures 40,000 drivers. The annual number of auto accidents for individual drivers is modeled by mutually independent Poisson random variables with mean 0.16.

Calculate the coefficient of variation for the total annual number of auto accidents for all 40,000 drivers.

- A) $0.01$
- B) $0.16$
- C) $0.40$
- D) $1.00$
- E) $2.50$

## Explanation

Independent Poisson counts add to a Poisson count whose mean is the sum:

$$
\lambda_{\text{total}} = 40{,}000(0.16) = 6400
$$

For a Poisson distribution the variance equals the mean, so

$$
\text{SD} = \sqrt{6400} = 80
$$

The coefficient of variation is the standard deviation divided by the mean:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{CV} &= \frac{80}{6400} \\
          &= \mathbf{0.0125}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 2.50, is a *single* driver's coefficient of variation, $\sqrt{0.16}/0.16 = 1/0.4$. Pooling is what collapses it: for a Poisson total the CV is $1/\sqrt{\lambda}$, so multiplying the portfolio by 40,000 divides the relative volatility by 200. That shrinkage is the whole reason insurers write large books of independent risks.
