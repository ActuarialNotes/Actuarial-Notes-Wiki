The table below describes the assets available to the investor. All assets are uncorrelated.

| Asset | Expected Return | Standard Deviation |
|---|---|---|
| A | 10% | 25% |
| B | 10% | 25% |
| C | 10% | 25% |
| D | 2% | 0% |

The table below describes two different portfolio investment options available to the investor:

| Asset | Portfolio 1 Weights | Portfolio 2 Weights |
|---|---|---|
| A | 30% | 30% |
| B | 0% | 30% |
| C | 0% | 30% |
| D | 70% | 10% |

## Part a (1 point)

Define risk pooling and risk sharing. Provide an example of how each is achieved in the insurance industry.

### Explanation
- Risk Pooling:  increase investment by adding uncorrelated securities to a portfolio
- Risk Sharing:  keep total investment fixed and allocate amongst additional securities
- Risk Pooling Example:  selling additional policies to uncorrelated insureds
- Risk Sharing Example (1):  selling more insurance but cede some business to reinsurers
- Risk Sharing Example (2): selling shares of the company to additional investors

### Examiner Report
In this part the candidate was expected to define and provide insurance relevant examples for both risk pooling and risk sharing.
In order to receive full credit the candidates needed to fully define risk pooling and risk sharing including that additional risks were uncorrelated (or similar adjective) and that for risk sharing the portfolio size remained fix.
Common mistakes included not fully defining risk pooling or risk sharing and not providing applicable insurance related examples.

## Part b (1.5 points)

Calculate each portfolio's expected return, variance and Sharpe ratio.

### Explanation
**Portfolio 1**

$$E(r) = (0.3)(0.1) + (0.7)(0.02) = 0.044$$

$$\text{Variance} = (0.3^2)(0.25^2) + (0.7^2)(0^2) = 0.005625$$

$$\text{Sharpe} = \frac{0.044 - 0.02}{0.005625^{1/2}} = 0.32$$

**Portfolio 2**

$$E(r) = (0.3)(0.1)(3) + (0.1)(0.02) = 0.092$$

$$\text{Variance} = (0.3^2)(0.25^2)(3) + (0.1^2)(0^2) = 0.016875$$

$$\text{Sharpe} = \frac{0.092 - 0.02}{0.016875^{1/2}} = 0.5543$$

### Examiner Report
In this part the candidate was expected to calculate the expected return, variance, and Sharpe ratio for two different portfolios.
In order to receive full credit the candidates needed to correctly calculate each of the six required elements.
Common mistakes included calculating standard deviation, rather than variance, and using incorrect formulas for variance and/or Sharpe ratio.

## Part c (0.5 points)

An investor currently holds Portfolio 1 and is considering moving all investments from Portfolio 1 into Portfolio 2. Explain how this will affect the investor's overall risk.

### Explanation
This will increase risk as portfolio 2 has a greater variance than portfolio 1

### Examiner Report
In this part the candidate was expected to identify the impact to the total risk by shifting an investment entirely in Portfolio 1 to Portfolio 2.
In order to receive full credit the candidate needed to correctly identify the impact to overall risk and identify a reason for the impact.  Simply stating, "risk increases" was not enough for full credit.
Common mistakes included not providing a reason for the change in risk.

## Part d (1.5 points)

Construct a portfolio that would give the investor the same expected return as Portfolio 1 and the same Sharpe ratio as Portfolio 2. Briefly explain how this will affect the investor's overall risk.

### Explanation
Invest 10% in each of assets A, B and C, and invest the remaining 70% in the risk-free asset D.

$$E(r_p) = (0.1)(3)(0.1) + (0.7)(0.02) = 0.044$$

$$Var_p = (3)(0.1)^2(0.25)^2 = 0.00188$$

$$\text{Sharpe}_p = \frac{0.044 - 0.02}{0.00188^{1/2}} = 0.5543$$

The overall risk is reduced, since the risk measure, standard deviation, is reduced from 0.075 to 0.043.

Alternatively:

Combine Portfolio 2 with the risk-free asset D in proportions $w$ and $1 - w$. Matching Portfolio 1's expected return and Portfolio 2's Sharpe ratio:

$$E(r_c) = 0.044 = w(r_p) + (1 - w)(0.02)$$

$$\text{Sharpe}_c = \frac{r_p - 0.02}{sd_p} = 0.5543$$

$$sd_c = \frac{0.044 - 0.02}{0.5543} = 0.0433$$

$$sd_c = w \times sd_p = w(0.1299) = 0.0433 \rightarrow w = 0.3333$$

Invest 33.33% in Portfolio 2 and 66.67% in D. Then $sd_c = (0.3333)(0.1299) = 0.0433 < 0.075$, so this will reduce overall risk.

### Examiner Report
In this part the candidate was asked to construct a portfolio with certain characteristics of Portfolio 1 and Portfolio 2.
In order to receive full credit the candidate needed to correctly calculate the percentage allocation to each of assets A, B, C, and D as well as illustrate that the resulting expected return matched that of Portfolio 1 and the resulting Sharpe ratio matched that of Portfolio 2.  The candidate also needed to state the impact to overall risk of the new portfolio compared to Portfolio 1 or 2.
Common mistakes included incorrectly calculating the percentages to each asset, and not identifying the impact to overall risk.
