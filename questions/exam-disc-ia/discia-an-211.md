An engineering firm's professional liability policy has a \$1,000,000 limit with defense costs inside the limit and a soft hammer clause under which the insurer pays 50% of loss and defense incurred after a refused settlement. After \$100,000 of defense, the insurer recommends settling for \$250,000, and the firm refuses. The case goes to trial, with \$200,000 more of defense costs, and ends in a \$600,000 judgment. How much does the insurer pay?

- A) \$350,000
- B) \$450,000
- C) \$625,000
- D) \$900,000

## Explanation

Total cost is the judgment plus all defense: $I + E = 600{,}000 + 100{,}000 + 200{,}000 = 900{,}000$. The insurer pays the refused settlement plus defense to date in full, then half of everything above that:

$$
\begin{align*}
S + E_0 &= 250{,}000 + 100{,}000 \\
&= 350{,}000 \\
\text{Paid} &= 350{,}000 + 0.50\,(900{,}000 - 350{,}000) \\
&= \mathbf{625{,}000}
\end{align*}
$$

The total is inside the \$1,000,000 limit, so the limit does not bind.

- **A** is the full hammer clause, under which the insurer pays nothing after the refusal.
- **B** shares only the later defense costs, $\text{Paid} = 350{,}000 + 0.5\,(200{,}000)$, leaving the judgment above the refused settlement out of the split.
- **D** ignores the hammer clause and pays the whole cost, as if the firm had not refused the recommended settlement.
