Given the following information for a book of business as of December 31, 2016:

| Calendar Year | Earned Premium (\$000) |
|---|---|
| 2015 | 3,910 |
| 2016 | 4,410 |

**Rate Change History**

| Effective Date | Average Rate Change |
|---|---|
| July 1, 2014 | -2.0% |
| July 1, 2015 | 4.2% |
| July 1, 2016 | 3.6% |

**Reported Loss and ALAE (\$000) Capped at \$100,000 as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 1,116 | 1,448 | 1,610 |
| 2015 | 1,975 | 2,572 | |
| 2016 | 2,145 | | |

**Excess Loss and ALAE (\$000) History — Trended Reported Loss and ALAE**

| Accident Year | Unlimited | Excess of \$100,000 |
|---|---|---|
| 2009 | 3,538 | 718 |
| 2010 | 3,193 | 130 |
| 2011 | 1,990 | 234 |
| 2012 | 4,580 | 1,949 |
| 2013 | 2,369 | 120 |

- All policies are annual.
- Exposures are written evenly throughout each calendar year.
- Annual premium trend = 2.8%.
- Annual frequency trend = -2%.
- Annual severity trend capped at \$100,000 = 4%.
- Fixed expense ratio = 4%.
- Variable expense ratio = 22%.
- Profit and contingencies provision = 6%.
- ULAE provision = 6% of loss and ALAE.
- Rates are to be in effect for one year.
- There is no loss development beyond 36 months.
- Assume full credibility.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the ultimate loss and ALAE capped at \$100,000 for accident years 2015 and 2016.

### Answer
AY 2015: \$2,860 thousand; AY 2016: \$3,100 thousand

### Explanation
Develop the *capped* triangle with factors derived from the *capped* triangle — using capped data throughout keeps large claims from destabilizing the link ratios, and the excess loss provision in part b restores the uncapped level later.

**Step 1 — age-to-age factors.**

| Accident Year | 12-24 | 24-36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | $1{,}448/1{,}116 = 1.2975$ | $1{,}610/1{,}448 = 1.1119$ |
| 2015 | $2{,}572/1{,}975 = 1.3023$ | |
| **Selected (average)** | **1.2999** | **1.1119** |

**Step 2 — cumulative development factors.** There is no development beyond 36 months, so the 36-to-ultimate tail is 1.000:

$$\text{CDF}_{24} = 1.1119 \times 1.000 = 1.1119 \qquad \text{CDF}_{12} = 1.2999 \times 1.1119 = 1.4453$$

**Step 3 — apply each CDF to the loss at its own maturity.** As of December 31, 2016, AY 2015 is at 24 months and AY 2016 is at 12 months:

$$\text{AY 2015 ultimate} = 2{,}572 \times 1.1119 = \mathbf{2{,}860}$$

$$\text{AY 2016 ultimate} = 2{,}145 \times 1.4453 = \mathbf{3{,}100}$$

(both in \$000, capped at \$100,000 per occurrence).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to form age-to-age factors from the capped triangle, cumulate them into development factors, and apply each factor to the loss at the matching maturity.

Common errors included pairing a cumulative factor with the wrong accident year's losses; applying a single age-to-age factor instead of the cumulative factor (in particular developing AY 2016 only from 12 to 24 months); and loading the losses for excess claims at this stage, when the question explicitly asks for losses capped at \$100,000.

## Part b (4.5 points)

Determine the indicated rate change effective July 1, 2017 using the results from part a. above.

### Answer
+28.3%

### Explanation
This is a loss ratio indication:

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{\text{trended ultimate loss \& LAE ratio} + F}{1 - V - Q_T} - 1$$

with $F = 4\%$, $V = 22\%$ and $Q_T = 6\%$. Everything below is the work of getting the loss ratio onto the level of the proposed rates.

**Step 1 — trend periods.** Rates take effect July 1, 2017 and are in effect one year, so the last policy is written June 30, 2018. Policies are annual and written evenly, so the average written date is January 1, 2018 and the average accident (and average earned) date of the proposed period is **July 1, 2018**. The experience periods are calendar/accident years, whose average dates are July 1 of each year:

| Year | From | To | Trend period |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 7/1/2015 | 7/1/2018 | 3 years |
| 2016 | 7/1/2016 | 7/1/2018 | 2 years |

The same periods apply to premium and to losses, because calendar year earned premium and accident year losses share the same average date.

**Step 2 — on-level factors (parallelogram method).** Cumulate the rate changes into a rate level index, starting at 1.000 before July 1, 2014:

| In effect from | Change | Cumulative index |
|---|---|---|
| before 7/1/2014 | — | 1.00000 |
| 7/1/2014 | -2.0% | 0.98000 |
| 7/1/2015 | +4.2% | $0.98 \times 1.042 = 1.02116$ |
| 7/1/2016 | +3.6% | $1.02116 \times 1.036 = 1.05792$ |

The current rate level is 1.05792. With annual policies written evenly and every change falling at mid-year, each calendar year splits into a $\tfrac{1}{8}$ triangle at the level two changes back, $\tfrac{3}{4}$ at the level in force through the year, and a $\tfrac{1}{8}$ triangle at the level introduced on July 1:

$$\overline{RL}_{2015} = 0.125(1.00000) + 0.750(0.98000) + 0.125(1.02116) = 0.98765$$

$$\overline{RL}_{2016} = 0.125(0.98000) + 0.750(1.02116) + 0.125(1.05792) = 1.02061$$

$$\text{OLF}_{2015} = \frac{1.05792}{0.98765} = 1.0712 \qquad \text{OLF}_{2016} = \frac{1.05792}{1.02061} = 1.0366$$

**Step 3 — trended on-level earned premium.** Premium trend of 2.8% is applied over the periods from step 1:

| CY | Earned premium | OLF | Premium trend | Trended on-level EP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 3,910 | 1.0712 | $1.028^3 = 1.0864$ | 4,550 |
| 2016 | 4,410 | 1.0366 | $1.028^2 = 1.0568$ | 4,831 |

**Step 4 — trended ultimate loss and ALAE.** The capped severity trend is 4% and the frequency trend is -2%, so pure premium trends at $1.04 \times 0.98 = 1.0192$ per year:

| AY | Ultimate capped (part a) | Loss trend | Trended capped loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 2,860 | $1.0192^3 = 1.0587$ | 3,028 |
| 2016 | 3,100 | $1.0192^2 = 1.0388$ | 3,220 |

**Step 5 — excess loss provision.** The losses above are capped at \$100,000 but the rates must fund losses in full, so an excess factor is built from the trended history. The exhibit gives unlimited and excess losses, so capped losses are the difference:

| AY | Unlimited | Excess of \$100,000 | Capped |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2009 | 3,538 | 718 | 2,820 |
| 2010 | 3,193 | 130 | 3,063 |
| 2011 | 1,990 | 234 | 1,756 |
| 2012 | 4,580 | 1,949 | 2,631 |
| 2013 | 2,369 | 120 | 2,249 |
| **Total** | **15,670** | **3,151** | **12,519** |

$$\text{Excess ratio} = \frac{3{,}151}{12{,}519} = 0.2517 \qquad \text{Excess loss factor} = 1.2517$$

The ratio is taken to **capped** loss, not to unlimited loss, because the factor multiplies capped losses. A five-year all-year ratio is used because the individual years are extremely volatile — 0.042 in 2010 against 0.741 in 2012 — which is the whole reason excess losses are handled by a long-term average rather than left in the two-year experience period.

**Step 6 — loss and LAE ratios.** Load each trended capped loss for excess losses and then for ULAE at 6% of loss and ALAE:

| Year | Trended capped loss | $\times 1.2517$ | $\times 1.06$ | Trended on-level EP | Loss & LAE ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 3,028 | 3,790 | 4,018 | 4,550 | 88.3% |
| 2016 | 3,220 | 4,030 | 4,272 | 4,831 | 88.4% |

The two years are stable, so select the all-year ratio, **88.4%**.

**Step 7 — indication.** Fixed expenses enter the numerator as a ratio to premium; only variable expenses and profit go in the denominator:

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{0.884 + 0.04}{1 - 0.22 - 0.06} - 1 = \frac{0.924}{0.72} - 1 = 1.2833 - 1 = \mathbf{+28.3\%}$$

Alternatively, the excess provision can be selected as the average of the individual accident year excess-to-capped ratios (0.255, 0.042, 0.133, 0.741, 0.053), giving 24.5% and a factor of 1.245. Carrying that through a capped loss ratio of $3{,}028/4{,}550 = 66.5\%$ and $3{,}220/4{,}831 = 66.7\%$, selected 66.6%:

$$\frac{0.666(1.06)(1.245) + 0.04}{1 - 0.22 - 0.06} - 1 = +27.6\%$$

Either selection is defensible; the indication is an increase of roughly 28%.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to assemble a full loss ratio indication from the developed losses of part a: on-level the earned premium with the parallelogram method, trend both premium and losses over periods running to July 1, 2018, load capped losses for excess losses and ULAE, form the loss ratio, and combine it with the fixed expense, variable expense and profit provisions.

Common errors included using incorrect trend periods or omitting trend altogether; missing one of the three rate changes when computing the average rate level, or mis-measuring the parallelogram areas; forming the excess provision as a ratio to unlimited losses rather than to the non-excess (capped) losses actually being loaded, or omitting excess losses entirely; placing the fixed expense ratio in the denominator instead of the numerator; and stopping at the indicated rate *level* without subtracting 1 to state the indicated rate *change*.

## Part c (0.25 points)

Briefly describe one reason the insurer might not take the full rate change determined in part b. above.

### Explanation
An indication of nearly +28% is far larger than a typical filing, and marketplace considerations can override it. Competitors are unlikely to be moving rates by that much, so taking the full increase at once would price the book well above the market, drive renewals to competitors and shrink the portfolio. The insurer may prefer to cap the change and phase the increase in over successive filings to protect retention and market share.

Other acceptable reasons include regulatory constraints — the change requires prior approval in many jurisdictions and a regulator may not accept an increase of that size — and operational constraints, such as systems that cannot implement the revised rates in the intended timeframe.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to give one specific reason an insurer would deviate from its indication, drawn from marketing, competitive, regulatory or operational considerations.

Common errors included arguing that the volatility of the excess loss provision justified taking less than the indication, and arguing that a two-year experience period was not credible enough — the question states that full credibility is to be assumed.
