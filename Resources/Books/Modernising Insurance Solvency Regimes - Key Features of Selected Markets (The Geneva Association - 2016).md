A study by The Geneva Association comparing insurance solvency regimes, most of them recently modernised, along selected essential elements. Based on a questionnaire answered by eleven insurance groups and eight supervisory bodies, it covers Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, the European Union, Japan, Mexico, Singapore, South Africa, Switzerland and the United States, and looks at how assets and liabilities are valued, how regulatory capital requirements are set, whether internal models are allowed, and how capital resources are assessed.

> - [[Exam 6I (CAS)|Exam 6I]] — objectives B2, B4

## 1 Foreword
- The primary goal of any insurance solvency regime is to secure the interests of policyholders; one key element is the requirement for insurers to hold capital.
- The IAIS is developing its global Insurance Capital Standard (ICS) as part of ComFrame; the report sheds light on key features of solvency regimes in selected jurisdictions and compares new and emerging regimes with more established ones.

## 2 Introduction
- ComFrame is a set of international supervisory requirements focusing on the group-wide supervision of internationally active insurance groups (IAIGs); like other global standard setting bodies, the IAIS has no legal authority to prescribe or enforce its standards, including the ICS.

## 3 Key Findings
- Regulatory capital requirements in the countries concerned are [[Risk-Based Capital|risk-based]] or developing into being more risk-based over time; strategic and reputational risks are generally not accounted for in the capital calculation.
- Assets are valued in many regimes on principles compatible with IFRS/GAAP or local statutory rules; liability valuation is heterogeneous across jurisdictions.
- Capital requirements are in most cases set at a predetermined confidence level and specified at solo entity level; group-level requirements do not exist in all the countries examined.
- Regimes generally provide a "ladder of intervention"; internal models can be used only upon supervisory approval; capital resources are classified into two or three tiers by loss absorbency.
- Qualitative requirements are imposed in all regimes, and an [[ORSA|Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (ORSA)]] in a large number of the countries examined.

## 4 Choice of Jurisdictions and Methodology
- The countries were chosen to give a broad, geographically representative sample of countries that have adopted a risk-based solvency capital approach or are doing so.
- The questionnaire, addressed to one company and one supervisory representative per jurisdiction, covered valuation principles, risk sensitivity, calibration, qualitative requirements, group issues, internal models, multi-layer supervisory systems and qualifying capital.

## 7 Concluding Remarks
- All regimes examined follow a risk-based approach to the regulatory capital requirement; required capital is often set at a confidence level of 99 or 99.5 per cent over a one-year horizon, mostly with VaR and in some cases TailVaR.
- Asset values are often derived directly from IFRS, while liability valuation is heterogeneous in methodologies, assumptions, conservatism, margins over current estimates (MOCE) and adequacy testing; qualitative requirements including an ORSA are prescribed in most regimes.
- The common elements are interpreted and applied in different ways, which the IAIS will have to take into account in striving for a single ICS.
