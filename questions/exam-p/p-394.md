The loss, $X$, subject to reimbursement under an insurance policy, has density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\left(\dfrac{1}{\beta}\right)e^{-(x-d)/\beta}, & \text{for } x \geq d \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $d$ is the deductible, and $\beta$ is a positive constant.

Determine the absolute value of the difference between the mode of $X$ and the 10th percentile of $X$.

- A) $\beta\ln\left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right)$
- B) $\beta\ln\left(\dfrac{10}{9}\right)$
- C) $\beta\ln\left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right) + d$
- D) $\beta\ln\left(\dfrac{10}{9}\right) + d$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right)$

## Explanation

This is an exponential density shifted so that it starts at $d$ rather than 0.

**Mode.** Differentiating,

$$
f'(x) = -\frac{1}{\beta^2}e^{-(x-d)/\beta} < 0
$$

so $f$ is strictly decreasing on its whole domain. The density is largest at the left endpoint, and the mode is $x = d$.

**10th percentile.** Integrate to get the distribution function and set it to 0.10:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.10 &= F(p) = \int_d^p \frac{1}{\beta}e^{-(x-d)/\beta}\,dx \\
     &= 1 - e^{-(p-d)/\beta}
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
\begin{align*}
0.90 &= e^{-(p-d)/\beta} \\
\ln(0.9) &= -\frac{p-d}{\beta} \\
p &= d - \beta\ln(0.9) \\
  &= d + \beta\ln\left(\frac{10}{9}\right)
\end{align*}
$$

**The difference.** The deductible $d$ appears in both quantities and cancels:

$$
\begin{align*}
|p - d| &= \left|d + \beta\ln\left(\tfrac{10}{9}\right) - d\right| \\
        &= \mathbf{\beta\ln\left(\frac{10}{9}\right)}
\end{align*}
$$

Choices (C) and (D) are the 10th percentile itself rather than its distance from the mode — they keep the $d$ that the subtraction removes. Choice (A) uses $\ln(11/10)$, which comes from writing $-\ln(0.9)$ as $\ln(1.1)$; the correct reciprocal of $0.9$ is $10/9$, not $11/10$.
