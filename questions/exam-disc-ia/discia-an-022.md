For one group of policies at year end, an insurer has unearned premium of \$6.0 million and \$0.5 million of future installment premiums on those policies. For the remaining coverage it expects \$5.6 million of losses, \$0.7 million of loss adjustment expense and \$0.4 million of maintenance costs. Commissions of \$0.9 million were paid and expensed at issue. A separately marketed group is expected to earn a \$0.3 million profit. Under SSAP No. 53, what premium deficiency reserve is required for the first group?

- A) \$0
- B) \$0.2 million
- C) \$0.7 million
- D) \$1.1 million

## Explanation

A premium deficiency reserve is needed when anticipated losses, LAE, acquisition costs and maintenance costs exceed the unearned premium plus future installments. Acquisition costs already expensed are left out.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Costs} &= 5.6 + 0.7 + 0.4 \\
&= 6.7 \\
\text{Resources} &= 6.0 + 0.5 \\
&= 6.5 \\
\text{PDR} &= 6.7 - 6.5 \\
&= 0.2
\end{align*}
$$

Deficiencies are not offset by anticipated profits in other policy groupings. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — offsets the deficiency with the other group's \$0.3 million profit, which SSAP No. 53 forbids.
- **C** — leaves out the \$0.5 million of future installments, giving \$0.7 million.
- **D** — counts the \$0.9 million of commissions that were already expensed, giving \$1.1 million.
