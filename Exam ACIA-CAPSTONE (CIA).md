# ACIA Capstone Exam
The **ACIA Capstone Exam** is the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' exam toward the Associate (ACIA) designation.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The overarching goal of the ACIA Capstone Exam is to help participants prepare for the actuarial profession and tasks related to entry-level actuarial positions. Participants are expected to demonstrate integration of knowledge of actuarial concepts and communicate results with a few short assignments. The exam assumes knowledge acquired in a recognized university degree, as well as from the ACIA module, focusing on application and communication.
>
> The ACIA Capstone Exam is administered as an open-book, six-hour exam, requiring analysis in the context of a problem and submission of written responses to specified tasks. The exam is split into two sections: a four-hour mandatory common section and a two-hour specialized section, long- or short-term, selected at exam registration.
>
> Participants will complete the exam through the learning management system, with secure online proctoring. The exam is open book and participants are responsible for bringing their own reference material to the exam. Strict adherence to the exam rules and regulations, which will be communicated in advance, is mandatory.
>
> General learning objectives are shown below, with Bloom’s taxonomy classification in parentheses.
>
> 1. Demonstrate a clear understanding of actuarial problems. (B5)
>
> 2. Apply mathematical, statistical, and financial models and methods to solve actuarial problems. (C3)
>
> 3. Produce efficient and readable computer codes to implement actuarial models in various platforms, such as R and Excel. (C6)
>
> 4. Perform an actuarial analysis to support decision-making in a given business environment. (C4)
>
> 5. Formulate and support recommendations based on actuarial analysis in a business context. (C6)
>
> 6. Write an effective communication (e.g., executive summary, email, presentation, excerpts from a report) on assumptions, methods, and results of actuarial analysis to an appropriate audience (e.g., peers, internal stakeholders, management). (C6)
>
> The exam is composed of two parts – Part 1 Common (C) and Part 2a Specialized Long-term (SLT) or 2b Specialized Short-term (SST), as outlined below. Please refer to the Appendix to the CIA Capstone Exam Syllabus and to the ACIA Module Syllabus for more details on the background knowledge.
>
> - 1. C (4 hours) — from an accredited university degree: A3.9 to A3.13; A4.1 to A4.4; A4.7 to A4.14; A5, except A5.3, A5.4 and A5.5 — from the ACIA module: A, B, C
>
> - 2a. SLT (2 hours) — from an accredited university degree: Common knowledge, A4.5 and A4.6 — from the ACIA module: A, B, C, D1, E
>
> - 2b. SST (2 hours) — from an accredited university degree: Common knowledge and A4.15; A5.3 and A5.4 — from the ACIA module: A, B, C, D2, E
>
> Note: Prerequisite knowledge is expected to be acquired either through the accredited university degree or another actuarial organizations and ACIA modules. While some references relative to the Capstone Exam topics are found in the appendix, it is not possible to provide all potential references to all sections as accredited universities and other actuarial organizations use various source materials.
>
> Performance of a participant’s answers will be evaluated on the following principles:
>
> 1. The overall quality of the answers’ technical components:
>    - a. The accuracy of the steps leading to the results (e.g., method, code, answers, formulas used in worksheets).
>    - b. The relevance and coherence of the analyses performed with code and worksheets provided.
>
> 2. The overall quality of the communication:
>    - a. The ability to effectively communicate a complex analysis and solution to the targeted audience for the appropriate purpose.
>    - b. The ability to appropriately address the problem or the given mandate.
>
> Participants are highly encouraged to document and comment their computer code to help demonstrate their ability to translate an abstract actuarial problem into a computer program. Performance will also be assessed on the nature of the assignments.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- A1. Probability
>
> **A1.1 Probability**
>
> 1. Define set functions, [[Sample Space|sample space]], and [[Event|events]]. Define [[Probability|probability]] as a set function on a collection of [[Event|events]] and state the basic [[Axioms of Probability|axioms of probability]].
> 2. Calculate [[Probability|probabilities]] of [[Mutually Exclusive Events|mutually exclusive events]].
> 3. Calculate [[Probability|probabilities]] using addition and [[Multiplication Rule|multiplication rules]].
> 4. Define independence and calculate [[Probability|probability]] of [[Independent Events|independent events]].
> 5. Calculate [[Probability|probabilities]] using [[Combinatorics|combinatorics]], such as combinations and [[Permutation|permutations]].
> 6. Define and calculate [[Conditional Probability|conditional probabilities]].
> 7. State [[Bayes's Theorem|Bayes’s theorem]] and use it to calculate [[Conditional Probability|conditional probabilities]].
>
> **A1.2 Univariate random variables**
>
> 1. Explain and apply the concepts of [[Random Variable|random variables]], [[Probability|probability]] and [[Probability Density Function (PDF)|probability density functions]], and [[Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)|cumulative distribution functions]].
> 2. Calculate [[Conditional Probability|conditional probabilities]].
> 3. Explain and calculate expected value, [[Mode|mode]], [[Median|median]], [[Percentile|percentile]], and [[Higher Moment|higher moments]].
> 4. Explain and calculate [[Variance|variance]], [[Standard Deviation|standard deviation]], and [[Coefficient of Variation|coefficient of variation]].
> 5. Define probability-generating functions and moment-generating functions and use them to calculate [[Probability|probabilities]] and [[Moment|moments]].
> 6. Determine the sum of [[Independent Random Variables|independent random variables]] (Poisson and normal).
>
> **A1.3 Multivariate random variables**
>
> 1. Explain and perform calculations concerning [[Joint Probability|joint probability]] and [[Probability Density Function (PDF)|probability density functions]] and [[Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)|cumulative distribution functions]].
> 2. Determine conditional and [[Marginal Probability|marginal probability]] and [[Probability Density Function (PDF)|probability density functions]] and [[Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)|cumulative distribution functions]].
> 3. Calculate [[Moment|moments]] for joint, conditional, and [[Marginal Random Variable|marginal random variables]].
> 4. Explain and apply [[Joint Moment-Generating|joint moment-generating]] functions.
> 5. Calculate [[Variance and Standard Deviation|variance and standard deviation]] for conditional and [[Marginal Probability Distribution|marginal probability distributions]].
> 6. Calculate [[Joint Moment|joint moments]], such as the [[Covariance|covariance]] and the [[Correlation Coefficient|correlation coefficient]].
> 7. Determine the [[Probability Distributions|distribution]] of a transformation of jointly [[Distributed Random Variable|distributed random variables]].
> 8. Determine the [[Probability Distributions|distribution]] of [[Order Statistics|order statistics]] from a set of [[Independent Random Variables|independent random variables]].
> 9. Calculate [[Probability|probabilities]] and [[Moments for Linear Combinations|moments for linear combinations]] of [[Independent Random Variables|independent random variables]].
> 10. State and apply the [[Central Limit Theorem|central limit theorem]].
