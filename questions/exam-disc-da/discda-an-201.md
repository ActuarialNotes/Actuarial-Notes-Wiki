An analyst produces four graphics while studying homeowners water damage claims:

I. Forty histograms of claim size, one for each state, looked at for a few seconds each
II. A scatter plot of claim size against home age, drawn to see whether any pattern exists
III. A line chart for the board with one peril highlighted and the title "Water damage now drives half of our claim growth"

Which of these are presentation (explanatory) visualizations rather than exploratory ones?

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) I and II only
- D) III only

## Explanation

Exploratory graphics are made quickly, for the analyst, to find out what the data say; most are looked at briefly and thrown away. A presentation, or explanatory, graphic is built for an audience to show a finding already established, with a title that states the point and attention drawn to it. Only III is made for an audience and carries a stated finding. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — forty histograms scanned a few seconds each is the textbook picture of exploration: turning over many rocks to find a gemstone.
- **B** — a plot drawn "to see whether any pattern exists" is a question, not an answer; it is exploratory.
- **C** — combines the two exploratory graphics and leaves out the only one built for an audience.
