**The Facility Association** (FA) is the non-profit entity through which Canada's automobile insurers jointly provide coverage to drivers who cannot obtain it in the voluntary market. Every insurer licensed to write auto in a participating jurisdiction is a member, and each shares FA's results in proportion to its **voluntary market share** — so all insurers bear the residual market whether or not they service it.

> $$\text{Member share of FA result} = \frac{\text{Member's voluntary premium}}{\text{Total voluntary premium}}$$

- **How business reaches FA.** A driver unable to obtain coverage applies through a broker to a **servicing carrier** — an insurer that administers FA business under contract, issuing policies and handling claims for a fee, but not bearing the risk. FA's rates are filed with each provincial regulator like any insurer's and are typically well above voluntary market rates.
- **FA's programs:** the **Facility Association Residual Market** (FARM) for private passenger and commercial auto that no insurer will write, plus **[[Risk Sharing Pool|risk sharing pools]]** in several provinces — a different mechanism, described below — and an Uninsured Automobile Fund in some jurisdictions.
- **FARM versus a risk sharing pool** is the distinction most often examined:
  - *FARM* — the policy is issued by FA, the driver knows, and the rate is FA's higher rate.
  - *[[Risk Sharing Pool|RSP]]* — the policy is issued by an ordinary insurer at its ordinary rate; the insurer transfers the risk to the pool without the driver knowing.
- **Who ends up in FARM:** drivers with serious conviction or claim records, unusual commercial vehicles, drivers with no insurance history who fail every filed underwriting rule, and — in provinces where rates are suppressed — ordinary risks that no longer fit anyone's rules.
- **Interpreting FA volume.** Growth signals rate inadequacy or classification restriction, not a change in driver behaviour. FA publishes volume by province, which makes it a public indicator of each province's rate regime.

> [!example]- FARM or the Risk Sharing Pool? {Example}
> For each driver, say which residual mechanism is likely to apply and what the driver experiences.
>
> 1. A driver with two impaired-driving convictions in three years.
> 2. A newly licensed $19$-year-old whom the insurer would rather not write but who meets its filed rules.
> 3. An owner-operator with a specialty heavy vehicle no insurer's rating plan contemplates.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > 1. **FARM.** No insurer's filed underwriting rules accept this record, so the driver applies through a broker to a servicing carrier and pays FA's rates — several times a standard premium. The driver knows they are in the residual market, and that is intentional: the price signal is part of the deterrent.
> > 2. **Risk sharing pool.** The insurer is obliged to write the driver ([[Take-All-Comers Rule]]) at its filed rate because the driver meets its rules. Believing the filed rate inadequate for this risk, the insurer writes the policy and cedes it to the pool. **The driver pays the normal rate and never learns of the transfer** — which is exactly the design intent: the pool exists so that take-all-comers does not force insurers to absorb losses on business regulation compels them to write.
> > 3. **FARM**, most likely, since the vehicle falls outside every voluntary rating plan. FA maintains commercial and specialty programs for precisely this case.
> >
> > The pattern: **FARM handles risks nobody will write; the pool handles risks everybody must write.** Both socialise the result across the industry by market share, but only one is visible to the policyholder.
