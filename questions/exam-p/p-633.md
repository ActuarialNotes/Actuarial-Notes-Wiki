An insurance company sells auto policies to a trucking company and a shuttle service. The number of auto claims filed by the trucking company and the number of auto claims filed by the shuttle service are independent Poisson random variables having variances 2 and 3 respectively.

Calculate the probability that the trucking company files exactly 6 auto claims, given that the trucking company and the shuttle service together file a total of 8 auto claims.

- A) $0.003$
- B) $0.012$
- C) $0.041$
- D) $0.054$
- E) $0.184$

## Explanation

For a Poisson distribution the variance equals the mean, so $N_1$ (trucking) has mean 2 and $N_2$ (shuttle) has mean 3. A sum of independent Poissons is Poisson, so $N_1 + N_2$ has mean 5.

The conditioning event forces $N_2 = 2$ whenever $N_1 = 6$:

$$
P[N_1 = 6 \mid N_1 + N_2 = 8] = \frac{P[N_1 = 6]\,P[N_2 = 2]}{P[N_1 + N_2 = 8]}
$$

Evaluating each piece:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[N_1 = 6] &= \frac{2^6 e^{-2}}{6!} = 0.01203 \\
P[N_2 = 2] &= \frac{3^2 e^{-3}}{2!} = 0.22404 \\
P[N_1 + N_2 = 8] &= \frac{5^8 e^{-5}}{8!} = 0.06528
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
\frac{(0.01203)(0.22404)}{0.06528} = \mathbf{0.0413}
$$

There is a shortcut: conditional on a Poisson total of 8, each claim independently belongs to the trucking company with probability $2/5$, so the count is binomial and $\binom{8}{6}(0.4)^6(0.6)^2 = 0.0413$ — the same number.

Choice (B), 0.012, is the unconditional $P[N_1 = 6]$, with the total of 8 ignored. Conditioning raises it more than threefold: knowing eight claims arrived makes six from the trucking company far less remarkable.
