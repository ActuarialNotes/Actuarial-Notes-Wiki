A general contractor completed an office building last year; its own crew built the walls, and an electrical subcontractor installed the wiring. This year faulty wiring starts a fire that damages the walls, the wiring itself and a tenant's computers. The owner and tenant sue the contractor. Under the contractor's CGL (CG 00 01 04 13), which damage does the "damage to your work" exclusion remove?

- A) The walls and the wiring, because both are the contractor's work in the products-completed operations hazard
- B) None of the three, because the damage arose out of work a subcontractor performed
- C) Only the walls, because the contractor's own crew performed them
- D) All three, because the damage arose out of the contractor's completed work

## Explanation

Exclusion l removes property damage to "your work" arising out of it and included in the products-completed operations hazard, but it does not apply if the damaged work *or the work out of which the damage arises* was performed on the named insured's behalf by a subcontractor. The fire arose out of the subcontractor's wiring, so the exception reaches the walls as well as the wiring. The tenant's computers are not the contractor's work at all. None of the three is removed, so the answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** stops at the first sentence of the exclusion and ignores the subcontractor exception.
- **C** applies the exception only to work the subcontractor *performed*, missing that it also applies when the damage *arises out of* a subcontractor's work.
- **D** treats the exclusion as reaching other people's property; it applies only to the insured's own work.
