A policy has an ordinary deductible of 500, a coinsurance percentage of 80%, and a maximum payment by the insurer of 5,000.

Calculate the smallest ground-up loss for which the insurer pays the maximum amount.

- A) $5{,}500$
- B) $6{,}250$
- C) $6{,}750$
- D) $7{,}000$
- E) $7{,}250$

## Explanation

The payment random variable applies the provisions in order — deductible, then coinsurance, then the cap:

$$Y = \min\!\bigl(0.80\,(X - 500)_+,\; 5{,}000\bigr)$$

The maximum is first reached when the coinsured excess equals 5,000:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.80\,(X - 500) &= 5{,}000 \\
X - 500 &= \frac{5{,}000}{0.80} = 6{,}250 \\
X &= \mathbf{6{,}750}
\end{align*}
$$

The two common errors are both on the list. Choice (B) forgets to add the deductible back after dividing by the coinsurance factor. Choice (A) applies the cap before the coinsurance, as if the insurer paid $80\%$ of a limit of 5,000.

Note the maximum **payment** of 5,000 is a limit on $Y$, not on $X$; a policy stating a maximum *covered loss* of 5,000 would mean something different.
