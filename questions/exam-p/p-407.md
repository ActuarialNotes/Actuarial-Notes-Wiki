The lifetime of a new electronic device is exponentially distributed with a median of three years.

Calculate the variance of the remaining lifetime, given the device did not fail before 0.5 years.

- A) $3.8$
- B) $4.3$
- C) $9.0$
- D) $14.7$
- E) $18.7$

## Explanation

The mean $\beta$ is not given directly, so recover it from the median:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 3] &= 0.5 = e^{-3/\beta} \\
\ln 0.5 &= -\frac{3}{\beta} \\
\beta &= \frac{3}{\ln 2} = 4.32809
\end{align*}
$$

An exponential distribution has variance equal to the square of its mean:

$$
\text{Var}(X) = \beta^2 = 18.73232
$$

Now the conditioning. The exponential distribution is **memoryless**: given survival past 0.5 years, the *remaining* lifetime has exactly the same exponential distribution, with the same mean $\beta$. So the conditioning changes nothing, and

$$
\text{Var}(\text{remaining lifetime} \mid X > 0.5) = \beta^2 = \mathbf{18.73}
$$

Choice (C), 9.0, is the square of the *median* — taking 3 as the mean rather than as the median. Because $\beta = 3/\ln 2$ exceeds 3, the true variance is more than twice that. The 0.5-year survival information is deliberately irrelevant; recognising that is the point of the question.
