Let $X$ and $Y$ be random variables with $E(X) = 2$, $E(Y) = 0$, $\text{Var}(X) = 1$, $\text{Var}(Y) = 4$ and $\rho = \text{Corr}(X, Y) = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Determine the constant $c$ for which $X + Y$ and $cX + Y$ are uncorrelated.

- A) $-3$
- B) $-5/2$
- C) $-2$
- D) $-5/6$
- E) $-9/11$

## Explanation

First convert the correlation into a covariance:

$$
\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \rho\,\sigma_X\,\sigma_Y = 0.5(1)(2) = 1
$$

Uncorrelated means zero covariance. Expanding the covariance bilinearly:

$$
\begin{align*}
0 &= \text{Cov}(X + Y,\; cX + Y) \\
  &= c\,\text{Var}(X) + \text{Cov}(X, Y) + c\,\text{Cov}(Y, X) + \text{Var}(Y) \\
  &= c(1) + 1 + c(1) + 4 \\
  &= 2c + 5
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
c = \mathbf{-\frac{5}{2}}
$$

Choice (A), $-3$, uses the *correlation* $\rho = 1/2$ where the covariance belongs: $c(1) + 0.5 + c(0.5) + 4 = 0$ gives $c = -3$. Correlation is the standardized quantity; only covariance is bilinear, so it is $\text{Cov}(X,Y) = 1$ that goes into the expansion.
