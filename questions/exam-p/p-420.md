A scientist estimates the time (in tens of millions of years) before a major asteroid will hit the earth using a random variable $X$ with probability density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
xe^{-x}, & \text{for } x > 0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the probability that the next time the earth is hit by a major asteroid occurs between 10 million and 20 million years from now.

- A) $0.0005$
- B) $0.0867$
- C) $0.3298$
- D) $0.6702$
- E) $0.9995$

## Explanation

The units matter: $X$ is measured in *tens of millions* of years, so 10 million years is $x = 1$ and 20 million years is $x = 2$.

Integrate the density over $[1,2]$, using integration by parts with $u = x$ and $dv = e^{-x}dx$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[1 < X < 2] &= \int_1^2 xe^{-x}\,dx \\
             &= -xe^{-x}\bigg|_1^2 + \int_1^2 e^{-x}\,dx \\
             &= \left(-2e^{-2} + e^{-1}\right) - e^{-x}\bigg|_1^2 \\
             &= \left(-2e^{-2} + e^{-1}\right) + \left(-e^{-2} + e^{-1}\right) \\
             &= -3e^{-2} + 2e^{-1} \\
             &= \mathbf{0.329753}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.0005, is what you get by reading the interval as 10 to 20 in the density's own units instead of converting — the far tail of $xe^{-x}$ is essentially empty out there. The density is a gamma with shape 2 and scale 1, whose mean is 2, so an interval straddling the mean should carry a substantial share of the probability, which rules that reading out immediately.
