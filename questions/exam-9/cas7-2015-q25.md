## Part a (0.5 points)

Define each of the following:

- i. Insurance underwriting risk
- ii. Liquidity risk

### Explanation
- **i. Insurance underwriting risk** — the risk associated with writing insurance: inadequate premium to cover the exposure (including product design, inadequate reserve or premium, accumulation, catastrophe and policyholder behaviour risk).
- **ii. Liquidity risk** — the risk of having insufficient *liquid* assets to meet a sudden cash demand or current obligations as they come due.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the definitions of underwriting and liquidity risk. Candidates generally performed very well on this question, particularly the definition of underwriting risk. The most common error was stating that liquidity risk arises from "insufficient assets" without specifying that those assets must be liquid. Some candidates referred to liquidating assets before their maturity; however, the real risk is not being able to sell the assets for their full value.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Identify an event scenario that illustrates comovement of insurance underwriting risk and liquidity risk. Briefly describe how the potential for this scenario could affect the assessment of these two risk types.

### Explanation
Any one scenario affecting both risks, e.g.:

- **A hurricane hitting the East Coast** — for a property insurer, many policyholders are affected and claims increase (underwriting risk). Even with more than enough assets, if they are not liquid or cannot be converted to cash at full value, the company may not be able to pay policyholders within a reasonable timeframe (liquidity risk).
- **A major economic downturn** — e.g. workers compensation claim frequency rises beyond what pricing assumed (an underwriting loss), and the higher claim volume demands payment quickly, possibly forcing assets to be sold at a discount.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify a single event that would affect both underwriting and liquidity risk. Most candidates were able to identify such an event, but some struggled to adequately explain how the event would impact each type of risk. In attempting to describe the impact, some candidates failed to describe how the event would cause an increased demand for liquid assets.

## Part c (0.75 points)

Identify a risk mitigation strategy for the scenario identified in part b. above. Briefly describe how this strategy would reduce each component of risk considered here.

### Explanation
- **Catastrophe reinsurance** — reduces underwriting risk by limiting losses from the catastrophe, and reduces liquidity risk because the reinsurer pays part of the losses, so the primary does not need to raise as much cash as quickly (the cost is spread over time as reinsurance premiums); or
- **A catastrophe bond** — in a large hurricane or earthquake the investors' principal covers a significant portion of the loss (reducing underwriting risk) and provides an influx of liquid assets for the heightened claim payments.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide a risk mitigation strategy to reduce the two types of risk.
This was the most challenging part of the question. Most candidates provided a strategy, but sometimes the strategy only addressed one of underwriting and liquidity risk without addressing the other.
