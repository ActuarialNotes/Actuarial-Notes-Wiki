## Part a (1 point)

Describe two consequences of financial distress for insurance companies.

### Explanation
Two of:

- **The insurance product loses value** — the product is the insurer's promise to pay claims; if funds may not be there, customers demand a discount or shop elsewhere, cutting revenue and profitability and possibly starting a vicious cycle.
- **Raising capital becomes costly** — new shares are issued at a discount and borrowing rates are high; even good assets cannot be sold for the best price because the insurer cannot wait for the best offer.
- **Market overreaction** — the fall in market capital when an insurer enters distress tends to be a multiple of the drop in book value that caused it.
- **Agency problems** — management (acting for shareholders) may be better off taking more risk during distress, at the expense of policyholders, who are the primary debtholders.

### Examiner Report
Candidates scored well on this part. To obtain full credit, the candidate had to provide two valid consequences, and explain why each would occur to an insurance company in financial distress.
Common errors included:

- Insufficient descriptions
- Providing two consequences that were essentially the same, and so only earning credit for one of them.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two reasons why mutual insurance companies tend to purchase more reinsurance than publicly owned insurance companies.

### Explanation
- Mutual companies lack access to capital markets, so they have fewer ways to raise funds if they fall into distress.
- In a mutual the policyholders are the owners, and they are more risk-averse than the shareholders of public companies.

### Examiner Report
Candidates scored well on this part. Only a brief description of each reason was required for full credit. The most common error was to provide two reasons that were essentially the same, and so only earn credit for one of them.
