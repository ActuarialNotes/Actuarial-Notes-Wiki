An ordinary least squares regression model is fit with the following model form:

$$
E(Y_i) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_i
$$

After fitting the model, the following plot with the original data (points) and three sets of 95% intervals are provided:

![Scatter plot of Y against X (no axis values) with an upward-sloping cloud of about 50 points and three pairs of 95% interval bounds. Interval 1 (dashed) is the widest pair: two roughly parallel lines, slightly bowed, that enclose almost every point. Interval 2 (dotted) is the narrowest pair: it hugs the middle of the point cloud, is narrowest near the centre of the X range and flares apart toward both ends. Interval 3 (solid) is two straight lines that are close together at the left edge of the plot and fan steadily apart toward the right, so it is narrow on the left and very wide on the right](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masi-2019s-q36-regression-intervals.png)

Let "CI" be the 95% confidence interval for $E(Y_i)$, and let "PI" be the 95% prediction interval for $Y_i$.

Determine which one of the following best describes the intervals shown above.

- A) Interval 1 = CI, Interval 2 = PI
- B) Interval 1 = PI, Interval 2 = CI
- C) Interval 1 = CI, Interval 3 = PI
- D) Interval 1 = PI, Interval 3 = CI
- E) None of (A), (B), (C), or (D) are correct

## Explanation

Both intervals are centred on the fitted line and differ only in the standard error under the square root. At a point $x_0$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{CI:} \quad \hat{y}_0 &\pm t_{0.025,\,n-2}\, s\sqrt{\frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x_0 - \bar{x})^2}{S_{xx}}} \\
\text{PI:} \quad \hat{y}_0 &\pm t_{0.025,\,n-2}\, s\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x_0 - \bar{x})^2}{S_{xx}}}
\end{align*}
$$

Two consequences identify the curves:

- Both are **narrowest at $x_0 = \bar{x}$** and widen symmetrically toward either end of the data, because of the $(x_0 - \bar{x})^2$ term.
- The PI carries the extra 1 for the variance of a new observation, so it is much wider — wide enough to contain about 95% of the points — and the 1 dominates, so its bands are nearly parallel. The CI for the mean is narrow and hugs the fitted line, flaring visibly at the ends.

**Interval 2** (dotted) is narrow, sits in the middle of the point cloud, and pinches in near the centre of the $X$ range: the **CI**.

**Interval 1** (dashed) is wide, nearly parallel, and encloses almost every point: the **PI**.

**Interval 3** (solid) is narrow at the left edge and widens steadily all the way to the right. It is not narrowest at $\bar{x}$, so it cannot be either interval.

Interval 1 = PI and Interval 2 = CI, so the answer is **B**.
