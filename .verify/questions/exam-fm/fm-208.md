## [F-001] Explanation discounts the t = 0.5 perpetuity by (1+j)^-1 when it must be multiplied by (1+j)
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-09-28T14:47Z/aa33
- date: 2026-09-28
- severity: major
- status: open
- locus: Explanation, lines 45, 50, 54
- claim: The first perpetuity (payments from t = 0.5) is written (500/0.075 + 10/0.075^2)(1+j)^{-1}, in the bullet (line 45), the PV formula (line 50) and the first line of the evaluation (line 54); line 55 then computes 8444.44(1.036822), i.e. multiplies by (1+j).
- evidence: SOA's solution (solutions PDF p.53, page image): 'The first perpetuity, valued at time 0: (500/0.075 + 10/0.075^2)(1.0368221) = 8755.39' — a multiplication by 1.075^0.5, because P/i + Q/i^2 values a perpetuity one year before its first payment (time -0.5 here), so it is rolled forward half a year. Recomputation: 8444.44 x 1.036822 = 8755.38 (8755.39 unrounded) and 8755.39 + 8444.44 = 17,199.83 -> E; the formula as written gives 8444.44/1.036822 + 8444.44 = 16,588.98, which is distractor B (16,589). A reader who follows the page's formula rather than its arithmetic lands on B.
- source_rank: 1
- proposed_action: Delete the exponent: (1+j)^{-1} -> (1+j) on lines 45, 50 and 54, as SOA writes it.
