A manufacturer's two divisions each plan to buy a currency hedge. One will receive about €5 million next year, and the other will pay about €5 million for components in the same month. The new chief risk officer cancels both hedges. Which of the forces behind the move to enterprise risk management does this decision reflect?

- A) External pressure from regulators and rating agencies
- B) Quantification through catastrophe and financial models
- C) A portfolio point of view of the firm's risks
- D) Boundaryless benchmarking of practices across industries

## Explanation

The two exposures offset each other, a natural hedge, so the firm as a whole has little currency risk. Separate hedges look sensible to each division head but are unnecessary for the enterprise. Recognizing that the organization's risk is not the sum of its parts is the **portfolio point of view**. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — External pressures are demands from outside parties that senior management take responsibility for enterprise-wide risk; no outside party is involved here.
- **B** — Quantification refers to the growing ability to measure risks, such as through catastrophe models and value at risk. Netting two known amounts needs no model.
- **D** — Boundaryless benchmarking is the sharing of ERM practices across organizations and industries.
