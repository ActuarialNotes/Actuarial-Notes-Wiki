An insurance company writes predominantly long-tailed lines of business in a highly competitive environment. The company's incentive plan is structured around achieving both top line growth and target calendar year combined ratios. Recently the company has seen a number of accounts go to competitors for lower rates and increased coverage.

## Part a (0.25 points)

State a goal of agency theory.

### Explanation
To align management and owner interests

Alternatively:

To understand the impacts of potential divergence

### Examiner Report
Candidates mostly performed well. They were expected to identify that mangers are the agents of the owners and that owners and management potentially have divergent interests. Common errors included:

- Confusing agents and principals
- Misunderstanding "agents" to mean sales agents
- Providing a response not related to agency theory
- Using a wrong definition of "owner"

## Part b (0.5 points)

In the context of agency theory, discuss the problems this company may be facing.

### Explanation
Since incentive plan ties to growth and CR, senior management might take very aggressive growth strategy in short term that could be result in selecting wrong risks, adverse reserve development in the long run

Alternatively:

The incentive is structured around top line growth and combined ratios, this could lead to rate increase to obtain this which then leads to accounts leaving. Thus, top line growth and combined ratios look good but policy growth is low and could lead long term to adverse selection.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify the conflict between behavior encouraged by the incentive plan and actions taken in the company's best long-term interests. This was the most challenging item part. By far the most common mistake was not differentiating between short-term and longterm impacts.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two actions the company could take to implement effective underwriting cycle management.

### Explanation
Any two of the following actions (the examiner's report rates the first as the best response):

- **Intellectual property:** focus on intellectual property — maintain investments in key talent, processes and systems, and maintain core market relationships.
- **Retain top talent:** continue to invest in staff and talent development even if market share is decreasing due to losing accounts.
- **Core market channels:** maintain presence in core distribution channels and markets during a soft market.
- **Systems, models and data:** continue to invest in systems and IT to keep important customer information.
- **Underwriting incentives:** set incentives to support the portfolio goals, not a naïve strategy like growth only; underwriters should not lose jobs or bonuses for not hitting targets during bad markets.
- **Avoid market overreaction:** don't overreact to losing business at lower prices; hold prices, since the market will turn and the company will then have capacity for additional insureds, which will drive growth and hit the plan ratio.
- **Owner education:** advise the owners that in soft markets we do not want to grow, as business is written at unprofitable levels, so we should be cutting back.
- **Scenario planning:** have a well-defined multiple-scenario plan, so that when the company faces pressure to act on an underwriting cycle change, it already has a plan.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to make suggestions for the company to improve its underwriting cycle management, and they generally performed well. One common error was to say "educate management" instead of saying "educate owners"
