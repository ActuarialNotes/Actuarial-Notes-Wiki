The base fare charged by airlines for flights from City A to City B is uniformly distributed on $[100, 225]$. In addition to the base fare, the airlines charge a processing fee of 15 and a 30% fuel surcharge applied to the base fare.

Calculate the standard deviation of the total charge for a flight from City A to City B.

- A) $10.8$
- B) $36.1$
- C) $41.1$
- D) $46.9$
- E) $61.9$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the base fare. The total charge is

$$
T = X + 15 + 0.3X = 1.3X + 15
$$

The base fare's standard deviation comes from the uniform formula:

$$
\text{SD}(X) = \frac{225 - 100}{\sqrt{12}} = \frac{125}{3.4641} = 36.084
$$

Now apply the two transformation rules. The additive 15 shifts the distribution without changing its spread, and the multiplier 1.3 scales the standard deviation directly:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{SD}(T) &= 1.3\,\text{SD}(X) \\
             &= 1.3(36.084) \\
             &= \mathbf{46.91}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 36.1, is $\text{SD}(X)$ with the surcharge ignored. Choice (C), 41.1, scales the variance by 1.3 instead of $1.3^2$: $\sqrt{1.3}\,(36.084) = 41.14$. The processing fee plays no part — a constant added to every fare cannot make the fares more variable, so only the proportional 30% surcharge affects the standard deviation.
