Given the following information:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Reported Loss (\$000): 12 Months | 24 Months |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 1,000 | 1,950 |
| 2012 | 1,200 | 2,650 |
| 2013 | 1,800 | 3,500 |
| 2014 | 1,500 | 3,300 |
| 2015 | 2,000 | 4,100 |
| 2016 | 2,400 | |

- $\text{Var}(C_{i,k+1} \mid C_{i1}, \dots, C_{ik})$ is constant for all $i$ where $C_{i,k}$ is the accumulated total claims amount of accident year $i$ up to development year $k$.

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the age-to-age factor for 12 to 24 months using the most appropriate formula according to Mack.

### Explanation
As the variance is constant, the factor is weighted by $C_{ik}^2$:

$$f_{12-24} = \frac{1000 \times 1950 + 1200 \times 2650 + \dots + 2000 \times 4100}{1000^2 + 1200^2 + \dots + 2000^2} = 2.06$$

Alternatively:

Variance proportional to 1, so use the $C_{ik}^2$-weighted average of the link ratios:

$$f_{12-24} = \frac{1000^2 \times \frac{1950}{1000} + 1200^2 \times \frac{2650}{1200} + \dots + 2000^2 \times \frac{4100}{2000}}{1000^2 + 1200^2 + \dots + 2000^2} = 2.060$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand that the variance assumption stated in the problem (constant variance) differed from the original Mack (1994) chain ladder assumptions.  Candidates were expected to apply the correct formula to calculate the loss development factor from 12-24
months, given the alternate variance assumption.
A common error was applying the incorrect formula given the variance assumption stated in the problem

## Part b (1 point)

Plot the residuals against the cumulative reported loss as of 12 months and interpret the results.

### Explanation
Residual $= c_{i,k+1} - c_{i,k} \times f_{k0}$:

| Accident Year | Loss at 12 months | Expected Loss at 24 months | Actual Loss at 24 months | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 1000 | 1000 × 2.060 = 2060 | 1950 | 1950 – 2060 = -110 |
| 2012 | 1200 | 2472 | 2650 | 178 |
| 2013 | 1800 | 3709 | 3500 | -209 |
| 2014 | 1500 | 3091 | 3300 | 209 |
| 2015 | 2000 | 4121 | 4100 | -21 |

![Scatter plot of the residuals against cumulative reported loss at 12 months: (1000, -110), (1200, 178), (1500, 209), (1800, -209), (2000, -21), scattered above and below zero with no pattern](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas7-2017-q3-residual-plot.png)

Interpretation: the residuals are randomly scattered around zero with no trend or change in magnitude, so the constant-variance assumption seems reasonable.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the residuals by accident year given the variance assumption stated in the problem.  Candidates were also expected to understand how to plot the residuals graphically and interpret the results to determine if the stated variance assumption was reasonable.

Common errors include:

- Using an incorrect formula to calculate residuals given the development factor calculation in
Part a.
- Incorrect labeling on the x-axis of the graph (years, cumulative losses at 24 months, etc.)
- Not giving an interpretation for the residuals – some candidates only stated what the residual plot looked like without giving an interpretation of the results
