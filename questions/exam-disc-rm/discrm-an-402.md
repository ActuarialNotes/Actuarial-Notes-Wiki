A contractor's workers compensation policy was written on estimated payroll of \$800,000 in one class rated at \$6.00 per \$100, with an experience mod of 1.15. At audit, payroll was \$860,000. The contractor also paid \$100,000 to a subcontractor doing the same work, which kept no payroll records and for which the contractor has no certificate of insurance. What is the audit adjustment?

- A) Additional premium of \$4,140
- B) Additional premium of \$9,600
- C) Additional premium of \$11,040
- D) Additional premium of \$66,240

## Explanation

Payroll for anyone whose injury could make the insurer liable under Part One is in the premium base. With no payroll records for the uninsured subcontractor and no proof it had coverage, the contract price is added.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Estimated} &= \frac{800{,}000}{100} \times 6.00 \times 1.15 \\
&= 55{,}200 \\
\text{Final} &= \frac{860{,}000 + 100{,}000}{100} \times 6.00 \times 1.15 \\
&= 66{,}240 \\
\text{Adjustment} &= 66{,}240 - 55{,}200 \\
&= 11{,}040
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Leaves out the uninsured subcontractor: $\frac{860{,}000}{100} \times 6.00 \times 1.15 - 55{,}200$.
- **B** — Leaves out the mod, which applies to the audited premium as it did to the estimate.
- **D** — Is the final premium, not the difference from the deposit already paid.
