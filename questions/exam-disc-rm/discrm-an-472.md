A property owner hires a small contractor to repair its roof. The contract's hold-harmless clause makes the contractor responsible for any injury claims arising from the work. A passer-by is injured by falling debris and recovers \$300,000 from the property owner, and the contractor, which has few assets, cannot reimburse it. Which disadvantage of noninsurance transfer does this illustrate?

- A) If the party accepting the loss cannot pay it, the firm remains responsible
- B) Noninsurance transfers usually cost more than buying insurance
- C) Noninsurance transfers cannot shift losses that are not commercially insurable
- D) Noninsurance transfers move the loss to the party least able to control it

## Explanation

A hold-harmless clause transfers the financial consequences of a loss by contract, without insurance. Its weakness is that the transfer is only as good as the party who accepts it: if that party cannot pay, the loss stays with the firm. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Noninsurance transfers often cost *less* than insurance; that is one of their advantages.
- **C** — Noninsurance transfers *can* shift some losses that are not commercially insurable; that is another advantage.
- **D** — The contractor doing the work is better placed to control the hazard, which is also an advantage of the transfer.
