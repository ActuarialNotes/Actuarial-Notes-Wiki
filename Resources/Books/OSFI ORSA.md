## The five key elements

1. **Comprehensive identification and assessment of risks** — *all* material risks, including those not captured in the [[MCT|regulatory capital]] formula: strategic risk, reputational risk, [[Concentration Risk|concentration]], [[Climate Risk|climate]], cyber, liquidity.
2. **Relating risk to capital** — determining the capital needed to support the insurer's current risk profile and to continue its current business, and using that to set the **[[Internal Target Capital Ratio|internal target]]** ([[OSFI Target Capital|Guideline A-4]]).
3. **Oversight** — the board and senior management own the ORSA; it is not an actuarial deliverable handed to them.
4. **Monitoring and reporting** — the ORSA is a continuing process feeding decisions, reported at least annually.
5. **Internal controls and objective review** — an independent challenge of the assessment.

## ORSA against MCT, and against FCT

| | Set by | Basis | Question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[[MCT]]** | OSFI, industry-wide | Standardised factors on a point-in-time balance sheet | Does this insurer hold the capital the formula requires? |
| **[[FCT]]** | The [[Appointed Actuary]], under [[CIA CSOP\|CSOP]] §2500 | Forward-looking base and plausible adverse scenarios | Is the insurer's financial condition satisfactory over the forecast period? |
| **ORSA** | Management and the board, under E-19 | The insurer's own risk profile and own capital model | How much capital does *this* insurer actually need, and for which risks? |

The three are complementary and the exam asks candidates to distinguish them: who owns it, what it covers, what it produces, and what each misses. [[CIA FCT 1]] §4 addresses producing an **integrated FCT and ORSA report** — permitted, but the accountability for each half stays where it was.

## The supplementary risk considerations

The appendix lists risk categories the insurer should consider beyond the capital formula — the principal reason ORSA exists is that a standardised test cannot see a risk peculiar to one insurer's business model.
