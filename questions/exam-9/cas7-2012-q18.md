Two insurance companies want to select appropriate risk measures, based on their aggregate loss distributions, for capital allocation purposes. The four risk measures under consideration are:

- Standard deviation
- Semi-standard deviation
- VaR
- TVaR

## Part a (1.5 points)

Company A writes homeowners and commercial property insurance in several coastal states. Its main concern is hurricane risk. Assess the appropriateness of each risk measure for Company A and determine the best one.

### Explanation
- **Standard deviation:** not appropriate, since it doesn't work well with skewed distributions like lines subject to hurricane losses.
- **Semi-standard deviation:** not appropriate, since it ends up ignoring part of the distribution and won't highlight hurricane risk enough (it is based on total loss).
- **VaR:** not appropriate, since it is only one point; VaR(95) may not be high enough to capture hurricane risk.
- **TVaR:** while not ideal (a distortion measure or RTVaR would be better), it is **the best one in the list**, because it averages all loss values above the VaR, better capturing hurricane risk.

Alternatively:

The standard deviation and semi-standard deviation are mean-based risk measures; they would not be appropriate here, because for hurricane risk you are more concerned with the tail — hurricane losses might be large but infrequent. VaR and TVaR are more appropriate as tail risk measures, and TVaR even more so, because it measures the average loss in the tail, not just the probability of experiencing a loss above a certain threshold, like the VaR.

## Part b (1.5 points)

Company B writes personal auto insurance in several mid-western states. Its main concern is the price adequacy of their book. Assess the appropriateness of each risk measure for Company B and determine the best one.

### Explanation
- **Standard deviation:** not appropriate here, because it builds in positive (beneficial) deviations.
- **Semi-standard deviation:** **the best one**, since it uses the whole distribution and focuses only on adverse outcomes, even the small ones.
- **VaR and TVaR:** not appropriate, for two reasons: estimating at high levels ignores too much of the risk, and estimating at low levels puts too little weight on the high-risk (tail) events. For price adequacy you really want to look at the whole distribution.

Alternatively:

To assess the risk of price inadequacy, a mean-based risk measure is more appropriate because it takes the entire loss distribution into account, so use either the standard deviation or the semi-standard deviation. The semi-standard deviation is slightly more appropriate, as it focuses only on adverse outcomes, which is what we are concerned about.

### Examiner Report
Most candidates did fairly well at assessing the risk measures, although oftentimes they defaulted to defining the measure as opposed to truly assessing its value for the given insurer.  Semistandard deviation was the most misunderstood of the four risk measures.  A selection of the most appropriate risk measure for each situation was also required.  A number of candidates did not do this.  Of those candidates who did correctly identify the best measure, some failed to explain why that was indeed the best measure for the given situation.
