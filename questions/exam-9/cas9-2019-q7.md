Given the following bonds with annual coupon payments:

| Bond | Par Value | Time to Maturity (in years) | Annual Coupon Rate | Bond Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$100 | 1 | 4% | \$99.52 |
| 2 | \$100 | 2 | 2% | Not Given |
| 3 | \$100 | 3 | 2% | Not Given |
| 4 | \$100 | 4 | 0% | \$81.03 |

The following forward rate agreements, valued as of today, are available for an investor to purchase:

| Forward Rate Agreement | Lending Period Begins | Term | Principal | Annual Effective Interest Rate | Value (to Lender) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | in 2 years | 1 year | \$700,000 | 6.6% | \$6,000 |
| B | in 3 years | 1 year | \$1,600,000 | 7.7% | \$19,500 |

## Part a (2.25 points)

Calculate the price of Bond 3 assuming annually compounding interest.

### Explanation
Valuing each FRA from the buyer's side, at time 0. The 1- and 4-year spot rates come from Bonds 1 and 4:

$$y_1 = \frac{104}{99.52} - 1 = 4.5\%, \qquad y_4 = \left(\frac{100}{81.03}\right)^{0.25} - 1 = 5.4\%$$

FRA B fixes the forward rate from year 3 to year 4, which gives the 3-year spot rate:

$$19{,}500 = \frac{1{,}600{,}000\,(0.077 - f_3)}{(1 + y_4)^4} \implies f_3 = 6.20\%$$

$$(1 + y_3)^3 = \frac{(1 + y_4)^4}{1 + f_3} \implies y_3 = 5.14\%$$

FRA A fixes the forward rate from year 2 to year 3, which gives the 2-year spot rate:

$$6{,}000 = \frac{700{,}000\,(0.066 - f_2)}{(1 + y_3)^3} \implies f_2 = 5.60\%$$

$$(1 + y_2)^2 = \frac{(1 + y_3)^3}{1 + f_2} \implies y_2 = 4.90\%$$

$$\text{Price of Bond 3} = \frac{2}{1.045} + \frac{2}{1.049^2} + \frac{102}{1.0514^3} = 91.50$$

Alternatively:

The report accepted several readings of the FRA. The other sample answers, each with the same $y_1 = 4.5\%$ and $(1 + y_4)^4 = 100 / 81.03 = 1.234$:

- **Lender value** (same steps, the FRA valued from the lender's side): $19{,}500 = \dfrac{1{,}600{,}000\,(f_3 - 0.077)}{(1 + y_4)^4}$, so $f_3 = 9.20\%$ and $y_3 = 4.16\%$; then $6{,}000 = \dfrac{700{,}000\,(0.066 - f_2)}{(1 + y_3)^3}$ (as printed), $f_2 = 7.60\%$ and $y_2 = 2.48\%$. Price of Bond 3 $= \dfrac{2}{1.045} + \dfrac{2}{1.0248^2} + \dfrac{102}{1.0416^3} = 94.08$.
- **Buyer value, FRA discounted by the forward rate to the beginning of the FRA period**: $19{,}500 = \dfrac{1{,}600{,}000\,(0.077 - f_3)}{1 + f_3}$, so $f_3 = 6.40\%$; $6{,}000 = \dfrac{700{,}000\,(0.066 - f_2)}{1 + f_2}$, so $f_2 = 5.69\%$; $f_1 = \dfrac{(1 + y_4)^4}{(1 + y_1)(1 + f_3)(1 + f_2)} - 1 = 5.01\%$. Price of Bond 3 $= \dfrac{2}{1.045} + \dfrac{2}{(1.045)(1.0501)} + \dfrac{102}{(1.045)(1.0501)(1.0569)} = 91.68$.
- **Buyer value, no discounting**: $19{,}500 = 1{,}600{,}000\,(0.077 - f_3)$, so $f_3 = 6.48\%$; $6{,}000 = 700{,}000\,(0.066 - f_2)$, so $f_2 = 5.74\%$; $f_1 = \dfrac{(1 + y_4)^4}{(1 + y_1)(1 + f_3)(1 + f_2)} - 1 = 4.88\%$. Price of Bond 3 $= \dfrac{2}{1.045} + \dfrac{2}{(1.045)(1.0488)} + \dfrac{102}{(1.045)(1.0488)(1.0574)} = 91.75$.
- **Forward rate = given yield rate** (assume $f_3 = 7.70\%$ and $f_2 = 6.60\%$): $(1 + y_3)^3 = \dfrac{(1 + y_4)^4}{1 + f_3} = 1.146$ and $(1 + y_2)^2 = \dfrac{(1 + y_3)^3}{1 + f_2} = 1.075$, so Price of Bond 3 $= \dfrac{2}{1.045} + \dfrac{2}{1.075} + \dfrac{102}{1.146} = 92.79$. Working through forward rates instead, $f_1 = \dfrac{(1 + y_4)^4}{(1 + y_1)(1 + f_3)(1 + f_2)} - 1 = 2.9\%$ and Price of Bond 3 $= \dfrac{2}{1.045} + \dfrac{2}{(1.045)(1.029)} + \dfrac{102}{(1.045)(1.029)(1.066)} = 92.79$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use the given bond and forward rate agreement (FRA) assumptions to calculate the appropriate spot rates and/or forward rates to price bond 3 appropriately. Candidates were expected to demonstrate how to calculate spot rates from annual coupon bonds, how to calculate spot rates from forward rates, and how to calculate the price of a bond.

Understanding how to price an FRA was not a requirement; therefore, many interpretations of the FRA were accepted, including:

- Evaluating the FRA from the perspective of both the buyer and lender
- Evaluating the price of the FRA at the beginning of the FRA period
- Evaluating the price of the FRA at the end of the FRA period
- Evaluating the price of the FRA at time 0
- Candidates treating the given effective interest rates as forward rates

Common mistakes included:

- Using forward rates (or spot rates) as spot rates (or forward rates)
- Applying the derived forward rates from the FRA for years 1-2 and 2-3 rather than 2-3 and 3-4
- Discounting the forward rate agreement equation setup to time 1 rather than time 0
- Using the wrong coupon payments when calculating the price of Bond 3
- Using the wrong spot rate when discounting the coupon payments
- Using continuous compounding
- Calculation errors in getting from the correct formulas to the final answer

## Part b (0.75 points)

An investor has developed the following probability distribution for future short rates in year 4:

| Scenario | Year 4 Future Short Rate | Probability |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6% | 0.25 |
| 2 | 7% | 0.50 |
| 3 | 10% | 0.25 |

The investor is interested in purchasing Bond 4 but requires a liquidity premium of 1% in year 4 to engage in this long-term bond strategy. Based on the investor's expectation of future interest rates and liquidity premium criteria, recommend whether the investor should purchase Bond 4.

### Explanation
Let $r_t$ be the future short rate from $t$ to $t+1$. The liquidity premium is $f_3 - E(r_4)$, which must be > 1% for the investor to engage in a long-term bond.

$$E(r_4) = (.25)(.06) + (.50)(.07) + (.25)(.10) = 7.5\%$$

$$\text{Liquidity Premium} = 6.2\% - 7.5\% = -1.3\% < 1\%$$

The investor should not purchase Bond 4 because it doesn't offer the required liquidity premium.

Alternatively:

Compare prices. With $f_3 = E(r_4) + \text{Liquidity Premium} = (.25)(.06) + (.50)(.07) + (.25)(.10) + 1\% = 8.5\%$, and carrying forward $f_1 = 2.9\%$ and $f_2 = 6.6\%$ from the last approach in part a, the price of Bond 4 with the forward rate expectation is

$$\frac{100}{(1.045)(1.029)(1.066)(1.085)} = 80.40 < 81.03 \text{ (given)}$$

The bond is overpriced based on the investor's liquidity premium expectations, so do not purchase Bond 4.

Or compare yields: $y_4 = (81.03/100)^{0.25} = 5.4\%$ (as printed), and with $f_3 = 8.5\%$ as above,

$$\text{Investor's expectations} = \left[(1 + y_1)(1 + f_1)(1 + f_2)(1 + f_3)\right]^{0.25} - 1 = (1.045 \times 1.029 \times 1.066 \times 1.085)^{0.25} - 1 = 5.6\%$$

Since 5.4% < 5.6%, the current Bond 4 four-year annual yield does not meet the investor's expectations, so do not recommend purchasing Bond 4.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate an expected return given the future short rate distribution and apply the liquidity premium requirement. This expected return should have been directly compared to a calculated forward rate from part a. Based on that comparison, candidates were expected to provide a recommendation on whether to invest in bond 4.

Common mistakes included:

- Not addressing the liquidity premium requirement at all
- Subtracting the liquidity premium requirement from the expected short rate
- Not carrying over forward rate assumptions from part a.
- Providing an incorrect comparison (such as the expected short rate compared to the spot rate)
- Providing an incorrect recommendation based on the comparison of forward rate and expected short rate
- Incorrectly calculating the present value of the bond prices
