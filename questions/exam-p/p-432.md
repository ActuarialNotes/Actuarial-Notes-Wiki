A large life insurance company gets a steady inflow of new policyholders each month. In the past, the number of new policyholders per month, $N_{\text{past}}$, was normally distributed with mean 500, standard deviation $\sigma$, and $P[N_{\text{past}} < 400] = 0.1056$.

The company has just undertaken a new marketing strategy, which is projected to have a positive effect on new sales. The projected number of new policyholders per month, $N_{\text{future}}$, is normally distributed with mean 550 and standard deviation $1.25\sigma$.

Calculate $P[370 < N_{\text{future}} < 730]$.

- A) $0.903$
- B) $0.928$
- C) $0.970$
- D) $0.976$
- E) $0.985$

## Explanation

Recover $\sigma$ from the past distribution:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.1056 &= P\left[Z < \frac{400 - 500}{\sigma}\right] \\
\frac{-100}{\sigma} &= -1.25 \\
\sigma &= 80
\end{align*}
$$

The future distribution has mean 550 and standard deviation $1.25(80) = 100$. Standardize both endpoints:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{370 - 550}{100} &= -1.8 \\
\frac{730 - 550}{100} &= 1.8
\end{align*}
$$

The interval is symmetric about the new mean, so

$$
\begin{align*}
P[370 < N_{\text{future}} < 730] &= P[-1.8 < Z < 1.8] \\
                                 &= 0.9641 - 0.0359 \\
                                 &= \mathbf{0.9282}
\end{align*}
$$

Watch the two 1.25s — they are unrelated. One is the $z$-score that recovers $\sigma$ from the past data; the other is the multiplier on $\sigma$ for the future distribution. Using $\sigma = 80$ for the future interval instead of 100 would give $z = \pm 2.25$ and 0.976, which is choice (D).
