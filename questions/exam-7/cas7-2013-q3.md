Given the following information:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Paid Loss 12 (\$000) | Cumulative Paid Loss 24 (\$000) | Cumulative Paid Loss 36 (\$000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 2,750 | 4,250 | 5,100 |
| 2011 | 2,700 | 4,300 | |
| 2012 | 2,900 | | |

The expected accident year loss emergence pattern (growth function) is approximated by a Weibull function of the form:

$$G(x \mid \omega, \theta) = 1 - \exp\left(-(x/\theta)^{\omega}\right)$$

Parameter estimates are: $\omega = 1.5$ and $\theta = 20$

## Part a (3 points)

Calculate the process standard deviation of the reserve estimate for accident years 2010 through 2012 using the LDF method.

### Explanation
$$\text{Process SD} = \sqrt{R\,\sigma^2} \qquad \sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n-p}\sum \frac{(c - \mu)^2}{\mu}$$

with $n = 6$ incremental cells and $p = 2 + 3 = 5$ parameters ($\omega$, $\theta$ and the three accident-year ultimates).

Evaluate the growth function at the average ages 6, 18 and 30 months:

$$G(6) = 1 - e^{-(6/20)^{1.5}} = 0.1515 \qquad G(18) = 0.5742 \qquad G(30) = 0.8407$$

Check whether truncation is needed: extending the triangle out 3 years, $G(66) = 0.9975$, which is reasonably close to 1, so the function does not need to be truncated.

| AY | $G(x)$ | LDF $= 1/G(x)$ | Ult = Cum Pd × LDF | Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 0.8407 | 1.1895 | 6,066.4 | 966.4 |
| 2011 | 0.5742 | 1.7416 | 7,488.7 | 3,188.7 |
| 2012 | 0.1515 | 6.6007 | 19,142 | 16,242 |
| Total | | | | 20,397.1 |

Expected incremental losses, $\mu = [G(y) - G(x)] \times \text{Ult}_{AY}$ (e.g. $1{,}995.7 = (0.8407 - 0.5742)(7{,}488.7)$):

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 919 | 2,564.3 | 1,616.7 |
| 2011 | 1,134.5 | 3,165.5 | |
| 2012 | 2,900 | | |

Actual incremental losses:

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 2,750 | 1,500 | 850 |
| 2011 | 2,700 | 1,600 | |
| 2012 | 2,900 | | |

$$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{6 - 5}\left[\frac{(2{,}750 - 919)^2}{919} + \frac{(2{,}700 - 1{,}134.5)^2}{1{,}134.5} + \cdots\right] = 7{,}387.84$$

$$\text{Process SD} = \sqrt{R\,\sigma^2} = \sqrt{7{,}387.84 \times 20{,}397.1} = 12{,}275.607$$

Alternatively:

Truncating the pattern at 66 months ($G(66) = 0.998$) gives truncated LDFs of 6.57, 1.736 and 1.188, ultimates of 19,053, 7,465 and 6,059, and total reserves of 20,277. The chi-square sum over the six cells gives $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{6-5}(7{,}335) = 7{,}335$, so

$$\text{Process SD} = \sqrt{20{,}277 \times 7{,}335} = 12{,}196$$

### Examiner Report
The candidate was asked to determine the process standard deviation given a specific model.  To get there, several basic calculations were needed such as determining ultimate losses given specific data and a specific curve, resulting reserves, and the chi squared formula – including corresponding degrees of freedom.  Common errors included using cumulative instead of incremental actual and fitted data triangles, not recognizing the use of the average age in the application of the Weibull model, not correctly determining the degrees of freedom to use, and general math errors.  Candidates often did not calculate the process standard deviation correctly - either incorrectly taking the square root of σ2 or calculating the correct process variance but forgetting that the standard deviation was requested.
Truncation was not specifically mentioned in the question and could be subject to candidate interpretation of model results.  Both truncated and untruncated approaches were accepted for full credit.

## Part b (2 points)

Graph the normalized residuals plotted against the increment age of loss emergence. Based on your graphical results, discuss the appropriateness of the Weibull model above.

### Explanation
Normalized residual $\varepsilon = \dfrac{c - \mu}{\sqrt{\mu\,\sigma^2}}$, e.g. for AY 2010 at 36 months $\dfrac{850 - 1{,}616.7}{\sqrt{7{,}387.4 \times 1{,}616.7}} = -0.222$.

| AY | Age 6 | Age 18 | Age 30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 0.7027 | -0.245 | -0.222 |
| 2011 | 0.5407 | -0.324 | |
| 2012 | 0 | | |

![Normalized residuals plotted against increment age: at age 6 the residuals are 0.70, 0.54 and 0; at age 18 they are -0.25 and -0.32; at age 30 the residual is -0.22](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas7-2013-q3-normalized-residuals.png)

If the model were appropriate, the residuals would be randomly scattered around zero at each age. Instead, the residuals at age 6 are all non-negative ($\mu$ underestimates) and the residuals at later ages are negative ($\mu$ overestimates). This trend from positive to negative as age increases means the Weibull model may not be appropriate for this loss experience.

### Examiner Report
The candidate is asked to calculate and graph normalized residual using the results from part a).  A very common error in the determination of the normalized residuals was to fail
to divide by the σ2 resulting in a set of residuals incorrectly scaled.  Another common mistake was to take the square root of the chi-squared results from part a) which failed to recognize the possibility of negative residuals.  Candidates generally graphed their calculated residuals correctly, but a common mistake observed was to graph the residual against incremental loss size which was not what the question specified.  In the evaluation of the graph, most candidates recognized that the Weibull was not appropriate given the data and resulting residuals; however, explanations as to why were oftentimes incomplete or non-existent.
