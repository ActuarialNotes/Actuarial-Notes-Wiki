Policyholders of a particular type of insurance policy will have either zero claims or one claim. The probability of zero claims is 20%. If a claim is received, the claim size follows a normal distribution with mean 50,000 and standard deviation $c$.

The probability that a randomly selected policyholder has a claim exceeding 55,000 is 0.1837.

Calculate $c$.

- A) $5546$
- B) $6125$
- C) $6490$
- D) $6756$
- E) $7120$

## Explanation

A policyholder's claim exceeds 55,000 only if a claim occurs *and* it is large enough. The two steps multiply:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.1837 &= P[\text{claim occurs}] \cdot P[X > 55{,}000] \\
       &= 0.80 \cdot P[X > 55{,}000]
\end{align*}
$$

so the conditional tail probability is

$$
P[X > 55{,}000] = \frac{0.1837}{0.80} = 0.22962
$$

Now standardize. The $z$-value with upper tail 0.22962 is $z = 0.74008$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{55{,}000 - 50{,}000}{c} &= 0.74008 \\
c &= \frac{5000}{0.74008} \\
  &= \mathbf{6756}
\end{align*}
$$

Skipping the division by 0.80 and using $z$ for an upper tail of 0.1837 gives $z = 0.9014$ and $c = 5000/0.9014 \approx 5547$, which is choice (A). The 20% zero-claim mass is what separates the unconditional probability from the normal tail, and it is the whole content of the question.
