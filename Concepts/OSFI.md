**The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions** (OSFI) is Canada's federal prudential regulator: it supervises federally regulated insurers, banks and pension plans, sets capital requirements through the [[MCT]] guideline, and issues the guidelines governing [[ORSA]], [[FCT]], [[Corporate Governance]], reinsurance and the [[Appointed Actuary]]. OSFI regulates **solvency**, not [[Rate Regulation|rates]] or [[Market Conduct Regulation|market conduct]].

- **Mandate.** OSFI's statutory objectives are to protect the rights and interests of policyholders and creditors *while* allowing institutions to compete and take reasonable risks. The second half matters: OSFI's mandate explicitly does **not** include preventing all failures — a zero-failure regime would be achieved only by prohibiting risk-taking.
- **Supervisory framework.** OSFI rates each institution on inherent risk and quality of risk management by significant activity, arriving at a **composite risk rating** and an intervention **stage** (0 normal, 1 early warning, through 4 non-viability). Escalation brings reporting requirements, business restrictions, and eventually control of assets.
- **Tools.** Guidelines (the *A* capital, *B* prudential and *E* corporate-governance series), the [[Canadian Annual Return|Annual and Quarterly Returns]], on-site examinations, supervisory letters, capital add-ons and internal target increases, and the power to take control of an insurer.
- **What OSFI does not do.** It does not approve premium rates, adjudicate consumer complaints, or licence agents — those are provincial. It shares information with provincial regulators through the [[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators]] and with [[PACICC]], which handles the aftermath of a failure.
- OSFI has extended prudential supervision into **[[Climate Risk|climate]]** (Guideline B-15, standardised climate scenario exercises), **[[Concentration Risk|concentration]]** and **[[Model Risk|model risk]]**, on the view that these are solvency issues rather than social policy.

> [!example]- Would OSFI Intervene? {Example}
> A federally regulated insurer reports an [[MCT]] ratio of $142\%$. Its supervisory target is $150\%$ and its internal target is $180\%$. Its capital plan projects $138\%$ at the next quarter.
>
> What happens?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Below the **supervisory target** of $150\%$ but above the $100\%$ minimum, so the insurer is not at the point of non-viability — but it is squarely in intervention territory.
> >
> > | Ratio | Status |
> > |---|---|
> > | Below internal target ($180\%$) | Board and OSFI notified; capital restoration plan expected |
> > | Below supervisory target ($150\%$) | Heightened supervision; likely **Stage 1 or 2** |
> > | Below minimum ($100\%$) | Serious intervention; capital is insufficient |
> >
> > Expect OSFI to require a written capital restoration plan with dates, to restrict dividends and other capital distributions, to increase reporting frequency, and to challenge the assumptions in the projection. The projected *decline* to $138\%$ is the aggravating fact: a ratio below target and falling is a different conversation from one below target and recovering.
> >
> > The insurer's own [[ORSA]] should already have identified this and set the internal target that was breached first — an internal target that gave no early warning is itself a supervisory finding.
