An insurance policy insures against two perils. Let $X$ and $Y$ be the number of monthly claims for each of these perils. The joint probability function of $X$ and $Y$ is given by

$$
p(x,y) = \frac{2x + y + 1}{36}, \qquad \text{for } x = 0, 1, 2 \text{ and } y = 0, 1, 2.
$$

Calculate the variance of the marginal distribution of $X$.

- A) $0.56$
- B) $0.64$
- C) $0.75$
- D) $0.80$
- E) $0.89$

## Explanation

Sum the joint function over $y$ for each value of $x$. For a fixed $x$ the three terms are $2x+1$, $2x+2$ and $2x+3$:

$$
\begin{align*}
p_X(0) &= \frac{1 + 2 + 3}{36} = \frac{6}{36} \\
p_X(1) &= \frac{3 + 4 + 5}{36} = \frac{12}{36} \\
p_X(2) &= \frac{5 + 6 + 7}{36} = \frac{18}{36}
\end{align*}
$$

These total 1, confirming the normalizing constant.

Now the two moments:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X] &= 0\left(\frac{6}{36}\right) + 1\left(\frac{12}{36}\right) + 2\left(\frac{18}{36}\right) \\
     &= \frac{48}{36} = \frac{4}{3}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= 0\left(\frac{6}{36}\right) + 1\left(\frac{12}{36}\right) + 4\left(\frac{18}{36}\right) \\
       &= \frac{84}{36} = \frac{7}{3}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X) &= \frac{7}{3} - \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 \\
              &= \frac{21 - 16}{9} = \frac{5}{9} \\
              &= \mathbf{0.556}
\end{align*}
$$

Summing over $y$ leaves $p_X(x) = (6x + 6)/36 = (x + 1)/6$, which is why the marginal probabilities come out in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. Nothing about independence is needed or assumed — marginalizing first reduces this to a one-variable calculation.
