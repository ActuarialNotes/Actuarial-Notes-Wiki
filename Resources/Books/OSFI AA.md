## 2. The legal requirements

The [[Insurance Companies Act]] requires the **board of directors** to appoint an actuary, and to notify OSFI of the appointment and of any revocation. The role's statutory duties include valuing the [[Insurance Contract Liabilities|policy liabilities]] in accordance with [[Accepted Actuarial Practice|accepted actuarial practice]], reporting to the board on the valuation, meeting with the board or the audit committee, performing **[[FCT|financial condition testing]]**, and the **[[Duty to Report|duty to report]]** to management and directors — and, where they do not act, to the Superintendent — on any matter that has material adverse effects on the financial condition of the company. The Act also gives the actuary access to records and a statutory protection for reporting in good faith.

## 3. Qualifications

A Fellow of the [[Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)|CIA]] with relevant and recent Canadian P&C experience. OSFI expects the insurer to satisfy itself of the qualification, and can require a change of appointee.

## 4–5. Peer review

Peer review is **independent review of the appointed actuary's work by another qualified actuary**, and it is the check on a role that is otherwise self-verifying.

- **Work to be reviewed** (§4.3) — the valuation of insurance contract liabilities, the FCT, and the actuarial input to the [[MCT|regulatory capital]] calculation.
- **[[Materiality|Materiality]]** (§4.4) — the depth of review scales with what could be materially wrong.
- **Contents of the report** (§4.5) and **cycle** (§4.6) — a full review on a defined cycle with interim reviews between, timed (§4.7) so findings can affect the year-end work rather than arrive after it.
- **Selecting a reviewer** (§5.1) — independence is the criterion; §5.2 addresses the relationship with the **[[External Auditor|external audit]]**, which is a different function with a different objective; §5.3 covers changing reviewer, where OSFI wants to know why.
