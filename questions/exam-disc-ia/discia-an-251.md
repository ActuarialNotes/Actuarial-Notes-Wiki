Congress passes a statute of general application on business recordkeeping that does not mention insurance. Read literally, it conflicts with a state law that regulates how insurers keep policyholder records. Which law governs the insurers' records in that state?

- A) The federal statute, because federal law always overrides conflicting state law
- B) Neither, until the NAIC adopts a model law resolving the conflict
- C) The state law, because the federal statute does not specifically relate to insurance
- D) The federal statute, because insurance is commerce among the states

## Explanation

The McCarran-Ferguson Act provides that no act of Congress is to be read to invalidate, impair or supersede a state law regulating the business of insurance **unless the act specifically relates to the business of insurance**. A general recordkeeping statute does not, so the state insurance law governs.

- **A** states the ordinary preemption rule, which McCarran-Ferguson reverses for state insurance laws.
- **B** gives the NAIC a power it lacks: its model laws bind no one until a state enacts them.
- **D** is the reasoning that made insurance subject to federal law in the first place, which is why Congress passed McCarran-Ferguson to keep regulation with the states.
