Losses under an insurance policy are uniformly distributed on $[0, 1000]$. The policy has a deductible of 400.

A loss occurred for which the insurance benefit was less than 400.

Calculate the probability that the benefit was more than 300.

- A) $0.100$
- B) $0.125$
- C) $0.250$
- D) $0.750$
- E) $0.875$

## Explanation

The benefit is $\max(\text{loss} - 400,\ 0)$, so every statement about the benefit translates into one about the loss:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{benefit} < 400 &\iff \text{loss} < 800 \\
\text{benefit} > 300 &\iff \text{loss} > 700
\end{align*}
$$

The conditional probability is therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{benefit} > 300 \mid \text{benefit} < 400] &= P[\text{loss} > 700 \mid \text{loss} < 800] \\
                                                  &= \frac{P[700 < \text{loss} < 800]}{P[\text{loss} < 800]}
\end{align*}
$$

For a uniform distribution both pieces are interval proportions:

$$
\begin{align*}
&= \frac{(800 - 700)/1000}{800/1000} \\
&= \frac{100}{800} \\
&= \mathbf{0.125}
\end{align*}
$$

Translating to the loss scale first is what makes this straightforward — the benefit itself has a point mass at zero (every loss below 400 pays nothing), which the conditioning event $\{\text{benefit} < 400\}$ still includes. Choice (C), 0.250, comes from conditioning on the loss exceeding the deductible as well, shrinking the denominator to $[400, 800]$.
