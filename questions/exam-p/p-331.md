According to a survey, $x\%$ of respondents have health insurance, $y\%$ have disability income insurance, and $z\%$ have only health insurance.

Calculate the probability that a randomly selected respondent has only disability income insurance.

- A) $(y-x+z)/100$
- B) $(y-x-z)/100$
- C) $(y-x-2z)/100$
- D) $(y-x+2z)/200$
- E) $(y-z)/100$

## Explanation

Let $H$ = health insurance and $D$ = disability income insurance. Then

$$P(H) = \frac{x}{100}, \qquad P(D) = \frac{y}{100}, \qquad P(H \text{ and not } D) = \frac{z}{100}$$

Let $P(H \text{ and } D) = \dfrac{k}{100}$. Since $P(H) = P(H \text{ and not } D) + P(H \text{ and } D)$,

$$x = z + k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k = x - z$$

Since $P(D) = P(D \text{ and not } H) + P(D \text{ and } H)$,

$$
\begin{align*}
P(D \text{ and not } H) &= P(D) - P(D \text{ and } H) \\
&= \frac{y}{100} - \frac{k}{100} = \frac{y-(x-z)}{100} = \frac{y-x+z}{100}
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **A**.
