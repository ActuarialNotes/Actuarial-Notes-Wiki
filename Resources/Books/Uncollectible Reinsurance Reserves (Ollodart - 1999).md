A paper by Bruce E. Ollodart introducing concepts and methods for estimating uncollectible reinsurance reserves, which may be required when an insurer or reinsurer has ceded reinsurance losses recoverable that have become uncollectible. Its abstract lists the topics: sources of uncollectible reinsurance, insolvency issues, data sources, relevant legal concepts, financial reporting issues, contract termination issues, reserve estimation methods and federal income tax matters. It concerns loss and loss adjustment expense reserves for property and casualty exposures, under treaty and facultative reinsurance.

## Sources of Uncollectible Reinsurance
- The paper groups the sources of uncollectible [[Reinsurance Recovery|reinsurance recoveries]] into three general categories: disputes, financial difficulty and other.
- Disputes
    - Arise when the ceding company and the reinsurer interpret complex contract language differently in a way that materially changes the recovery and cannot be resolved easily; attorney involvement is usually required to estimate reserves for disputed reinsurance.
- Financial Difficulty
    - The author's label for reinsurers that have trouble paying their claims: those that pay late, pay only a portion of what they owe, or cannot pay at all.
    - Late Payers
    - Weakening Financial Strength
    - Receivership
        - Rehabilitation
        - Liquidation
        - Information on Receiverships
- Other Sources of Uncollectible Reinsurance
    - The two most common are governmental/legal actions and criminal actions.
    - Governmental/Legal Actions
    - Criminal Actions
