A plaintiff wins a \$900,000 judgment against two defendants. The jury assigns 20% of the fault to the insured and 80% to a co-defendant with no insurance and no assets. In a state with joint and several liability, how much of the judgment can the plaintiff collect from the insured?

- A) \$180,000
- B) \$450,000
- C) \$720,000
- D) \$900,000

## Explanation

When defendants are jointly and severally liable, each is independently liable for the full extent of the injuries, so the plaintiff may collect the whole judgment from any one of them. The insured would then seek contribution from a co-defendant who cannot pay. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — \$180,000 is the insured's share under several liability only, as in a state that has abolished joint and several liability.
- **B** — \$450,000 splits the judgment equally, which neither rule does.
- **C** — \$720,000 is the insolvent co-defendant's share.
