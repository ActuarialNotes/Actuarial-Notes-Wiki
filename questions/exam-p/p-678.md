Grades on a final exam are uniformly distributed over the interval $[65, 95]$.

Calculate the probability that a randomly selected student's grade is within one standard deviation of the mean.

- A) $0.29$
- B) $0.40$
- C) $0.58$
- D) $0.68$
- E) $0.79$

## Explanation

For a uniform on $[a, b]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E(X) &= \frac{65 + 95}{2} = 80 \\
\text{Var}(X) &= \frac{(95 - 65)^2}{12} = 75 \\
\text{SD}(X) &= \sqrt{75} = 8.660254
\end{align*}
$$

The interval $80 \pm 8.660$ runs from 71.3397 to 88.6603. A uniform assigns probability in proportion to length, so

$$
P\left[80 - \sqrt{75} < X < 80 + \sqrt{75}\right] = \frac{88.6603 - 71.3397}{30} = \mathbf{0.5774}
$$

Choice (D), 0.68, is the familiar "68% within one standard deviation" figure — but that is a property of the *normal* distribution, not a universal one. For any uniform the answer is $2/\sqrt{12} = 0.577$, independent of the endpoints: the flat density spreads mass into the tails that a bell curve concentrates near the middle.
