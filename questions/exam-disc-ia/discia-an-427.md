An HO-3 (HO 00 03 05 11) has a \$100,000 Coverage E limit and a \$5,000 Coverage F limit. A guest falls on an icy walk at the insured's home. The insurer pays \$2,000 of the guest's medical bills under Coverage F, spends \$25,000 defending the guest's suit, and the suit ends in a \$130,000 judgment, which the insurer pays up to its limit. How much does the insurer pay in total?

- A) \$102,000
- B) \$127,000
- C) \$132,000
- D) \$157,000

## Explanation

Coverage E pays damages up to its limit; defense costs are claim expenses paid **in addition to** the limit; and Coverage F is a separate limit:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Total} &= 2{,}000 + \min(130{,}000,\ 100{,}000) + 25{,}000 \\
&= 127{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The insured owes the remaining \$30,000 of the judgment personally. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats the defense costs as eroding the Coverage E limit.
- **C** pays the whole judgment, ignoring the \$100,000 limit, and leaves out the defense costs.
- **D** pays the whole judgment as well as the defense costs, ignoring the limit.
