A developer has bought a former rail yard where soil contamination is already known. The state has approved a remediation plan with an expected cost of \$4,000,000, and the developer's lender is worried that the cleanup could cost far more. Which policy addresses that worry?

- A) Site pollution legal liability
- B) Contractor's pollution liability
- C) Remediation cost cap
- D) Underground storage tank policy

## Explanation

A remediation cost cap, or cleanup stop-loss, is bought once contamination is known and a cleanup plan is agreed. It pays cost overruns above the expected cost and a self-insured buffer, usually with coinsurance, because the risk is how much the cleanup will cost, not whether there is a loss.

- **A** covers third-party claims and cleanup from pollution conditions at a site, including unknown pre-existing conditions. It is not designed to pay overruns on a known, planned cleanup.
- **B** follows a contractor's operations at job sites, covering pollution the contractor causes.
- **D** covers releases from scheduled storage tanks and helps an owner meet financial responsibility rules.
