An insurer writes property and workers compensation insurance in California. To model the total annual losses for this insurer, the following two copulas are being considered:

- i. $C(u,v) = uv$
- ii. $C(u,v) = \min(u,v)^{0.25}(uv)^{0.75}$

## Part a (1 point)

Define and briefly explain the right-tail concentration function that may be used to characterize a copula.

### Explanation
$$R(z) = \frac{\Pr(U > z \text{ and } V > z)}{1 - z} = \frac{1 - 2z + C(z,z)}{1 - z}$$

The right-tail concentration function is used to look for right-tail dependence in loss distributions: it measures how much of a copula's probability sits in its right tail. Copulas such as the Gumbel and heavy-right-tail (HRT) have high values of $R = \lim_{z \to 1} R(z)$, while symmetric copulas like the normal and Frank's have $R = 0$. If it is large, the copula can model distributions with strong correlation in the right tail.

### Examiner Report
For the a. part, many candidates offered a definition for right-tail concentration function or the right-tail concentration formula itself.  It was common for candidates not to have both the written explanation and the formula.
Most candidates had difficulty with the b. part.  Candidates often failed to both derive the function and graph the function as required by the question.  When graphing the functions, Candidates commonly failed to label their graphs or show any work as to how they plotted the points on the graph.  Additionally, it was common for candidates on the second copula to intercept R(1) with the x-axis, this was incorrect.

## Part b (2 points)

Derive and graph the right-tail concentration functions for the two copulas.

### Explanation
**i.** $C(u,v) = uv$:

$$R(z) = \frac{1 - 2z + z^2}{1 - z} = \frac{(1 - z)^2}{1 - z} = 1 - z$$

The graph is a straight line falling from $R(0) = 1$ to $R(1) = 0$.

**ii.** $C(u,v) = \min(u,v)^{0.25}(uv)^{0.75}$:

$$R(z) = \frac{1 - 2z + \min(z,z)^{0.25}(zz)^{0.75}}{1 - z} = \frac{1 - 2z + z^{0.25}\left(z^2\right)^{0.75}}{1 - z} = \frac{1 - 2z + z^{1.75}}{1 - z}$$

| $z$ | 0 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 0.9 | 0.95 | 0.99 | 0.999 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $R(z)$ | 1 | 0.78 | 0.595 | 0.42 | 0.316 | 0.283 | 0.257 | 0.251 |

The graph falls from $R(0) = 1$ along a gently convex curve and levels off near 0.25 as $z \to 1$ — it does not reach the $z$-axis.

## Part c (1 point)

Explain which copula is more appropriate to model total losses for this insurer. Briefly describe how the graph in part b. above supports your answer.

### Explanation
Property and workers compensation in California might become highly correlated in the event of a large earthquake, so a copula with a heavy right tail is needed. The second copula has the heavier right tail: its $R(z)$ approaches 0.25 as $z \to 1$, while the product copula's goes to 0. Therefore the second copula is more appropriate than the first (the product copula).

Alternatively:

WC and property are generally independent, but there may be correlation in extreme events — an earthquake during a work day could cause both property damage and injuries to employees — so $R(z) > 0$ as $z \to 1$ could be appropriate. For copula ii, $R(z) \to 0.25$ as $z \to 1$, but for copula i, $R(z) \to 0$. Thus copula ii is more appropriate. (The two are similar for smaller $z$, which is also what we'd expect: normally independent.)

### Examiner Report
For the c. part, many candidates correctly selected the proper copula but failed to explain why that copula was the best choice, and in some cases there were no graphs or data present in part b.
but the candidate was still able to guess and select correctly.  We required a visual graph to be present in b. in order to get full credit in c.  We also gave limited partial credit to candidates who selected the first copula as the answer and provided an explanation that property and workers compensation losses are independent.
