The Actuarial Standards Board's guidance to actuaries providing a statement of actuarial opinion relating to the [[Asset Adequacy Analysis|asset adequacy analysis]] of life insurance, annuity, or health insurance reserves and other liabilities, pursuant to applicable law. This revised edition (Doc. No. 203) was adopted by the ASB in September 2021 and is effective for all statements of actuarial opinion within its scope issued on or after June 1, 2022.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam CP-321 (SOA)|Exam CP-321]] — Outcomes 5c; the standard excluding its Appendices.

## Transmittal Memorandum
- ASOP No. 22 was first adopted in 1993; in 2001 the ASB adopted revised versions of ASOP No. 7 and ASOP No. 22 and repealed ASOP No. 14.
- The ASB revised the standard in response to the NAIC's adoption of the Valuation Manual (effective January 1, 2017, with VM-30 giving the annual actuarial opinion and memorandum requirements) and of Actuarial Guideline LI on asset adequacy testing of long-term care insurance reserves.
- The first exposure draft was approved in December 2018 and the second in March 2020; fourteen and eight comment letters were received.

## 2 Definitions
- 2.1 Asset
- 2.2 Asset Adequacy Analysis
    - An analysis of the adequacy of reserves and other liabilities being tested, in light of the assets supporting such reserves and other liabilities, as specified in the statement of actuarial opinion.
- 2.3 Cash Flow
- 2.4 Cash Flow Risk
    - The risk that the amount or timing of cash flows will differ from expectations or assumptions.
- 2.5 Cash Flow Testing
    - The projection and comparison of the timing and amount of cash flows under one or more scenarios in order to evaluate cash flow risks.
- 2.6 Gross Premium Reserve
- 2.7 Gross Premium Reserve Test
- 2.8 Liability
- 2.9 Moderately Adverse Conditions
    - Conditions that include one or more unfavorable, but not extreme, events that have a reasonable probability of occurring during the testing period.
- 2.10 Scenario
- 2.11 Subsequent Events

## 3 Analysis of Issues and Recommended Practices
- 3.1 Asset Adequacy Analysis
    - The actuary should choose a block of assets such that the statement value of those assets is no greater than the statement value of the reserves and other liabilities being tested, and determine whether additional assets are needed to support them under moderately adverse conditions.
    - 3.1.1 Analysis Methods
        - The actuary should consider using cash flow testing, referring to ASOP No. 7; a gross premium reserve test is given as an example of another analysis method.
