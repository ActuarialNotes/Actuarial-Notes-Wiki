**Public Auto Insurance** is the delivery of compulsory automobile coverage by a government-owned monopoly rather than by competing private insurers. Four Canadian provinces use it in some form: **British Columbia (ICBC)**, **Saskatchewan (SGI)**, **Manitoba (MPI)** — all monopolies for basic coverage — and **Quebec (SAAQ)**, which is public for bodily injury only, with property damage left to private insurers.

- **Arguments made for the public model:** lower expense ratio (no commissions, no advertising, no profit margin, a single administrator); universal availability with no [[Residual Market|residual market]] problem; premiums collected efficiently with vehicle registration; and the ability to fund road safety programs from the same body.
- **Arguments made against:** no competition and therefore no innovation or service pressure; the political dynamic that suppresses rates before elections and requires large catch-up increases afterward; cross-subsidies that are decided politically rather than actuarially; and, where the monopoly extends to optional coverage, no consumer choice at all.
- **Rate setting is public.** The insurer files with a provincial utilities board and the hearing is adversarial and on the record — which makes the actuarial work unusually transparent, and unusually contested.
- **Capital and solvency.** A public insurer is not subject to [[OSFI]] and often not to the [[MCT]], so its capital adequacy is governed by a legislated rate stabilisation or capital target instead. Where governments have drawn surpluses into general revenue, the resulting capital deficiency has been a recurring controversy — ICBC's is the best-documented Canadian case.
- **Comparisons are hard and often abused.** Cross-province premium comparisons are meaningless unless the **product** is held constant: benefit levels, access to [[Tort Litigation|tort]], accident benefit limits, whether bodily injury is even in the private premium, and vehicle and driving-density differences all vary. See [[Autorité des marchés financiers|the Quebec example]] for the clearest illustration.
- **Evaluation** should follow the [[Social Insurance]] framework: objective, sustainability, efficiency, equity, and effect on the private market — including the fact that a basic-coverage monopoly still leaves a private market for optional and excess coverage in BC, Saskatchewan and Manitoba.

> [!example]- Is the Public Insurer Cheaper? {Example}
> Province X (public monopoly) reports an average auto premium of $\$1{,}150$ and an expense ratio of $18\%$. Province Y (private, competitive) reports $\$1{,}680$ and an expense ratio of $28\%$. Both have similar populations and vehicle counts.
>
> Can one conclude the public model is cheaper?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Not from these figures. The expense difference is real but explains only part of the gap, and the product may not be the same.
> >
> > **Decompose the premium.** At a $70\%$ target loss ratio, expenses of $28\%$ versus $18\%$ account for roughly $10$ points of premium — about $\$150$ on Y's premium. The remaining gap of roughly $\$380$ must come from **loss cost**, not from efficiency.
> >
> > **Then ask why loss costs differ:**
> >
> > - **Benefit design.** Does X restrict [[Tort Litigation|tort]] access, or cap [[Statutory Accident Benefits|accident benefits]] more tightly? A cheaper product is cheaper for that reason, not because of the ownership model.
> > - **Exposure.** Traffic density, urbanisation, theft rates, weather, repair costs and litigation propensity all differ by province and none is under the insurer's control.
> > - **Capital adequacy.** Is X's premium actually covering its costs? A public insurer running down a rate stabilisation reserve reports a low premium and accumulates a deficit — the ICBC pattern. The honest comparison uses premium *adequate to fund the benefits*, not premium *charged*.
> > - **Off-premium funding.** If X funds part of its scheme from vehicle registration levies or general revenue, the premium understates the cost to the citizen.
> >
> > **The defensible statement** is narrow: the public model demonstrably delivers a lower expense ratio, and that advantage is genuine. Everything else in the comparison is product design and exposure, and attributing it to ownership is the error the question is testing.
