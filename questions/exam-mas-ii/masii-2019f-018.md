You wish to model the effect of gender on a random count outcome variable $X$. To achieve this, the variable for gender, denoted by F, is transformed such that it takes the value of 1 for females, otherwise it takes the value of 0. You fit the following model to your data:

$$
\begin{align*}
X_i &\sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_i) \\
\log(\lambda_i) &= \alpha + \beta_F F_i \\
\alpha &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 10) \\
\beta_F &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 10)
\end{align*}
$$

You are given the following output from the model:

| | Mean | Standard Deviation | Median |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | 2 | 0.5 | 1.9 |
| $\beta_F$ | 0.5 | 0.1 | 0.5 |

You obtain the following samples from the posterior distribution:

| Sample | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | 1.9 | 1.8 | 2.1 | 2.1 | 2.2 | 2.1 | 1.7 |
| $\beta_F$ | 0.4 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.4 | 0.7 | 0.5 | 0.6 |

Calculate the estimate for the posterior median of the average female count based on this information.

- A) Less than 2.5
- B) Greater than or equal to 2.5 but less than 7.5
- C) Greater than or equal to 7.5 but less than 12
- D) Greater than or equal to 12 but less than 13
- E) Greater than or equal to 13

## Explanation

For a female, $F_i = 1$, so the average count is

$$
\lambda = e^{\alpha + \beta_F}.
$$

The quantity of interest is a *function of two correlated parameters*, so it must be computed sample by sample — you cannot take medians from the summary table and combine them. Evaluate $\alpha + \beta_F$ for each of the seven draws:

| Sample | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha + \beta_F$ | 2.3 | 2.3 | 2.6 | 2.5 | 2.9 | 2.6 | 2.3 |

Sorted: 2.3, 2.3, 2.3, **2.5**, 2.6, 2.6, 2.9 — the median of the seven values is 2.5.

Because $e^x$ is monotone increasing, the median passes straight through the transformation:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{median}(\lambda) &= e^{2.5} \\
                       &= 12.18
\end{align*}
$$

(Taking the table medians instead would give $e^{1.9 + 0.5} = e^{2.4} = 11.0$, which lands in band C — that is the trap.)

This falls in $[12,\, 13)$, so the answer is **D**.
