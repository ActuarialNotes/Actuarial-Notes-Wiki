An EY paper explaining how a Non-Life insurer can assess the eligibility requirements for the Premium Allocation Approach (PAA) under IFRS 17 in practice, and the steps it can take to determine how much of its business is eligible. It sets out the main sources of difference between the PAA and the General Measurement Model (Building Block Approach, BBA), a basis for the materiality assessment of groups with contracts longer than one year, and the operational impact of eligibility testing.

## 1 Executive summary
- Many Non-Life insurers will seek to use the [[Premium Allocation Approach|PAA]] for as much of their business as possible, as it is easier to apply and more aligned to current accounting than the [[General Measurement Model]]; contracts that cannot use it must apply the BBA.
- Why PAA for Non-Life insurers?
    - Under the PAA the [[Liability for Remaining Coverage|liability for remaining coverage]] (LFRC) resembles the unearned premium reserve less deferred acquisition costs less premium receivables (plus any additional unexpired risk reserve); it is simpler to calculate, more comparable with current accounting and peers, and its disclosure requirements are expected to be less onerous.
- Which contracts qualify for the PAA?
    - A group is eligible if the coverage period of each contract in it is one year or less, or if the PAA would produce an LFRC for the group that would not differ materially from the BBA's (IFRS 17.53).
    - Materiality is as defined by IAS 1 and IAS 8, assessed at the level of the group, by comparing the LFRC under the PAA and BBA at each reporting date across a range of reasonably expected future scenarios.

## 2 Main sources of difference between the PAA and BBA
- The [[Liability for Incurred Claims|liability for incurred claims]] is in nearly all situations the same under both approaches, so only the LFRC is compared; the sources of difference discussed are not exhaustive.
- Changing expectations of profitability for the remaining coverage
    - The PAA does not recognise a change in remaining profitability until the exposure is earned, while under the BBA the [[Contractual Service Margin|CSM]] is adjusted first; an increase in expected future claims gives a higher LFRC under the BBA, until it exceeds the remaining CSM (Example 1).
    - The effect is greater for longer-duration contracts, uncertain expected loss ratios, contracts exposed to shocks and contracts with longer settlement periods.
- Change in yield curves
    - The BBA's best-estimate cash flows and risk adjustment use current yield curves while its CSM and the PAA's interest accretion use those at initial recognition, so a change in yield curves changes the BBA's LFRC but not the PAA's unless the group becomes onerous (Example 2).
- Uneven earnings pattern
    - The BBA releases the CSM on [[Coverage Units|coverage units]], while PAA revenue follows the passage of time or the expected pattern of release of risk, so seasonal claims, such as property catastrophe exposure, can make the LFRC differ (Example 3).

## 3 Basis for the PAA eligibility assessment
- A group with contracts longer than one year may use the PAA if the entity reasonably expects its LFRC would not differ materially from the BBA's; significant expected variability in the fulfilment cash flows before a claim is incurred fails the criterion (IFRS 17.53(a), 17.54).
- IFRS 17 gives no specific guidance on materiality thresholds or on what "reasonably expects" entails, so judgement is needed for both; testing may be performed on a representative sample of groups.
- Applying IFRS 17 PAA eligibility criteria
    - Diagram 1: a group qualifies if all its contracts have a coverage period of one year or less, or if its future LFRCs do not differ materially under reasonably possible future scenarios.
- Do the future LFRCs differ materially in reasonably possible future scenarios?
    - The LFRC difference between the PAA and BBA is calculated in a base case and shocked scenarios — loss ratios, yield curves, earnings patterns — and measured against a metric such as the total expected premium or the LFRC itself (watching for a gearing effect late in the contract).
    - If the largest difference over all scenarios exceeds a threshold set by management (and discussed with auditors) the group fails; the test is performed on initial recognition only (Diagram 2).

## 4 Operational impact of PAA eligibility testing
- A bespoke model is needed that inputs data from the policy systems, models the LFRC for the base case and shocked scenarios under both approaches, applies shocks at any future period and lets the user set the scenario parameters and materiality thresholds.
- Its set-up cost is expected to be minimal compared with implementing the BBA at full scale, while groups found ineligible must adopt the BBA, which has a significant operational impact.
