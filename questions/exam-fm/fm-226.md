The price of a 36-year zero-coupon bond is 80% of its face value.

A second bond, with the same price, same face value, and same annual effective yield rate, offers annual coupons with the coupon rate equal to $\dfrac{4}{9}$ of the annual effective yield rate.

Calculate the number of years until maturity for the second bond.

- A) 45
- B) 54
- C) 63
- D) 72
- E) 81

## Explanation

Let $i$ be the yield rate, $F$ the face value, $P$ the price and $n$ the second bond's term in years.

**First bond.** $P = 0.8F = Fv^{36}$, so $0.8 = v^{36}$ and $i = 0.8^{-1/36} - 1 = 0.006218$.

**Second bond.** Its coupon rate is $\frac{4}{9}i$, so each coupon is $\frac{4}{9}(0.006218)F = 0.0027634F$. Pricing it at the same yield:
$$
\begin{align*}
P = 0.8F &= Fv^n + \tfrac{4}{9}(0.006218)F\,a_{\overline{n}|0.006218} \\
0.8 &= v^n + 0.0027634\,a_{\overline{n}|0.006218}
\end{align*}
$$

On the BA II Plus: $I/Y = 0.6218$, $PV = -0.8$, $PMT = 0.0027634$, $FV = 1$, CPT $N$ gives $n = 72$.

The same result follows exactly, without the calculator. Since $i\,a_{\overline{n}|} = 1 - v^n$, the coupon term is $\frac{4}{9}i\,a_{\overline{n}|} = \frac{4}{9}(1 - v^n)$:
$$
\begin{align*}
0.8 &= v^n + \tfrac{4}{9}(1 - v^n) \\
\tfrac{5}{9}v^n &= 0.8 - \tfrac{4}{9} \\
&= 0.35556 \\
v^n &= 0.64
\end{align*}
$$

Since $0.64 = 0.8^2 = \left(v^{36}\right)^2 = v^{72}$, the second bond has $n = 72$ years.
