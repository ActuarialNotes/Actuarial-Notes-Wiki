# Exam F1FIE
**F1FIE – Financial Products** is an exam of the finance, investments and ERM track of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Fellowship (FCIA) pathway.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The aim of this exam is for candidates to develop the following skills:
>
> 1. Understand different types of financial products and their uses.
>
> 2. Understand the main principles and techniques of pricing that are relevant to investments.
>
> 3. Apply these principles and techniques within the context of investing.
>
> 4. Understand how pricing links to wider business processes (e.g., business planning and capital setting).
>
> 5. Create and evaluate hypothetical scenarios, including using judgment to assess the implications of actions and to develop appropriate proposals or recommendations relating to investment products.
>
> Exams are administered as an open-book, four-hour exam requiring analysis in the context of a problem and written responses to specific questions. Candidates will complete the exam through the CIA learning management system.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Principal terms {0%}
>
> 1. Define [[Principal|principal]] terms used in [[Financial Product|financial products]] and securities. (A1)

> [!example]- Mathematics, statistics and stochastic calculus {15%}
>
> 1. Understand and apply concepts of [[Probability|probability]] and [[Statistics Important|statistics important]] in [[Mathematical Finance|mathematical finance]]. (C3)
> 2. Understand the importance of the [[No-Arbitrage Condition|no-arbitrage condition]] in [[Risk-Neutral Valuation|asset pricing]]. (B2)
> 3. Apply the concept of [[Martingale|martingale]] in [[Risk-Neutral Valuation|asset pricing]]. (C3)
> 4. Understand and apply [[Itô's Integral|Itô’s integral]] and [[Stochastic Differential Equation|stochastic differential equations]]. (B3)
> 5. Understand and apply [[Itô's Lemma|Itô’s lemma]]. (C3)
> 6. Understand and apply [[Jensen's Inequality|Jensen’s inequality]]. (C3)
> 7. Assess mathematical considerations for analyzing [[Financial Time Series|financial time series]]. (C5)
> 8. Understand the importance of the [[Feynman-Kac Theorem|Feynman-Kac theorem]] to [[Diffusion Process|diffusion processes]]. (C3)

> [!example]- Introduction to option pricing theory {20%}
>
> 1. Understand and apply various techniques for analyzing [[Conditional Heteroscedastic|conditional heteroscedastic]] models, including ARCH (autoregressive conditionally heteroscedastic) and GARCH ([[Generalized Autoregressive|generalized autoregressive]] conditionally heteroscedastic). (C3)
> 2. Perform calculations and evaluate results of [[Risk-Neutral Valuation|option-pricing]] techniques and theory for equity and [[Interest Rate Derivative|interest rate derivatives]]. (C6)
> 3. Apply the basic concepts of [[Currency Market|currency markets]] ([[Purchase-Price Parity|purchase-price parity]], law of one price, etc.). (B3)
> 4. Identify differences and implications of real-world versus [[Risk-Neutral Probability|risk-neutral probability]] measures. (B4)
> 5. Understand and apply [[Girsanov's Theorem|Girsanov’s theorem]] in changing measures. (C3)
> 6. Understand and apply the [[Black-Scholes-Merton PDE]] (partial [[Differential Equation|differential equation]]). (C3)
> 7. Identify limitations of the [[Black-Scholes Pricing Formula|Black-Scholes pricing formula]].
> 8. Describe and apply approaches for relaxing the assumptions used in the [[Black-Scholes Model|Black-Scholes formula]]. (C3)
> 9. Apply and assess alternatives to the [[Black-Scholes Model|Black-Scholes-Merton]] model or alternative techniques that can be used to deal with [[Option-Pricing Technique Limitation|option-pricing technique limitations]]. (B6)
> 10. Demonstrate an understanding of [[Interest Rate|interest rate]] models. (B2)
> 11. Understand and apply the concept of calibration and describe the issues related to calibration. ([[B6]])
> 12. Understand and assess the Heath-Jarrow-Morton (HJM) model and the [[HJM No-Arbitrage Condition|HJM no-arbitrage condition]]. (B6)

> [!example]- Introduction to credit and liquidity risk {20%}
>
> 1. Understand [[Event|events]] and causes of the 2008 [[Global Credit Crisis|global credit crisis]] relating to [[Mortgage-Backed Security|mortgage-backed securities]] (MBS) and [[Credit-Default Swap|credit-default swaps]] (CDS). (B2)
> 2. Understand the basic concepts of [[Credit Risk Modelling|credit risk modelling]] such as [[Probability|probability]] of default, loss given default, [[Exposure Base|exposure]] at default, and [[Expected Loss|expected loss]]. (B3)
> 3. Apply and assess credit-valuation models. ([[B6]])
> 4. Apply and assess the [[Merton Asset-Value|Merton asset-value]] models in the context of [[Credit Risk|credit risk]]. (B6)
> 5. Understand and apply [[Credit-Default Swap|credit-default swaps]]. (C3)
> 6. Apply and assess mortgage-default models in the [[Valuation|valuation]] of [[Mortgage-Backed Security|mortgage-backed securities]] (B6)
> 7. Measure and perform [[Marking-To-Market Counterparty Credit Risk|marking-to-market counterparty credit risk]] in [[Credit Derivative|credit derivatives]]. (C5)
> 8. Understand the rationale, markets, and [[Risk|risks]] of [[Structured Finance|structured finance]]. (B4)
> 9. Compare the use of credit-spread measures in [[Portfolio|portfolio]] construction. (C2)
> 10. Describe [[Risk|risk]] considerations in investment-grade versus [[High-Yield Bond Portfolio|high-yield bond portfolios]]. (C2)
> 11. Understand the concept of [[Liquidity Risk|liquidity risk]] and the threat it represents to [[Financial Intermediary|financial intermediaries]] and markets. (B2)
> 12. Measure and monitor [[Liquidity Risk|liquidity risk]], using various liquidity-measurement tools and ratios. (C5)
> 13. Understand [[Liability-Termination Provision|liability-termination provisions]] such as [[Book-Value Surrender|book-value surrender]] and the impact on a [[Company's Overall Liquidity Risk|company’s overall liquidity risk]]. (B3)
> 14. Apply [[Liquidity Risk|liquidity risk]] models, including [[Modelling Cash Flow|modelling cash flow]] of various types of assets under various scenario analysis and time horizons. (D6)
> 15. Create [[Liquidity Risk Management Plan|liquidity risk management plans]] and procedures, including addressing appropriate product design, [[Investment Guideline|investment guidelines]], and reporting given a [[Desired Liquidity Risk Level|desired liquidity risk level]]. (C6)
> 16. Be familiar with [[Rating Agency Expectation|rating agency expectations]] for liquidity and the implications for [[Company Rating|company ratings]]. (D4)

> [!example]- Derivatives and hedging {10%}
>
> 1. Compare and contrast the various kinds of [[Volatility|volatility]]. (C4)
> 2. Compare and contrast various approaches for setting [[Volatility|volatility]] assumptions in [[Hedging|hedging]]. (C4)
> 3. Compare and contrast different approaches to [[Hedging|hedging]]. (C4)
> 4. Perform [[Delta Hedging|delta hedging]] and understand the interplay between [[Hedging|hedging]] assumptions and [[Hedging|hedging]] outcomes. (B3)
> 5. Describe and understand [[Economic Scenario|economic scenarios]] where [[Hedging Strategy|hedging strategies]] may be ineffective for certain [[Portfolio|portfolios]]. (B2)
> 6. Demonstrate how [[Interest Rate Swap|interest rate swaps]], forwards, and futures can be used to modify a [[Portfolio's Risk|portfolio’s risk]] and return. (C3)
> 7. Utilize derivatives to achieve [[Targeted Equity|targeted equity]] and [[Interest Rate Risk Exposure|interest rate risk exposures]]. (C3)
> 8. Discuss methods of [[Forecasting Volatility|forecasting volatility]] and understand the applications of [[Volatility|volatility]] and [[Variance Swap|variance swaps]]. (C5)

> [!example]- Fixed-income securities {20%}
>
> 1. Explain the [[Cash Flow|cash-flow]] characteristics and pricing of [[Government Security|government securities]]. (B2)
> 2. Analyze [[Par Yield Curve|par yield curves]], [[Spot Curve|spot curves]], and [[Forward Curve|forward curves]] and their relationship to [[Traded Security Price|traded security prices]]. (B4)
> 3. Analyze the different characteristics of securities issued by [[Government Agency|government agencies]]. (B4)
> 4. Evaluate features of provincial and [[Municipal Bond|municipal bonds]], and the role of [[Rating Agency|rating agencies]] in pricing them. (C5)
> 5. Describe the [[Cash Flow|cash flow]] of various [[Corporate Bond|corporate bonds]] considering underlying [[Risk|risks]] such as [[Interest Rate|interest rate]], [[Credit|credit]], and [[Event Risk|event risks]]. (A2)
> 6. Analyze [[Cash-Flow Pattern|cash-flow patterns]] and underlying drivers and [[Risk|risks]] of [[Mortgage-Backed Security|mortgage-backed securities]] and [[Collateralized Mortgage Obligation|collateralized mortgage obligations]]. (D4)
> 7. Construct [[Portfolio|portfolios]] of [[Fixed-Income Security|fixed-income securities]] using the following broad categories: (D6)
> 8. Managing funds against a [[Immunization|target return]]; and
> 9. Evaluate strategies for managing funds and [[Immunizing Fund|immunizing funds]] against single and [[Multiple Liability|multiple liabilities]].
> 10. Understand and discuss approaches to setting expectations for [[Fixed-Income Return|fixed-income returns]]. (D5)
> 11. Evaluate the effects of [[Capital Markets Expectation|capital markets expectations]] on the [[Yield Curve|yield curve]]. (C5)
> 12. Perform calculations of [[Modified Duration|modified duration]], [[Convexity|convexity]], and [[Duration Times Spread|duration times spread]] (DTS) and its applications towards various [[Fixed-Income Strategy|fixed-income strategies]] (D5)
> 13. Apply the model to price common [[Interest Sensitive Instrument|interest sensitive instruments]], including [[Callable Bond|callable bonds]], [[Bond Option|bond options]], [[Cap on Non-Pecuniary Damages|caps]], [[Payoff|floors]] and [[Swaption|swaptions]]. (C3)

> [!example]- Guaranteed investment products and annuities {15%}
>
> 1. Identify and evaluate the impact of [[Embedded Option|embedded options]] in liabilities, specifically [[Variable Annuities Guaranteed Rider|variable annuities guaranteed riders]] ([[Guaranteed Minimum Accumulation Benefit|guaranteed minimum accumulation benefit]] [GMAB], [[Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit|guaranteed minimum death benefit]] [GMDB], [[Guaranteed Minimum Withdrawal Benefit|guaranteed minimum withdrawal benefit]] [GMWB], and [[Guaranteed Minimum Income Benefit|guaranteed minimum income benefit]] [GMIB]). (C5)
> 2. Understand [[Risk|risks]] associated with [[Guaranteed Rider|guaranteed riders]], including market, insurance, [[Policyholder|policyholder]] behaviour, basis, [[Credit|credit]], regulatory, and accounting. (B2)
> 3. Perform [[Risk Management|risk management]] and [[Hedging|dynamic hedging]] for [[Existing GMxB|existing GMxB]] and its [[Embedded Option|embedded options]]. (D6)
> 4. Analyze [[Hedgeable Component|hedgeable components]] that are equity, [[Interest Rate|interest rate]], [[Volatility|volatility]], and [[Option Greeks|cross Greeks]]. (C4)
> 5. Analyze partially hedgeable or [[Unhedgeable Component|unhedgeable components]] that are [[Policyholder|policyholder]] behaviour, [[Mortality|mortality]] and [[Lapse|lapse]], [[Basis Risk|basis risk]], [[Counterparty Exposure|counterparty exposure]], [[Foreign Bond|foreign bonds]] and equities, [[Correlation|correlation]], and operation failures. (C2)
> 6. Compare and contrast static vs. [[Hedging|dynamic hedging]]. (C4)
> 7. Analyze how differences between modeled and actual outcomes for guarantees affect financial results over time. ([[D4]])
