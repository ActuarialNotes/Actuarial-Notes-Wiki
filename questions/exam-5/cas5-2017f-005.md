Given the following information for an insurance company as of December 31, 2016:

| Accident Year | Earned Premium (\$000) | Reported Loss (\$000) | Cumulative Loss Development Factors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 3,000 | 1,500 | 1.05 |
| 2013 | 3,500 | 1,925 | 1.10 |
| 2014 | 3,300 | 1,749 | 1.20 |
| 2015 | 3,200 | 1,984 | 1.35 |
| 2016 | 3,800 | 2,470 | 1.40 |

- All policies are annual.
- Annual loss cost trend = 3%.
- The company has increased rates by 5% every year on January 1.
- The company writes policies uniformly throughout the year.

Calculate accident year 2016 trended ultimate loss using the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method with the expected loss ratio based on accident years 2012 through 2014 experience.

### Answer
Approximately \$3,075 thousand

### Explanation
The expected loss ratio must be built on a **common basis** — losses developed and trended to the 2016 cost level, premium brought to a single rate level — and then applied to premium on that same basis.

**Step 1 — trended ultimate losses for 2012–2014.** Develop each year to ultimate, then trend from its accident year to 2016 at 3%:

| AY | Reported | $\times$ CDF | $\times 1.03^{2016-AY}$ | Trended Ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 1,500 | 1.05 | $1.03^{4}$ | 1,772.68 |
| 2013 | 1,925 | 1.10 | $1.03^{3}$ | 2,313.85 |
| 2014 | 1,749 | 1.20 | $1.03^{2}$ | 2,226.62 |
| **Total** | | | | **6,313.14** |

**Step 2 — on-level earned premium (parallelogram method).** Rates rise 5% every January 1 and policies are annual, written uniformly. A January 1 change means each calendar year's earned premium comes half from the prior year's writings and half from the current year's:

$$ARL_Y = 0.5 \times (\text{level of year } Y{-}1 \text{ writings}) + 0.5 \times (\text{level of year } Y \text{ writings})$$

Indexing the 2011 writings at 1.000, the writings level for year $Y$ is $1.05^{\,Y-2011}$, and the current rate level is that of the 2016 writings, $1.05^{5} = 1.276282$:

| CY | Average rate level | OLF | On-level EP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | $0.5(1.00) + 0.5(1.05) = 1.025000$ | 1.245153 | 3,735.46 |
| 2013 | $0.5(1.05) + 0.5(1.05^2) = 1.076250$ | 1.185860 | 4,150.51 |
| 2014 | $0.5(1.05^2) + 0.5(1.05^3) = 1.130063$ | 1.129390 | 3,726.99 |
| 2016 | $0.5(1.05^4) + 0.5(1.05^5) = 1.245894$ | 1.024390 | 3,892.68 |

$$\text{Total 2012–2014 on-level EP} = 3{,}735.46 + 4{,}150.51 + 3{,}726.99 = 11{,}612.96$$

**Step 3 — expected loss ratio.** One ratio across the three years, rather than an average of yearly ratios:

$$ELR = \frac{6{,}313.14}{11{,}612.96} = 0.5436$$

**Step 4 — Bornhuetter-Ferguson for accident year 2016.** The expected losses use the **on-level** 2016 premium, matching the basis the ELR was derived on:

$$\text{Ultimate}_{2016} = \text{Reported} + ELR \times \text{Premium} \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{CDF}\right)$$

$$= 2{,}470 + 0.5436 \times 3{,}892.68 \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{1.40}\right) = 2{,}470 + 604.6 = \$3{,}074.6 \text{ thousand}$$

Because all the losses were trended to the 2016 level and accident year 2016 is already at that level, this ultimate is the trended ultimate; no further trending applies.

Answers in the range of roughly \$3,060–\$3,075 thousand were accepted, depending on whether premium was brought to the current 2016 rate level or the average 2016 rate level, and on which averaging was used for the loss ratio.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how to develop losses to ultimate, on-level premium, and trend losses to the appropriate time period. Answers that either brought premium to the current 2016 rate level or the average 2016 rate level were both awarded credit.

Additionally, candidates were expected to select a loss ratio and apply the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method correctly. If reasoning/assumptions were stated then the candidate could select any loss ratio from accident years 2012-2014 (weighted average, straight average, exclude certain years, etc.) to apply to the BF method and receive credit.

Common errors included:

- On-leveling or trending to the wrong dates
- Not applying development factors to losses
- Only providing IBNR portion of BF calculation rather than the ultimate loss
- Selecting a loss ratio not based on 2012-2014
