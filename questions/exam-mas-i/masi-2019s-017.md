You are given the following information:

- $\tau$ is a parameter of a distribution
- The true value of $\tau$ is 2
- $\hat{\alpha}$ and $\hat{\beta}$ are two uncorrelated estimators for $\tau$
- $E(\hat{\alpha}) = 2.0$
- $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\alpha}) = 5.0$
- $E(\hat{\beta}) = 3.0$
- $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}) = 1.0$

Consider the class of estimators of $\tau$ which are of the form: $w\hat{\alpha} + (1 - w)\hat{\beta}$.

Calculate the value of $w$ that results in an estimator with the smallest mean squared error.

- A) Less than 0.2
- B) At least 0.2, but less than 0.4
- C) At least 0.4, but less than 0.6
- D) At least 0.6, but less than 0.8
- E) At least 0.8

## Explanation

Write the mean squared error of the combined estimator as variance plus squared bias, then minimize over $w$.

The mean of $\hat{\tau} = w\hat{\alpha} + (1-w)\hat{\beta}$ and its bias against the true value $\tau = 2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E(\hat{\tau}) &= 2.0w + 3.0(1-w) \\
&= 3 - w \\
\text{Bias}(\hat{\tau}) &= (3 - w) - 2 \\
&= 1 - w
\end{align*}
$$

Because the two estimators are uncorrelated, the covariance term drops out of the variance:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\tau}) = 5w^2 + (1-w)^2
$$

So

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{MSE}(w) &= 5w^2 + (1-w)^2 + (1-w)^2 \\
&= 5w^2 + 2(1-w)^2
\end{align*}
$$

Set the derivative to zero:

$$
\begin{align*}
10w - 4(1-w) &= 0 \\
14w &= 4 \\
w &= 0.2857
\end{align*}
$$

The second derivative is $14 > 0$, so this is the minimum. This falls in $[0.2, 0.4)$, so the answer is **B**.
