# GB Module 1
**GB Module 1 – Actuarial work and group benefits applications** is one of the two modules of the group benefits track of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Fellowship (FCIA) pathway.

> [!question]- About the module
>
> The primary audience for the FCIA modules is individuals who desire to be admitted as Fellows of the CIA.
>
> Individuals seeking continuing professional development credit can also register for the FCIA modules.
>
> Each FCIA candidate through Pathways 1 and 2 is required to complete both FCIA modules, while Pathway 3 candidates will have to complete the second module. It is anticipated that FCIA module candidates will have six months from the date of module purchase to complete each FCIA module. As a general guideline, candidates should allocate approximately 100 hours to complete all activities included in a module.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Introduction to risk and insurance
>
> 1. Describe [[Risk|risk]] including [[Insurable Risk|insurable risk]].
> 2. Provide examples of types of [[Insurable Risk|insurable risks]] and classes of [[Life Insurance|life insurance]] that respond to [[Insurable Risk|insurable risk]].
> 3. Describe and provide examples of perils and [[Hazard|hazards]].
> 4. Explain the conflicts that can arise in the objectives of [[Risk Management|risk management]].
> 5. Identify and describe the five steps of the [[Risk Management Process|risk management process]]:
>    - a. Step 1 – Identifying and analyzing [[Exposure Base|exposures]];
>    - b. Step 2 – [[Formulating Option|Formulating options]];
>    - c. Step 3 – Selecting the best technique;
>    - d. Step 4 – Implementing the [[Risk Management Plan|risk management plan]]; and
>    - e. Step 5 – Monitoring results and modifying the plan.

> [!example]- Insurance categories and functions
>
> 1. Describe the basic insurance relationship of [[Pooling Fund|pooling funds]] to [[Pay Loss|pay losses]].
> 2. Explain the major functions of insurance:
>    - a. Spread of [[Risk|risk]].
>    - b. Aid to security.
>    - c. Aid to [[Credit|credit]].
>    - d. [[Loss Prevention|Loss prevention]].
>    - e. Source of [[Capital|capital]].
>    - f. Source of employment.
> 3. Identify and describe categories of [[Insurance|insurance]].
> 4. Identify and describe categories of [[Retirement Scheme|retirement schemes]].
> 5. Describe the different forms of [[Insurer|insurance companies]] (e.g., [[Stock Mutual|stock mutual]], [[Government Insurer|government insurers]], [[Captive Insurance Company|captive insurance companies]].)
> 6. Explain [[Financial Stability|financial stability]] and returns for [[Insurer|insurers]] including the differences for [[Stock Company|stock companies]] and mutuals.

> [!example]- Regulatory framework
>
> 1. Describe the different roles of government in the regulation of [[Insurer|insurance companies]] including federal control and provincial and territorial control.
> 2. Describe the major laws and [[Regulations Impacting Insurance|regulations impacting insurance]] in Canada including:
>    - a. The [[Insurance Companies Act]].
>    - b. Provincial and [[Territorial Insurance Act|territorial insurance acts]].
>    - c. Regulations.
> 3. Describe the role of [[Privacy Law|privacy laws]] in [[Life Insurance|life insurance]] including the [[Personal Information Protection]] and [[Electronic Documents Act]].
> 4. Describe how the [[United State|United States]] and Canada monitor the [[Insurance Market|insurance market]] and regulate it.
> 5. Describe why [[Consumer Protection|consumer protections]] are needed.
> 6. Explain the purposes of [[Regulatory Consumer Law|regulatory consumer laws]] and regulations.
> 7. Recognize the key points of a sample of regulations or laws relating to [[Market Regulation|market regulation]].

> [!example]- Insurance company operations
>
> 1. Describe the major functions of an [[Insurer|insurance company]], including:
>    - a. Finance, accounting, and investment.
>    - b. Actuarial.
>    - c. Marketing, agency, or production.
>    - d. [[Underwriting]].
>    - e. [[Claim|Claims]].
>    - f. [[Reinsurance]].
>    - g. [[Risk Management|Risk management]].
> 2. Describe basic [[Insurance Administration|insurance administration]] and how [[Actuary|actuaries]] are involved.
> 3. Describe [[Underwriting Regime|underwriting regimes]], applications for insurance, preferred criteria, and [[Screening Test|screening tests]], and how they relate to selection of [[Risk|risk]].
> 4. For [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]]:
>    - a. Explain the purpose of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]].
>    - b. Describe the primary methods and [[Types of Reinsurance|types of reinsurance]].
>    - c. Describe the [[Main Reinsurance Contract Provision|main reinsurance contract provisions]].
> 5. Describe the roles and responsibilities of [[Actuary|actuaries]] in [[Insurer|insurance companies]].

> [!example]- Insurance documents and processes
>
> 1. Understand insurance as a contract and describe the major components of an [[Insurance Policy|insurance policy]].
> 2. Identify and explain key [[Insurance Document|insurance documents]] including:
>    - a. The policy.
>    - b. Certificates of insurance.
>    - c. [[Endorsement|Endorsements]].
>    - d. [[Binders-cover Note|Binders/cover notes]].
> 3. Describe the major sections of an [[Insurance Policy|insurance policy]] including:
>    - a. [[Coverage Summary|Coverage summary]].
>    - b. [[Insuring Agreement|Insuring agreements]].
>    - c. [[Statutory Condition|Statutory conditions]].
>    - d. [[Policy Condition|Policy conditions]].
>    - e. [[Signature Clause|Signature clause]].
> 4. Describe the process and requirements for assigning, terminating, and renewing an [[Insurance Contract|insurance contract]].
> 5. Identify the stakeholders in an [[Insurance Policy|insurance policy]] ([[Policyholder|policyholder]], insureds, including employees and dependents, etc.).
> 6. Describe [[Insurance Documentation|insurance documentation]] and processes.

> [!example]- The insurance marketplace
>
> 1. Describe basic [[Insurance Policy|insurance products]]:
>    - a. [[Life Insurance|Life insurance]] and [[Annuities|annuities]].
>    - b. Property and casualty (P&C).
>    - c. Health and [[Group Insurance|group insurance]].
>    - d. Government-sponsored and [[Public Plan|public plans]]:
>       - i. [[Employment Insurance]];
>       - ii. [[Workers' Compensation|Workers’ compensation]];
>       - iii. [[Health Care|Health care]]; and
>       - iv. [[Pension Plan|Pension plans]].
> 2. Describe key issues related to customers of [[Insurance Policy|insurance products]].

> [!example]- Consulting firm operations
>
> 1. Describe the traditional functions of a [[Consulting Firm|consulting firm]]:
>    - a. Administration and consulting of [[Pension Plan|pension plans]].
>    - b. [[Rate Regulation|Filing requirements]].
>    - c. Actuarial [[Valuation|valuation]] and [[Financial Reporting|financial reporting]] requirements.
>    - d. [[Defined Benefit Plan|Benefit plan]] design and amendments.
>    - e. Audit and [[Peer Review|peer review]].
>    - f. [[Appointed Actuary]] tasks and responsibilities.
>    - g. Product design and pricing.
>    - h. Negotiation of [[Premium Rate|premium rates]].
>    - i. Sales and [[Probability Distributions|distribution]] of insurance.
>    - j. [[Third-party Administration|Third-party administration]].
>    - k. [[Risk Management|Risk management]].
> 2. Describe the roles and responsibilities of [[Actuary|actuaries]] in [[Consulting Firm|consulting firms]].

> [!example]- Introduction to employee benefits
>
> 1. Identify the [[Main Component|main components]] of [[Total Compensation|total compensation]].
> 2. Describe the role of [[Employee Benefit|employee benefits]] within [[Total Compensation|total compensation]].
> 3. Describe the role of [[Group Insurance|group insurance]] within [[Employee Benefit|employee benefits]].

> [!example]- Principles of actuarial evidence
>
> 1. Describe the role of [[Actuary|actuaries]] in [[Family Law|family law]], including the [[Valuation|valuation]] of [[Pension Plan Benefit|pension plan benefits]] in marriage (relationship) breakdown.
> 2. Describe the role of [[Actuary|actuaries]] in [[Civil Litigation|civil litigation]], including damages in [[Personal Injury|personal injuries]], fatalities, [[Wrongful Termination|wrongful termination]], insurance and [[Reinsurance Dispute|reinsurance disputes]], [[Pension Dispute|pension disputes]], and other litigation.
> 3. Describe the role conferred to [[Actuary|actuaries]] by the [[Criminal Code]] of Canada in calculating the [[Criminal Rate|criminal rate]] of [[Interest|interest]].
> 4. Describe and distinguish the roles of a [[Fact Witness|fact witness]], an [[Expert Witness|expert witness]], and a provider of [[Litigation Advice|litigation advice]].
> 5. Describe the context provided by the common law, legislation, and the rules of civil procedure on actuarial [[Evidence|evidence]] practice.
> 6. Describe the role of [[CIA Rule|CIA Rules]] of [[Professional Conduct]] and the [[Actuarial Standards of Practice|Standards of Practice]] (Part 1000, general, and Part 4000, actuarial evidence) in guiding the professionalism of actuarial evidence practice. [[Show Awareness|Show awareness]] of the importance of professionalism for [[Actuary|actuaries]] whose actuarial evidence practice is incidental to full-time practice in another actuarial practice area, such as insurance or [[Pension|pensions]].
> 7. Describe the functions fulfilled by other professionals in the litigation area, such as [[Life-Care Planner|life-care planners]], [[Vocational Expert|vocational experts]], accountants, economists, engineers, [[Personal Financial Planner|personal financial planners]], [[Business Valuator|business valuators]], appraisers and [[Structured Settlement Broker|structured settlement brokers]], and how these [[Professionals Complement|professionals complement]] and compete with the function of [[Actuary|actuaries]] in actuarial evidence.
