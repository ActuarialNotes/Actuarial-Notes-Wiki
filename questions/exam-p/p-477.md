The time, in years, until replacement for a new telephone pole has probability density function

$$
f(t) = \begin{cases}
kt, & \text{for } 0 < t < 20 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $k$ is a constant.

Calculate the probability that a new telephone pole will be replaced within ten years given that it is not replaced within five years.

- A) $0.19$
- B) $0.20$
- C) $0.25$
- D) $0.33$
- E) $0.94$

## Explanation

The conditional probability is

$$
P[T \leq 10 \mid T \geq 5] = \frac{P[5 \leq T \leq 10]}{P[T \geq 5]}
$$

Both pieces integrate $kt$, whose antiderivative is $kt^2/2$. The constant $k$ appears in numerator and denominator alike and cancels, so it never needs to be found:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[T \leq 10 \mid T \geq 5] &= \frac{\int_5^{10} kt\,dt}{\int_5^{20} kt\,dt} \\
                           &= \frac{t^2\big|_5^{10}}{t^2\big|_5^{20}} \\
                           &= \frac{100 - 25}{400 - 25} \\
                           &= \frac{75}{375} \\
                           &= \mathbf{0.20}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.19, is the unconditional $P[5 \leq T \leq 10] = 75/400$. Compare a new pole over its first five years: with $k = 1/200$, $P[T \leq 5] = 25/400 = 0.0625$. Because the density rises with $t$, a pole that has already lasted five years is *more* likely to be replaced in the next five years (0.20) than a new one is — whereas an exponential lifetime, being memoryless, would make the two probabilities equal.
