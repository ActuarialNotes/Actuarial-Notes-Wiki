You are given the following information:

- An insurer has observed the following nine losses:

$$
500,\ 600,\ 750,\ 880,\ 940,\ 1000,\ 1050,\ 1060,\ 1400
$$

- A uniform kernel function with bandwidth 75 is used to estimate the distribution of loss sizes
- $\hat{F}(Y)$ is the kernel-smoothed estimate of the cumulative distribution function

Calculate $\hat{F}(1{,}000)$.

- A) Less than 0.55
- B) At least 0.55, but less than 0.60
- C) At least 0.60, but less than 0.65
- D) At least 0.65, but less than 0.70
- E) At least 0.70

## Explanation

With a uniform kernel of bandwidth $b$, each observation $y_i$ is spread evenly over $[y_i - b,\ y_i + b]$. Its kernel CDF at $x$ is 0 below that interval, 1 above it, and linear across it:

$$
K_i(x) = \frac{x - (y_i - b)}{2b}, \qquad y_i - b \le x \le y_i + b
$$

The smoothed CDF averages these: $\hat{F}(x) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} K_i(x)$. With $b = 75$ the window is 150 wide. At $x = 1{,}000$:

- 500, 600, 750 and 880 all have $y_i + 75 \le 1{,}000$, so each contributes 1.
- $940$: interval $[865, 1015]$, so $K = (1000 - 865)/150 = 0.9$.
- $1000$: interval $[925, 1075]$, so $K = (1000 - 925)/150 = 0.5$.
- $1050$: interval $[975, 1125]$, so $K = (1000 - 975)/150 = 0.1667$.
- $1060$: interval $[985, 1135]$, so $K = (1000 - 985)/150 = 0.1$.
- $1400$: interval starts at 1,325, so it contributes 0.

$$
\begin{align*}
\hat{F}(1{,}000) &= \frac{4 + 0.9 + 0.5 + 0.1667 + 0.1 + 0}{9} \\
&= \frac{5.6667}{9} \\
&= 0.6296
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[0.60, 0.65)$, so the answer is **C**.
