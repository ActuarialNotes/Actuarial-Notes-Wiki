A loan is originally scheduled to be paid in installments of 1000 payable at the end of each month for three years. The amortization is calculated with an annual nominal interest rate of 9% convertible monthly. After paying one full year of scheduled installments, the borrower increases monthly payments to 2000, resulting in a final drop payment.

Calculate the number of months after the first year that it takes for the borrower to pay off the loan.

- A) 10
- B) 11
- C) 12
- D) 13
- E) 14

## Explanation

The monthly interest rate is $\dfrac{0.09}{12} = 0.0075$. After the 12 scheduled payments of the first year, 24 payments of 1000 remain, so the outstanding balance is their present value:

$$
\begin{align*}
OB_{12} &= 1000\,a_{\overline{24}|0.0075} \\
        &= 1000(21.88915) \\
        &= 21{,}889.15
\end{align*}
$$

The borrower now pays 2000 a month. The number of payments of 2000 that this balance supports solves

$$
\begin{align*}
2000\,a_{\overline{n}|0.0075} &= 21{,}889.15 \\
a_{\overline{n}|0.0075} &= 10.94457 \\
v^{n} &= 1 - 10.94457(0.0075) \\
      &= 0.917916 \\
n &= -\frac{\ln 0.917916}{\ln 1.0075} \\
  &= 11.46
\end{align*}
$$

So the borrower makes 11 full payments of 2000 and a smaller drop payment at the 12th month: the loan is paid off 12 months after the first year.
