A public policy practice note, dated September 2010, in which the American Academy of Actuaries' Health Practice Financial Reporting Committee describes practices it considers commonly employed by U.S. health actuaries, to assist actuaries who must prepare financial statements by giving examples of common approaches. It is organized as nineteen questions on contract reserves under statutory (SAP) and GAAP accounting, and is not a promulgation of the Actuarial Standards Board or binding on any actuary.

## Introduction
- The statutory accounting guidance discussed is based on the March 2009 NAIC Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual, in particular SSAP 54 and Appendix A-010, Minimum Reserve Standards for Individual and Group Health Insurance Contracts.
- GAAP guidance historically resided in FAS 60 and is now found in ASC Topic 944; the note refers to FAS 60 as the source of relevant GAAP guidance throughout.
- A contract reserve for a policy is defined as the excess of the present value of future policy claims over the present value of future net premiums.

## 1 What is the purpose of contract reserves?
- Contract reserves arise when the future pattern of expected claim payments does not match the future pattern of benefit net premiums, and represent the portion of current and past premiums needed to pre-fund future increases in claims.

## 2 What is the main GAAP and SAP guidance on contract reserves, and how does that guidance differ between the two bases of accounting?
- FAS 60 requires a provision for adverse deviation in the assumptions used to calculate benefit reserves, and the lock-in principle: the assumptions in place when a contract was first recognized continue to be used unless testing indicates a premium deficiency, when they are unlocked and replaced with current assumptions.
- SSAP 54 provides guidance on policy reserves for accident and health contracts, which it defines to include both unearned premium reserves and contract reserves.
