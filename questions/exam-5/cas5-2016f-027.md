Given the following information:

| Accident Year | Selected Ultimate Claim Counts as of December 31, 2014 | Reported Claim Counts as of December 31, 2014 | Reported Claim Counts as of December 31, 2015 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 7,500 | 1,000 | 3,500 |
| 2014 | 8,600 | 600 | 3,400 |

| Maturity | Cumulative Percent of Claim Counts Reported |
|---|---|
| 36 | 55% |
| 24 | 30% |
| 12 | 8% |

## Part a (1 points)

Compare actual reported claim count emergence to expected claim count emergence on reported claim counts in calendar year 2015.

### Answer
Actual 5,300 versus expected 4,234 — actual emergence exceeded expected by 1,066 counts, about 25%.

### Explanation
First fix each accident year's maturity. At 12/31/2014 accident year 2013 is at **24 months** and accident year 2014 is at **12 months**; one calendar year later they sit at 36 and 24 months respectively.

**Expected emergence.** The counts still to be reported at the start of the year are the unreported balance, and the fraction of *that* balance expected to emerge during the year is the growth in the reporting pattern divided by the portion not yet reported:

$$\text{Expected} = \left(\text{Ultimate} - \text{Reported}\right) \times \frac{\%_{\text{end}} - \%_{\text{begin}}}{1 - \%_{\text{begin}}}$$

The denominator is $1 - \%_{\text{begin}}$, not 1, because the multiplier is applied to what remains unreported rather than to the ultimate.

$$\text{AY 2013} = (7{,}500 - 1{,}000) \times \frac{0.55 - 0.30}{1 - 0.30} = 6{,}500 \times 0.3571 = 2{,}321$$

$$\text{AY 2014} = (8{,}600 - 600) \times \frac{0.30 - 0.08}{1 - 0.08} = 8{,}000 \times 0.2391 = 1{,}913$$

$$\text{Total expected} = 2{,}321 + 1{,}913 = 4{,}234$$

**Actual emergence** is simply the change in reported counts over calendar year 2015:

$$\text{AY 2013} = 3{,}500 - 1{,}000 = 2{,}500 \qquad \text{AY 2014} = 3{,}400 - 600 = 2{,}800$$

$$\text{Total actual} = 2{,}500 + 2{,}800 = 5{,}300$$

**Comparison.**

| AY | Actual | Expected | Actual $-$ Expected | Actual / Expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 2,500 | 2,321 | +179 | 1.077 |
| 2014 | 2,800 | 1,913 | +887 | 1.464 |
| **Total** | **5,300** | **4,234** | **+1,066** | **1.252** |

Actual emergence exceeded expected in **both** accident years, and by 25% in total. Counts are reporting faster than the assumed pattern, or the selected ultimates are too low — either way the prior selections warrant revisiting, with accident year 2014 the more adverse of the two.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate both actual and expected calendar year 2015 emergence for each of the two accident years, and then to actually make the comparison — by subtraction, by division, or by displaying both figures and commenting on which is larger.

Common mistakes included not calculating the calendar year emergence for the actual side; calculating emergence for only one of the two accident years; and computing both values but never comparing them — saying merely that they "don't match", or setting the results side by side with no commentary.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Briefly describe a potential limitation of the actual vs. expected calculation performed in part a. above and propose an alternative calculation that addresses this limitation.

### Explanation
Any one limitation paired with an alternative that addresses it. For example:

**Limitation — the comparison is made over a full calendar year, but reporting is not uniform within it.** Claims emerge more heavily early in the period, so a single annual comparison can mask when in the year the deviation arose, and can look adverse simply because of where the evaluation falls.
*Alternative:* perform the actual-versus-expected comparison over **shorter increments** — quarterly or monthly emergence against a pattern interpolated to the same increments — so the timing of the deviation is visible.

**Limitation — the expected pattern comes from the reported claim count development technique, but the selected ultimates may not.** If the ultimate counts were selected using a different method, the reporting pattern used to spread them is inconsistent with the selection it is being applied to.
*Alternative:* derive the emergence pattern from the technique that actually produced the selected ultimates — for instance comparing the historical **closed** claim count development triangle against those selected ultimates to build a consistent pattern.

**Limitation — the calculation relies on historical development factors, so it adapts slowly.** If reporting has genuinely speeded up, expected emergence will understate actual year after year, and the method will keep signalling a deviation without accommodating the new pattern.
*Alternative:* build **disposal rate triangles** and apply a Berquist-Sherman adjustment to restate the historical pattern onto the current reporting rate before computing expected emergence.

**Limitation — it ignores shifts in mix of business or in the kind of claims being reported.** A change in the volume of small, immaterial claims moves the count-based ratio without implying any change in ultimate claim dollars.
*Alternative:* run the same comparison on **paid or closed claim counts**, or on claim dollars, to see whether the count signal is corroborated.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to state a limitation of the method used in part a, propose an alternative methodology that corrects it, and briefly describe the limitation and/or how the alternative addresses it.

Common mistakes included offering a limitation that was really just an inaccurate calculation of the percent reported or of the ultimate; not recognizing that the data are claim **counts** rather than claim dollars and proposing to limit dollar amounts; and suggesting industry data as the answer to highly leveraged data, which would remain highly leveraged at an early maturity no matter how much of it there is.
