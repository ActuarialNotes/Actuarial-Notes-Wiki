An insurance company writes three lines: Auto Liability (AL), Auto Physical Damage (PD) and Workers' Compensation (WC).

- The total required capital is \$5,000,000.
- Capital is allocated based on a Co-CTE risk measure.
- The expense ratio is 27% for each line of business.
- The interest rate for discounting is 2.5%.
- The cost of capital is 15%.
- Expenses are paid at the beginning of the year and losses are paid at the end of each year.

| Line | Premium | Undiscounted Loss Ratio | Loss Reserve Duration | 99.5% Co-CTE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AL | 5,000,000 | 70.0% | 2.5 | 2,275,000 |
| PD | 2,000,000 | 62.5% | 0.8 | 1,625,000 |
| WC | 8,000,000 | 77.5% | 3.5 | 2,600,000 |
| Total | 15,000,000 | 73.0% | 2.8 | 6,500,000 |

Determine whether the Workers' Compensation line of business adds value to the company on a risk-adjusted basis.

### Explanation
Discounted loss ratio as LossRatio / (1+Interest)^(duration)

- WC: $0.775/(1.025)^{3.5} = 0.7108$

Net profit as (1 – DiscountedLossRatio – ExpenseRatio) \* Premium

- WC: (1-0.7108-0.27) \* 8,000,000 = 153,238

Capital allocated as (Co-CTE / Total Co-CTE) \* CapitalRequired

- WC: (2,600/6,500) \* 5,000,000 = 2,000,000

RAROC as (NetProfit/ CapitalAllocated)

- WC: 153,238/2,000,000 = 7.7%

Alternate solution is to calculate the cost of capital in dollars ,

- WC: 2,000,000\*(0.15) = 300,000
- EVA = 153,238 – 300,000 =  - 146,762

Conclusion

- 7.7% < 15%
- WC: Does not add value because RAROC is lower than cost of capital

Alternate,

- EVA < 0  ( -146,762 <0)
- WC: Does not add value because EVA is lower than cost of capital

### Examiner Report
To receive full credit, the candidate was expected to know how to calculate RAROC at the correct point in time and make the appropriate comparison and conclusion regarding whether the line adds value.

Generally, the candidates scored favorably. Common errors included:

- bringing all values to t = 1 when it should be t = 0
- not explicitly stating the comparison that brings them to the conclusion of adding value
- inclusion of investment income in profit calculation
- not calculating the allocating capital at all or using duration to allocate the capital

Less frequent errors included inappropriate discounting of losses and contemplating duration in the cost of capital
