In a vacation timeshare marketing business, the value of each timeshare point is modeled by a random variable, $X$, which follows a gamma distribution with mean 6 and variance 18.

Calculate the probability that the value of a timeshare point exceeds 4.

- A) $0.54$
- B) $0.56$
- C) $0.58$
- D) $0.60$
- E) $0.62$

## Explanation

A gamma distribution with shape $\alpha$ and scale $\theta$ has mean $\alpha\theta$ and variance $\alpha\theta^2$. Solve for the parameters:

$$
\begin{align*}
\alpha\theta   &= 6 \\
\alpha\theta^2 &= 18
\end{align*}
$$

Dividing the second by the first gives $\theta = 3$, and then $\alpha = 2$.

With $\alpha = 2$ the density is $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{3^2\Gamma(2)}xe^{-x/3}$, and integration by parts gives the tail:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 4] &= \int_4^\infty \frac{1}{9}xe^{-x/3}\,dx \\
         &= -\frac{1}{3}xe^{-x/3}\bigg|_4^\infty + \frac{1}{3}\int_4^\infty e^{-x/3}\,dx \\
         &= \frac{4}{3}e^{-4/3} + e^{-4/3} \\
         &= \frac{7}{3}e^{-4/3} \\
         &= \mathbf{0.6151}
\end{align*}
$$

The integer shape parameter is what makes this tractable by hand: for $\alpha = 2$ the gamma tail has the closed form $e^{-x/\theta}\left(1 + x/\theta\right)$, which at $x/\theta = 4/3$ gives $e^{-4/3}(1 + 4/3) = \tfrac{7}{3}e^{-4/3}$. Treating the value as exponential with mean 6 instead would give $e^{-4/6} = 0.51$, outside the answer range — the variance of 18 is well below the $6^2 = 36$ an exponential would require, so the distribution is genuinely gamma with $\alpha > 1$.
