For its group life policies, an insurer models the number of claims per group as independent Poisson random variables with common mean 16.

The insurer randomly selects 64 of its groups.

Calculate the probability that the average number of claims per group is between 15 and 18.

- A) $0.29$
- B) $0.38$
- C) $0.95$
- D) $0.98$
- E) $1.00$

## Explanation

A Poisson distribution has variance equal to its mean, so each group has variance 16 and standard deviation 4.

By the Central Limit Theorem, the **sample mean** of 64 groups is approximately normal with the same mean 16, but with standard deviation reduced by $\sqrt{n}$:

$$
\text{SD}(\bar{X}) = \frac{4}{\sqrt{64}} = \frac{4}{8} = 0.5
$$

Standardize both endpoints:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[15 < \bar{X} < 18] &= P\left[\frac{15-16}{0.5} < Z < \frac{18-16}{0.5}\right] \\
                     &= P[-2 < Z < 4] \\
                     &= \mathbf{0.9772}
\end{align*}
$$

The upper limit is four standard deviations out and contributes essentially nothing, so the answer is set almost entirely by the lower tail.

No continuity correction is needed here: the *average* of 64 counts moves in steps of $1/64$, so the adjustment would be $1/128$ rather than the $1/2$ used for a single count — far too small to matter. Choice (C), 0.95, is roughly what a two-sided $\pm 2$ interval would give, missing that the interval is lopsided.
