An insurer decides not to renew any homeowners policy on a house built before 1960, whatever the house's condition, wiring or loss history. Under a state law based on the NAIC Unfair Trade Practices Act, this practice is

- A) permitted, because insurers may choose which risks to renew
- B) unfair discrimination based solely on the age of the residential property
- C) a prohibited rebate, because it changes the terms of the policy
- D) permitted, so long as the insurer gives the notice the policy requires

## Explanation

The Act prohibits refusing to insure, refusing to renew, canceling or limiting coverage on a residential property risk **solely because of the age of the residential property**, between risks of the same class and essentially the same hazard. Refusing every pre-1960 house regardless of its condition is exactly that.

- **A** overstates underwriting freedom. An insurer may select risks on their hazards, but not on a ground the Act forbids.
- **C** misnames the practice. A rebate is an inducement to buy, such as returning part of the premium.
- **D** confuses a procedural requirement with the substantive prohibition: proper notice does not make an unfairly discriminatory non-renewal lawful.
