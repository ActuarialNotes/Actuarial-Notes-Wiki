Given the following reinsurance company data (\$000) evaluated as of December 31, 2013:

| Calendar/Accident Year | Earned Risk Pure Premium | Adjusted Premium | Aggregate Reported Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 5,000 | 5,000 | 3,000 |
| 2011 | 4,500 | 5,500 | 2,500 |
| 2012 | 5,000 | 6,000 | 2,500 |
| 2013 | 6,500 | 6,500 | 1,500 |
| Total | 20,000 | 23,000 | 9,500 |

| Age | Reported Loss Development Factors to Ultimate |
|---|---|
| 12 months | 2.50 |
| 24 months | 2.00 |
| 36 months | 1.60 |
| 48 months | 1.25 |
| 60 months | 1.00 |

- Adjusted premium incorporates changes in primary rates, underwriting, and exposure affecting the loss potential.

Calculate the Stanard-Bühlmann (Cape Cod) ultimate loss ratio for 2011.

### Explanation
**Step 1 — report lags and used-up premium.** The percent reported is $1/\text{CLDF}$ at each year's age as of December 31, 2013; used-up premium is adjusted premium times percent reported.

| AY | Adjusted Premium | CLDF | % Reported | Used-Up Premium | Reported Loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 5,000 | 1.25 | 80.0% | 4,000.0 | 3,000 |
| 2011 | 5,500 | 1.60 | 62.5% | 3,437.5 | 2,500 |
| 2012 | 6,000 | 2.00 | 50.0% | 3,000.0 | 2,500 |
| 2013 | 6,500 | 2.50 | 40.0% | 2,600.0 | 1,500 |
| Total | 23,000 | | | 13,037.5 | 9,500 |

**Step 2 — Cape Cod ELR.**

$$\text{ELR} = \frac{\text{Total reported loss}}{\text{Total used-up premium}} = \frac{9{,}500}{13{,}037.5} = 72.9\%$$

**Step 3 — 2011 IBNR**, applying the ELR to the adjusted premium:

$$\text{IBNR}_{2011} = \text{ELR} \times \text{Adjusted Premium} \times (1 - \text{\% Reported}) = 0.729 \times 5{,}500 \times 0.375 = 1{,}504$$

**Step 4 — 2011 ultimate loss ratio**, on the earned risk pure premium:

$$\text{LR}_{2011} = \frac{\text{Reported Loss}_{2011} + \text{IBNR}_{2011}}{\text{Earned Risk Pure Premium}_{2011}} = \frac{2{,}500 + 1{,}504}{4{,}500} = 89.0\%$$

### Examiner Report
The average score for this question was quite high.  Approximately 77% of the candidates received full or nearly full credit.
Common mistakes:

1. Using the wrong premium base when calculating the ultimate loss ratios.  Many candidates used the Adjusted Premium instead of the Earned Risk Pure Premium.
2. Candidates calculated the expected loss ratio correctly but then used an expected loss ratio method instead of the Bornhuetter-Ferguson approach (Note: This produces a very similar ultimate loss ratio as the correct answer but it is just by coincidence.  ELR × Adjusted Premium = 72.9% × 5,500 = 4,007.7 which translates into an 89.1% ultimate loss ratio [4,007.7 / Earned Risk Pure Premium].)
3. Candidates calculated an all accident year loss ratio instead of just accident year 2011.
