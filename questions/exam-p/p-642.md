The lifetime of a car windshield is exponentially distributed with mean 9 years.

Calculate the variance of the lifetime of a car windshield, given that it has lasted at least five years.

- A) $14$
- B) $16$
- C) $81$
- D) $106$
- E) $196$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the lifetime. Adding a constant shifts a distribution without changing its spread, so

$$
\text{Var}[X \mid X \ge 5] = \text{Var}[X - 5 \mid X \ge 5]
$$

The exponential is memoryless: given survival past 5, the *remaining* lifetime $X - 5$ has the same exponential distribution as $X$ itself. Therefore

$$
\text{Var}[X \mid X \ge 5] = \text{Var}[X] = 9^2 = \mathbf{81}
$$

Choice (E), 196, adds the five years already survived to the mean and squares the result: $(9 + 5)^2 = 196$. Choice (A), 14, is that shifted mean $9 + 5$ itself. The conditional *mean* does move to 14 — a windshield that has lasted five years is expected to last 14 in total — but the conditional variance does not move at all, because a shift changes location, not spread.
