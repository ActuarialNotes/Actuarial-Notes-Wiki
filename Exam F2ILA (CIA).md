# Exam F2ILA
**F2ILA – Product Design and Pricing** is an exam of the individual life and annuities track of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Fellowship (FCIA) pathway.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The aim of this exam is for candidates to develop the following skills:
>
> 1. Understand the main principles and techniques of pricing that are relevant to life insurance, including techniques to estimate the key components of a technical price.
>
> 2. Apply these principles and techniques within the context of life insurance.
>
> 3. Understand how pricing links to wider business processes (e.g., business planning, estimating reserves, and capital setting).
>
> 4. Create and evaluate hypothetical scenarios, including using judgment to assess the implications of possible actions and to develop appropriate proposals or recommendations relating to pricing life insurance products.
>
> Exams are administered as an open-book, four-hour exam requiring analysis in the context of a problem and submission of written responses to specific questions. Candidates will complete the exam through the CIA learning management system. The ranges of weights (in parentheses) attached to the examination topics further below apply to most of the administered exams. Candidates should also recognize that questions often cover multiple learning objectives, including communication.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Principal terms {0%}
>
> 1. Define the [[Principal|principal]] terms used in [[Pricing Life Insurance Product|pricing life insurance products]]. (A1)

> [!example]- Data {10%}
>
> 1. Differentiate the types of data and information required for pricing, their sources, and main uses. ([[A4]])
> 2. Apply the concepts of [[Homogeneity|homogeneity]] and [[Credibility|credibility]] in selecting data for pricing analyses. (B3)
> 3. Analyze the implications to data requirements of different characteristics of insurance. ([[B4]])
> 4. Evaluate the influence of the [[Insurer's Internal Environment|insurer’s internal environment]] (such as changes in operations or IT) on data used for pricing analyses. (B5)
> 5. Evaluate the influence of [[External Environment|external environments]] on data used for pricing analyses. (B5)
> 6. Understand the possible causes of [[Data Error|data errors]] and the effects of [[Inadequate Data|inadequate data]] and information. (B2)
> 7. Create appropriate [[Data-Validation Process|data-validation processes]]. (C6)

> [!example]- Drivers of product design {25%}
>
> 1. Describe and explain the [[Insurance Market Stakeholder|insurance market stakeholders]], how they interact, and their needs: (B2)
>    - a. Customers;
>    - b. Distributors;
>    - c. [[Internal Stakeholder|Internal stakeholders]] ([[Claim|claims]], [[Underwriting|underwriting]], marketing, admin/operations, etc.)
> 2. Describe, explain, and evaluate drivers of product design (B5):
>    - a. Company strengths and weaknesses;
>    - b. [[Economic Condition|Economic conditions]];
>    - c. [[Marketplace Demographics|Marketplace demographics]];
>    - d. Consumer behaviour;
>    - e. [[Distribution Channel|Distribution channel]] behaviour;
>    - f. Market evolution, such as availability of [[Market Data|market data]] and advancement of technology;
>    - g. [[Market Shock|Market shocks]] (i.e., COVID-19 and the loss of [[Fluid-Tested Underwriting|fluid-tested underwriting]]);
>    - h. Competition;
>    - i. Regulatory requirements.

> [!example]- Design and purpose of various product types, benefits, and features {25%}
>
> 1. Describe, in detail, product types, benefits, and features of life and [[Annuity Contract|annuity products]]. (B2)
> 2. Construct and recommend [[Designs Consistent|designs consistent]] with the [[Market Need|market needs]] and [[Constraint|constraints]] identified in the [[Idea-Generation Step|idea-generation step]]. (C6)
> 3. Evaluate feasibility of designs with respect to: (D5)
>    - a. Profitability;
>    - b. [[Product Risk Profile|Product risk profile]];
>    - c. Systems implementation;
>    - d. Marketing and competition;
>    - e. Operations.
> 4. Describe and apply the principles of [[Macro Pricing|macro pricing]] (project-based analysis vs. [[Unit-Based Pricing|unit-based pricing]]) (C3)
> 5. Describe and evaluate how diversification of [[Insurance Risk|insurance risks]] can reduce [[Volatility|volatility]]. (C5)

> [!example]- Relationship between the product features, risks, assumptions, and modelling {25%}
>
> 1. Identify and evaluate the setting of appropriate assumptions for product characteristics, like: (B5)
>    - a. Riders.
>    - b. [[Policyholder Dividend|Policyholder dividends]].
>    - c. [[Equity-linked Account-fund Value|Equity-linked account/fund values]].
>    - d. [[Embedded Option|Embedded options]].
>    - e. Return of [[Insurance Premium|premium]] (e.g., on death, [[Lapse|lapse]], maturity).
>    - f. [[Secondary Guarantee|Secondary guarantees]].
>    - g. [[Premium Payment Pattern|Premium payment patterns]].
>    - h. [[Payout Annuity Benefit|Payout annuity benefits]].
>    - i. [[Crediting Methodology|Crediting methodology]].
> 2. Identify and evaluate the setting of appropriate assumptions for [[Risk|risks]] and other factors, like (B5):
>    - a. [[Mortality]], morbidity, [[Lapse|lapse]].
>    - b. [[Company Experience|Company experience]] and [[Industry Data|industry data]].
>    - c. The marketplace.
>    - d. [[Underwriting]].
>    - e. [[Distribution Channel|Distribution channel]] characteristics.
>    - f. [[Business Mix|Business mix]].
>    - g. [[Reinsurance]].
>    - h. [[Expenses]] (fixed, variable, marginal).
>    - i. Taxes (income and [[Insurance Premium|premium]]); and
>    - j. [[Investment Strategy|Investment strategy]].
> 3. Analyze results and recommend actions with respect to [[Risk|risk]] and profit measures, like: (D5)
>    - a. [[IFRS 17]];
>    - b. [[Rate of Return|Rate-of-return]] measures:
>       - i. ROE, IRR, ROI, ROA;
>       - ii. [[Risk-adjusted Version|Risk-adjusted versions]] of i. above;
>    - c. [[Market-consistent Pricing|Market-consistent pricing]]; and
>    - d. Embedded value.
> 4. Analyze the [[Regulatory Capital|capital requirements]] for a product and solutions such as [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] and [[Securitization|securitization]]. (C4)
> 5. Describe the use of [[Generalized Linear|generalized linear]], stochastic, and [[Multi-State Model|multi-state models]], the advantages and disadvantages of each, and the methods for their construction (C2).
> 6. Interpret and analyze model results described in 5. ([[B4]])
> 7. Describe and apply methods for [[Modelling Guarantee|modelling guarantees]] linked to [[Investment Performance|investment performance]] and their [[Hedging|hedges]] (GMMB, GMDB, GMAB) (C2)
> 8. Draw insights from [[Financial Data|financial data]] to validate past decisions and support [[Future Proposal|future proposals]]. (C3)
> 9. Understand source-of-earnings analysis and its use in [[Decision-Making|decision-making]] with regards to [[Product Development|product development]] and pricing (C5).
> 10. Understand, explain, and use results for purposes of [[Decision-Making|decision-making]]. (C3)
> 11. Understand and describe Part 2700 of the [[CIA Standard|CIA Standards]] of Practice – [[Policyholder Dividend Determination]]. (C2)

> [!example]- Actuarial requirements of product implementation and monitoring {15%}
>
> 1. Describe and evaluate compliance with [[Illustration Regulation|illustration regulations]] and other [[Policy Form Regulation|policy-form regulations]] (C5).
> 2. Evaluate variation in the [[Actual Experience|actual experience]] from expected in assumptions – including (but not limited to) [[Business Mix|business mix]], [[Mortality|mortality]], [[Investment Return|investment returns]], [[Expenses|expenses]], and [[Policyholder|policyholder]] behavior such as policy and [[Premium Persistency|premium persistency]] – through the use of [[Experience Study|experience studies]]. (C5)
> 3. Evaluate and explain the impact of the variation in the [[Actual Experience|actual experience]] from expected to other [[Actuary|actuaries]], [[Insurance Professional|insurance professionals]], and a [[Non-Technical Audience|non-technical audience]]. (D5)
> 4. Recommend changes to non-guaranteed elements for deviations from expected. ([[C6]])
