A small manufacturing company, consisting of five senior employees and ten junior employees, randomly selects four employees to attend a professional conference.

Calculate the probability that at least three senior employees are chosen.

- A) $0.060$
- B) $0.073$
- C) $0.077$
- D) $0.099$
- E) $0.111$

## Explanation

Four employees are drawn without replacement from 15, of whom 5 are senior, so the number of seniors chosen, $X$, is hypergeometric.

"At least three" out of four means exactly 3 or exactly 4, and those two cases are disjoint:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \geq 3] &= \frac{\binom{5}{3}\binom{10}{1} + \binom{5}{4}\binom{10}{0}}{\binom{15}{4}} \\
            &= \frac{(10)(10) + (5)(1)}{1365} \\
            &= \frac{105}{1365} \\
            &= \mathbf{0.07692}
\end{align*}
$$

Each numerator term pairs a choice of seniors with a choice of juniors filling the remaining seats, and the denominator counts all committees of four. Taking only the $X = 3$ term gives $100/1365 = 0.0733$, which is choice (B) — a reminder that "at least" here still has a second case, even though four seniors is the smaller of the two.
