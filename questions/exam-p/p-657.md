30% of the participants in this year's charity drive will make a cash donation and 60% will donate items to the charity auction and it is possible that some will do both.

Determine the value or range of values of the probability $p$ that an individual randomly chosen from among this year's participants will donate in neither of the two indicated ways.

- A) $0 \le p \le 0.1$
- B) $p = 0.1$
- C) $0.1 \le p \le 0.4$
- D) $p = 0.3$
- E) $0.7 \le p \le 1.0$

## Explanation

Let $c$ be the event of a cash donation and $i$ the event of donating items, with $P(c) = 0.3$ and $P(i) = 0.6$. The overlap is not given, so the answer is a range rather than a number, and the two extremes bound it.

If $c$ and $i$ are **mutually exclusive**, the union is as large as possible:

$$
p = 1 - 0.3 - 0.6 = 0.1
$$

If $c$ is entirely **contained in** $i$ — every cash donor also donates items — the union is as small as possible, just $i$ itself:

$$
p = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4
$$

Every intermediate overlap gives an intermediate value, so

$$
\mathbf{0.1 \le p \le 0.4}
$$

Choice (B), $p = 0.1$, is the mutually-exclusive endpoint reported as though it were the whole answer. The problem explicitly says "it is possible that some will do both", which is precisely what makes the disjoint case only one end of a range. Note that containment is possible here only because $P(c) < P(i)$; the overlap can be anything from 0 up to 0.3.
