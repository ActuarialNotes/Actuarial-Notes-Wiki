Losses due to automobile accidents have a probability density function given by

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{3}{1000}\left(\dfrac{1000}{x}\right)^4, & \text{for } x > 1000 \\[6pt]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

An automobile policy has a deductible of 1100.

Calculate the expected value of the policy payout for a random loss.

- A) $140$
- B) $400$
- C) $413$
- D) $826$
- E) $1240$

## Explanation

The payout is $\max(X - 1100, 0)$, and since the density starts at 1000 the integral runs from the deductible upward:

$$
E[\text{payout}] = \int_{1100}^{\infty} (x - 1100)\,\frac{3}{1000}\left(\frac{1000}{x}\right)^4 dx
$$

Split the integrand into two power terms:

$$
= \int_{1100}^{\infty} 3(1000)^3 x^{-3}\,dx - \int_{1100}^{\infty} 3.3(1000)^4 x^{-4}\,dx
$$

Each integrates directly:

$$
\begin{align*}
&= \left.\frac{3(1000)^3 x^{-2}}{-2}\right|_{1100}^{\infty} - \left.\frac{3.3(1000)^4 x^{-3}}{-3}\right|_{1100}^{\infty} \\
&= \frac{3(1000)^3}{2(1100)^2} - \frac{3.3(1000)^4}{3(1100)^3} \\
&= 1239.67 - 826.45 \\
&= \mathbf{413.22}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 400, is $E[X] - 1100$. The mean of this density is $\int_{1000}^{\infty} x f(x)\,dx = 1500$, so subtracting the deductible from it gives 400 — but that treats every loss as reduced by 1100, when losses between 1000 and 1100 pay nothing at all. Choice (E), 1240, is the first of the two integrals above, dropped before the second is subtracted.
