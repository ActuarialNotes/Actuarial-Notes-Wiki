A representative of a market research firm contacts consumers by phone to conduct surveys. The specific consumer contacted by each phone call is randomly determined. The probability that a phone call produces a completed survey is 0.25.

Calculate the probability that the eighth phone call produces the third completed survey.

- A) $0.03$
- B) $0.08$
- C) $0.13$
- D) $0.21$
- E) $0.31$

## Explanation

Split the requirement into two independent parts: what the first seven calls must do, and what the eighth must do.

**The first seven calls must yield exactly two completed surveys.** That is a binomial probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
\binom{7}{2}(0.25)^2(0.75)^5 &= 21(0.0625)(0.23730) \\
                             &= 0.31146
\end{align*}
$$

**The eighth call must be a completed survey**, which is fixed by the stopping condition and has probability 0.25.

Multiplying:

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= 0.31146(0.25) \\
  &= \mathbf{0.0779}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 0.31, is the first factor alone — the probability of two successes in seven calls, without requiring the eighth to succeed. The binomial coefficient must count only the first seven trials; using $\binom{8}{3}$ instead would allow the third success to fall anywhere among the eight, which is a different event.
