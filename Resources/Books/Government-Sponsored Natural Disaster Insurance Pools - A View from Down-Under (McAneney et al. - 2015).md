A review article on the provision of catastrophe insurance by the public sector in the US, France, New Zealand, Spain and the United Kingdom, and its absence in the Netherlands, where flood risk is viewed as a national security concern. It sets them in the context of the Australian home insurance market, where insurers increasingly use risk-reflective, multi-peril premiums, and weighs government pools' ability to raise funds after an event against the financial unsustainability that political intervention to keep cover affordable in high-risk areas brings. Published in the International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction, volume 15.
