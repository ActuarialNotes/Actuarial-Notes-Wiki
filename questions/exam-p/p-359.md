Every member of a certain committee is either an X or a Y.

Thirty percent of the Xs on the committee are male. Forty percent of the Ys on the committee are female. Sixty percent of the committee members are Ys. A randomly selected member of the committee is male.

Calculate the probability that he is a Y.

- A) $0.36$
- B) $0.48$
- C) $0.60$
- D) $0.67$
- E) $0.75$

## Explanation

Read the three percentages carefully — two are conditional on group, one is not, and the Y statement is given for *females*:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[M \mid X] &= 0.3 \\
P[F \mid Y] &= 0.4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad P[M \mid Y] = 0.6 \\
P[Y]        &= 0.6 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad P[X] = 0.4
\end{align*}
$$

The question asks to reverse the conditioning, from "male given Y" to "Y given male", which is Bayes' theorem. The denominator is the total probability of drawing a male, split over the two groups:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[Y \mid M] &= \frac{P[M \mid Y]P[Y]}{P[M \mid Y]P[Y] + P[M \mid X]P[X]} \\
            &= \frac{(0.6)(0.6)}{(0.6)(0.6) + (0.3)(0.4)} \\
            &= \frac{0.36}{0.36 + 0.12} \\
            &= \frac{0.36}{0.48} \\
            &= \mathbf{0.75}
\end{align*}
$$

Choices (A) and (B) are the numerator and denominator of that last fraction left unreduced — both are quantities the calculation passes through. Choice (C) is the prior $P[Y] = 0.6$, the answer you would give by ignoring the evidence that the member is male; because Ys are more likely to be male than Xs are, the evidence should pull the probability above the prior.
