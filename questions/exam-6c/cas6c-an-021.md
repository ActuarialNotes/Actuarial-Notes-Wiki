OSFI's Guideline B-15, *Climate Risk Management*, groups climate-related risks into physical risks (acute and chronic) and transition risks, and notes that both can also lead to liability risks. A federally regulated P&C insurer is reviewing its exposures.

Match each exposure to the category of climate-related risk that best describes it. An option may be used more than once or not at all.

## Part a (1 point)
New federal emissions regulation lowers the market value of the insurer's bond holdings in carbon-intensive issuers.

- A) Acute physical risk
- B) Chronic physical risk
- C) Transition risk
- D) Liability risk

### Answer
C

### Explanation
B-15 defines transition risks as the financial risks of adjusting toward a low-greenhouse-gas economy, which "can emerge from current or future government policies, legislation, and regulation to limit GHG emissions." A regulation that reprices carbon-intensive assets is the textbook case. **A** and **B** are about the physical climate, and nothing here was damaged by weather. **D** requires a claim or legal action, and none is described.

## Part b (1 point)
An unusually severe wildfire season produces a spike in homeowners claims.

- A) Acute physical risk
- B) Chronic physical risk
- C) Transition risk
- D) Liability risk

### Answer
A

### Explanation
B-15 defines acute physical risks as those from "the increasing severity and frequency of climate-related extremes and events". A wildfire season is an event. **B** is the gradual, long-term version, and **C** concerns the economy's adjustment, not the weather itself.

## Part c (1 point)
Corporate policyholders face lawsuits alleging they failed to manage their climate-related risks, and they claim under their liability policies.

- A) Acute physical risk
- B) Chronic physical risk
- C) Transition risk
- D) Liability risk

### Answer
D

### Explanation
B-15 notes that physical and transition risks "can also lead to liability risks, such as the risk of climate-related claims under liability policies, as well as litigation and direct actions against financial institutions for failing to manage their climate-related risks." The loss reaches the insurer through its liability coverage, so this is liability risk rather than **C**, the underlying policy shift.

## Part d (1 point)
Over decades, rising sea levels gradually raise expected flood losses along the coast where much of its property book is located.

- A) Acute physical risk
- B) Chronic physical risk
- C) Transition risk
- D) Liability risk

### Answer
B

### Explanation
B-15 defines chronic physical risks as those from "longer-term gradual shifts of the climate". A slow rise in sea level is a gradual shift, not a single event, which makes **A** wrong. The distinction matters for the actuary: a chronic trend makes past loss experience a biased guide to future cost even when no single catastrophe occurs.
