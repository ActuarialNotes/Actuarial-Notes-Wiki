A Canadian HR Reporter news article of 12 April 2007 on an Ontario Superior Court of Justice decision that the City of Toronto must pay accidental death and “killed in the line of duty” benefits to the estate of a firefighter who died of cancer. The court overturned an arbitrator’s decision that he was not entitled to them.

## Firefighter who died of cancer was killed in the line of duty, court says
- Bruce Ritchie, a district fire chief with Toronto Fire Services and more than 25 years’ service, died of renal cell cancer in 1998 at the age of 53; in 2004 the Workplace Safety Insurance Board (WSIB) found that his work, which frequently exposed him to hazardous substances, significantly contributed to his cancer
- The city’s insurance policy paid a death benefit only for death by “external, violent and accidental means (independently of any physical illness or infirmity)”; an arbitrator ruled that Ritchie died of an illness, so his estate was not entitled to accidental death benefits or the “killed in the line of duty” benefits in the collective agreement between the firefighters’ union and the City of Toronto
- The Ontario Superior Court of Justice overturned the arbitrator’s decision, finding that the arbitrator had not adequately considered the WSIB’s determination that the cancer was at least partly caused by exposures to toxic substances over his career
- The court ruled that the cancer was caused by unexpected events causing exposure to toxins while performing his duties, considered his death accidental, held that he was killed in the line of duty, and awarded his estate the benefits provided in the collective agreement
- The article cites the decision as Toronto Professional Firefighters’ Assn. v. Toronto (City), 2007 CarswellOnt 1900 (Ont. S.C.J.)
