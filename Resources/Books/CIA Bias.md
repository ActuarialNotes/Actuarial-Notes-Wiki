The CIA's April 2023 practice resource document on **[[Bias in Actuarial Practice|bias]] and fairness** in P&C pricing and underwriting. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective A1; the syllabus assigns **Sections 1, 2 and 3 only**.

## Contents

- **Executive summary**
- **Section 1 — Intent, scope and cross-references.** The document is guidance, not a [[Standards of Practice|standard of practice]]; it applies to pricing and underwriting work, and cross-references the CIA's Rules of Professional Conduct and the [[Standards of Practice|Consolidated Standards of Practice]].
- **Section 2 — Historical issues and current evolution.** How [[Risk Classification Restrictions|restrictions on rating variables]] arose, and why proxy discrimination through correlated variables (postal code, [[Credit|credit information]], occupation) is the live problem rather than direct use of a prohibited variable.
- **Section 3 — Definitions.** The section examiners lean on:
  - **Bias** — a systematic deviation of an estimate from the quantity being estimated. Statistical bias, data bias and societal bias are different things and the word is used for all three.
  - **Direct and indirect discrimination** — direct: the model uses a protected characteristic. Indirect: it uses a variable correlated with one, producing the same effect.
  - **Fairness** — has no single definition; the document sets out competing formalisations (group fairness, individual fairness, actuarial fairness) that cannot all be satisfied at once.
  - **Contrasting bias and fairness** — an unbiased model can be unfair, and a fair model can be biased. Keeping the two apart is the point of the section.
  - **Ethics** — the frame in which the trade-off gets resolved, since neither statistics nor law settles it.
- Sections 4–7 (analysis and measurement, next steps for the practitioner, further research, an illustrative example) and the appendices are **outside the assigned scope**.

## Why it is on the syllabus

[[Unfair Discrimination|Unfair discrimination]] is a regulatory constraint on Canadian ratemaking, not just an ethical one: [[Ontario Reg. 664]] prohibits specified rating variables outright, and a filing that reaches a prohibited variable indirectly can be rejected even when the variable is predictive. This document supplies the vocabulary — direct versus indirect discrimination, proxy variables, the several fairness definitions — that a written answer needs.
