A food distributor reviews its commercial liability loss exposures: (1) customers who visit its warehouse showroom; (2) its delivery trucks on public roads; (3) injuries to its warehouse employees; (4) illness caused by contaminated food it has already sold and delivered. Which of these does a standard CGL policy treat, with the others sent to other policies?

- A) (1) and (4)
- B) (1), (2) and (4)
- C) (1) only
- D) (1), (3) and (4)

## Explanation

The CGL covers a business's premises and operations exposure and its products and completed operations exposure. Showroom visitors are premises and operations; food already sold and delivered is products. Auto liability goes to a business auto policy (the CGL's auto exclusion), and injury to employees goes to workers compensation and employers liability (the CGL's employer's liability exclusion). The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** keeps the trucks in the CGL; injury from an auto owned by the insured is excluded and belongs to business auto.
- **C** misses that the products-completed operations hazard is part of the CGL.
- **D** keeps employee injuries in the CGL; they belong to workers compensation and employers liability.
