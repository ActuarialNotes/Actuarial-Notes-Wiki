Given the following ground-up information for insurance claims subject to a single excess-of-loss reinsurance treaty as of December 31, 2014:

| Claim Number | Occurrence Number | Date of Loss | Reported ALAE | Reported Loss | Total Reported Loss and ALAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | Feb 29, 2013 | \$12,000 | \$80,000 | \$92,000 |
| 2 | 2 | May 28, 2013 | 18,000 | 145,000 | 163,000 |
| 3 | 3 | Sep 5, 2013 | 8,000 | 70,000 | 78,000 |
| 4 | 3 | Sep 5, 2013 | 3,250 | 30,000 | 33,250 |
| 5 | 4 | Jan 24, 2014 | 9,000 | 90,000 | 99,000 |
| 6 | 5 | Mar 7, 2014 | 20,000 | 225,000 | 245,000 |
| 7 | 6 | Oct 18, 2014 | 3,750 | 55,000 | 58,750 |

| Accident Year | Historical Per-Occurrence Retention |
|---|---|
| Jan 1, 2013 – Dec 31, 2013 | \$150,000 |
| Jan 1, 2014 – Dec 31, 2014 | 200,000 |

- The historical pre-occurrence retentions apply to combined loss and ALAE.
- The reinsurance treaty stipulates that the reinsurer must be notified of any occurrences whose reported loss and ALAE reaches 50% of the retention.

Demonstrate how the data above indicates the presence of two aspects of reinsurance loss reserving that make it somewhat more difficult than primary loss reserving.

### Explanation
Two of the following three:

**1. Claim report lags to reinsurers are generally longer.** The reinsurer is notified only when an occurrence's loss and ALAE reaches 50% of the retention, so it would not yet know about claims 5 and 7. Claim 5 is likely to breach the 50% threshold, and the reinsurer would probably learn of it a year later — long after the primary insurer.

**2. Persistent upward development of most claim reserves (often from a tendency to underestimate ALAE).**

| Claim | ALAE / Loss |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.15 |
| 2 | 0.1241 |
| 3 | 0.1143 |
| 4 | 0.1083 |
| 5 | 0.1 |
| 6 | 0.0889 |
| 7 | 0.0682 |

The ratio decreases from the oldest claims to the newest: ALAE tends to be under-reserved early and develops upward, so the AY 2014 claims, with lower ALAE-to-loss ratios than AY 2013, may be understated.

**3. Heterogeneity of patterns.** The retention changed from \$150,000 in 2013 to \$200,000 in 2014, so each accident year's development pattern differs and losses before 2014 cannot be compared directly with those after — made harder still with only two accident years.
