Given the following automobile policies issued during calendar years 2013 through 2015:

| Effective Date | Expiration Date | Number of Policies |
|---|---|---|
| April 1, 2013 | September 30, 2013 | 100 |
| October 1, 2013 | March 31, 2014 | 110 |
| April 1, 2014 | September 30, 2014 | 105 |
| October 1, 2014 | March 31, 2015 | 100 |
| April 1, 2015 | September 30, 2015 | 110 |
| October 1, 2015 | March 31, 2016 | 105 |

- All policies have a 6-month term.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the written car-years for calendar year 2014.

### Answer
102.5 car-years

### Explanation
A car-year is one car insured for **one year**. Every policy here runs only six months, so each policy contributes $0.5$ car-years, not 1.

Written exposure is booked in the calendar year the policy is **written**, in full, at the moment of writing. Two of the six cohorts are written during 2014:

| Effective Date | Policies | Car-years per policy | Written car-years in CY 2014 |
|---|---|---|---|
| April 1, 2014 | 105 | 0.5 | $105 \times 0.5 = 52.5$ |
| October 1, 2014 | 100 | 0.5 | $100 \times 0.5 = 50.0$ |

The October 1, 2013 cohort is written in 2013 even though it is still running in 2014, and the April 1, 2015 cohort is written in 2015 — neither contributes.

$$\text{CY 2014 written car-years} = 0.5 \times (105 + 100) = \mathbf{102.5}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to show how written exposures are calculated for 6-month policies: identify the cohorts written inside the calendar year and halve the policy counts because the exposure unit is a car-*year*.

Common errors included not halving the counts for the six-month term, omitting one of the two 2014 cohorts, and computing earned exposures instead of written exposures.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Calculate the in-force car-years as of December 31, 2014.

### Answer
50 car-years

### Explanation
In-force exposure at a point in time is the **full-term** exposure of every policy that is providing coverage on that date. Work through the six cohorts and ask which are live on December 31, 2014:

| Effective Date | Expiration Date | In force on 12/31/2014? | In-force car-years |
|---|---|---|---|
| April 1, 2013 | September 30, 2013 | No (expired) | 0 |
| October 1, 2013 | March 31, 2014 | No (expired) | 0 |
| April 1, 2014 | September 30, 2014 | No (expired) | 0 |
| October 1, 2014 | March 31, 2015 | Yes | $100 \times 0.5 = 50$ |
| April 1, 2015 | September 30, 2015 | No (not yet written) | 0 |
| October 1, 2015 | March 31, 2016 | No (not yet written) | 0 |

Only the October 1, 2014 cohort straddles year-end:

$$\text{In-force car-years at 12/31/2014} = 100 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{50}$$

Note that in-force exposure is *not* prorated for the portion of the term already elapsed — the full 0.5 car-years of each in-force policy counts, even though half of it has already been earned by December 31.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to show how in-force exposures are calculated for 6-month policies, identifying the single cohort still providing coverage on the valuation date and taking its full six-month exposure.

Common errors included not halving the counts for the six-month term, and including cohorts that had already expired or had not yet been written as of December 31, 2014.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Calculate the earned car-years for calendar year 2015.

### Answer
106.25 car-years

### Explanation
Earned exposure accrues **pro rata over the policy term**, so each cohort contributes the fraction of its term that falls inside 2015. Three cohorts overlap calendar year 2015:

| Effective Date | Expiration Date | Policies | Car-years written | Fraction earned in CY 2015 | Earned car-years |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| October 1, 2014 | March 31, 2015 | 100 | $100 \times 0.5 = 50$ | $3/6 = 0.5$ | 25.00 |
| April 1, 2015 | September 30, 2015 | 110 | $110 \times 0.5 = 55$ | $6/6 = 1.0$ | 55.00 |
| October 1, 2015 | March 31, 2016 | 105 | $105 \times 0.5 = 52.5$ | $3/6 = 0.5$ | 26.25 |
| | | | | **Total** | **106.25** |

Compactly,

$$\text{CY 2015 earned car-years} = 0.5 \times \left[ 100 \times 0.5 + 110 + 105 \times 0.5 \right] = 0.5 \times 212.5 = \mathbf{106.25}$$

*Alternatively*, work from written exposure and the change in the unearned exposure reserve:

$$\text{Earned} = \text{Written} + \left( \text{Unearned}_{\text{beg}} - \text{Unearned}_{\text{end}} \right)$$

CY 2015 written car-years $= 0.5(110 + 105) = 107.5$; unearned at 12/31/2014 $= 100 \times 0.5 \times 0.5 = 25$; unearned at 12/31/2015 $= 105 \times 0.5 \times 0.5 = 26.25$. Then

$$\text{Earned} = 107.5 + (25 - 26.25) = 106.25$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to show how earned exposures are calculated for 6-month policies, prorating each overlapping cohort by the share of its term falling in calendar year 2015.

Common errors included applying the parallelogram method, which approximates earning under an assumption of uniform writing throughout the year and is inappropriate when the policies are written on two discrete dates; not halving the counts for the six-month term; and missing the 100 policies effective October 1, 2014, which earn their second quarter in 2015.
