An insurer sells a homeowners insurance policy that has a deductible of 2. Homeowners loss amounts follow an exponential distribution with mean 1.

Calculate the expected claim payment made for a homeowners loss.

- A) $e^{-2}$
- B) $2e^{-2}$
- C) $e^{-1}$
- D) $3e^{-2}$
- E) $1$

## Explanation

With mean 1 the density is $f(x) = e^{-x}$ for $x > 0$, and the payment is $\max(X - 2, 0)$:

$$
E[\text{payment}] = \int_2^{\infty} (x - 2)e^{-x}\,dx
$$

Integrating by parts with $u = x - 2$ and $dv = e^{-x}dx$:

$$
\begin{align*}
&= \left. -(x - 2)e^{-x} \right|_2^{\infty} + \int_2^{\infty} e^{-x}\,dx \\
&= 0 + \left. -e^{-x} \right|_2^{\infty} \\
&= \mathbf{e^{-2}}
\end{align*}
$$

There is a shortcut worth knowing: the exponential is memoryless, so given $X > 2$ the excess $X - 2$ is again exponential with mean 1. The expected payment is therefore $P[X > 2] \times 1 = e^{-2}$ — the same answer with no integration.

Choice (E), 1, is $E(X)$, the expected loss with no deductible applied. Here the deductible sits twice the mean out in the tail, so it removes all but $e^{-2} = 0.135$ of the expected loss — the payment is a small fraction of 1, not 1 itself.
