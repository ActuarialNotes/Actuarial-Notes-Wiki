An insurance group's personal auto company decides to compete by cutting its expenses so that it can charge the lowest rates in the broad personal auto market. At which level is this strategy set, and which strategy is it?

- A) Corporate level, cost leadership
- B) Corporate level, concentration
- C) Business level, cost leadership
- D) Business level, focused differentiation

## Explanation

Business-level strategy answers how a business competes within its industry, while corporate-level strategy answers which industries the organization competes in. The auto company is deciding how to compete in its market, on low cost across a broad market, which is cost leadership. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Cost leadership is a business-level generic strategy; the decision doesn't change which industries the group is in.
- **B** — Concentration is a corporate-level choice to compete within a single industry, not a way of competing within it.
- **D** — Focused differentiation targets a narrow segment on uniqueness; this company targets the broad market on price.
