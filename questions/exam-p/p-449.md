Two random variables $X$ and $Y$ are each defined on a set of positive integers and have joint probability function $p(x,y)$. A portion of the corresponding joint cumulative distribution function $F(x,y)$ is given in the following table:

| $y$ | $x = 3$ | $x = 4$ | $x = 5$ | $x = 6$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 0.53 | 0.62 | 0.67 | 0.75 |
| 9 | 0.65 | 0.76 | 0.84 | 0.93 |

Calculate $p(4,9) + p(5,9)$.

- A) $0.01$
- B) $0.02$
- C) $0.03$
- D) $0.04$
- E) $0.05$

## Explanation

The two point probabilities together cover the rectangle $3 < X \leq 5$, $8 < Y \leq 9$ — that is, $X \in \{4,5\}$ and $Y = 9$, since both variables take integer values.

Recovering a rectangle's probability from a joint cumulative distribution function needs all four corners, with the inclusion–exclusion pattern:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[3 < X \leq 5,\ 8 < Y \leq 9] &= F(5,9) - F(3,9) - F(5,8) + F(3,8)
\end{align*}
$$

Subtracting the two "one side" corners removes the region below and the region to the left, but removes their overlap twice, so $F(3,8)$ is added back.

Reading the four values from the table:

$$
\begin{align*}
p(4,9) + p(5,9) &= 0.84 - 0.65 - 0.67 + 0.53 \\
                &= \mathbf{0.05}
\end{align*}
$$

Taking the difference along one row only — $F(5,9) - F(3,9) = 0.19$ — would give $P[3 < X \leq 5, Y \leq 9]$, which still includes every $y$ up to 8. The second subtraction and the add-back are what strip the calculation down to the single row $y = 9$.
