The standard governing the selection and use of **trending procedures** in property/casualty [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]]. On the [[Exam 5 (CAS)|Exam 5]] syllabus for objectives A4 and A8 (evaluating data, and calculating and applying [[Loss Trend|loss]], [[Premium Trend|premium]], and [[Exposure Trend|exposure]] trends). Revised September 2009.

## Section 1 — Purpose, Scope, and Effective Date

- Applies when an actuary performs professional services involving a trending procedure in P&C ratemaking
- Trending here means adjusting historical data for **changes over time** — it does not cover [[Loss Development|loss development]], which is a separate adjustment for maturity

## Section 2 — Definitions

- **Trending procedure** — the process of adjusting for changes over time in the frequency, severity, exposure, or premium underlying the estimate
- **Experience period** — the historical period whose data is being adjusted
- **Forecast period** — the future period to which the trended estimate applies

## Section 3 — Analysis of Issues and Recommended Practices

### 3.1 Purpose or Use of the Trending Procedure

- The intended use governs the choice of procedure; a trend selected for an overall indication may not be appropriate for a [[Classification Ratemaking|classification]] analysis

### 3.2 Selection of Data

- Consider whether the data is appropriate, available, and sufficiently [[Credibility|credible]]
- **Internal vs. external data** — external indices (economic, industry) may supplement thin internal experience (see [[External Information in Reserving|external information]])
- Consider [[Homogeneity|homogeneity]] of the data being trended

### 3.3 Adjustments to Data

- Adjust for known changes before measuring trend: [[On-Leveling|rate level changes]], [[Mix of Business|shifts in mix]], coverage and [[Policy Provision Changes|policy provision changes]], [[Large Loss|large losses]] and [[Catastrophe Loss|catastrophes]], and changes in [[Deductible|deductibles]] or limits
- Failing to adjust risks attributing a one-time shift to an ongoing trend

### 3.4 Selection of the Trending Procedure

- Consider the appropriateness of **linear vs. exponential** fits, the length of the fit period, and whether separate [[Frequency|frequency]] and [[Severity|severity]] trends should replace a single pure premium trend
- Consider **seasonality** and whether data should be organized on a rolling or calendar basis

### 3.5 Trending Period

- The trend period runs from the **average date of loss of the experience period** to the **average date of loss of the forecast period** — the single most common source of error in a trend calculation
- Two-step trending may be appropriate where recent trend differs from long-term trend

### 3.6 Evaluation of the Trending Procedure

- Test the reasonableness of the result; consider whether the indicated trend is supportable and whether it should be tempered by judgment

### 3.7 Reliance on Data Supplied by Others

- Reliance is permitted, with disclosure

## Section 4 — Communications and Disclosures

- Disclose the trending procedure used, the data relied upon, the trend period, and material assumptions
- Disclose any deviation from the standard
