An investor purchases a geometrically decreasing perpetuity-due that provides annual payments. The first payment is 1, and each subsequent payment is 1% less than the previous payment.

At the time of purchase, the Macaulay duration of the perpetuity-due is 12.00 years.

Calculate the modified duration of the perpetuity-due, in years, at the time of purchase.

- A) 10.97
- B) 11.11
- C) 11.19
- D) 12.81
- E) 12.88

## Explanation

The payments are $1, 0.99, 0.99^{2}, \ldots$ at times $0, 1, 2, \ldots$, so the price of the geometric perpetuity-due, as a function of the annual effective interest rate $i$, is a geometric series with ratio $\dfrac{0.99}{1+i}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(i) &= 1 + \frac{0.99}{1+i} + \left(\frac{0.99}{1+i}\right)^{2} + \left(\frac{0.99}{1+i}\right)^{3} + \cdots \\
     &= \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{0.99}{1+i}} \\
     &= \frac{1+i}{0.01+i} \\
     &= 1 + \frac{0.99}{0.01+i}
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore, the modified duration is

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{MOD} &= -\frac{P'(i)}{P(i)} \\
        &= -\frac{-\dfrac{0.99}{(0.01+i)^{2}}}{\dfrac{1+i}{0.01+i}} \\
        &= \frac{0.99}{(0.01+i)(1+i)}
\end{align*}
$$

Since Macaulay duration is modified duration times $(1+i)$, the Macaulay duration is $\dfrac{0.99}{0.01+i}$. Setting it equal to 12 and solving for $i$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{0.99}{0.01+i} &= 12 \\
0.01+i &= 0.0825 \\
i &= 0.0725
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore, the modified duration is

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{MOD} &= \frac{12}{1+i} \\
        &= \frac{12}{1.0725} \\
        &= 11.18881
\end{align*}
$$
