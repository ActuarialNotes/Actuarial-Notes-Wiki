A motorist currently has no traffic tickets.

The amount of time between now and when the motorist receives the first ticket is exponentially distributed with mean 0.80 years.

The motorist plans to drive more carefully after receiving the first ticket. Hence the mean time from the first ticket to the second is greater than 0.80. The amount of time between the first ticket and the second ticket is exponentially distributed and is independent of when the motorist receives the first ticket.

The variance of the number of years from now until the second ticket is 2.65.

Calculate the expected amount of time, in years, between the motorist's first and second traffic tickets.

- A) $0.83$
- B) $0.96$
- C) $1.42$
- D) $1.85$
- E) $2.01$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the time to the first ticket and $Y$ the time from the first to the second. Both are exponential, with means 0.8 and $\mu$ respectively, and they are independent.

An exponential distribution has variance equal to the square of its mean. Because $X$ and $Y$ are independent, the variances of the *total* time add:

$$
\begin{align*}
2.65 &= \text{Var}(X + Y) \\
     &= \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y) \\
     &= (0.8)^2 + \mu^2
\end{align*}
$$

Solving:

$$
\begin{align*}
\mu^2 &= 2.65 - 0.64 = 2.01 \\
\mu   &= \sqrt{2.01} \\
      &= \mathbf{1.418}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 2.01, is $\mu^2$ reported without the square root. The result satisfies the problem's stipulation that $\mu > 0.80$, which is the check that the right root was taken — and note that the two waiting times are *not* identically distributed, so the total is not a gamma distribution and its variance must be built from the two pieces separately.
