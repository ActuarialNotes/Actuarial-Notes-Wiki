A May 2012 Canadian Institute of Actuaries educational note (document 212031), prepared by the Committee on Post-Employment Benefit Plans, intended to assist actuaries in determining health care trend rate assumptions when providing advice on the financial condition of a post-retirement benefit plan. It starts with a definition of a health care trend rate, discusses its development, considerations and limitations for use in a valuation, and gives more specific information on the types of claims trend rates that apply in health care benefits.

## Memorandum
- The note was approved for distribution by the Practice Council on May 2, 2012, and is not a Standard of Practice; the memorandum points to subsection 1220 of the Standards of Practice on familiarity with educational notes.

## 1 Introduction
- Post-employment benefit plans continue employee group benefits such as life insurance and health care benefits after active employment ceases; plan sponsors must recognize their cost on an accrual basis in their financial statements.
- Unlike the survival assumptions, there is no established practice, data or industry standard for the trend in health care costs used to derive future health care benefit payments.
- For valuation purposes the difficulty lies in determining health care trend rates for long projection periods, which can extend out for 70 years or more.
