Given the following information:

| Risk | True Expected Cost | Company A Insured Risks | Company A Charged Rate | Company B Insured Risks | Company B Charged Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | 170 | 5,000 | 160 | 5,000 | 150 |
| Low | 130 | 5,000 | 140 | 5,000 | 150 |
| Total | 150 | 10,000 | 150 | 10,000 | 150 |

- There are no underwriting expenses or profit provisions.
- Market consists of 10,000 high risk insureds and 10,000 low risk insureds.
- Both companies write only one line of business.
- 20% of all insureds shop at renewal and base their purchasing decision on price.

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the profitability for each company after one renewal cycle.

### Explanation
At renewal 20% of each company's insureds in each risk class shop on price and move to the cheaper company for their class:

- **High risk:** Company A charges 160 and Company B 150, so A's high-risk shoppers move to B: 5,000 × 20% = 1,000.
- **Low risk:** A charges 140 and B 150, so B's low-risk shoppers move to A: 1,000.

| | High risks | Low risks |
|---|---|---|
| Company A | 5,000 - 1,000 = 4,000 | 5,000 + 1,000 = 6,000 |
| Company B | 5,000 + 1,000 = 6,000 | 5,000 - 1,000 = 4,000 |

Profit = risks × (charged rate - true expected cost):

$$\text{Company A: } 4{,}000 \times (160 - 170) + 6{,}000 \times (140 - 130) = -40{,}000 + 60{,}000 = +20{,}000$$

$$\text{Company B: } 6{,}000 \times (150 - 170) + 4{,}000 \times (150 - 130) = -120{,}000 + 80{,}000 = -40{,}000$$

Company A earns a profit of 20,000 (1.4% of its 1,480,000 premium); Company B loses 40,000 (2.7% of its 1,500,000 premium) — it is experiencing adverse selection.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the profit/loss for two companies after risks moved between the two companies. Using the results of this analysis, candidates were expected to suggest two possible ways for the company with a loss to reduce the risk of insolvency.

Candidates were expected to calculate profit/loss after a specific number of risks moved between the two companies.

Common errors included:

- Not calculating the new risk count correctly.
  - Using incorrect percentage of risks moved.
  - Applying correct percentage to total risks instead of High/Low breakdown.
- Not using the correct formula to calculate profit/loss.
  - Subtracting expected rate from charged rate instead of other way around.
  - Using the charged rate for company A instead of the expected cost to calculate profit/loss for company B.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two possible actions for the company experiencing adverse selection to reduce the risk of insolvency.

### Explanation
Company B could:

- **Refine its rates** by introducing the high/low risk classification, raising the rate on high risks toward their 170 expected cost (and, as part of the same change, lowering it for low risks), so that each class is priced to its cost.
- **Tighten underwriting** on high-risk applicants, declining or restricting them so that fewer underpriced risks enter the book.
- **Change its marketing** to target low-risk insureds, for example by advertising to or through channels that reach the low-risk segment, improving its mix of business.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to list two possible ways the company with a negative profit could reduce the risk of insolvency.

Common errors included:

- Reduce expenses. Not acceptable because the question states there are "no underwriting expenses or profit provisions."
- Increase investment income. Not acceptable because the question states there are "no underwriting expenses or profit provisions."
- Exit the line of business entirely. Not acceptable because the question states the company only writes one line of business so this is essentially the company shutting down.
- Purchase reinsurance. Not acceptable because this is not a mechanism to increase profitability as there is a cost component.
- First suggestion is raise rate for high risk and second suggestion is lower rates for low risk.
This is not acceptable as two separate responses as it would not reduce the risk of insolvency to do the second point without the first.
