A Congressional Research Service report (R45242, version 14, updated January 9, 2023) on the role of private insurers in U.S. flood insurance and the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP). It describes the current role of private insurers in the NFIP, the issues and barriers to private flood insurance outside it, and the potential effects of increased private sector involvement in the flood market.

## Summary
- The NFIP is the main source of primary flood insurance coverage in the United States, collecting over $4.6 billion in premiums, fees and surcharges for over five million flood insurance policies.
- One of the primary reasons behind the creation of the NFIP in 1968 was the withdrawal by private insurers from providing flood insurance coverage; private insurers have been involved in administering the NFIP through sales and servicing of policies and claims.
- Private insurers have expressed increased interest in providing flood coverage, which may offer more flexible policies, integrated coverage with homeowners insurance or lower-cost coverage for some consumers.
- Private coverage would not be guaranteed to be available to all floodplain residents, unlike the NFIP, and consumer protections could vary in different states.
