A company has liabilities of 573 due at the end of year 2 and 701 due at the end of year 5.

A portfolio comprises two zero-coupon bonds, Bond A and Bond B.

Determine which portfolio produces a Redington immunization of the liabilities using an annual effective interest rate of 7.0%.

- A) Bond A: 1-year, current price 500; Bond B: 6-years, current price 500
- B) Bond A: 1-year, current price 572; Bond B: 6-years, current price 428
- C) Bond A: 3-years, current price 182; Bond B: 4-years, current price 1092
- D) Bond A: 3-years, current price 637; Bond B: 4-years, current price 637
- E) Bond A: 3.5 years, current price 1000; Bond B: Not used

## Explanation

This solution uses Macaulay duration and convexity; the same conclusion would follow from modified duration and convexity.

For Redington immunization the assets must (1) have the same present value as the liabilities, (2) have the same duration, and (3) have a greater convexity.

The liabilities have present value $573/1.07^2 + 701/1.07^5 = 1000$. Portfolios C and D cost $182 + 1092 = 637 + 637 = 1274$, so only portfolios A, B and E have a present value of 1000.

The duration of the liabilities is $[2(573)/1.07^2 + 5(701)/1.07^5]/1000 = 3.5$. The duration of a zero-coupon bond is its term, and a portfolio's duration is the price-weighted average of the terms:

- Portfolio A: $[500(1) + 500(6)]/1000 = 3.5$
- Portfolio B: $[572(1) + 428(6)]/1000 = 3.14$, which eliminates portfolio B
- Portfolio E: $3.5$

The convexity of the liabilities is $[4(573)/1.07^2 + 25(701)/1.07^5]/1000 = 14.5$. The convexity of a zero-coupon bond is the square of its term:

- Portfolio A: $[500(1) + 500(36)]/1000 = 18.5 > 14.5$, so portfolio A provides Redington immunization.
- Portfolio E: $3.5^2 = 12.25$, which is less than the liability convexity, so E fails.
