![[Media/Figures/Duty_to_Defend.svg|340]]

The **Duty to Defend** is a liability insurer's obligation to provide and pay for the defence of a claim against its insured. It is broader than the duty to indemnify, because it is triggered by the allegations in the pleadings rather than by what is finally proven, so an insurer must defend a claim it may never have to pay.

## The pleadings rule

A court reads the statement of claim and asks whether any allegation, if proven, would fall within coverage. If one would, the insurer must defend the entire action, including the uncovered allegations bundled with it. Ambiguity is resolved in favour of the duty.

> [!example]- Must the insurer defend this contractor? {Example}
> A contractor is sued for $\$2$ million. The claim alleges negligent construction causing water damage, which the CGL policy covers, and intentional misrepresentation in the contract, which the policy expressly excludes. The insurer's counsel believes the negligence allegation is weak and the misrepresentation is the plaintiff's real case.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - The pleadings contain an allegation, negligent construction, that would fall within coverage if proven.
> > - That triggers the duty however weak the insurer thinks the allegation is.
> > - The covered and uncovered allegations arise from the same facts and cannot be defended separately, so the duty extends to the whole defence.
> > - The insurer should defend under a reservation of rights, stating that the misrepresentation allegation is excluded and indemnity for it is denied.
> > - Insurer-appointed counsel could steer any finding of liability toward intentional rather than negligent conduct, so the insured is entitled to independent counsel at the insurer's expense.
> > - Even if the insured is found liable only for misrepresentation and no indemnity is paid, the defence cost is a real loss and is reserved for.
> > - Yes, the insurer must defend the whole action.

## Reservation of rights

Where coverage is uncertain, the insurer defends while expressly reserving its right to deny indemnity later. Defending without reserving rights can waive the coverage defence. Refusing to defend and being found wrong exposes the insurer to the insured's own defence costs and to a [[Bad Faith Damages|bad faith]] claim.

## Independent counsel

When the defence could be conducted so as to steer the outcome toward an uncovered allegation, the insured is entitled to independent counsel at the insurer's expense. That is a significant cost exposure.

## Defence costs and the limit

In some policies defence costs erode the limit ("wasting" or "defence within limits" policies, common in professional liability). In others they sit outside it. The difference changes the insurer's total exposure and must be reflected in reserving.

Defence cost is [[Allocated Loss Adjustment Expense|ALAE]], and on liability lines with a broad duty to defend it can rival indemnity in size. It is incurred on a defended claim even when no indemnity is paid. Because it accrues steadily through litigation while indemnity lands at settlement, it develops on a different pattern, which is why a combined loss-and-ALAE triangle can mislead.

## When the duty ends

The duty ends when the limit is exhausted by payment, or when it becomes clear that no covered allegation remains. It does not end merely because the insurer believes the claim is meritless.

> [!trap] Refusing to defend a claim the insurer expects to win on coverage
> The duty follows the allegations, not the insurer's view of the merits. An insurer that declines to defend and is wrong pays the insured's defence costs, loses control of the defence and faces a bad faith claim. Defend under a reservation of rights instead.
