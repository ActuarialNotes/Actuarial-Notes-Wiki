Lerner of Troutman Pepper Locke LLP (revised 04/2025), that summarizes the surplus lines eligibility and filing requirements of each U.S. jurisdiction for both foreign (U.S.) and alien surplus lines insurers. ## Preface
- The NRRA today
    - The Nonadmitted and Reinsurance Reform Act (NRRA) came into effect on July 21, 2011 as part of the [[Dodd-Frank|Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act]], to create a simpler and more efficient surplus lines tax payment and regulatory system by limiting regulatory authority over surplus lines transactions to the home state of the insured.
    - One-State Compliance
        - Only the insured's home state may require a surplus lines broker to be licensed and only the home state may collect premium taxes for nonadmitted insurance; the NRRA's preemption does not extend to workers' compensation insurance.
    - Uniform Standards for Surplus Lines Eligibility
        - A U.S. domiciled surplus lines insurer must be authorized to write in its domiciliary jurisdiction and maintain capital and surplus of at least $15 million, or the home state's minimum if higher, under the NAIC Non-admitted Insurance Model Act; an alien insurer must be listed on the NAIC's IID List.
    - Exempt Commercial Purchasers (ECPs)
        - For certain sophisticated commercial purchasers, the NRRA preempts state diligent search requirements if the broker has disclosed that the insurance may be available from the admitted market and the purchaser has requested in writing that the broker place it with a nonadmitted insurer.
    - States' Participation in National Producer Database
    - Calculation of Surplus Lines Taxes under the NRRA
        - With no national tax-sharing arrangement adopted, surplus lines premium taxes are calculated at the insured's home state tax rate and retained 100% by the home state, regardless of where the risks are located.

## Eligibility and Filing Requirements by State
- Requirements by State
    - Surplus Lines Laws – Generally
        - Every U.S. jurisdiction has a surplus lines law, regulation is focused primarily on surplus lines brokers, and there is almost no rate and form regulation of surplus lines insurers.
        - In most states surplus lines insurers should write only business that licensed insurers will not write, which specially licensed brokers "export" after a diligent search of licensed insurers.
    - NAIC Approval
    - Eligibility Requirements of Individual States
        - After the NRRA, a surplus lines transaction is subject only to the eligibility requirements of the insured's home state, which must be consistent with the NRRA.
    - Independently Procured/Direct Placement Insurance
        - A direct placement occurs when an insured goes out of state and buys insurance from an unauthorized carrier without using a resident agent or surplus lines broker; State Board of Insurance v. Todd Shipyards Corporation is the landmark decision, and 43 U.S. jurisdictions have direct placement statutes.
