Given the following:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| General Expenses | \$225,000 |
| Written Premium | \$3,750,000 |
| Earned Premium | \$3,000,000 |
| Other Acquisition Expense | 8.0% |
| Commission | 12.0% |
| Taxes, Licenses & Fees | 3.0% |

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Projected Ultimate Loss and LAE Ratio | 62.0% |
| Target Underwriting Profit Ratio | 5.0% |

- All expenses are paid at policy inception.
- Commission and Taxes, Licenses & Fees are 100% variable.
- All other expense categories are 50% variable.

Calculate the indicated rate change assuming the data is fully credible.

### Answer
-5.48%

### Explanation
The work is in splitting the expense provisions into fixed and variable halves before the indication formula is applied — and in choosing the right premium base for the one expense given in dollars.

**Step 1 — put general expenses on a ratio basis.** Expenses are all paid at policy inception, so they are incurred when the policy is *written*. The correct denominator is therefore **written** premium, not earned premium:

$$\text{General expense ratio} = \frac{225{,}000}{3{,}750{,}000} = 6.0\%$$

(Using earned premium would give $225{,}000/3{,}000{,}000 = 7.5\%$ and overstate the provision.)

**Step 2 — split each category.** Commission and taxes, licenses and fees are wholly variable; general expenses and other acquisition are half of each:

| Category | Total | Fixed | Variable |
|---|---|---|---|
| General Expenses | 6.0% | 3.0% | 3.0% |
| Other Acquisition Expense | 8.0% | 4.0% | 4.0% |
| Commission | 12.0% | — | 12.0% |
| Taxes, Licenses & Fees | 3.0% | — | 3.0% |
| **Total** | **29.0%** | **7.0%** | **22.0%** |

So the fixed expense ratio is $F = 7\%$ and the variable expense ratio is $V = 22\%$.

**Step 3 — the loss ratio indication.** With a target underwriting profit ratio $Q_T = 5\%$:

$$1 + \Delta = \frac{\text{Loss \& LAE ratio} + F}{1 - V - Q_T} = \frac{0.62 + 0.07}{1 - 0.22 - 0.05} = \frac{0.69}{0.73} = 0.94521$$

$$\Delta = 0.94521 - 1 = \mathbf{-5.48\%}$$

The negative sign matters: the projected loss, expense and profit provisions total less than the current premium supports, so rates are indicated to come **down**.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to convert the dollar general expenses into a ratio using written premium, separate each expense category into its fixed and variable components under the stated 100% / 50% splits, and apply the resulting fixed and variable expense provisions in the loss ratio indication.

Common errors included dividing general expenses by earned premium instead of written premium, and dropping the negative sign from the final answer — reporting a 5.48% increase where the arithmetic gives a decrease.
