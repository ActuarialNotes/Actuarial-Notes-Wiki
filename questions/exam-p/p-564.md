The length of an exchange student's phone call depends on whether the call is domestic or international. Lengths of domestic calls are exponentially distributed with a mean of 0.75 minutes, and lengths of international calls are exponentially distributed with a mean of 0.50 minutes. Each call has a 40% probability of being a domestic call and a 60% probability of being an international call.

Calculate the probability that the length of a randomly selected phone call by the student is between one and three minutes.

- A) $0.178$
- B) $0.200$
- C) $0.222$
- D) $0.250$
- E) $0.378$

## Explanation

The call length is a **mixture** of two exponential distributions, so compute the interval probability separately under each and weight by the call-type probabilities.

**Domestic** (mean 0.75):

$$
\begin{align*}
P[1 < X < 3 \mid D] &= e^{-1/0.75} - e^{-3/0.75} \\
                    &= 0.26360 - 0.01832 \\
                    &= 0.24528
\end{align*}
$$

**International** (mean 0.50):

$$
\begin{align*}
P[1 < X < 3 \mid I] &= e^{-1/0.5} - e^{-3/0.5} \\
                    &= 0.13534 - 0.00248 \\
                    &= 0.13286
\end{align*}
$$

Apply the law of total probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[1 < X < 3] &= 0.4(0.24528) + 0.6(0.13286) \\
             &= 0.09811 + 0.07971 \\
             &= \mathbf{0.17783}
\end{align*}
$$

Averaging the two *means* first and treating the result as a single exponential would be wrong: a mixture of exponentials is not exponential. Doing that with mean $0.4(0.75)+0.6(0.5) = 0.60$ gives $e^{-1/0.6} - e^{-3/0.6} = 0.182$, close enough to look plausible but not the correct method.
