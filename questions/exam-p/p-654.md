A broker solicits bids on a financial instrument. The bids are treated as independent random variables, each with a uniform probability density function on $[0, 10]$.

Calculate the expected value of the maximum of three bids.

- A) $6.7$
- B) $7.5$
- C) $8.0$
- D) $8.3$
- E) $8.8$

## Explanation

Let $X_1, X_2, X_3$ be the bids and $X_{(3)} = \max(X_1, X_2, X_3)$. The maximum is below $x$ exactly when all three are, and independence turns that into a product:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(X_{(3)} \le x) &= P(X_1 \le x)P(X_2 \le x)P(X_3 \le x) \\
                 &= \left[F(x)\right]^3 = \left(\frac{x}{10}\right)^3 = \frac{x^3}{1000}
\end{align*}
$$

Differentiating gives the density of the maximum:

$$
f_{(3)}(x) = \frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{x^3}{1000}\right] = \frac{3x^2}{1000}
$$

and therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
E\left[X_{(3)}\right] &= \int_0^{10} x\left(\frac{3x^2}{1000}\right)dx = \int_0^{10} \frac{3x^3}{1000}\,dx \\
                      &= \left.\frac{3x^4}{4000}\right|_0^{10} = \mathbf{7.5}
\end{align*}
$$

The general result is worth remembering: for $n$ independent uniforms on $[0, b]$, the maximum has mean $\frac{n}{n+1}b$, so three bids give $\frac{3}{4}(10) = 7.5$.

Choice (A), 6.7, is that formula with $n = 2$: $\frac{2}{3}(10) = 6.67$, the expected maximum of only *two* bids. Each extra bid pushes the expected maximum closer to 10 but never reaches it.
