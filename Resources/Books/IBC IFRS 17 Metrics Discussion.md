The [[Insurance Bureau of Canada|IBC]]'s June 2025 discussion paper on **key performance indicators under [[IFRS 17]]** — how the Canadian industry should measure and compare itself now that the pre-2023 ratios no longer mean what they used to. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective C4.

## What changed, and why the old metrics broke

IFRS 17 took effect for Canadian insurers in 2023 and was intended to bring new transparency to insurers' underwriting and investment results. Four consequences the paper identifies:

- **Financial results and equity may appear more volatile.**
- **Key financial metrics under IFRS 4 — such as the combined ratio and the loss ratio — have changed**, because the components they were built from changed. Earned premium is no longer the top line; [[Insurance Revenue|insurance revenue]] is, and it excludes investment components and is recognised as coverage is provided.
- **The impact of financial risks and investment income is presented separately** — the split between the [[Insurance Service Result|insurance service result]] and [[Insurance Finance Income or Expenses|insurance finance income or expenses]]. A combined ratio that ignores the unwinding of the discount is not the same measure as one that includes it (see [[MSA Legend]]'s partially- and fully-discounted variants).
- **Greater granularity in contract groupings for valuation** ([[Level of Aggregation|level of aggregation]]) has created additional complexity.

## The questions the paper works through

1. **What are the key considerations in developing KPI metrics under IFRS 17?** The selection depends on the **availability of industry-wide data** and on **who the user is** — a regulator, an investor and a broker do not want the same number.
2. **How do IFRS 17 metrics for P&C differ from IFRS 4 metrics?** **In general they are not comparable.** Earned premiums, expenses and the resulting ratios are constructed differently, and there are important caveats even where a metric keeps its name.
3. **Is the process of establishing new IFRS 17 KPIs still ongoing?** Yes. **It will be a few years before consistent KPI metrics emerge** and year-over-year comparison becomes reliable — a transitional period in which historical series are broken.
4. **Can individual companies create custom KPIs?** Companies may have internal KPIs differing from those published; the paper's concern is comparability of the public set.

The paper also derives an **insurance service ratio** and gives its direct-basis formula.

## Why it is on the syllabus

Objective C4 asks candidates to evaluate an insurer's financial health. This paper is the reminder that the tools for doing so are themselves in transition: a ratio series that spans 2022 and 2023 is a discontinuity, not a trend, and an analyst who reads it as a trend reaches the wrong conclusion. It pairs directly with [[MSA Legend]], which is where the redefined ratios are actually specified.
