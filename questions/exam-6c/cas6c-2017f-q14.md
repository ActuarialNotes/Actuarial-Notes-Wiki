## Part a (3.75 points)

Calculate the company's net income and total comprehensive income for 2016.

### Explanation
Note that for this question we accepted both discounting at mid-year or at year-end.

**Assuming mid-year payments**

Net earned premium:

$$\text{NEP} = (100{,}000 + 80{,}000 - 90{,}000) \times 75\% = 67{,}500$$

Discount factors at 3%:

$$\text{AY 2015} = \frac{1-0.75}{1-0.75} \times 1.03^{-0.5} = 0.9853$$

$$\text{AY 2016} = \frac{0.75-0.5}{1-0.5} \times 1.03^{-0.5} + \frac{1-0.75}{1-0.5} \times 1.03^{-1.5} = 0.9710$$

Discount factors at 2.75%:

$$\text{AY 2015} = \frac{1-0.75}{1-0.75} \times 1.0275^{-0.5} = 0.9865$$

$$\text{AY 2016} = \frac{0.75-0.5}{1-0.5} \times 1.0275^{-0.5} + \frac{1-0.75}{1-0.5} \times 1.0275^{-1.5} = 0.9733$$

- Gross discounted at 3% $= 16{,}000 \times 0.9853 + 41{,}000 \times 0.9710 = 55{,}576$
- Net discounted at 3% $= 55{,}576 \times 0.75 = 41{,}682$
- Net PfAD claims $= 10\% \times 41{,}682 = 4{,}168$
- Net PfAD interest rate $= (16{,}000 \times 0.9865 + 41{,}000 \times 0.9733) \times 0.75 - 41{,}682 = 85$
- Net PfAD reinsurance $= (55{,}576 - 41{,}682) \times 1\% = 139$
- **Net APV claim liabilities** $= 41{,}682 + 4{,}168 + 85 + 139 = 46{,}074$

$$\text{Net paid claims} = [(39{,}000 + 50{,}000 + 75{,}000) - (35{,}000 + 60{,}000)] \times 0.75 = 69{,}000 \times 0.75 = 51{,}750$$

$$\text{Net incurred losses} = 51{,}750 + 46{,}074 - 47{,}876 = 49{,}948$$

- Net investment income $= 100 + 200 + 250 + 300 + 400 - 50 = 1{,}200$
- Other comprehensive income (OCI) $= 350$

Net income for 2016 = EP - Incurred - Expenses + Investment Income:

$$67{,}500 - 49{,}948 - 15{,}000 + 1{,}200 = 3{,}752$$

Total comprehensive income for 2016 = Net income + OCI $= 3{,}752 + 350 = 4{,}102$

**Assuming end of year payments**

### Examiner Report
The candidate is expected to be able to calculate net income and total comprehensive income from their component parts and calculate the other needed items such as earned premium, net incurred losses, and net investment income.

The candidate is expected to be able to differentiate between fair value and historical value of assets.

For subpart a., provide commentary on the following:

- To achieve full credit the candidate was expected to convert the unpaid claims triangles, discount them, apply the PfADs, calculate net incurred losses, earned premium, and net income. Given a portfolio of assets (AFS, HTM, HFT) the candidate was expected to understand how the different types of assets flow through to the income statement and be able to calculate the total comprehensive income.

Common mistakes include:

- Not applying the reinsurance on the earned premiums, or the discounted unpaid claims
- Future payments were calculated incorrectly, or based on the paid triangle and not on the unpaid triangle
- Discount factors or discounted future payments were calculated incorrectly
- Net paid claims were not calculated
- Candidates used the APV unpaid losses in the net income calculation and did not calculate the net incurred loss
- Net investment income and Other Comprehensive income were incorrect

## Part b (0.5 points)

Contrast the use of fair value and historical cost to value an asset.

### Explanation
Recording an asset or liability at fair value means recording it at a value that it would be bought or sold for in the open market. Recording at historical cost means evaluating an asset at the original purchase price less depreciation.

Alternatively:

There is a trade-off between the reliability of the historical cost method (in that it is objectively verifiable) and accuracy of the fair value approach (in that it is more consistent with the actual market value).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to contrast fair value against historical cost by providing one item for each.
A common mistake includes:

- Not listing one item for each fair value and historical cost
