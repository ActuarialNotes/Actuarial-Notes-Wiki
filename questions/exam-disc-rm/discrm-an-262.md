An insurance holding company decides to sell its life insurance subsidiary and buy a specialty property-casualty insurer. At which level of strategy is this decision made?

- A) Business level, because it changes how the property-casualty unit competes
- B) Business level, because it involves a choice of cost leadership or differentiation
- C) Implementation, because it changes the company's structure
- D) Corporate level, because it decides which businesses the company is in

## Explanation

Corporate strategy answers which businesses an organization should be in and how they fit together, treating the company as a portfolio of businesses. Selling one subsidiary and buying another is that kind of decision. Business strategy is how a given business competes in its market. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — The decision changes the portfolio of businesses, not how any one unit competes.
- **B** — Cost leadership and differentiation are business-level choices within a market.
- **C** — Implementation is a step of the strategic management process, not a level of strategy. Choosing which businesses to own is formulation, at the corporate level.
