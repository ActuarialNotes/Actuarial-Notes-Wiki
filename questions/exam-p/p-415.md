A specialty store sells only baby carriages and car seats. The price of a baby carriage is 300 and the price of a car seat is 100. The proprietor knows that 60% of the people stopping at the store do not make a purchase, 20% buy a baby carriage, and 35% buy a car seat. No customer buys more than one of each item. If a customer buys both a baby carriage and a car seat, the proprietor gives a 10% discount on the total.

Calculate the revenue the proprietor expects on a day that 200 people come to the store.

- A) $8{,}200$
- B) $17{,}800$
- C) $18{,}400$
- D) $18{,}440$
- E) $29{,}800$

## Explanation

The 20% and 35% figures **overlap** — a customer buying both is counted in each — so the three purchase categories have to be separated first. Out of 200 people, let $C$ buy only a carriage, $S$ only a seat, and $B$ both.

The four groups partition the 200 visitors:

$$
C + S + B + 0.60(200) = 200
$$

and the two given percentages are

$$
\begin{align*}
C + B &= 0.20(200) = 40 \\
S + B &= 0.35(200) = 70
\end{align*}
$$

Adding those two double counts the overlap, so substituting into the first equation gives

$$
\begin{align*}
40 + 70 - B + 120 &= 200 \\
B &= 30
\end{align*}
$$

and then $C = 10$, $S = 40$.

Now price each group. The both-items customers pay $10\%$ less than $300 + 100 = 400$, so 360 each:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Revenue} &= 10(300) + 40(100) + 30(360) \\
               &= 3000 + 4000 + 10{,}800 \\
               &= \mathbf{17{,}800}
\end{align*}
$$

The 60%-no-purchase figure is what makes the overlap solvable: without it, $B$ could be anything from 0 to 40.
