An insurance policy covers a house for ten calendar years for losses due to hailstorm and theft. Only one loss due to hailstorm and only one loss due to theft are covered by the policy during any one calendar year.

For the $k$th calendar year let $H_k$ be the event that the policy incurs a loss due to hailstorm and let $T_k$ be the event that the policy incurs a loss due to theft. Suppose that the events $H_1, \ldots, H_{10}, T_1, \ldots, T_{10}$ are mutually independent, and that

$$
P[H_k] = 0.2, \quad P[T_k] = 0.1, \quad \text{for all } k.
$$

Calculate the probability that the total number of hailstorm and theft losses covered by the policy during the ten calendar years is less than two.

- A) $0.17$
- B) $0.23$
- C) $0.77$
- D) $0.80$
- E) $0.83$

## Explanation

There are twenty independent events in all: ten hailstorm trials with $p = 0.2$ and ten theft trials with $p = 0.1$. Because the two groups have *different* success probabilities, the total is not a single binomial — handle each group separately.

**No losses at all.** Every one of the twenty must fail:

$$
P[0] = (0.8)^{10}(0.9)^{10} = (0.10737)(0.34868) = 0.03744
$$

**Exactly one loss.** It is either a hailstorm loss (one of ten) with the rest clean, or a theft loss (one of ten) with the rest clean:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[1] &= 10(0.2)(0.8)^9(0.9)^{10} + (0.8)^{10}10(0.1)(0.9)^9 \\
     &= 0.09361 + 0.04160 \\
     &= 0.13520
\end{align*}
$$

Adding:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{fewer than two}] &= 0.03744 + 0.13520 \\
                         &= \mathbf{0.1726}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 0.83, is the complement — the probability of two *or more* losses. With an expected $10(0.2) + 10(0.1) = 3$ losses over the decade, fewer than two is the unlikely outcome, which is the sanity check that rules out the answers above one half.
