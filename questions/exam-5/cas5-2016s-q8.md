Given the following information for a book of business as of December 31, 2015:

| Rate Change History | |
|---|---|
| Effective Date | Change |
| October 1, 2013 | +4.5% |
| April 1, 2015 | +2.5% |

| Calendar Year | 2013 | 2014 | 2015 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earned Premium | $1,870,000 | $2,228,000 | $2,404,000 |
| Earned Exposures | 1,420 | 1,530 | 1,610 |

Cumulative Reported Loss + ALAE ($) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 2,150,000 | 2,395,000 | 2,495,000 |
| 2014 | 925,000 | 1,085,000 | |
| 2015 | 1,250,000 | | |

Cumulative Reported Loss + ALAE excluding Catastrophes ($) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 750,000 | 895,000 | 975,000 |
| 2014 | 825,000 | 975,000 | |
| 2015 | 900,000 | | |

- All policies are semi-annual.
- Exposures are written evenly throughout each calendar year.
- Annual severity trend = 5%.
- Annual frequency trend = -1%.
- Annual premium trend = 2%.
- Fixed expense ratio = 5%.
- Variable expense ratio = 22%.
- Profit and contingencies provision = 6%.
- ULAE provision = 7% of loss and ALAE.
- Projected catastrophe load including ALAE = $235 per exposure.
- There is no loss development beyond 36 months.
- Rates are to be in effect for one year.

Calculate the indicated rate change for policies effective January 1, 2017 using the latest three accident years of experience and assuming full credibility.

## Explanation

**Step 1 — on-level the premium (parallelogram method).** Policies are semi-annual, so a rate change earns in over six months. The portion of each accident year at each rate level and the resulting average rate level:

| Accident Year | Area A (1.000) | Area B (1.045) | Area C (1.071) | Average rate level | On-level factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 0.9375 | 0.0625 | 0 | 1.002813 | 1.0681 |
| 2014 | 0.0625 | 0.9375 | 0 | 1.04219 | 1.0278 |
| 2015 | 0 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 1.05806 | 1.01235 |

**Step 2 — trend the on-level premium** at 2% from each accident year's average written date to the average written date under the new rates:

| Accident Year | Earned premium | OLF | Trend | Trended on-level EP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 1,870,000 | 1.0681 | $1.02^{4.25}$ | 2,172,722 |
| 2014 | 2,228,000 | 1.0278 | $1.02^{3.25}$ | 2,442,161 |
| 2015 | 2,404,000 | 1.01235 | $1.02^{2.25}$ | 2,544,577 |
| Total | | | | **7,159,468** |

**Step 3 — develop and trend the non-catastrophe losses.** Catastrophes are handled by the separate load, so the development factors come from the ex-cat triangle:

| | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-ult |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selected | 1.1875 | 1.0894 | 1.00 |
| Age-to-ultimate | 1.2937 | 1.0894 | 1.00 |

Loss trend combines the two given trends, $(0.99 \times 1.05)$ per year:

| Accident Year | Reported ex-cat | ATU | Trend | Trended ultimate | Loss ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 975,000 | 1.0000 | $(0.99 \times 1.05)^{4.25}$ | 1,149,499 | 52.91% |
| 2014 | 975,000 | 1.0894 | $(0.99 \times 1.05)^{3.25}$ | 1,204,680 | 49.33% |
| 2015 | 900,000 | 1.2937 | $(0.99 \times 1.05)^{2.25}$ | 1,270,373 | 49.92% |
| Total | | | | 3,624,552 | **50.63%** |

The yearly ratios are stable, so the all-year weighted ratio of $50.63\%$ is selected.

**Step 4 — convert the catastrophe load to a ratio** by dividing the per-exposure load by the average trended on-level premium per exposure:

$$\frac{235}{7{,}159{,}468 / (1{,}420 + 1{,}530 + 1{,}610)} = 14.97\%$$

**Step 5 — the indication.** ULAE is 7% of loss and ALAE, the fixed expense ratio is 5%, and variable expenses (22%) and profit (6%) are the permissible-loss-ratio denominator:

$$\frac{(50.63\% + 14.97\%)(1.07) + 5\%}{1 - 22\% - 6\%} - 1 = 4.433\%$$

Alternatively: trending premium in two steps — restating each year's average written premium to the latest level of $1{,}511.5$ and then trending 7/1/2015 to 10/1/2017 — gives trended premium of $7{,}206{,}483$, a loss ratio of $65.155\%$ including the catastrophe dollars, and an indication of $3.77\%$.
