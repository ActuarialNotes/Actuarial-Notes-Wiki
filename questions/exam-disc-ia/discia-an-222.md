A player in a recreational ice hockey league is hurt by an opponent's body check that was within the league's rules. He sues the opponent for negligence. Which defense is the opponent most likely to win on?

- A) Comparative negligence, reducing the award by the player's share of fault
- B) Last clear chance, because the player could have avoided the check
- C) Primary assumption of risk, because the contact is inherent in the sport
- D) Statute of repose, because the injury occurred during an organized game

## Explanation

Under **primary** assumption of risk the defendant owed no duty of care against risks inherent in the activity, such as contact in a sport. A legal body check is part of hockey, so the opponent breached no duty and the claim fails completely rather than being reduced.

- **A** would only reduce the award, and it presumes a breach of duty, which there was not.
- **B** is an exception that helps a negligent plaintiff recover in a contributory negligence state. It does not fit these facts.
- **D** is a time limit running from a defendant's act, such as making a product. It has nothing to do with a sports injury.
