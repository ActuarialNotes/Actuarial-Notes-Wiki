You are building a Linear Mixed Model to estimate the number of polices written by sales representatives in various agencies in the company. You are given the following information:

- The number of polices written ($Y_{ij}$) is measured for a sales representative $i$ who works in agency $j$.
- $L_j$ and $M_j$ represent characteristics of individual agencies.
- $N_{ij}$ represents a characteristic of an individual sales representative.
- $\mu_j$ (as printed; the model writes $u_j$) is a random effect for each agency that is normally distributed with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2_{agency}$.
- $\varepsilon_{ij}$ is a residual that is normally distributed with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$.
- The general model specification is in the following form:

$$
Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times L_j + \beta_2 \times M_j + \beta_3 \times N_{ij} + \beta_4 \times M_j \times N_{ij} + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}
$$

Determine the hierarchical model specification.

- A) Level 1: $Y_{ij} = b_{0j} + b_{1j} \times M_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times L_j + \beta_3 \times N_{ij} + u_j$, $b_{1j} = \beta_2 + \beta_4 \times N_{ij}$
- B) Level 1: $Y_{ij} = b_{0j} + b_{1j} \times M_j$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times L_j + \beta_3 \times N_{ij} + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$, $b_{1j} = \beta_2 + \beta_4 \times N_{ij}$
- C) Level 1: $Y_{ij} = b_{0j} + b_{1j} \times N_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij}$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times L_j + \beta_2 \times M_j + u_j$, $b_{1j} = \beta_3 + \beta_4 \times M_j$
- D) Level 1: $Y_{ij} = b_{0j} + b_{1j} \times N_{ij}$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times L_j + \beta_2 \times M_j + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$, $b_{1j} = \beta_3 + \beta_4 \times M_j$
- E) Level 1: $Y_{ij} = b_{0j} + b_{1j} \times N_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij}$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times L_j + \beta_3 \times M_j + u_j$, $b_{1j} = \beta_2 + \beta_4 \times M_j$

## Explanation

The hierarchy follows the data: **Level 1** is the sales representative within an agency, so it carries the representative-level covariate $N_{ij}$ and the residual $\varepsilon_{ij}$. **Level 2** is the agency, so it carries the agency-level covariates $L_j$, $M_j$ and the agency random effect $u_j$.

That immediately eliminates (A) and (B), which put the agency characteristic $M_j$ at Level 1, and (D), which strands $\varepsilon_{ij}$ at Level 2 — a residual belongs with the observation.

Between (C) and (E), substitute (C) back and check:

$$
\begin{align*}
Y_{ij} &= b_{0j} + b_{1j}N_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij} \\
       &= \big(\beta_0 + \beta_1 L_j + \beta_2 M_j + u_j\big) + \big(\beta_3 + \beta_4 M_j\big)N_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij} \\
       &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 L_j + \beta_2 M_j + \beta_3 N_{ij} + \beta_4 M_j N_{ij} + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}
\end{align*}
$$

which is the general specification exactly. Option (E) swaps $\beta_2$ and $\beta_3$ between the two levels, so it would attach $\beta_3$ to $M_j$ and $\beta_2$ to $N_{ij}$.

Note where the interaction comes from: the cross-level term $\beta_4 M_j N_{ij}$ appears because the agency characteristic $M_j$ is allowed to modify the Level 1 *slope* on $N_{ij}$.

The answer is **C**.
