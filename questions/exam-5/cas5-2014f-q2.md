Given the following policy data:

| Policy | Effective Date | Expiration Date | Initial Policy Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | June 1, 2012 | May 31, 2013 | 480 |
| 2 | July 1, 2012 | December 31, 2012 | 125 |
| 3 | March 1, 2013 | February 28, 2014 | 225 |
| 4 | August 1, 2013 | March 31, 2014 | 300 |

- Six months after the policy expires, the initial policy premium on every policy increases by 8% due to the final audit.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate calendar year 2013 earned premium as of December 31, 2013.

### Explanation
CY 2013 EP = Policy 1: 5/12 x 480 + 480 x 0.08 = 238.4
2: 125 x 0.08 = 10
3: 10/12 x 225 = 187.5
4: 5/8 x 300 = 187.5
CY total EP = 238.4 + 10 + 187.5 + 187.5 = 623.4

### Examiner Report
The most common mistakes included multiplying the 8% audit by the 2013 earned premium for policy 1 (instead of total premium), not including the audit for policy 2, and multiplying the policy 4 WP by 5/12. Other mistakes were including the policy 2 premium or including the wrong %
earned for some or all of the policies 1 through 3.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate calendar year 2013 written premium as of December 31, 2013.

### Explanation
CY 2013 WP
Policy 1: 480 x 0.08 = 38.4
2: 125 x 0.08 = 10
3: 225
4: 300
Total = 573.4

### Examiner Report
The most common errors were not including the audits for policies 1 and/or 2. Other mistakes included including audit premiums for policies 3 and/or 4.

## Part c (0.25 points)

Calculate policy year 2013 earned premium as of December 31, 2013.

### Explanation
PY 2013 EP
Policy 1: 0
2: 0
3: 225 x 10/12 = 187.5
4: 300 x 5/8 = 187.5
Total = 375

### Examiner Report
The most common errors included multiplying the policies by a wrong % of earned amount, especially policy 4, where many candidates used 5/12 as the % earned.

## Part d (0.25 points)

Calculate policy year 2013 written premium as of December 31, 2014.

### Explanation
PY 2013 WP
Policy 1: 0
2: 0
3: 225 x 1.08 = 243
4: 300 x 1.08 = 324
Total = 567

### Examiner Report
The most common errors included not multiplying the premiums for policies 3 and 4 by the 8%
audit.
