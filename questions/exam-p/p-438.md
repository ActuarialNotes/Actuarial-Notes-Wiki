A delivery truck, when filled to capacity, can carry only three items of Type A in addition to only two items of Type B.

One day, six items of Type A and four items of Type B await delivery. The ten items are brought to the loading dock one at a time in random order.

Calculate the probability that the first five items brought to the loading dock will fill the delivery truck to capacity.

- A) $1/210$
- B) $1/21$
- C) $1/10$
- D) $21/100$
- E) $10/21$

## Explanation

The truck holds exactly three Type A items and two Type B items. So the first five items fill it to capacity precisely when those five consist of 3 Type A and 2 Type B — no more, no fewer, of either.

Translating the arrival order into a random subset: the first five items are an unordered random sample of size 5 drawn without replacement from the ten, so the count of Type A among them is hypergeometric.

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \frac{\binom{6}{3}\binom{4}{2}}{\binom{10}{5}} \\
  &= \frac{(20)(6)}{252} \\
  &= \frac{120}{252} \\
  &= \mathbf{\frac{10}{21}}
\end{align*}
$$

The order within those five never matters, which is why no permutation factor appears — recognising that the arrival sequence reduces to a subset is the step that turns this into a one-line calculation. Choice (B), $1/21$, is the reciprocal-flavoured trap; note the answer should be *large*, since 3 A's and 2 B's is close to the most likely split given the 6-to-4 population.
