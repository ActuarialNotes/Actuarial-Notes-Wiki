Given the following information about an insurance product:

**Territory Factors**

| Territory | Factor |
|---|---|
| A | 0.85 |
| B | 1.00 |
| C | 1.35 |

- Fixed expense per exposure = \$50.
- Variable expense ratio = 17%.
- Underwriting profit provision = 3%.
- LAE provision = 16% of loss cost.
- Base rate = \$435.
- Policy fee = \$55.
- Policy fee is an additive fee added to each exposure in the last step of the rate calculation.

Based on a separate analysis, an actuary projects the following for calendar-accident year 2018:

| Territory | Earned Exposures | Ultimate Loss Cost |
|---|---|---|
| A | 150 | \$300 |
| B | 200 | \$350 |
| C | 100 | \$500 |

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate the projected total underwriting profit for calendar-accident year 2018.

### Answer
-\$26,372 (an underwriting **loss**), equal to -11.67% of premium

### Explanation
Underwriting profit is what is left of premium after loss and LAE, fixed expenses and variable expenses:

$$\text{UW profit} = P - (L + \text{LAE}) - E_F - E_V$$

**Step 1 — projected premium.** The rate per exposure is $\text{base rate} \times \text{territory factor}$, with the \$55 policy fee added last:

| Territory | Rate per exposure | Exposures | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | $435(0.85) + 55 = 424.75$ | 150 | \$63,712.50 |
| B | $435(1.00) + 55 = 490.00$ | 200 | \$98,000.00 |
| C | $435(1.35) + 55 = 642.25$ | 100 | \$64,225.00 |
| **Total** | | **450** | **\$225,937.50** |

**Step 2 — projected loss and LAE.** The loss costs are per exposure, and LAE is a 16% load on loss:

| Territory | Loss cost | Exposures | Loss | Loss + LAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | \$300 | 150 | \$45,000 | \$52,200 |
| B | \$350 | 200 | \$70,000 | \$81,200 |
| C | \$500 | 100 | \$50,000 | \$58,000 |
| **Total** | | | **\$165,000** | **\$191,400** |

$$L + \text{LAE} = 165{,}000 \times 1.16 = \$191{,}400$$

**Step 3 — expenses.** The \$50 is a fixed expense *per exposure* — it is not the same thing as the \$55 policy fee, which is a component of premium:

$$E_F = 50 \times 450 = \$22{,}500 \qquad E_V = 0.17 \times 225{,}937.50 = \$38{,}409.38$$

The variable expense ratio applies to the **whole** premium, policy fee included.

**Step 4 — underwriting profit.**

$$225{,}937.50 - 191{,}400 - 22{,}500 - 38{,}409.38 = -\$26{,}371.88$$

Alternatively, solve for the profit provision the current rates actually deliver. Setting the pure premium indication to a factor of 1.0,

$$\frac{\dfrac{191{,}400}{225{,}937.50} + \dfrac{22{,}500}{225{,}937.50}}{1 - 0.17 - Q} = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 0.84714 + 0.09959 = 0.83 - Q$$

$$Q = 0.83 - 0.94672 = -0.11672$$

The product is running at a **-11.67%** profit provision against a 3% target, and $-0.11672 \times 225{,}937.50 = -\$26{,}372$ reconciles with the dollar answer.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use the pure premium framework to compute the underwriting profit actually realized: build premium from the base rate, territory factor and policy fee, load losses for LAE, and subtract both fixed and variable expenses.

Common errors included omitting expenses entirely; failing to apply the variable expense ratio to the full premium including the policy fee; forgetting to load losses for LAE; and confusing the \$50 fixed expense with the \$55 policy fee — adding both as premium, or subtracting both as expenses.

## Part b (1.5 points)

Calculate the indicated policy fee, indicated territory factors, and indicated base rate.

### Answer
Policy fee \$62.50; territory factors A = 0.857, B = 1.000, C = 1.429; base rate \$507.50

### Explanation
Each piece of the rating algorithm is set to recover the piece of cost it is designed to carry.

**Indicated policy fee.** The additive fee exists to cover the fixed expense per exposure. Because it is part of premium, it must also carry its own variable expense and profit load, so it is grossed up by the variable permissible loss ratio:

$$\text{Policy fee} = \frac{\text{fixed expense per exposure}}{1 - V - Q_T} = \frac{50}{1 - 0.17 - 0.03} = \frac{50}{0.80} = \$62.50$$

Note the \$50 fixed expense is the input, not the \$55 fee currently charged.

**Indicated territory factors.** Relativities are the ratio of each territory's loss cost to the base territory's. Territory B has a factor of 1.00 today, so it is the base:

| Territory | Ultimate loss cost | Indicated factor |
|---|---|---|
| A | \$300 | $300/350 = 0.857$ |
| B | \$350 | $350/350 = 1.000$ |
| C | \$500 | $500/350 = 1.429$ |

The loss costs are already per exposure, so no further division by exposures is needed.

**Indicated base rate.** The base rate must cover loss and LAE for the *base* territory (the territory factor of 1.000 already prices the others off it), grossed up for variable expense and profit. Fixed expenses are excluded — the policy fee handles them:

$$\text{Base rate} = \frac{350 \times 1.16}{1 - 0.17 - 0.03} = \frac{406}{0.80} = \$507.50$$

Alternatively, solve for the base rate $B$ that makes total premium net of variable expense and profit equal to total loss and LAE:

$$\left[150(0.857) + 200(1.000) + 100(1.429)\right] \times B \times 0.80 = 191{,}400$$

$$471.43 \, B \times 0.80 = 191{,}400 \;\Longrightarrow\; B = \frac{191{,}400}{377.14} = \$507.50$$

As a check, the indicated algorithm balances exactly: premium becomes $150(435.00 + 62.50) + 200(507.50 + 62.50) + 100(725.00 + 62.50) = \$267{,}375$, against which loss and LAE of \$191,400, fixed expenses of \$22,500, variable expenses of $0.17(267{,}375) = \$45{,}453.75$ and target profit of $0.03(267{,}375) = \$8{,}021.25$ sum to \$267,375. That is an overall increase of $267{,}375/225{,}937.50 - 1 = 18.3\%$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to derive each component of the rating algorithm separately: territory relativities from the projected loss costs, a policy fee from the fixed expense per exposure, and a base rate from the base territory's loss cost.

Common errors included using the \$55 current policy fee instead of the \$50 fixed expense when computing the indicated fee; dividing the already-per-exposure loss costs by exposures again when forming relativities; calculating an indicated *total* rate rather than a base rate; and using the all-territory average loss cost instead of the base territory's loss cost.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Management suggests reaching the targeted profit by only increasing the base rate. Discuss this approach.

### Explanation
A uniform base rate increase would raise the overall rate level to the indicated amount and, if the mix of business does not shift, would restore the 3% target profit in aggregate. It is also the simplest change to file and to implement. But it leaves the current territory relativities and the current \$55 policy fee in place, and part b shows both are wrong:

- **Territory C is underpriced.** Its current factor of 1.35 is below the indicated 1.429, so C would still not pay its own loss cost after a uniform increase; territories A and B would keep subsidizing it. Territory A's current 0.85 sits above its indicated 0.857 only marginally, so the distortion is concentrated in C.
- **The policy fee is inadequate.** At \$55 it recovers less than the \$62.50 needed to cover the \$50 fixed expense per exposure grossed up for variable expense and profit.

The consequence is **adverse selection**. Competitors pricing territory C accurately will undercut the insurer in A and B while leaving it to write the underpriced C business. The mix drifts toward C, the aggregate rate level that looked adequate at the old mix becomes inadequate, and profitability deteriorates even though the overall indication was taken. Rates that vary from cost by territory are also less equitable and harder to defend as not unfairly discriminatory.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to weigh a simple base rate change against a full revision of the rating algorithm, recognizing that a uniform increase corrects the overall level but not the distribution of rates across territories.

Common errors included offering only a single reason where the question asked for a discussion; stating a position such as agreeing with management without supporting it; and misidentifying which territories are mispriced — in particular arguing that territory C was overpriced when the indicated relativity is above the current one.
