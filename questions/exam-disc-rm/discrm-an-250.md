An insurer's property treaty excludes chemical plants. A producer submits a large chemical plant that the insurer would like to write if it can reinsure part of it. Which arrangement fits?

- A) Adding the plant to the existing treaty, since treaties reinsure all business automatically
- B) A quota share treaty on the insurer's whole property book
- C) Facultative reinsurance negotiated for that plant alone
- D) An aggregate stop loss on the insurer's property results

## Explanation

Facultative reinsurance covers one risk at a time: the insurer offers the specific risk to a reinsurer, which underwrites it and decides whether to accept and on what terms. It is the usual answer for a risk the treaties exclude. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — A treaty reinsures automatically only the business that falls within its terms, and this plant is excluded.
- **B** — A new quota share on the whole book wouldn't be negotiated for one risk, and it would likely carry the same exclusion.
- **D** — A stop loss protects the year's total results, not the large single risk the insurer wants to share.
