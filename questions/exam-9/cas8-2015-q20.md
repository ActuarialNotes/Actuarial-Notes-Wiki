An actuary is using the following exposure curve to rate a non-proportional reinsurance treaty:

$$G(x) = \frac{(1 - b^x)}{(1 - b)}$$

The actuary is also given the following information:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Maximum Possible Loss | \$5,000,000 |
| Insured Value | \$5,000,000 |
| Gross Premium | \$6,000 |
| Expected Loss Ratio | 60% |
| Retention of non-proportional reinsurance treaty | \$150,000 |
| Expected Ceded Risk Premium | \$2,705 |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Briefly describe a method to allocate gross premium for the non-proportional reinsurance treaty between the ceding company and the reinsurer.

### Explanation
First estimate the total expected pure premium under the underlying business.  Then apportion the pure premium between the reinsurer and ceding company by using exposure curves to the appropriate layer of each party

Alternatively:

Use the expected loss ratio to turn premium to losses, then use the exposure curve to allocate losses to the cedant and the reinsurer.

### Examiner Report
This question required candidates to identify that they were allocating risk premiums between the ceding company and the reinsurer by using exposure curves.

Most candidates received full or partial credit, the most common error was to not identify that gross premium would be allocated as a result of the risk premium/loss exposure that the ceding company and reinsurer were covering.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Given the probability of a total loss is 0.03, calculate the parameter $b$ in the formula above.

### Explanation
The probability of a total loss is $p = G'(1)/G'(0)$:

$$p = 0.03 = \frac{G'(1)}{G'(0)} = \frac{\dfrac{-b^1 \ln(b)}{(1 - b)}}{\dfrac{-b^0 \ln(b)}{(1 - b)}} = b$$

Alternatively, this is a special case of MBBEFD where $bg = 1$: $p = 0.03$, $g = 1/.03 = 33.33$, $b = 0.03$.

### Examiner Report
This question required candidates to calculate b which could have been approached a few ways. Candidates could have derived G(x) and used the formula $p = 0.03 = \dfrac{G'(1)}{G'(0)}$ to solve for b. They could have also recognized that this was a special case of the MBBEFD curves such that p = b = .03. Most candidates were able to recognize this and derive G(x).

## Part c (1.5 points)

Given the answer in part b. above, calculate the limit of the non-proportional reinsurance treaty.

### Explanation
Total risk premium = gross premium × expected loss ratio = 6000 × 0.60 = 3,600.

Ceded risk premium = total risk premium × exposure factor, so

$$\text{Exposure Factor} = \frac{\text{Ceded Risk Premium}}{\text{Total Risk Premium}} = \frac{2705}{3600} = 0.7514$$

$$\text{Exposure Factor} = G\!\left(\frac{\text{Retention} + \text{Limit}}{\text{MPL/Insured Value}}\right) - G\!\left(\frac{\text{Retention}}{\text{MPL/Insured Value}}\right) = G\!\left(\frac{150{,}000 + \text{Limit}}{5{,}000{,}000}\right) - G\!\left(\frac{150{,}000}{5{,}000{,}000}\right) = G(y) - G(0.03)$$

$$G(y) - \frac{1 - 0.03^{0.03}}{1 - 0.03} = G(y) - 0.1029 = 0.7514 \;\rightarrow\; G(y) = 0.7514 + 0.1029 = 0.8543 = \frac{1 - 0.03^y}{1 - 0.03}$$

$$0.97 \times 0.8543 = 0.8287 = 1 - 0.03^y \;\rightarrow\; 0.03^y = 0.1713 \;\rightarrow\; y \ln(0.03) = \ln(0.1713)$$

$$-1.7644 = -3.5066\,y \;\rightarrow\; y = 0.5032 = \frac{150{,}000 + \text{Limit}}{5{,}000{,}000} \;\rightarrow\; \text{Limit} = 2{,}365{,}859$$

### Examiner Report
There were two common errors that occurred on part c. The first error involved candidates incorrectly calculating the exposure factor by dividing the ceded risk premium by gross premium rather than the risk premium (\$6,000 x 60%). The second error involved candidates forgoing the subtraction of the \$150,000 retention at the end of the problem to get the limit of the non-proportional reinsurance treaty.
