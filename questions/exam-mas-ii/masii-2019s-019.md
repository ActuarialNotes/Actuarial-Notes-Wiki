You are modeling how many claims, $Y_i$, each individual policy $i$ will have next year. You are using the following model:

$$
\Pr(Y_i = y_i) = \binom{10}{y_i}p^{y_i}(1-p)^{10-y_i}
$$

$$
f(p) = \frac{\Gamma(10)}{\Gamma(4)\Gamma(6)}p^{3}(1-p)^{5}
$$

Given the following five data points:

$$
0,\, 0,\, 0,\, 1,\, 3
$$

Calculate the mean of the posterior distribution of $p$.

- A) Less than 0.05
- B) At least 0.05, but less than 0.10
- C) At least 0.10, but less than 0.15
- D) At least 0.15, but less than 0.20
- E) At least 0.20

## Explanation

The prior is a beta distribution: matching $p^{3}(1-p)^{5}$ to $p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}$ gives

$$
p \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha = 4,\ \beta = 6).
$$

The beta is conjugate to the binomial. Across the five policies there are $n = 5$ binomial experiments of 10 trials each, so

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{successes} &= 0+0+0+1+3 = 4 \\
\text{failures} &= 5(10) - 4 = 46
\end{align*}
$$

The posterior is therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
p \mid \mathbf{y} &\sim \text{Beta}(\alpha + 4,\ \beta + 46) \\
                  &= \text{Beta}(8,\ 52)
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[p \mid \mathbf{y}] &= \frac{8}{8 + 52} \\
                     &= \frac{8}{60} \\
                     &= 0.1333
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[0.10,\, 0.15)$, so the answer is **C**.
