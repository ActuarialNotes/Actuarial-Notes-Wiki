A study of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Research Council – Experience Research Committee, dated January 2020 and published as Canadian Group Life Waiver Study (2009–2015), that updates disabled life mortality and recovery experience using the CIA's 2009–2015 Group Long-Term Disability (LTD) Termination Study. It compares the updated rates, and the waiver liabilities calculated from them, with those from previous Canadian and American disabled life studies, since almost all group life waiver plans now follow a group LTD definition of disability.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam CP-321 (SOA)|Exam CP-321]] — Outcomes 2d; pages 4-25 and Appendix G (background only).

## Executive Summary
- Key findings: Québec is noticeably distinct from the rest of Canada, particularly in early-duration recovery rates; female disabled mortality rates are approximately two-thirds of the male rates; and Canadian experience is improving, by about 1% per year for both mortality and recoveries.
- American mortality rates are observed at 350% of the Canadian rates and American recovery rates are approximately half of the Canadian experience.
- Study outcomes: graduated tables for terminations due to recovery or death while disabled, and an analysis of the new tables against previous Canadian and American disability studies.

## Introduction
- The CIA published a Group Life Waiver study in 2001, by Peter Muirhead, based on 1988–1994 Canadian Group LTD termination experience; before it, most group life waiver of premium liabilities were calculated from the 1970 Intercompany Group Life Waiver of Premium Table (Krieger Table).

## Study Objective
- A graduated table of disabled mortality and recovery rates by gender, age at disability, duration since disability and geographical region; an analytic comparison to previous studies; sample life waiver liability levels and their sensitivity to the mortality, recovery and discount rate assumptions; and summaries by region, cause of disability and level of benefit.
