Given the following annual information regarding an insurance company:

- Surplus is \$400,000.
- Written Premium is \$398,352.
- Expenses are \$100,000.
- Expected Loss + ALAE is \$300,000.
- Risk-free rate is 4%.
- Total Market Value is \$657,152.
- Premiums are collected and expenses are paid at the beginning of each year.
- Losses are paid at the end of each year.

## Part a (1.25 points)

Assume the company uses a pricing strategy that involves a fixed target return on surplus.

Calculate the assumed client retention rate implied by the market value.

### Explanation
Working in thousands, with $y = 4\%$:

$$\text{Current economic value} = S + P - E - \frac{L}{1+y} = 400 + 398.352 - 100 - \frac{300}{1.04} = 409.890$$

$$\text{Franchise value} = \text{Total economic value} - \text{Current economic value} = 657.152 - 409.890 = 247.262$$

Under a fixed target return, the franchise value is each year's renewal profit discounted at $d$:

$$\text{Franchise value} = \left(P - E - \frac{L}{1+y}\right)\frac{d}{1-d}$$

$$247.262 = \left(398.352 - 100 - \frac{300}{1.04}\right)\frac{d}{1-d} \quad\Rightarrow\quad d = 0.96154$$

Since $d = \dfrac{cr}{1+y}$:

$$cr = d\,(1+y) = 0.96154 \times 1.04 = 1.000$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the formulas for Total Economic Value, Current Economic Value and Franchise Value.  They were also expected to know the relationship between d and cr.
Candidates generally did well in this part.  Due to multiple steps involved, it was quite common to see a calculation error.
Common errors included:

1. Assuming Total Economic Value = Franchise Value + Surplus
2. When converting from d to cr (last step): dividing by 1.04 instead of multiplying by 1.04

## Part b (0.5 points)

Assume the company uses a pricing strategy that varies the target return on surplus ($k$) according to this formula: $k = a + b \times y$

where:

- $a = 8\%$
- $b = 1.5$

Calculate the assumed client retention rate implied by the market value.

### Explanation
The franchise value is the same as in part a, 247.262. Under the variable pricing strategy:

$$\text{Franchise value} = \frac{cr \times S \times [a + y(b-1)]}{(1+y)(1+y-cr)}$$

$$247.262 = \frac{cr \times 400 \times [0.08 + 0.04 \times (1.5 - 1)]}{1.04 \times (1.04 - cr)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad cr = 0.90$$

Equivalently, writing the denominator as $\dfrac{d}{1-d}$ gives $d = 0.86538$, and $cr = d\,(1+y) = 0.86538 \times 1.04 = 0.90$.

Alternatively, re-calculate the premium under the new target return, then the franchise value:

$$k = 0.08 + 1.5 \times 0.04 = 0.14$$

$$\text{Written premium} = \frac{S\,(k - y) + L}{1+y} + E = \frac{400\,(0.14 - 0.04) + 300}{1.04} + 100 = 426.923$$

$$\text{Current economic value} = 400 + 426.923 - 100 - \frac{300}{1.04} = 438.461$$

$$\text{Franchise value} = 657.152 - 438.461 = 218.691$$

$$218.691 = \left(426.923 - 100 - \frac{300}{1.04}\right)\frac{d}{1-d} \quad\Rightarrow\quad d = 0.8504$$

$$cr = d\,(1+y) = 0.8504 \times 1.04 = 0.884$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the formula for Franchise Value under the variable pricing strategy.  Again, candidates generally did well in this part.  There were two primary approaches to this part where the candidate could assume the same Franchise Value as part a (ie. Sample 1 and
Sample 2) or assume the Premium and Franchise Value needed to both be re-calculated (ie.
Sample 3).
Common errors included:

1. Assuming the premium is unaffected and thus assuming the same retention as Part a
2. For Sample 1, forgetting (1+y) in the denominator
3. For Sample 3, not recalculating the Franchise value after having recalculated Premium

## Part c (0.5 points)

Describe which pricing strategy the company is more likely to be using.

### Explanation
It is more likely to be using the variable pricing strategy in part b.  It is unlikely that a company has 100% retention or that a company would assume 100% retention.  A retention of 90% is more reasonable.

Alternatively:

Since the duration of the franchise value under the variable pricing strategy is lower, the company is more likely using this strategy so that the duration of franchise value is less sensitive to changes in interest rates.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know at least one advantage of the variable pricing strategy or be able to recognize that a company generally would not assume 100% retention. Again, candidates generally did well in this part and frequently commented on the retention being unreasonable.
Many candidates recognized that companies would use a variable pricing strategy to minimize the sensitivity of the Franchise Value to interest rate changes.
Common errors included:

- Not clearly stating the pricing strategy that the company will more likely use

## Part d (0.5 points)

Calculate the duration of franchise value using the pricing strategy assumptions in part b. above.

### Explanation
$$D_F = \frac{a - b + 1}{(1+y)(a + b\,y - y)} + \frac{1}{1 + y - cr}$$

$$D_F = \frac{0.08 - 1.5 + 1}{1.04 \times (0.08 + 1.5 \times 0.04 - 0.04)} + \frac{1}{1.04 - 0.90} = 3.104$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the formula for the duration of Franchise Value.  Candidates didn't do as well in this part as the other parts.  Candidates either had trouble recalling the formula for the duration or they had calculation errors.
Common errors included:

- Switching the +/- signs in the numerator of the duration formula (eg. a + b – 1)
- Mistaking the portion of the denominator of the duration formula as (a+b*y-1)
