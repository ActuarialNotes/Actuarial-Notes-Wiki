Given the following data as of December 31, 2018:

| Cumulative Paid Claims (\$000) Gross of Salvage & Subrogation — Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 17,500 | 21,500 | 24,000 |
| 2016 | 19,000 | 23,500 | 25,700 |
| 2017 | 18,500 | 22,800 | |
| 2018 | 18,100 | | |

| Cumulative Received Salvage and Subrogation (\$000) — Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,150 | 4,050 | 5,300 |
| 2016 | 1,180 | 4,300 | 5,680 |
| 2017 | 1,200 | 4,250 | |
| 2018 | 850 | | |

| Value | Item |
|---|---|
| 1.40 | 12 to ultimate development factor for paid claims gross of salvage & subrogation |

- There is no development beyond 36 months.

## Part a (1.75 points)

Estimate the salvage and subrogation recoverable for accident year 2018 using the ratio approach. Justify the selected ultimate salvage and subrogation ratio.

### Answer
Approximately $4,750 thousand

### Explanation
The ratio approach develops the **ratio** of received salvage and subrogation to paid claims gross of S&S, rather than developing S&S dollars directly, and then applies the selected ultimate ratio to ultimate gross paid claims.

**Step 1 — the ratio triangle.**

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 0.0657 | 0.1884 | 0.2208 |
| 2016 | 0.0621 | 0.1830 | 0.2210 |
| 2017 | 0.0649 | 0.1864 | |
| 2018 | 0.0470 | | |

**Step 2 — develop the ratios.**

| Age | Factors | Selected |
|---|---|---|
| 12–24 | $2.8665$, $2.9463$, $2.8737$ | $2.8955$ |
| 24–36 | $1.1723$, $1.2079$ | $1.1901$ |
| 36–Ult | no development beyond 36 months | $1.0000$ |

**Step 3 — projected ultimate ratios, and the selection.**

| AY | Projected ultimate ratio |
|---|---|
| 2015 | $0.2208$ — at 36 months, fully developed |
| 2016 | $0.2210$ — at 36 months, fully developed |
| 2017 | $0.1864 \times 1.1901 = 0.2218$ |
| 2018 | $0.0470 \times 2.8955 \times 1.1901 = 0.1618$ |

**Justification for the selection.** The three older accident years land on an extremely tight cluster — 0.2208, 0.2210 and 0.2218 — and two of them are fully developed rather than projected, so that level is well established. Accident year 2018's projection of 0.1618 is far below it, but it rests entirely on a single 12-month ratio of 0.0470 that is itself out of line with the 0.062–0.066 seen at 12 months in every prior year. At that immaturity the ratio is heavily leveraged: the 12-to-ultimate factor is roughly 3.4, so the low reading is magnified more than threefold. The more reasonable reading is a random fluctuation, or a timing lag in recording recoveries, rather than a genuine change in recovery levels. **Select 0.221**, consistent with the mature years.

**Step 4 — the recoverable.** Note the question asks for the *recoverable* — what is still to be collected — not ultimate S&S:

$$\text{Ultimate gross paid} = 18{,}100 \times 1.40 = \$25{,}340 \text{ thousand}$$

$$\text{Ultimate S\&S} = 25{,}340 \times 0.221 = \$5{,}600 \text{ thousand}$$

$$\text{S\&S recoverable} = 5{,}600 - 850 = \$4{,}750 \text{ thousand}$$

A candidate who instead judged the low 2018 ratio to be a real shift in recovery experience and carried the indicated 0.1618 forward would obtain $25{,}340 \times 0.1618 - 850 \approx \$3{,}251$ thousand. Either selection earned full credit provided it was justified.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to apply the development method to the ratios of salvage and subrogation to paid claims. Credit was also given for recognizing that the 2015 and 2016 ultimate ratios were fully developed and could be used to select the 2018 ultimate without building ratio development tables — but justification of the selection was required for full credit.

Common mistakes included calculating ultimate S&S but not the recoverable; developing S&S dollars directly rather than the ratios; and calculating ultimate losses from a paid development triangle instead of using the stated 12-to-ultimate factor.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly discuss two advantages of the ratio approach as compared to the development approach.

### Explanation
**1. The ratios are a diagnostic that supports judgment.** Expressing recoveries as a ratio to paid claims produces a quantity that should be stable across accident years, so an unreasonable value stands out immediately — as accident year 2018's did in part a — and a more appropriate ratio can be selected in its place. Developing S&S dollars directly gives no comparable benchmark against which to judge a year.

**2. The ratio development factors are less leveraged and less volatile.** Received salvage and subrogation is small and erratic at early maturities, so link ratios built on S&S dollars alone are highly leveraged and swing widely. Ratios to paid claims move with the underlying claim volume, so their development factors are more stable and a small dollar fluctuation is not magnified as severely.

A third advantage is that the method explicitly recognizes the relationship between salvage and subrogation and **paid claims** — recoveries arise out of claims paid, so tying the estimate to paid claims reflects the actual driver of recoveries.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know two advantages of the ratio approach over development of salvage and subrogation dollars. Common mistakes included listing only one reason, identifying a relationship between S&S and "claims" or "losses" without specifying *paid* claims, and asserting that the ratio approach adjusts for changes in the mix of business.
