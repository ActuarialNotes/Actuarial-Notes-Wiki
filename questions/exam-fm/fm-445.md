An investor owns two bonds, Bond 1 and Bond 2. At today's annual effective interest rate, the bonds have the following characteristics:

|  | Bond 1 | Bond 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Present Value | 20,400 | 20,400 |
| Modified Duration (in years) | 11.735 | 13.101 |

If the annual effective interest rate decreases by 50 basis points today, the first-order Macaulay approximations of the present value for Bond 1 and Bond 2 are 21,635.83 and $X$, respectively.

Calculate $X$.

- A) 21,503.55
- B) 21,596.97
- C) 21,736.30
- D) 21,779.69
- E) 21,784.47

## Explanation

The first-order Macaulay approximation of the present value at a new rate $i$, from today's rate $i_0$, is

$$P(i) \approx P(i_0)\left(\frac{1+i_0}{1+i}\right)^{D_{MAC}}$$

where the Macaulay duration is $D_{MAC} = (1+i_0)D_{MOD}$. Today's rate is not given, so use Bond 1 to find information about $\dfrac{1+i_0}{1+i}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
21{,}635.83 &= 20{,}400\left(\frac{1+i_0}{1+i}\right)^{11.735(1+i_0)} \\
\left(\frac{1+i_0}{1+i}\right)^{11.735(1+i_0)} &= 1.060579902
\end{align*}
$$

Bond 2's exponent is $13.101(1+i_0)$, which is $13.101/11.735$ times Bond 1's. Next,

$$
\begin{align*}
\left(\frac{1+i_0}{1+i}\right)^{13.101(1+i_0)} &= 1.060579902^{13.101/11.735} \\
&= 1.067865963
\end{align*}
$$

The first-order Macaulay approximation for Bond 2 is:

$$
\begin{align*}
X &= 20{,}400\left(\frac{1+i_0}{1+i}\right)^{13.101(1+i_0)} \\
  &= 20{,}400(1.067865963) \\
  &= 21{,}784.47
\end{align*}
$$
