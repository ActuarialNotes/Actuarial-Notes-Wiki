Each of the 76 employees of a company is either a low-risk or a high-risk employee. This year, each low-risk employee's number of accidents has standard deviation 0.50 and each high-risk employee's number of accidents has standard deviation 5.50. The standard deviation of the total number of accidents for all the employees is 43.00. The frequencies of accidents among the employees are independent.

Calculate the number of low-risk employees.

- A) $1$
- B) $15$
- C) $38$
- D) $61$
- E) $75$

## Explanation

Work in variances, since those are what add for independent variables. Squaring each standard deviation:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{low-risk variance} &= 0.50^2 = 0.25 \\
\text{high-risk variance} &= 5.50^2 = 30.25 \\
\text{total variance} &= 43.00^2 = 1849
\end{align*}
$$

Let $n$ be the number of low-risk employees, so $76 - n$ are high-risk:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.25n + 30.25(76 - n) &= 1849 \\
0.25n + 2299 - 30.25n &= 1849 \\
-30n &= -450 \\
n &= \mathbf{15}
\end{align*}
$$

Checking: $15(0.25) + 61(30.25) = 3.75 + 1845.25 = 1849$, as required.

The high-risk employees dominate the total: they carry 99.8% of the variance despite being 80% of the headcount, because variance scales with the *square* of the standard deviation and $5.5/0.5 = 11$ becomes a factor of 121. Working with the standard deviations directly would give a badly wrong count.
