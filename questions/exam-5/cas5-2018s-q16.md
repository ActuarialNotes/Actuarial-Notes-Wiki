Given the following transactional data:

| Claim ID | Accident Date | Transaction Date | Amount Paid on Transaction Date | Ending Case Outstanding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | October 31, 2014 | February 1, 2015 | 200 | 300 |
|  |  | July 31, 2015 | 300 | 0 |
| B | April 28, 2015 | July 1, 2015 | 0 | 1000 |
|  |  | October 15, 2015 | 500 | 500 |
|  |  | August 28, 2016 | 500 | 200 |
| C | June 1, 2015 | July 7, 2015 | 100 | 650 |
|  |  | January 15, 2016 | 250 | 400 |
|  |  | May 15, 2017 | 300 | 0 |
| D | September 24, 2015 | March 1, 2016 | 0 | 325 |
|  |  | December 19, 2017 | 375 | 250 |

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate accident year 2015 paid claims as of:

- i. 12 months
- ii. 24 months
- iii. 36 months

### Explanation
Accident year 2015 contains claims B, C and D; claim A occurred in 2014. Every accident year is evaluated on the same calendar basis, so 12 months is December 31, 2015, 24 months December 31, 2016 and 36 months December 31, 2017. Paid claims are the cumulative payments made by each date:

| Claim | By 12/31/2015 | By 12/31/2016 | By 12/31/2017 |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | 500 | 500 + 500 = 1,000 | 1,000 |
| C | 100 | 100 + 250 = 350 | 350 + 300 = 650 |
| D | 0 | 0 | 375 |
| **Total** | **600** | **1,350** | **2,025** |

Paid claims: i. 600; ii. 1,350; iii. 2,025.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the definition of reported claims as well as how to aggregate claims by accident year as of different development periods.

Candidates were expected to aggregate paid claims by accident year as of different development periods.

Common errors included:

- Calculating the development periods from the accident date of each claim rather than assuming the same basis for all claims (January-December). For example, claim B (accident date April 28, 2015) would be as of 12 months of development at December 31, 2015 and not April 28, 2016.
- Including claim A, which does not fall in accident year 2015.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Calculate accident year 2015 reported claims as of:

- i. 12 months
- ii. 24 months
- iii. 36 months

### Explanation
Reported claims are cumulative paid plus the case outstanding at the evaluation date (the ending case of the claim's latest transaction on or before it). Claim D was not reported until March 1, 2016, so it contributes nothing at 12 months; claim B had no transaction in 2017, so its 2016 case of 200 still stands at 36 months.

| Claim | 12/31/2015 | 12/31/2016 | 12/31/2017 |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | 500 + 500 = 1,000 | 1,000 + 200 = 1,200 | 1,000 + 200 = 1,200 |
| C | 100 + 650 = 750 | 350 + 400 = 750 | 650 + 0 = 650 |
| D | 0 | 0 + 325 = 325 | 375 + 250 = 625 |
| **Total** | **1,750** | **2,275** | **2,475** |

Reported claims: i. 1,750; ii. 2,275; iii. 2,475.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the definition of reported claims as well as how to aggregate claims by accident year as of different development periods.

Common errors included:

- Calculating the development periods from the accident date of each claim rather than assuming the same basis for all claims (January-December). For example, claim B (accident date April 28, 2015) would be as of 12 months of development at December 31, 2015 and not April 28, 2016.
- Considering claim A, which does not fall in accident year 2015.
- Not taking into account the outstanding amount from 2016 on claim B as no transaction occurred on this claim in 2017.
- Incorrectly calculating the reported claims either by adding a variation of reserve to the cumulative paid claim or by adding a reserve as of a certain development period to the incremental paid claim.
