![[Media/Figures/Stakeholder_Reporting.svg|340]]

**Stakeholder Reporting** is the reporting of [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claim estimates]] and their changes to the parties who make decisions on them. Friedland names three: internal management, investors and regulators. Each relies on the estimate for different decisions:

1. **Internal management** uses it in pricing, underwriting, reinsurance purchasing, claims management, capital allocation and the choice of where to grow and which markets to exit.
2. **Investors** use the financial statements built on it. The estimate determines reported earnings and surplus, and with them the financial metrics by which investors judge the insurer's strength.
3. **[[Regulatory Reporting|Regulators]]** use the financial statements to supervise the insurer's financial position and solvency.

Rating agencies and auditors also read the estimate, and a finding about case adequacy or settlement rates is reported to the claims department whose practices it describes. The substance reported is the same for every audience. The depth and framing are adapted to each.

> [!example]- An insurer's unpaid claims are understated. How does that mislead its investors? {Example}
> - Understated liabilities overstate earnings and surplus.
> - The insurer then presents itself in a stronger position than it truly is, so investors may value it above its worth and invest capital they otherwise would not.
> - When the reserves are later strengthened, the correction falls on that period's earnings, and investors suffer the loss in earnings and share value.

## What each stakeholder needs

| Stakeholder | What the report gives |
| --- | --- |
| Management and the board | The change by accident year and by cause, whether the current selection is adequate, and what the finding means for pricing and underwriting |
| Investors and rating agencies | The development of prior estimates and its effect on reported earnings, with enough about its cause to tell a one-off from a continuing trend |
| Regulators | The statutory Annual Statement with [[Schedule P]], and the [[Statement of Actuarial Opinion]] with its supporting [[Actuarial Report|actuarial report]] |
| Claims and underwriting | The findings about [[Case Adequacy|case adequacy]], [[Settlement Rate|settlement rates]] or claim mix that came from their practices |

> [!example]- $\$5{,}000{,}000$ of adverse development has emerged on accident years 2021–2023, driven by higher severity on litigated general liability claims. What does each stakeholder need to be told? {Example}
> - Management needs the $\$5$M by accident year and cause, whether the current estimate should be strengthened further, and that today's rates rest on a severity assumption that is too low.
> - Investors need the effect on reported earnings and whether the higher severity is a one-off or a continuing trend.
> - Regulators need the development shown in Schedule P and an opinion and actuarial report that reflect the revised estimate and identify the changed assumptions.
> - The claims department needs the finding on litigated severity, with the case adequacy and settlement rate reviews that ruled other causes out.
