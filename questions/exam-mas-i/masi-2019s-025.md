A type of machine has a lifetime following an exponential distribution with mean $\mu$. All machine lifetimes are independent.

To estimate the expected lifetime of this machine, the factory measures lifetimes of 4 sample machines and takes the average excluding the lowest and highest values. Denote this estimate $\hat{\mu}$.

Calculate the ratio $E(\hat{\mu})/\mu$.

- A) Less than 0.8
- B) At least 0.8, but less than 1.0
- C) At least 1.0, but less than 1.2
- D) At least 1.2, but less than 1.4
- E) At least 1.4

## Explanation

The estimator is the average of the second and third order statistics of four exponential lifetimes, so we need their expected values. By memorylessness, the minimum of $m$ independent exponentials with mean $\mu$ is exponential with mean $\mu/m$, and once it occurs the remaining lifetimes start afresh. The gaps between successive order statistics therefore have means $\mu/4, \mu/3, \mu/2, \mu$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X_{(1)}] &= \frac{\mu}{4} \\
E[X_{(2)}] &= \frac{\mu}{4} + \frac{\mu}{3} \\
&= \frac{7\mu}{12} \\
E[X_{(3)}] &= \frac{7\mu}{12} + \frac{\mu}{2} \\
&= \frac{13\mu}{12}
\end{align*}
$$

Excluding the lowest and highest values leaves $\hat{\mu} = \frac{1}{2}\left(X_{(2)} + X_{(3)}\right)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E(\hat{\mu}) &= \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{7\mu}{12} + \frac{13\mu}{12}\right) \\
&= \frac{5\mu}{6}
\end{align*}
$$

So $E(\hat{\mu})/\mu = 5/6 = 0.833$ — trimming the long right tail biases the estimate low. This falls in $[0.8, 1.0)$, so the answer is **B**.
