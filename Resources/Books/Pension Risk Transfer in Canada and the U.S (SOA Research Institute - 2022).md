A February 2022 report prepared to provide an overview of pension risk transfer for use in the educational curriculum of the Society of Actuaries. It presents general principles and highlights major differences between Canada and the U.S. from a life insurer's perspective, focusing on group annuities, which it calls the most common solution for pension risk transfer. Other solutions, such as longevity insurance in Canada, are outside its scope.

## 1 Client Need
- Plan sponsors of defined benefit pension plans promise plan members a lifetime pension, and in a traditional plan the sponsor bears the risk of providing it.
- There are two types of group annuities: buy-out, where the life insurer pays plan members directly, and buy-in, where the insurer makes a bulk payment to the pension fund, which continues to pay members.
- Both types transfer the risks of paying the covered members to the life insurer; with a buy-in the sponsor keeps the operational risk of paying members.
- Buy-out annuities are more common than buy-in annuities in the U.S., where sponsors stop paying Pension Benefit Guarantee Corporation premiums for the members included in a buy-out.

## 2 Group Annuity Market
- The Canadian market grew from $2.7 billion in 2016 to $7.7 billion in 2021, and the U.S. market from $14.1 billion to $38.1 billion.
- As of early 2022 there were 8 life insurers active in the Canadian group annuity market and 19 in the U.S. market.
- A sponsor usually engages an actuarial consultant to run a competitive bidding process, starting from a request for quotation that specifies the members covered, the type of annuity, the tranching structure, and the quotation, premium payment and first payment dates.
- The weeks between notification of a win and receipt of the premium expose the insurer to market movements, so a mechanism adjusting the price between the quote date and the premium payment date is sometimes used.

## 3 The Policy / Contract
- The policy is between the insurer and the plan sponsor, and for buy-out annuities each annuitant is sent a certificate documenting their benefit entitlement.
- A buy-out annuity mirrors the benefits the pension plan provides, such as CPI-linked, temporary or integrated benefits; a buy-in annuity usually does, but could provide lower benefits.
- A group annuity that includes deferred annuitants must outline their options, such as commuting their pension, when they can retire, the early retirement penalty and the forms of pension.

## 4 Pricing Considerations
- The main pricing assumptions are expected asset return, expected asset defaults, the longevity base table and improvement scale, acquisition and maintenance expenses, annuitant behavior, provisions for adverse deviation and the profitability level.
- The report gives basic considerations for assets, longevity, liquidity requirements, market risk margins, data risk margins, annuitant behavior, commissions and the impact of reinsurance and offshore structures.

## 5 Risk Considerations
- The main risks mirror the assumptions: investment (credit and/or market), interest rate, [[Longevity Risk|longevity]], annuitant behavior and operational.
- Each risk is paired with approaches to mitigate it; for longevity, one is to rely on reinsurance to reduce the insurer's longevity risk.
