## [F-001] MLE's large-sample properties stated without regularity conditions; the page's own uniform example violates them
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-10-04T20:47Z/8861
- date: 2026-10-05
- severity: major
- status: open
- locus: bullet 'Methods', line 26
- claim: Maximum Likelihood Estimation maximizes l(theta); it is consistent, asymptotically normal and efficient, and invariant under transformation.
- evidence: Consistency, asymptotic normality N(theta, 1/(nI(theta))) and asymptotic efficiency hold only under regularity conditions: Stanford STATS 200 Lecture 14, Theorem 14.1 (pp.14-1-14-2) requires a common support for every f(x|theta), an interior theta0, a differentiable log-likelihood and a unique root of l'(theta) = 0; PSU STAT 415 Lesson 7 §7.1: 'subject to some regularity conditions'; Loss Data Analytics App. C §17.2.2: 'under certain regularity conditions'. The page's own first example, Uniform(0, theta), breaks them: Siegrist §6.3 (point/Likelihood.html) gives mse(X_(n)) = 2 theta^2/((n+1)(n+2)) - order 1/n^2, not a normal law with variance 1/(nI) - and calls the unbiased version 'super efficient'; Siegrist §6.5 (point/Unbiased.html) says the Cramer-Rao assumption is not satisfied for the uniform because the support depends on the parameter. Invariance is fine (Siegrist §6.3 invariance theorem). Same root as Efficiency F-002.
- source_rank: 3
- proposed_action: Qualify: 'under regularity conditions (support not depending on theta, smooth likelihood, interior maximum) it is consistent, asymptotically normal and efficient', and point to the uniform example below as the standard exception. Not applied - the fix is new wording.
- applied: false

- note: Methods bullet rewritten: MLE 'maximizes l(theta) and is invariant under transformation. Under regularity conditions - every f(x|theta) has the same support, the true theta is an interior point, the log-likelihood is differentiable in theta, and theta-hat is the unique root of l'(theta) = 0 - it is also consistent, asymptotically normal and asymptotically efficient. The uniform example below breaks the first condition: the support (0, theta) moves with theta, and the MLE X_(n) has MSE 2theta^2/[(n+1)(n+2)], shrinking like 1/n^2 rather than the 1/n of the regular case.' The conditions are the sources' own: Stanford STATS 200 Lecture 14 Theorem 14.1 (pp.14-1-14-2): 'Suppose certain regularity conditions hold, including: all PDFs/PMFs f(x|theta) in the model have the same support; theta0 is an interior point of Omega; the log-likelihood l(theta) is differentiable in theta; theta-hat is the unique value of theta that solves 0 = l'(theta)', then theta-hat is consistent and asymptotically normal, sqrt(n)(theta-hat - theta0) -> N(0, 1/I(theta0)); Lecture 15 p.15-4: the MLE 'achieves this lower bound asymptotically ... asymptotically efficient'; PSU STAT 415 Lesson 7 §7.1 'subject to some regularity conditions' (smooth f, MLE not on the edge of allowable values); Loss Data Analytics App. C §17.2.2 'under certain regularity conditions'. The uniform: Siegrist, Random §6.3 (point/Likelihood.html) mse(X_(n)) = 2h^2/((n+1)(n+2)) and an estimator whose MSE 'decreases on the order of 1/n^2' is 'super efficient'; §6.5 (point/Unbiased.html) the Cramer-Rao assumption fails because the support depends on the parameter. Recomputed: at n = 5, 2theta^2/42 = theta^2/21, the page's example value. Invariance kept (Siegrist §6.3 invariance theorem; PSU L7 equivariance). Wording matches the Efficiency and Minimum Variance pages' fixes.
