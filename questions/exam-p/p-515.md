Losses, $X$, in millions, are modeled by a distribution with density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
k(1-x)^3, & \text{for } 0 < x < 1 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $k$ is a constant.

Claim payments are limited to a maximum of 225,000.

Calculate the median claim payment.

- A) $159{,}000$
- B) $173{,}000$
- C) $187{,}000$
- D) $200{,}000$
- E) $225{,}000$

## Explanation

Normalize the density first:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= \int_0^1 k(1-x)^3dx = \frac{k}{4} \\
k &= 4
\end{align*}
$$

so the distribution function is

$$
F(x) = 1 - (1-x)^4
$$

Find the median **loss** by setting $F(x) = 0.5$:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 - (1-x)^4 &= 0.5 \\
(1-x)^4 &= 0.5 \\
1 - x &= 0.5^{1/4} = 0.84090 \\
x &= 0.1591
\end{align*}
$$

Losses are in millions, so the median loss is 159,100.

Now check the cap. Because 159,100 is **below** the 225,000 maximum, that loss is paid in full and the payment cap does not bind at the median:

$$
\text{median payment} = \mathbf{159{,}000}
$$

Choice (E), 225,000, is the cap itself — the answer if more than half of losses exceeded it. The density is heavily concentrated near zero, so in fact $F(0.225) = 1 - (0.775)^4 = 0.64$: about 64% of losses fall below the cap, which is why the median sits comfortably underneath it. Verifying that ordering is the step that decides between (A) and (E).
