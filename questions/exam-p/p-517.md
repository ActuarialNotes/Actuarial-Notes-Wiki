An insurance policy provides coverage for two types of claims. The numbers of annual claims of Type I and Type II are denoted by $X$ and $Y$, respectively.

The joint probability function of $X$ and $Y$ is given in the following table.

| $x$ \ $y$ | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.31 | $p$ | 0.05 |
| 1 | 0.21 | 0.13 | $q$ |

A Type I claim costs 750, and a Type II claim costs 1000. The expected annual Type I claims cost is 345.

Calculate the variance of $Y$.

- A) $0.25$
- B) $0.57$
- C) $0.67$
- D) $0.75$
- E) $0.82$

## Explanation

Two unknowns, so two equations are needed.

**The cost gives $E[X]$.** Since $X$ takes only the values 0 and 1, its mean is $P[X=1]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
750E[X] &= 345 \\
E[X] &= 0.46 = P[X = 1]
\end{align*}
$$

The bottom row must therefore sum to 0.46:

$$
0.21 + 0.13 + q = 0.46 \implies q = 0.12
$$

**The table must total 1**, which gives $p$:

$$
0.31 + p + 0.05 + 0.21 + 0.13 + 0.12 = 1 \implies p = 0.18
$$

Now sum down the columns for the marginal distribution of $Y$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[Y=0] &= 0.31 + 0.21 = 0.52 \\
P[Y=1] &= 0.18 + 0.13 = 0.31 \\
P[Y=2] &= 0.05 + 0.12 = 0.17
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y]   &= 0(0.52) + 1(0.31) + 2(0.17) = 0.65 \\
E[Y^2] &= 0(0.52) + 1(0.31) + 4(0.17) = 0.99
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(Y) &= 0.99 - (0.65)^2 \\
              &= \mathbf{0.5675}
\end{align*}
$$

The Type II cost of 1000 is never used — it is there to make the two claim types look symmetric. Only the Type I cost carries information, because it is what pins down $q$.
