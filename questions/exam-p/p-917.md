The aggregate claims $S$ for a compound Poisson process have Poisson claim count with mean 5. Each individual claim amount follows an exponential distribution with mean 4, and claim amounts are independent of one another and of the number of claims.

Calculate the standard deviation of $S$.

- A) $\sqrt{80}$
- B) $\sqrt{160}$
- C) $\sqrt{320}$
- D) $\sqrt{400}$
- E) $\sqrt{500}$

## Explanation

Let $N$ be the number of claims and $X$ a single claim amount, so $S = X_1 + \dots + X_N$. From the given distributions:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[N] &= \text{Var}(N) = 5 \\
E[X] &= 4, \quad E[X^2] = 2 \times 4^2 = 32 \\
\text{Var}(X) &= 32 - 4^2 = 16
\end{align*}
$$

Given $N = n$, $S$ is a sum of $n$ independent claims, so $E[S \mid N] = 4N$ and $\text{Var}(S \mid N) = 16N$. By the law of total variance:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(S) &= E[\text{Var}(S \mid N)] + \text{Var}(E[S \mid N]) \\
              &= E[16N] + \text{Var}(4N) \\
              &= 16(5) + 16(5) \\
              &= 160
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{SD}(S) &= \mathbf{\sqrt{160}} \\
             &\approx 12.65
\end{align*}
$$

Equivalently, since a Poisson count has $E[N] = \text{Var}(N) = 5$, $\text{Var}(S) = 5\,(\text{Var}(X) + E[X]^2) = 5\,E[X^2] = 5 \times 32 = 160$. Dropping the $\text{Var}(N)E[X]^2$ term leaves only $5 \times 16 = 80$, which is option A.
