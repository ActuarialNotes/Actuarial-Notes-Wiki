The Alberta Automobile Insurance Advisory Committee's October 2020 report to the Minister of Finance, recommending that Alberta abandon its tort-based automobile compensation system for a **pure [[No-Fault Insurance|no-fault]] model**. The syllabus assigned **pp. 14–42** — the executive summary and the summary of conclusions and recommendations.

## The argument, in the committee's terms

- **Cost and premium instability.** Alberta premium growth persistently outran inflation; the committee attributed the bulk of it to the cost of settling and litigating bodily-injury claims rather than to the medical care actually delivered.
- **Piecemeal reform does not hold.** The 2004 [[Minor Injury Guideline|minor injury]] cap on non-pecuniary damages and the Diagnostic and Treatment Protocols Regulation (DTPR) narrowed the gap for a time; the committee found the divergence between intent and result reopened.
- **Health outcomes favour no-fault.** The peer-reviewed evidence the committee reviewed — including the Quebec whiplash experience and New South Wales — associated tort systems (and legal involvement) with slower recovery and more claimed impairment.
- **Deterrence no longer justifies tort.** With compulsory insurance and near-universal coverage, the committee found the deterrent effect of a tort action weak relative to its transaction cost.
- **Recommendation.** Replace tort with a pure no-fault care and compensation model, delivered by an administrative body with medical review panels rather than duelling experts, and consolidate rate oversight in the AIRB.
