![[FSRA Risk Management - Cover.svg]]

[[Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario|FSRA]]'s **Information Guidance**, effective 20 September 2022, on how insurers should manage the operational risk of the *models* they use to rate and underwrite Ontario automobile insurance. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective A1.

## What kind of guidance this is

FSRA classifies it as **Information Guidance**: it describes FSRA's views and expectations but creates no new compliance obligation. That classification is itself examinable — it is the mechanism by which a principles-based regulator sets expectations without writing a rule.

## Scope — three model types

| Model type | Definition in the guidance |
|---|---|
| **Ratemaking** | Any model used to estimate future costs associated with the transfer of risk — in total, and by the components that build up to the total |
| **Risk classification system** | Any model used to assign risks to groups based on the expected future cost or benefit of the coverage |
| **Underwriting** | Any model used to decide whether a risk should be written, renewed or cancelled |

Defining underwriting models into scope is the notable move: an insurer can produce an unobjectionable rate filing and still reach an [[Unfair Discrimination|unfairly discriminatory]] outcome through the models that decide who gets a policy at all.

## What it expects

An operational risk management framework covering the full model lifecycle — development, validation, approval, deployment, monitoring and retirement — with accountability for each stage, documentation proportionate to the model's materiality, and independent challenge. The appendix sets out **foundational** and **sound** practices for each of the three model types.

## Where it fits

The guidance is part of FSRA's programme to move Ontario auto rate and underwriting regulation toward **principles-based, outcomes-focused** supervision, building on the Standard Filing Guidance and the Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices. It is the market-conduct counterpart to what [[OSFI]] does prudentially, and it complements [[CIA Models]] (the actuary's professional obligation when using a model) and [[CIA Reliance]] (the actuary's position when the model is someone else's).
