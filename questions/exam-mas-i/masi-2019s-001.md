Cars arrive according to a Poisson process at a rate of two per hour.

Calculate the probability that in a given hour, exactly one car will arrive during the first ten minutes and no other cars will arrive during that hour.

- A) Less than 0.03
- B) At least 0.03 but less than 0.06
- C) At least 0.06 but less than 0.09
- D) At least 0.09 but less than 0.12
- E) At least 0.12

## Explanation

Split the hour into the first ten minutes and the remaining fifty. A Poisson process has independent increments, so the two pieces are independent Poisson counts with means proportional to their lengths:

$$
\begin{align*}
N(1/6) &\sim \text{Poisson}\left(2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{6}\right) = \text{Poisson}(1/3) \\
N(1) - N(1/6) &\sim \text{Poisson}\left(2 \cdot \tfrac{5}{6}\right) = \text{Poisson}(5/3)
\end{align*}
$$

We need exactly one arrival in the first piece and none in the second:

$$
\begin{align*}
\Pr(\text{event}) &= \left(\tfrac{1}{3}e^{-1/3}\right)\left(e^{-5/3}\right) \\
&= \tfrac{1}{3}e^{-2} \\
&= 0.0451
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[0.03, 0.06)$, so the answer is **B**.
