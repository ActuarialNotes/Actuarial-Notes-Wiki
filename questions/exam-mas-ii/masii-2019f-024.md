Given the following types of Bayesian model diagnostic tools:

I. Leave one out cross validation
II. WAIC
III. Counterfactual plots

Determine which of the above diagnostics tools can rely on data not included in the training data set.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

The question turns on where each tool's inputs come from.

**I — leave-one-out cross validation.** Every observation used is a training observation; the method simply holds each one out in turn and scores it against a model fitted to the rest. Nothing outside the training set enters. It *estimates* out-of-sample performance, but it does so entirely from in-sample data.

**II — WAIC.** Computed from the log pointwise predictive density evaluated at the training observations, with a penalty for the effective number of parameters. Like LOO, it is an in-sample estimate of out-of-sample deviance and touches no external data.

**III — counterfactual plots.** These show the model's predicted response across a *chosen* range of a predictor while holding the other predictors fixed. The covariate values plotted are made up for the purpose — they need not correspond to any observation in the training data, and typically don't. That is the whole point: asking what the model implies for combinations that were never observed.

Only counterfactual plots rely on data outside the training set, so the answer is **C**.
