For which of the exponential, normal, and continuous uniform distributions does doubling the mean also double the median?

- A) all three
- B) all but the normal
- C) all but the uniform
- D) all but the exponential
- E) fewer than two

## Explanation

Check each family in turn.

**Normal and uniform.** Both are symmetric about their centre, so the median equals the mean. Doubling the mean therefore doubles the median outright.

**Exponential.** With mean $\theta$ the distribution function is $F(x) = 1 - e^{-x/\theta}$. Setting $F(m) = 0.5$:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 - e^{-m/\theta} &= 0.5 \\
e^{-m/\theta}     &= 0.5 \\
m                 &= \theta \ln 2
\end{align*}
$$

The median is a *fixed multiple* of the mean, so doubling $\theta$ doubles $m$ as well.

All three therefore have the property, and the answer is **(A)**.

The distinction the question is probing is between the median *equalling* the mean and the median *scaling with* the mean. Only the first fails for the exponential; the second is what is being asked, and it holds for any distribution whose parameter is a pure scale parameter. Choice (D) is the trap for anyone who stops at "the exponential's mean and median differ".
