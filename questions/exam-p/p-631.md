The joint probability function of $X$ and $Y$ is given by:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X = 1, Y = 0] &= \frac{1}{8} \\
P[X = 2, Y = 0] &= \frac{1}{4} \\
P[X = 1, Y = 1] &= \frac{1}{2} \\
P[X = 2, Y = 1] &= \frac{1}{8}
\end{align*}
$$

Calculate $\text{Var}(Y \mid X = 1)$.

- A) $\dfrac{4}{25}$
- B) $\dfrac{15}{64}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- D) $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- E) $\dfrac{4}{5}$

## Explanation

The two cells with $X = 1$ carry total probability $\frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{8}$, so

$$
P[Y = 0 \mid X = 1] = \frac{1/8}{1/8 + 1/2} = \frac{1}{5}, \qquad P[Y = 1 \mid X = 1] = \frac{4}{5}
$$

Then

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y \mid X = 1]   &= 0\left(\tfrac{1}{5}\right) + 1\left(\tfrac{4}{5}\right) = \tfrac{4}{5} \\
E[Y^2 \mid X = 1] &= 0^2\left(\tfrac{1}{5}\right) + 1^2\left(\tfrac{4}{5}\right) = \tfrac{4}{5} \\
\text{Var}[Y \mid X = 1] &= \tfrac{4}{5} - \left(\tfrac{4}{5}\right)^2 = \mathbf{\tfrac{4}{25}}
\end{align*}
$$

Faster: conditional on $X = 1$, $Y$ is Bernoulli with $p = 4/5$, so the variance is $p(1-p) = \frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{5} = \frac{4}{25}$.

Choice (B), $15/64$, is the *unconditional* $\text{Var}(Y)$: the marginal $P[Y = 1] = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$ gives $\frac{5}{8}\cdot\frac{3}{8} = \frac{15}{64}$. Conditioning on $X = 1$ pushes $Y$ closer to certainty, which is why the conditional variance is the smaller of the two.
