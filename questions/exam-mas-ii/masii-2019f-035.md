You assume that the number of claims for a single policy, $Y$, follows a Poisson distribution with mean parameter $\lambda$.

$$
f(Y \mid \lambda) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{Y}}{Y!} \quad \text{for } Y \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\}
$$

We initially choose an exponential prior for $\lambda$ with mean 7. You find that the mean number of claims per policy in your dataset is 2, and the mode of the posterior distribution of $\lambda$ is 2.1.

You are considering 3 possible alternative prior distributions.

I. Gamma with a mean of 7 and variance of 7
II. Exponential with a mean of 5
III. Uniform between 0 and 2

Determine which of the above alternative prior distributions will increase the mode (relative to the current mode) of the posterior distribution of $\lambda$.

- A) None
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

> [!note] Editor's note
> CAS publishes only the answer key for this paper (E), with no worked solution. With $n$ policies averaging 2 claims, a gamma prior with shape $\alpha$ and rate $\beta$ gives a $\text{Gamma}(\alpha + 2n,\ \beta + n)$ posterior, whose mode is $\frac{\alpha + 2n - 1}{\beta + n}$. The current exponential prior ($\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 1/7$) gives a mode of $\frac{2n}{n + 1/7}$, which is below 2, so the stated 2.1 cannot come from this model and the question has to be answered taking 2.1 as given.

> [!note] Editor's note
> I: a mean of 7 and a variance of 7 give $\alpha = 7$ and $\beta = 1$, so the mode is $\frac{2n + 6}{n + 1} = 2 + \frac{4}{n + 1}$, above 2.1 for any $n$ below 39, and the mode increases. II: an exponential with mean 5 ($\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 1/5$) gives $\frac{2n}{n + 1/5}$, lower than under the current prior, because the larger rate pulls the mode down.

> [!note] Editor's note
> III: a uniform prior on $[0, 2]$ truncates the posterior at 2, so its mode is 2, below the stated 2.1 (on the model as stated, whose mode is below 2, it would instead raise the mode). Taking 2.1 as given, only I increases the mode; "I only" is not among (A) to (D), so the answer is E.
