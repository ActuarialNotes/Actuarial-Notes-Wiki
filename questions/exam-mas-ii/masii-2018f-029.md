You are given the following possible reasons to use a Bayesian multi-level model.

- I. To improve estimates for repeat sampling
- II. To improve estimates for imbalance in sampling
- III. To avoid averaging

Determine which of the above reasons are valid.

- A) None are valid
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

The standard case for multilevel models (McElreath, *Statistical Rethinking*) rests on partial pooling: each cluster's estimate borrows strength from the others, in proportion to how little data the cluster has of its own. The reasons usually given are improved estimates for repeat sampling, improved estimates for imbalance in sampling, estimates of variation, and avoiding averaging.

**I is valid.** When several observations come from the same individual, location or time, a single-level model either pools everything (ignoring the clusters) or pools nothing (fitting each cluster alone). A multilevel model sits between the two and gives better estimates than either.

**II is valid.** When some clusters are sampled much more heavily than others, partial pooling shrinks the thinly sampled clusters further towards the population mean. The sparse clusters are not overfit, and the heavily sampled ones do not dominate.

**III is valid.** Averaging the data within clusters to feed a single-level model throws away variation and manufactures false confidence. A multilevel model uses the raw observations and keeps the uncertainty.

All three are valid. "I, II and III" is not among (A)–(D), so the answer is **E**.
