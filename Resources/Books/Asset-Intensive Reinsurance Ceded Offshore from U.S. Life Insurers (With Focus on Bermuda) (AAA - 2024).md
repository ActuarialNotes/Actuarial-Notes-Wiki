An American Academy of Actuaries issue brief, dated February 2024, on the significant increase in asset-intensive reinsurance (AIR) ceded by U.S.-domiciled life insurers to Bermuda-based entities. It offers a summary of motivations, common practices and relevant actuarial guidance for U.S.-based actuaries involved in these reinsurance transactions. It focuses solely on reinsurance to Bermuda-based entities, though some of the considerations may also apply to other jurisdictions, and is not a comprehensive comparison of the U.S. and Bermudian regulatory regimes.

## Motivations for Reinsuring U.S. Asset-Intensive Reinsurance to Bermuda-Based Reinsurers

## Actuarial Guidance for U.S.-based Actuaries
- Asset Adequacy Testing Considerations
    - The cedant's appointed actuary is responsible for opining on the adequacy of reserves, and this responsibility is undiminished by the presence of AIR programs.
- AAT Approaches for Ceded Business
- Leveraging the Reinsurer's SBA
- Performing Traditional Cash Flow Testing
- Evaluating Counterparty Risk
- Collateral Requirements (Regulatory and Treaty)
    - The most common forms of collateral are funds withheld, a collateral trust and a letter of credit (LOC).
- Aggregation
- Recapture/Termination Provisions
- Experience Refunds
- Data and Other Limitations
- Subsequent Retrocession (Out of Bermuda)
- Addressing Inadequacy
