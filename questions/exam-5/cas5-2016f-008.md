Given the following information about an insurance product:

- The product launched on January 1, 2012.
- All policies are annual.
- The rating algorithm is exposures multiplied by a fixed manual rate.
- The average written manual rate per exposure in 2013 = \$5,000.
- Exposures are written uniformly throughout the year.
- A large loss of \$2 million occurred and was paid in 2014. Underwriting guidelines have been revised such that further losses of this type are not expected.
- Losses do not develop after 36 months.
- The age-to-age factors in the latest diagonal are representative of future loss development.
- Rates will be in effect for two years.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Annual loss cost trend | 5% |
| Annual premium trend | 0% |
| Fixed expense ratio | 0% |
| Variable expense ratio | 22% |
| Profit and contingencies provision | 6% |
| ALAE provision | 12% of loss |
| ULAE provision | 7% of loss |

Rate Change History

| Effective Date | Change |
|---|---|
| July 1, 2014 | +7.5% |
| July 1, 2015 | +3.0% |

| Calendar Year | 2012 | 2013 | 2014 | 2015 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Written Exposures | 805 | 850 | 825 | 875 |

Cumulative Reported Loss (\$000)

| Accident Year | 12 months | 24 months | 36 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | | 1,100 | 1,150 |
| 2014 | 2,940 | 4,210 | |
| 2015 | 1,020 | | |

Calculate the indicated rate change for policies effective between July 1, 2017 and July 1, 2019 based on the most recent three accident years of experience and assuming full credibility.

### Answer
-12.7%

### Explanation
The rating algorithm is exposures times a single fixed manual rate, so premium can be put on level by **extension of exposures** — multiply each year's earned exposures by the current manual rate — rather than by the parallelogram method. With a 0% fixed expense ratio the indication is the plain loss ratio form.

**Step 1 — current rate level.** The 2013 average written manual rate is \$5,000, and no rate change took effect between the product's launch and the end of 2013, so \$5,000 *is* the rate in force through mid-2014. Applying the two subsequent changes:

$$\text{Current rate} = 5{,}000 \times 1.075 \times 1.03 = \$5{,}536.25$$

**Step 2 — calendar year earned exposures.** Policies are annual and written uniformly, so a calendar year earns half of its own writings and half of the prior year's:

| Calendar Year | Earned exposures | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 827.5 | $0.5(805) + 0.5(850)$ |
| 2014 | 837.5 | $0.5(850) + 0.5(825)$ |
| 2015 | 850.0 | $0.5(825) + 0.5(875)$ |

**Step 3 — on-level earned premium.** Premium trend is 0%, so extension of exposures alone brings premium to current level:

| Year | Earned exposures | Current rate | On-level EP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 827.5 | \$5,536.25 | \$4,581,247 |
| 2014 | 837.5 | \$5,536.25 | \$4,636,609 |
| 2015 | 850.0 | \$5,536.25 | \$4,705,813 |
| **Total** | 2,515.0 | | **\$13,923,669** |

**Step 4 — remove the large loss from the triangle.** The \$2 million loss is not expected to recur, so it must come out of *every* cell it touches — the 2014 row at both 12 and 24 months — before development factors are selected, and out of the 2014 incurred that is then developed:

Adjusted Cumulative Reported Loss (\$000)

| Accident Year | 12 months | 24 months | 36 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | | 1,100 | 1,150 |
| 2014 | 940 | 2,210 | |
| 2015 | 1,020 | | |

**Step 5 — development.** The latest diagonal is representative:

$$f_{12\text{-}24} = \frac{2{,}210}{940} = 2.351 \qquad f_{24\text{-}36} = \frac{1{,}150}{1{,}100} = 1.045$$

Losses do not develop past 36 months, so the cumulative factors are

$$\text{CDF}_{36} = 1.000 \qquad \text{CDF}_{24} = 1.045 \qquad \text{CDF}_{12} = 2.351 \times 1.045 = 2.458$$

**Step 6 — trend period.** Rates are effective 7/1/2017 through 7/1/2019, so the average written date is 7/1/2018; policies are annual, so the average accident date of the exposure period is six months later, **1/1/2019**. Trending from the July 1 midpoint of each accident year:

| Accident Year | Trend period | Trend factor at 5% |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 5.5 years | $1.05^{5.5} = 1.3078$ |
| 2014 | 4.5 years | $1.05^{4.5} = 1.2455$ |
| 2015 | 3.5 years | $1.05^{3.5} = 1.1862$ |

**Step 7 — trended ultimate loss.**

| AY | Reported (\$000) | CDF | Ultimate (\$000) | Trend | Trended ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 1,150 | 1.000 | 1,150.0 | 1.3078 | \$1,503,969 |
| 2014 | 2,210 | 1.045 | 2,310.5 | 1.2455 | \$2,877,725 |
| 2015 | 1,020 | 2.458 | 2,507.1 | 1.1862 | \$2,973,941 |
| **Total** | | | | | **\$7,355,635** |

**Step 8 — the indication.** The loss ratio is trended ultimate loss over on-level earned premium:

$$\text{Loss ratio} = \frac{7{,}355{,}635}{13{,}923{,}669} = 0.5283$$

ALAE and ULAE are each a load *on loss* and are **added**, not compounded:

$$1 + \Delta = \frac{0.5283 \times (1 + 0.12 + 0.07)}{1 - 0.22 - 0.06} = \frac{0.6287}{0.72} = 0.8731$$

$$\Delta = \mathbf{-12.7\%}$$

*Alternatively*, the pure premium form gives the same answer. The trended ultimate pure premium is $7{,}355{,}635 / 2{,}515.0 = \$2{,}925$; the indicated rate is $2{,}925 \times 1.19 / 0.72 = \$4{,}834$; and $4{,}834 / 5{,}536 - 1 = -12.7\%$. The fixed expense ratio of 0% is what lets the two forms agree exactly.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to derive calendar year earned exposures from the written exposures, apply the current manual rate to on-level the premium by extension of exposures, strip the \$2 million shock loss out of the development triangle and out of the 2014 incurred before developing, trend each accident year to an average accident date of 1/1/2019, and complete a loss ratio indication carrying the ALAE, ULAE, variable expense and profit provisions.

Common errors included not on-leveling the premium at all and using written rather than earned exposures; leaving the \$2 million loss in the triangle, or removing it from the development factors but not from the 2014 incurred that was developed to ultimate; selecting incorrect trend periods, usually by trending to the effective date rather than to the average accident date of a two-year rate period; and multiplying the ALAE and ULAE loads together ($1.12 \times 1.07$) instead of adding them ($1 + 0.12 + 0.07$).
