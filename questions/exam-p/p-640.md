A person mails three packages. Each package independently has probability $p$ of being lost in the mail, where $0 < p < 1$.

Determine the probability that exactly one package is lost in the mail, given that at least one package is lost in the mail.

- A) $(1-p)^2$
- B) $3p(1-p)^2$
- C) $\dfrac{3p}{(1-p)}$
- D) $\dfrac{p(1-p)^2}{1-(1-p)^3}$
- E) $\dfrac{3p(1-p)^2}{1-(1-p)^3}$

## Explanation

The number lost is binomial with $n = 3$. The conditioning event is the complement of losing none:

$$
P[\text{at least one lost}] = 1 - P[\text{none lost}] = 1 - (1-p)^3
$$

Exactly one lost is a subset of at least one lost, so the intersection is just the smaller event:

$$
P[\text{exactly one lost}] = \binom{3}{1}p(1-p)^2 = 3p(1-p)^2
$$

Dividing gives

$$
P[\text{exactly 1} \mid \text{at least 1}] = \mathbf{\frac{3p(1-p)^2}{1 - (1-p)^3}}
$$

Choice (B) is the numerator alone — the *unconditional* probability of exactly one loss, with the conditioning discarded. Choice (D) drops the binomial coefficient $\binom{3}{1} = 3$, counting only one of the three ways a single package can be the one lost.
