A warehouse company's occurrence CGL policy (ISO CG 00 01 04 13) incepts on July 1. In May, a forklift operator noticed that a slow leak from the company's chemical drums was staining a tenant's goods next door and mentioned it to a co-worker; neither is authorized to give or receive notice of occurrences or claims, and no one else at the company knew. The leak continues into August, when the tenant demands payment. Under the new policy, how does paragraph b(3) of the Coverage A insuring agreement, the known injury condition, treat this damage?

- A) It bars coverage, because an employee of the insured knew of the damage before the policy period
- B) It bars coverage, because the damage began before the policy period, whoever knew of it
- C) It does not bar coverage, because no listed insured or authorized employee knew of the damage before the policy period
- D) It does not bar coverage, because the tenant's demand was not made until after the policy began

## Explanation

Paragraph b(3) removes injury or damage that, before the policy period, was known to have occurred by an insured listed in paragraph 1 of Who Is an Insured (the named insured and, for an organization, its executive officers and directors) or by an "employee" authorized to give or receive notice of an occurrence or claim. The forklift operator and the co-worker are neither, so their knowledge does not trigger the condition. Paragraph c then says damage not known before the policy period includes its continuation, so the known injury condition does not bar coverage. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats knowledge by any employee as knowledge of the insured. Only authorized employees count.
- **B** confuses the known injury condition with a rule that injury must begin during the policy period. Paragraph b(3) turns on who knew, not only on when the damage began.
- **D** looks at when the claim was made. On an occurrence form a pre-policy demand would be one way of becoming aware, but the absence of a demand does not settle the question; awareness by any other means also counts.
