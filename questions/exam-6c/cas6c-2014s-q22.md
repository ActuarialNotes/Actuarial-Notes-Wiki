In A.M. Best's Capital Adequacy Ratio (BCAR) model, the required capital can be divided into seven components. Two of the components are the loss and loss-adjustment expense reserves factor and the net premiums written factor.

The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) uses a risk-based formula to calculate the required capital.

## Part a (1 point)

The BCAR model includes several adjustments in the calculation of loss and loss-adjustment expense reserves (B5) factor. Identify four adjustments that are **not** included in OSFI's formula.

### Explanation
- reserve deficiency of a company
- size of a company
- the volatility of a company's case-incurred loss development factors
- growth in a company's exposure

Alternatively:

- reserve deficiency
- diversification benefits
- size of company
- growth

### Examiner Report
This question compares the different components between the BCAR and the MCT. Although there is no direct comparison shown in any of the study material, the candidates are expected to understand both the tests and be able to compare them.

Most of the candidates can only list two different components.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Identify two differences between BCAR's net premiums written factor and that of OSFI's formula.

### Explanation
- BCAR use different risk factors by line of business while OSFI applies same factor
- OSFI's formula applies a risk on the max(NUPR, 50% of NWP)

Alternatively:

- BCAR: different risk factors by line; OSFI: same factor for all lines
- BCAR: include diversification benefits; OSFI: no diversification benefits

### Examiner Report
Most of the candidates be able to identify at least one difference.
