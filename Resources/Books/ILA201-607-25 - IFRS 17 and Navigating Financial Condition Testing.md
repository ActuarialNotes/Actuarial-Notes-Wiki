An Oliver Wyman article on how IFRS 17 changes financial condition testing (FCT), the risk management tool insurers use to assess their forecasted capital and solvency position over a three- to five-year horizon. It looks at the impact of IFRS 17 on FCT, how it compares to FCT performed on an IFRS 4 basis, and how insurers can adjust. It focuses on Canadian insurance business measured under the general measurement model.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam ILA-201I (SOA)|Exam ILA-201I]] — Outcomes 4d.

## Design and presentation of adverse scenarios
- The design of the FCT base scenario should be largely unchanged under [[IFRS 17]].
- The severity of an adverse scenario should be based on its inherent loss distribution, not on how an accounting or capital regime presents the results in time.
- Adverse non-financial shocks are in many cases absorbed by the [[Contractual Service Margin|contractual service margin]], yet because the CSM counts as available capital under the Canadian capital regime, the impact on the CSM still has an immediate adverse effect on capital ratios.

## Taxes are certain, but future tax provisions do change
- Non-deductibility of CSM: under IFRS 17, 90% of most CSM is non-deductible, creating a temporary difference between the tax and statement reserves and an associated future tax asset.
- Transition rules phase in the change in the maximum tax actuarial reserve from IFRS 4 to IFRS 17 over five years.
