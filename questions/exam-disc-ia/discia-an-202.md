A universal life policyholder loses her job and pays no premiums for three years. Her account value at the start of that period is \$18,000, and the monthly expense and cost of insurance charges total about \$400. What happens to the policy?

- A) It lapses at the first missed premium, as a whole life policy would without a nonforfeiture option
- B) It stays in force indefinitely, because universal life premiums are flexible
- C) It converts automatically to a reduced paid-up policy for the account value
- D) It stays in force only while the account value can pay the charges

## Explanation

Universal life lets the owner pay on any pattern, so long as enough is paid to keep the policy in force. Charges keep coming out of the account, so with no premiums it falls by roughly \$400 a month plus or minus interest:

$$
\frac{18{,}000}{400} = 45 \text{ months}
$$

That is longer than three years, so the policy survives the gap, but if the account falls too low to cover the charges, the coverage ends unless the owner pays more or reduces the benefits.

- **A** treats universal life like a fixed premium schedule. A missed payment does not lapse a universal life policy while the account can pay the charges.
- **B** stops at "flexible": flexibility does not remove the charges, and an exhausted account ends the coverage.
- **C** describes a nonforfeiture option of a scheduled premium policy, not how a universal life account works.
