AgriInsurance requires an actuarial opinion on the self-sustainability for all provincial production insurance programs.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Describe the objective of this certification.

### Explanation
The objective is to ensure that the program can have enough financial resources to operate without other capital injections and that it takes a reasonable time to regain surplus position after an adverse event

Alternatively:

To make sure that the program is self-sustainable over a 25 year period. It will remain at a surplus on a long-term horizon:

- Recovery from 95th percentile deficit in 15 years or less on average
- Recovery from 95th percentile deficit 80% probable after 25 years

Alternatively:

To ensure continuity of insurance program by making sure past deficits will be recovered. Federal government uses this certification and if not certified, may reduce contributions.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge on the self-sustainability certification in the AgriInsurance program.

Candidates were expected to describe the objective of the certification.

A common error was:

- Not being specific enough, such as
  - Ensure the program is in satisfactory financial condition for the forecast period.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Provide one similarity and two differences between the assessment of self-sustainability and Dynamic Capital Adequacy Testing (DCAT) analysis.

### Explanation
Any one of the following similarities:

- Both forward looking to get expectation of financials in the future
- Both test plausible adverse scenarios to determine impact on financial condition
- Both consider all material risks that may impact sustainability
- Both utilize scenario testing

Any two of the following differences:

- Fully stochastic for self-sustainability
- Longer time horizon for self-sustainability
- DCAT is usually done each year while self-sustainability certification is not required every year
- The period for projection of surplus position starts at the end of the 6th year after an event for self-sustainability while DCAT starts at the beginning
- Self-sustainability looks at the time needed to regain surplus position while DCAT does not
- DCAT has a base scenario which is consistent with financial plan

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to compare DCAT with the assessment of self-sustainability.

A common error was:

- Answering with MCT requirements instead of DCAT. For example:
  - DCAT requires a MCT Ratio over 100%, self-sustainability does not.
  - Self-sustainability allows the insurer to be at a deficit during forecast period, DCAT does not.
