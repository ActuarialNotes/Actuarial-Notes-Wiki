A **Superintendent of Insurance** is the provincial official who licenses insurers, agents and adjusters to operate in a province, supervises [[Market Conduct Regulation|market conduct]], and administers the provincial *Insurance Act*. The federal counterpart is the Superintendent of Financial Institutions ([[OSFI]]), who supervises solvency. The two roles are distinct, and both apply to the same insurer.

Every province has such an office, though the modern name varies: [[Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario|FSRA]] in Ontario, the [[Autorité des marchés financiers|AMF]] in Quebec, the Alberta Superintendent of Insurance, and BCFSA in British Columbia.

A Superintendent's core powers are to:

1. Issue and revoke the licence that lets an insurer write in the province.
2. Approve or refuse [[Rate Regulation|rate filings]] where the province regulates rates.
3. Investigate unfair practices.
4. Set the [[Statutory Accident Benefits|benefit]] and coverage terms prescribed by regulation.
5. Enforce agent and adjuster licensing.

A licence to write is required per province. A national insurer therefore holds a federal order to commence and carry on business plus a licence from every jurisdiction it operates in, which is the practical source of Canada's multi-jurisdiction compliance burden.

> [!example]- A federally incorporated insurer wants to begin writing personal property insurance in three new provinces next year. What approvals does it need, and from whom? {Example}
> - From [[OSFI]] it needs no new approval to enter a province as such. The expansion is a change in business plan that feeds capital adequacy and would be discussed with the supervisory team, and the [[ORSA]] should reflect the new exposure.
> - From each provincial Superintendent it needs a licence for that province before a single policy is written.
> - For personal property, most provinces do not pre-approve rates the way they do for auto, but the forms and endorsements may still need filing. The insurer checks each province separately, since there is no national filing.
> - The new catastrophe exposure in three territories raises separate OSFI questions of [[Earthquake Exposure Risk Margin|earthquake]] and [[Concentration Risk|concentration]] risk, and the [[Appointed Actuary]] reflects it in the next [[FCT]] report.
> - The insurer needs a licence from each of the three Superintendents, and possibly form filings in each, but no new entry approval from OSFI.

## Prudential supervision of provincial insurers

Provincially incorporated insurers are also prudentially supervised by their province. For them the Superintendent does the job OSFI does federally, usually applying the [[MCT]] guideline by adoption rather than writing a separate capital rule.

## Coordination between provinces

Superintendents coordinate through the [[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators]], which produces harmonised positions but has no power of its own. Each Superintendent must adopt those positions separately.

> [!trap] Treating OSFI as the insurer's only regulator
> OSFI supervises a federal insurer's solvency, but it does not license the insurer to write in a province or approve its rates. Those come from each provincial Superintendent, so a federal insurer answers to both.
