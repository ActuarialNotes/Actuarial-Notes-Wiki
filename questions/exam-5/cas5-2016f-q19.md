A company that self-insures has the following limited historical information:

Cumulative Reported Claims ($000) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 4,000 | 6,000 | 7,500 | 8,250 |
| 2013 | 5,000 | 7,500 | 9,375 | |
| 2014 | 6,000 | 9,000 | | |
| 2015 | 7,500 | | | |

Cumulative Paid Claims ($000) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 1,600 | 4,000 | 6,000 | 7,500 |
| 2013 | 2,000 | 5,000 | 7,500 | |
| 2014 | 2,400 | 6,000 | | |
| 2015 | 3,000 | | | |

Industry Benchmark Claims Development Factors:

| Age-Age | Reported | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| 60-Ult | 1.015 | 1.100 |
| 48-60 | 1.025 | 1.150 |
| 36-48 | 1.050 | 1.250 |
| 24-36 | 1.150 | 1.500 |
| 12-24 | 1.250 | 2.500 |

- Case outstanding for accident year 2011 as of December 31, 2015 = $500,000.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Use the industry benchmark claims development factors to estimate the unpaid claims for accident year 2011 as of December 31, 2015.

### Explanation
Accident year 2011 is past 48 months, so only case outstanding is given. The case development factor turns case outstanding into unpaid claims using the two benchmark patterns beyond that age:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Case development factor} &= \frac{\text{paid CDF} \times (\text{reported CDF} - 1)}{\text{paid CDF} - \text{reported CDF}} + 1 \\
                                &= \frac{1.100(1.015 - 1)}{1.100 - 1.015} + 1 \\
                                &= 1.194 \\
\text{Unpaid claims} &= 1.194 \times 500{,}000 \\
                     &= 597{,}059
\end{align*}
$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate unpaid claims for AY 2011 using the case outstanding technique.
Common mistakes included:

- Not using the correct formula for case outstanding factor.
- Using a 12-ult CDF instead of 60-ult CDF.
- Not including the case outstanding amount for AY 2011 in the final answer.

## Part b (1 point)

Assess the reasonableness of using the industry benchmark reported and paid claims development factors for this company.

### Explanation
The company's own age-to-age factors against the benchmarks:

Reported:

| AY | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 1.50 | 1.25 | 1.10 |
| 2013 | 1.50 | 1.25 | |
| 2014 | 1.50 | | |
| **Selected** | **1.50** | **1.25** | **1.10** |
| Industry | 1.25 | 1.15 | 1.05 |

Paid:

| AY | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 2.50 | 1.50 | 1.25 |
| 2013 | 2.50 | 1.50 | |
| 2014 | 2.50 | | |
| **Selected** | **2.50** | **1.50** | **1.25** |
| Industry | 2.50 | 1.50 | 1.25 |

The company's paid pattern matches the industry exactly, so the benchmark paid factors are appropriate. Its reported claims develop much faster than the benchmark at every age, so the benchmark reported factors are not appropriate here. The two together point to a difference in case reserve philosophy: this company sets case reserves lower relative to ultimate than the industry does.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate development factors for the company using the given historical company data and then compare the calculated company factors to given industry benchmark factors. Candidates were then expected to assess the reasonableness of using the industry factors for the company based on the comparison to the calculated company factors.
Common mistakes included:

- Not stating whether or not the given paid and reported industry factors were reasonable based on the comparison to the calculated company factors.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Given the response to part b. above, discuss the reasonableness of the estimate in part a. above.

### Explanation
Not reasonable. The estimate in part a. leans on the industry *reported* CDF, which part b. shows does not describe this company. The benchmark reported factor is too low for this book, so the case development factor built from it is too low and the unpaid estimate is understated.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to indicate that the estimate in a) would be understated and therefore unreasonable.
Common mistakes included:

- Candidates stated that the response in a) was inaccurate but did not state that the result in a) was understated.
- Indicating that the response in a) was overstated.
