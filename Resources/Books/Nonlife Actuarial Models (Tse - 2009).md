The loss-modelling reference on the MAS-I syllabus, covering learning objectives B1–B4 and B7–B9. Parts I and IV carry the exam material: claim [[Frequency|frequency]] and [[Severity|severity]] distributions, [[Aggregate Loss Model|aggregate loss models]], and parametric estimation with [[Censoring|censored]] and [[Truncation|truncated]] data.

## Part I — Loss Models

### 1 Claim-Frequency Distribution

- Claim frequency, the [[Poisson Distribution]] and the [[Poisson Process]]
- The negative binomial and other counting distributions ([[Negative Binomial Distribution]])
- The $(a, b, 0)$ and $(a, b, 1)$ classes; compound distributions
- Convolutions and mixture distributions

### 2 Claim-Severity Distribution

- Continuous distributions for claim size ([[Severity]])
- Mixed distributions and coverage modifications: [[Deductible|deductibles]], policy limits ([[Limited Expected Value]]) and [[Coinsurance]]
- Effects of [[Inflation]] on claim severity

### 3 Aggregate-Loss Models

- The individual risk model and the collective risk model ([[Aggregate Loss Model]])
- The compound distribution of aggregate loss and its moments
- Recursive computation and approximations

## Part II — Risk and Ruin

### 4 Risk Measures

- Axioms of coherent risk measures; VaR and conditional tail expectation

### 5 Ruin Theory

- Discrete- and continuous-time surplus processes and ruin probabilities

## Part III — Credibility

### 6 Classical Credibility

- Full and partial credibility ([[Limited Fluctuation Credibility]])

### 7 Bühlmann Credibility

- The [[Bühlmann Credibility]] model and the Bühlmann–Straub extension

### 8 Bayesian Approach

- [[Bayesian]] inference and [[Bayesian Credibility]]; conjugate priors

### 9 Empirical Implementation of Credibility

- Nonparametric, semiparametric and parametric estimation of credibility parameters

## Part IV — Model Construction and Evaluation

### 10 Model Estimation and Types of Data

- Complete versus incomplete data: [[Censoring]] and [[Truncation]]

### 11 Nonparametric Model Estimation

- Empirical distributions, Kaplan–Meier and Nelson–Aalen estimators
- Kernel density estimation

### 12 Parametric Model Estimation

- [[Method of Moments]] and matching quantiles
- [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation]], including with incomplete data
- Bayesian estimation

### 13 Model Evaluation and Selection

- Graphical comparisons: [[QQ Plot|quantile plots]] and $p$-$p$ plots
- Goodness-of-fit tests: Kolmogorov–Smirnov, Anderson–Darling, chi-square
- Information criteria: [[AIC]] and [[BIC]]

### 14 Basic Monte Carlo Methods

- Generating uniform and non-uniform random numbers; variance reduction

### 15 Applications of Monte Carlo Methods

- Simulation of aggregate losses, ruin probabilities and bootstrap estimation

## Appendix

- Review of statistics, answers to exercises, references and index
