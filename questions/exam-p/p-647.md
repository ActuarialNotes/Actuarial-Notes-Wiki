An unfair coin is tossed. If the outcome of the coin toss is heads, then a six-sided die with the probability that each even number is twice as likely as each odd number is rolled. If the outcome of the coin toss is tails, then a fair die is rolled.

Let $X$ be the random variable equal to the number on the face of the rolled die. Let $F$ be the cumulative distribution function for $X$.

$F(3) = 0.463$.

Calculate $P[X = 4]$.

- A) $0.167$
- B) $0.194$
- C) $0.204$
- D) $0.537$
- E) $0.667$

## Explanation

On the biased die each odd face has probability $q$ and each even face $2q$, so $3q + 6q = 1$ and the faces are $1/9$ (odd) and $2/9$ (even). Let $p = P[\text{Heads}]$. By the law of total probability,

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X = 1] &= p\left(\tfrac{1}{9}\right) + (1-p)\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right) = \tfrac{1}{6} - \tfrac{p}{18} \\
P[X = 2] &= p\left(\tfrac{2}{9}\right) + (1-p)\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right) = \tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{p}{18} \\
P[X = 3] &= p\left(\tfrac{1}{9}\right) + (1-p)\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right) = \tfrac{1}{6} - \tfrac{p}{18}
\end{align*}
$$

Adding them,

$$
F(3) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{p}{18} = 0.463 \implies p = 0.666
$$

Then

$$
P[X = 4] = 0.666\left(\tfrac{2}{9}\right) + (1 - 0.666)\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right) = 0.1480 + 0.0557 = \mathbf{0.204}
$$

Choice (A), 0.167, is $1/6$ — the fair-die answer, as though the coin never came up heads. The bias only moves the answer from 0.167 to 0.204 because it acts on a single face, but the direction is the useful check: an even face should come out *above* $1/6$, which rules out (A) immediately.
