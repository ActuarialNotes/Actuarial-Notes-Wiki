Greg buys a 20-year increasing annuity-immediate with annual payments. The first payment is 110 and each succeeding payment is equal to the previous payment plus $X$. The annuity is priced at 2000 based on an annual effective interest rate of 10%.

Calculate $X$.

- A) 16.64
- B) 17.45
- C) 19.19
- D) 21.00
- E) 22.68

## Explanation

The payments are $110, 110+X, 110+2X, \ldots, 110+19X$ at the ends of years 1 through 20. Split each one into a level part of 110 and an increment: payment $k$ carries $(k-1)X$, so the first payment carries none.

For an annuity-immediate whose first payment is $P$ and which increases by $Q$ each year,
$$PV = P\,a_{\overline{n}|} + Q\,\frac{a_{\overline{n}|} - nv^n}{i}$$

With $P = 110$, $Q = X$, $n = 20$ and $i = 10\%$:
$$2000 = 110\,a_{\overline{20}|0.10} + X\left[\frac{a_{\overline{20}|0.10} - 20v^{20}}{0.10}\right]$$

At 10%, $a_{\overline{20}|} = 8.513564$ and $20v^{20} = 20(0.14864363) = 2.972873$, so the bracket is
$$\frac{8.513564 - 2.972873}{0.10} = 55.40691$$

and
$$
\begin{align*}
2000 &= 110(8.513564) + 55.40691X \\
1063.51 &= 55.40691X \\
X &= 19.1945 \approx 19.19
\end{align*}
$$

**The bracket is not $(Ia)_{\overline{20}|}$.** The increasing annuity-immediate paying $1, 2, \ldots, n$ is $(Ia)_{\overline{n}|} = \dfrac{\ddot{a}_{\overline{n}|} - nv^n}{i}$, with $\ddot{a}$ in the numerator: $(Ia)_{\overline{20}|0.10} = \dfrac{1.1(8.513564) - 2.972873}{0.10} = 63.9205$. The same price can be written as a level annuity of $110 - X$ plus $X(Ia)_{\overline{20}|}$, since $110 + (k-1)X = (110 - X) + kX$:
$$2000 = (110 - X)\,a_{\overline{20}|} + X(Ia)_{\overline{20}|}$$

which makes the coefficient of $X$ equal to $(Ia)_{\overline{20}|} - a_{\overline{20}|} = 63.9205 - 8.5136 = 55.4069$, the same bracket. Using $(Ia)_{\overline{20}|}$ itself as the coefficient of $X$ with $110\,a_{\overline{20}|}$ counts one extra $X$ in every payment and gives $1063.51/63.9205 = 16.64$, option A.
