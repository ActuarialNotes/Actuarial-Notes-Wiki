A broker markets four separate products. The probabilities of selling these products to a client are:

| Product | Probability |
|---|---|
| Auto insurance | 0.55 |
| Homeowners insurance | 0.45 |
| Health insurance | 0.50 |
| Life insurance | 0.60 |

The sales of these products are mutually independent.

Calculate the probability that the broker sells more than two products to a client.

- A) $0.27$
- B) $0.35$
- C) $0.39$
- D) $0.57$
- E) $0.73$

## Explanation

"More than two" means exactly three or exactly four. Because the four sales are independent with *different* probabilities, this is not binomial — each of the four ways to sell exactly three has to be written out, omitting a different product each time:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[3] &= (0.55)(0.45)(0.50)(0.40) + (0.45)(0.45)(0.50)(0.60) \\
     &\quad + (0.55)(0.55)(0.50)(0.60) + (0.55)(0.45)(0.50)(0.60) \\
     &= 0.27525
\end{align*}
$$

Selling all four is a single product of the four probabilities:

$$
P[4] = (0.55)(0.45)(0.50)(0.60) = 0.07425
$$

Adding them:

$$
P[3 \text{ or } 4] = 0.27525 + 0.07425 = \mathbf{0.3495}
$$

Choice (A), 0.27, is $P[3]$ alone. "More than two" is an inclusive upper tail, so the all-four case belongs in the sum; leaving it out costs 0.074 — more than a fifth of the answer.
