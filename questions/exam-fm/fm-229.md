A company created a portfolio in order to protect its position using Redington immunization.

Under which of the following changes in the yield rate is the immunization strategy guaranteed to be effective?

- A) Only a small decrease in the yield rate
- B) Only a small increase in the yield rate
- C) Only a small change in the yield rate
- D) Any decrease in the yield rate
- E) Any change in the yield rate

## Explanation

Redington immunization sets up the portfolio so that three conditions hold at the current yield rate:

1. the present value of the assets equals the present value of the liabilities;
2. the (modified) duration of the assets equals that of the liabilities;
3. the convexity of the assets is greater than the convexity of the liabilities.

Together these make the net present value (assets minus liabilities) zero with a **local minimum** at the current yield rate, so a sufficiently small change in the yield rate, **in either direction**, leaves the assets worth at least as much as the liabilities.

The protection is only local: under a large change in the yield rate the position may or may not be protected. (It also assumes a single yield rate for all cash flows, so a change that moves short- and long-term rates by different amounts is outside what it guarantees.)

Therefore the strategy is guaranteed to be effective only for a **small change** in the yield rate, up or down (answer C).
