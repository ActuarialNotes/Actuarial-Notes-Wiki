A linear model has the following form:

$$
\begin{align*}
Y_i &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu_i,\ e^{\sigma}) \\
\mu_i &= \theta_0 + \theta_1 X_{1i} + \theta_2 X_{1i}^2 + \theta_3 X_{2i} + \phi X_{3i} \\
\theta_0 &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ 10) \\
\theta_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ 2) \\
\theta_2 &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ 0.2) \\
\theta_3 &\sim \text{Normal}(-1,\ 1) \\
\phi &= \gamma_1 + \gamma_2 X_{1i} \\
\gamma_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ 3) \\
\gamma_2 &\sim \text{Normal}(0.02,\ 0.6) \\
\ln(\sigma) &\sim \text{Uniform}(-3,\ 3)
\end{align*}
$$

Determine the number of parameters in this model.

- A) 5
- B) 6
- C) 7
- D) 8
- E) 9

## Explanation

A parameter is an unobserved quantity the model has to estimate, which in a Bayesian specification means one that is given a prior. The lines written with "$=$" define deterministic quantities built from parameters and data, so they add no parameters of their own.

- $Y_i$ and $X_{1i}, X_{2i}, X_{3i}$ are data.
- $\mu_i$ is defined by the linear predictor, so it is not a parameter.
- $\phi = \gamma_1 + \gamma_2 X_{1i}$ is also a definition: the slope on $X_{3i}$ varies with $X_{1i}$ (an interaction), but it is fully determined by $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$.
- $\sigma$ is a parameter. Its prior is placed on $\ln(\sigma)$, but that is still one unknown.

The quantities with priors are

$$
\theta_0,\ \theta_1,\ \theta_2,\ \theta_3,\ \gamma_1,\ \gamma_2,\ \sigma,
$$

which makes 7 parameters. So the answer is **C**.
