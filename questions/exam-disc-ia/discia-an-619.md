A building owner sets fire to its insured building, and the insurer denies the owner's claim. A bank holds a \$300,000 mortgage on the building and is shown in the declarations. The bank pays a premium installment the owner had skipped when the insurer asks, files a sworn proof of loss within 60 days of the insurer's notice, and had no knowledge of any change in risk. What does the mortgageholders condition provide?

- A) The bank recovers nothing, because a denied claim is denied for every party with an interest
- B) The bank recovers only once it has completed foreclosure on the building
- C) The bank recovers its interest, and the owner's debt to the bank is extinguished
- D) The bank recovers its interest, and the insurer takes over the bank's rights against the owner to that extent

## Explanation

When the insurer denies the owner's claim because of the owner's own acts, the mortgageholder still has the right to be paid if it pays premium due at the insurer's request, submits a sworn proof of loss within 60 days of notice, and has told the insurer of changes in ownership, occupancy or substantial change in risk it knew of. When the insurer pays the mortgageholder and denies the owner, the mortgageholder's rights under the mortgage are transferred to the insurer to the extent of its payment. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — The condition exists to protect the lender from the owner's acts.
- **B** — The mortgageholder can receive payment even if it has started foreclosure; nothing requires it to finish one.
- **C** — The owner still owes the debt; the insurer now holds the bank's rights against the owner for what it paid.
