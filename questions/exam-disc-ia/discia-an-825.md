A painting contractor's CGL has a \$1,000,000 each occurrence limit and CG 20 10 04 13 (Additional Insured – Owners, Lessees or Contractors – Scheduled Person or Organization) scheduling a building owner. The painting contract requires the contractor to insure the owner for \$500,000. While the painting is under way, a visitor trips over the contractor's drop cloth and sues the owner for \$800,000. What does the contractor's CGL pay on the owner's behalf?

- A) \$500,000
- B) \$800,000
- C) \$1,000,000
- D) \$0, because the owner is not the named insured

## Explanation

The visitor's injury was caused, at least in part, by the named insured's acts in performing its ongoing operations for the owner, so the owner is an additional insured for it. When a contract requires the coverage, CG 20 10 limits what is paid on the additional insured's behalf to the lesser of the amount the contract requires and the limits available.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid}_{\text{AI}} &= \min(800{,}000,\ 500{,}000,\ 1{,}000{,}000) \\
&= 500{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** pays the full damages, ignoring the contract cap.
- **C** applies the policy limit as if the endorsement had no contract cap.
- **D** — the endorsement adds the owner as an insured; it need not be the named insured.
