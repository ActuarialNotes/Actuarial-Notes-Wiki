# ACIA Module 2
**ACIA Module 2** is one of the two modules the Canadian Institute of Actuaries requires toward the Associate (ACIA) designation.

> [!question]- About the module
>
> The ACIA modules are a core educational requirement toward ACIA designation. They are intended to bridge accredited university education or education obtained via other actuarial organizations with the work environment.
>
> They are administered online via the CIA learning management system.
>
> The modules assume some knowledge of the topics acquired via a recognized university degree or through other Associate-level education. Outcomes that participants are expected to achieve often cover multiple learning objectives, with an emphasis on communications in most tasks.
>
> The modules contain recordings, short readings, and/or quizzes. Each module includes a case study that includes various deliverables such as emails, reports, memorandums, recorded presentations, or spreadsheets. These case studies apply theoretical topics covered in the participant’s university degree or other recognized Associate-level education to practical actuarial scenarios; the content should not be new to the participant, but the application may be.
>
> There is no formal prerequisite for the ACIA modules. Participants in an accredited university will be expected to have completed some actuarial courses prior to completing the first ACIA module, likely in their second year of university.
>
> The second module should be completed when a participant has nearly completed all courses relevant to the ACIA Capstone Exam. It is expected that these participants undertake the second module in the last year of their university program. Participants are responsible for ensuring they have the appropriate knowledge.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- D. Traditional actuarial solutions
> The [[Fourth Portion|fourth portion]] of the [[Module Complements University|module complements university]] or other [[Recognized Education|recognized education]] by reintroducing actuarial solutions to traditional problems. Fields of application of such traditional solutions could be [[Pension|pensions]], [[Life Insurance|life insurance]], or general insurance.
>
> **1. Complement of long-term actuarial mathematics**
>
> 1. Describe why and how models are used for pricing, reserving, and assessing profitability and sustainability of [[Life Insurance Product|life insurance products]] or [[Pension Arrangement|pension arrangements]].
> 2. Apply actuarial models and [[Cash Flow|cash-flow]] techniques used in pricing, reserving, and assessing profitability of contracts for [[Contingent Payment|contingent payments]] with appropriate allowance for [[Expenses|expenses]] of [[Life Insurance|life insurance]] and [[Pension Fund|pension fund]] applications.
> 3. Describe typical and practical applications of [[Traditional Long-Term|traditional long-term]] solutions, building on solutions commonly considered in a [[University Education|university education]].
> 4. Compare and justify actuarial solutions relative to long-term applications in the context of [[Life Insurance|life insurance]] and [[Pension|pension]].
> 5. Draw connections between simple solutions part of a [[University Education|university education]] and [[Advanced Actuarial Problems Relative|advanced actuarial problems relative]] to long-term applications.
>
> **2. Complement of short-term actuarial mathematics**
>
> 1. Describe why and how models are used for pricing, reserving, and assessing profitability of [[Short-Term Insurance Product|short-term insurance products]].
> 2. Apply actuarial models and [[Projected Cash-Flow|projected cash-flow]] techniques in pricing, reserving, and assessing profitability of contracts for [[Contingent Payment|contingent payments]], with appropriate allowance for [[Expenses|expenses]] of [[Short-Term Insurance Coverage|short-term insurance]].
> 3. Describe typical and practical applications of [[Traditional Short-Term|traditional short-term]] solutions, building on solutions already considered in [[University Education|university education]].
> 4. Compare and justify actuarial solutions relative to [[Short-Term|short-term]] applications.
> 5. Draw connections between simple solutions part of a [[University Education|university education]] and [[Advanced Actuarial Problems Relative|advanced actuarial problems relative]] to short-term applications.
>
> **3. Communication of results and uncertainties**
>
> 1. Recognize appropriate [[Model Output|model outputs]] to communicate the key elements of an actuarial solution and uncertainties, tailored to the intended audience.

> [!example]- E. Advanced actuarial models and predictive analytics applications
> This final portion of the [[Module Introduces Predictive Modelling|module introduces predictive modelling]] to actuarial problems, building on the [[Predictive Analytics Models Taught|predictive analytics models taught]] at [[Canadian University|Canadian universities]] or through other [[Recognized Actuarial Education|recognized actuarial education]] to prepare participants for the workplace. Examples will be applicable to all practice areas.
>
> **1. Notions in predictive analytics**
>
> 1. Identify the problem to be solved using [[Predictive Analytics|predictive analytics]] techniques.
> 2. Understand the business objectives, statistical objectives, and [[Constraint|constraints]] of a problem, including implementation limitations and challenges.
> 3. Explain how to create models that fit a [[Business|business]] problem and that can be validated, monitored, and updated.
> 4. Identify common issues and pitfalls such as [[Correlation|correlation]] and causation, impact of [[Data Biase|data biases]], and [[Overfitting|overfitting]] models.
>
> **2. Data and systems**
>
> 1. Describe measures of [[Data Quality|data quality]] and understand the importance of appropriate data.
> 2. Construct datasets and use appropriate tools for cleaning, restructuring, and transforming data to be suitable for solving real-world problems, including how to [[Missing Data|handle missing data]].
> 3. Describe various [[Tidy Data|data structures]], sources of data, and their characteristics, including extremely [[Large Dataset|large datasets]].
> 4. Describe the possible aims of a data analysis (e.g., descriptive, inferential, [[Predictive|predictive]]).
> 5. Explain the ethical, legal, and regulatory issues involved in using [[Personal Data|personal data]] and others’ work.
> 6. Explain the main issues to be addressed by a [[Data Governance Policy|data governance policy]] and its importance for an organization.
> 7. Explain the [[Risk|risks]] associated with use of data (including [[Algorithmic Decision-Making|algorithmic decision-making]]).
>
> **3. Model selection and validation**
>
> 1. Evaluate the appropriateness of a model and its assumptions for addressing the [[Business|business]] problem.
> 2. Understand the difference between a stochastic and a deterministic model and identify the [[Advantages-disadvantage|advantages/disadvantages]] of each.
> 3. Explain the purposes of scenario-based and [[Proxy|proxy]] models.
> 4. Understand the short- and [[Long-Run Property|long-run properties]] of a model, and how this may be relevant in deciding whether a model is suitable for a particular application.
> 5. Apply parsimony to model selection, balancing model complexity and [[Accuracy|accuracy]].
> 6. Discuss the [[Operational Excellence|business processes]] and [[Limitations Relative|limitations relative]] to the implementation of a predictive model.
> 7. Use test data to estimate and confirm model [[Parameter|parameters]].
> 8. Use graphical and [[Quantitative|quantitative]] tools to validate and monitor the model.
> 9. Plan and produce a model with [[Clear Documentation|clear documentation]], enabling detailed checking and [[High-Level Scrutiny|high-level scrutiny]].
> 10. Perform checks on the results of a model, including applying sensitivity of assumptions and/or scenario [[Test|tests]].
>
> **4. Communication of results and uncertainties**
>
> 1. Create appropriate [[Data Visualization|data visualizations]] to communicate the key elements of a [[Predictive Analytics|predictive modelling]] analysis, tailored to the intended audience.
> 2. Explain the meaning and value of [[Reproducible Research|reproducible research]] and describe the elements required to ensure a data analysis is reproducible.
