McGraw Hill describes this textbook as giving students and managers an appreciation of the strengths and limitations of an organization's accounting system, so that they can be more intelligent users of these systems. Zimmerman provides a framework for understanding accounting systems and a basis for analyzing proposed changes to them, and the 10th edition aims to show that managerial accounting is an integral part of the firm's organizational architecture, not just an isolated set of computational topics.
