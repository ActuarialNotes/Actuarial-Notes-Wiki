The following information from the Dynamic Capital Adequacy Testing (DCAT) analysis of a federally regulated property and casualty insurance company is available as at December 31, 2015. All amounts are in thousands of dollars (\$000s).

| Scenario | Component | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base Scenario | MCT Ratio | 230.0% | 241.0% | 252.4% | 264.4% |
| Base Scenario | Capital | 350,000 | 385,000 | 423,500 | 465,850 |
| Frequency / Severity (Loss Ratio) | MCT Ratio | 230.0% | 190.3% | 151.2% | 170.2% |
| Frequency / Severity (Loss Ratio) | Capital | 350,000 | 285,000 | 223,500 | 265,850 |
| Misestimation of Policy Liabilities (Unpaid Claims) | MCT Ratio | 230.0% | -12.5% | 18.4% | 48.4% |
| Misestimation of Policy Liabilities (Unpaid Claims) | Capital | 350,000 | -15,000 | 23,500 | 65,850 |
| Combined Economic Scenario | MCT Ratio | 230.0% | 159.2% | 187.0% | 213.4% |
| Combined Economic Scenario | Capital | 350,000 | 135,000 | 173,500 | 215,850 |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Describe the base scenario in the context of the DCAT.

### Explanation
- Realistic set of assumptions used to forecast the financial condition of a company throughout the forecast period, usually consistent with business plan.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to mention both of the following components:

- Forecast/ projection of the company's financial condition using a set of realistic/ reasonable assumptions
- Usually consistent with the business plan

One common mistake is that some candidates only mentioned that it should be consistent with the business plan.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Determine whether the company is in satisfactory financial condition. Briefly justify your answer.

### Explanation
- Base scenario MCT > 150% for all years => good
- But misestimation scenario has a negative capital amount in 2017 => to be satisfactory, all scenarios must have asset value > liability value for all years
- Therefore not satisfactory

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to mention the two conditions for a satisfactory opinion and whether each of these conditions were met.

Some candidates were confused that capital > 0 = MCT ratio > 0%. This is not true. It is important to distinguish the meaning of capital and MCT ratio.

Common mistakes included:

- Stating that the supervisory target is 100% instead of 150%
- Stating that the base MCT ratio has to be greater than the minimum instead of supervisory target
- Stating that meeting policyholder obligations is sufficient instead of stating the requirement for assets greater than liabilities (or equity greater than 0)
- Answering "no" without providing any supporting arguments

## Part c (0.5 points)

Identify two possible management actions that the actuary may consider under the Frequency / Severity (Loss Ratio) scenario.

### Explanation
- Reviewing reinsurance coverage, type, or contract terms at renewal
- Implementing rate increases, where possible
- Restricting writing in hazard prone areas
- Reviewing the target mix by line of business or jurisdiction
- Reviewing the type of products offered
- Selling or reinvesting assets
- Reduce expenses

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide any two management actions under the Loss Ratio scenario as listed in the DCAT guideline, or any other management actions that made logical sense.

Common mistakes included:

- Capital injections from financial markets
- Reduce number of policies underwritten to reduce capital

## Part d (0.5 points)

Identify two possible ripple effects that the actuary may consider under the Misestimation of Policy Liabilities scenario.

### Explanation
- Increases in the policy liabilities related to current and past reinsurance contracts that are swing-rated, have variable commission, or require reinstatements
- Increases in ultimate claim costs and claim expenses in connection with the runoff of the unearned premium for scenarios affecting claims liabilities
- Increases in ultimate claim costs and claim expenses in connection with future new and renewal business
- Forced sale or liquidation of assets
- Rating agency downgrade
- Regulatory intervention/ restriction
- Increase in loss ratio
- The effect on actuarial present value for scenarios affecting undiscounted policy liabilities

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide any two ripple effects under the Misestimation scenario as listed in the DCAT guideline, or any other possible ripple effects that made logical sense.
Common mistakes were the following:

- Reinsurer default, inadequate pricing, company insolvency, loss of investors' confidence were not considered to be valid answers.
- Some candidates misunderstood the definition of ripple effects and provided management actions or possible results of the scenario.
