This year, a dental insurance policyholder has probability 0.70 of having no fillings, probability 0.90 of having no root canals, and probability 0.35 of having at least one filling or root canal.

Calculate the probability that a policyholder has no root canals, given that the policyholder has no fillings.

- A) $0.50$
- B) $0.65$
- C) $0.72$
- D) $0.78$
- E) $0.93$

## Explanation

Let $F$ be the event of at least one filling and $R$ the event of at least one root canal. The conditional probability needed is

$$
P[R^c \mid F^c] = \frac{P[R^c \cap F^c]}{P[F^c]}
$$

The numerator is the event of no fillings *and* no root canals. By De Morgan's law that is the complement of "at least one filling or root canal", whose probability is given directly as 0.35:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[R^c \cap F^c] &= P\bigl[(R \cup F)^c\bigr] \\
                &= 1 - P[R \cup F] \\
                &= 1 - 0.35 \\
                &= 0.65
\end{align*}
$$

The denominator is given as $P[F^c] = 0.70$. So

$$
\begin{align*}
P[R^c \mid F^c] &= \frac{0.65}{0.70} \\
                &= \frac{13}{14} \\
                &= \mathbf{0.92857}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B) is the numerator 0.65 left undivided. Note the 0.90 is never needed: recognising that the given 0.35 already *is* the union's probability turns a three-piece inclusion–exclusion problem into a one-step complement. Had the events been independent the answer would be 0.90, so the data here imply they are not.
