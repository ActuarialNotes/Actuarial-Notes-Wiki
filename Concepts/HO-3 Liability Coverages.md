**HO-3 Liability Coverages** are the coverages of Section II of the ISO Homeowners 3 – Special Form (HO 00 03, 05 11 edition), which pay for an insured's liability to others and for others' medical bills. Section II has two coverages with their own limits and four additional coverages:

1. Coverage E, personal liability, which pays damages an insured is legally liable for because of bodily injury or property damage caused by an occurrence and defends the insured.
2. Coverage F, medical payments to others, which pays others' medical expenses without regard to fault.
3. Additional coverage A, claim expenses, paid in addition to the limit of liability.
4. Additional coverage B, first aid to others.
5. Additional coverage C, damage to property of others, up to \$1,000.
6. Additional coverage D, loss assessment, up to \$1,000.

Coverage E's limit applies per occurrence, and Coverage F's limit to one person in one accident, so a payment under one does not reduce the other.

## Coverage E: personal liability

If a claim is made or suit brought against an insured for damages because of "bodily injury" or "property damage" caused by an "occurrence" to which the coverage applies, the insurer pays up to its limit for damages the insured is legally liable for, including prejudgment interest. Any part of a judgment above the limit is the insured's own. The insurer provides a defense by counsel of its choice even if the suit is groundless, false or fraudulent. It may investigate and settle as it decides, and its [[Duty to Defend|duty to settle or defend]] ends when the limit for the occurrence is exhausted by paying judgments or settlements.

The coverage turns on three defined terms. An "occurrence" is an accident, including continuous or repeated exposure to substantially the same harmful conditions, that results in bodily injury or property damage during the policy period. "Bodily injury" is bodily harm, sickness or disease, including required care, loss of services and resulting death. "Property damage" is physical injury to, destruction of, or loss of use of tangible property.

An insured is:

1. The named insured and spouse.
2. Resident relatives.
3. Other residents under 21 in the care of one of them.
4. A full-time student who lived in the household before leaving for school, if under 24 and a relative, or under 21 and in the household's care.
5. Under Section II only, anyone legally responsible for animals or watercraft that an insured owns and the policy covers, but not someone using them in a business or without the owner's consent.
6. For a motor vehicle the policy covers, the insured's employees and anyone using it on an insured location with consent.

The insured location is the residence premises; other residences shown in the declarations or acquired during the period; premises used in connection with them; premises not owned by an insured where an insured is temporarily residing; vacant land other than farm land owned by or rented to an insured; land where a one- to four-family residence is being built for an insured; cemetery plots of an insured; and premises occasionally rented to an insured for non-business use.

## Coverage F: medical payments to others

The insurer pays the necessary medical expenses incurred or medically ascertained within three years of an accident causing bodily injury: reasonable charges for medical, surgical, x-ray, dental, ambulance, hospital, professional nursing, prosthetic devices and funeral services. Payment is not an admission of liability by anyone.

It does not apply to the named insured or regular residents of the household, other than residence employees. For anyone else it applies:

1. To a person on the insured location with an insured's permission.
2. To a person off the insured location if the injury arises from a condition on the insured location or the ways immediately adjoining, or is caused by an insured's activities, by a residence employee working for an insured, or by an animal owned by or in the care of an insured.

> [!example]- Does Coverage F pay each person's medical bills? {Example}
> (a) A neighbor's child hurt on the insured's trampoline, which she was invited to use; (b) a jogger bitten by the insured's dog in a public park; (c) the insured's 12-year-old son, who lives at home, hurt falling from the same trampoline; (d) a stranger hurt when a softball the insured hit at a city park strikes her.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - (a) Yes. She is a person on the insured location with an insured's permission.
> > - (b) Yes. He is off the insured location, and the injury was caused by an animal owned by an insured.
> > - (c) No. Coverage F does not apply to regular residents of the household, so his bills belong on the family's health insurance.
> > - (d) Yes. She is off the insured location, and the injury was caused by an insured's activities.

## Additional coverages

Claim expenses are paid in addition to the Coverage E limit, so defense costs do not use it up. The insurer pays expenses it incurs and costs taxed against an insured in suits it defends; premiums on bonds required in those suits, for bond amounts up to the Coverage E limit; reasonable expenses an insured incurs at its request, including lost earnings (not other income) of up to \$250 a day, to help investigate or defend; and post-judgment interest on the entire judgment until it pays or tenders its share up to the limit.

First aid pays the expenses an insured incurs for first aid to others for bodily injury the policy covers. First aid to an insured is not paid.

> [!example]- What does the insurer pay in total for this guest's fall? {Example}
> An HO-3 has a Coverage E limit of \$300,000 and a Coverage F limit of \$5,000 per person (illustrative limits). A guest falls on a loose stair tread at the insured's home. The insured pays \$150 for first aid at the scene, the insurer pays the guest's \$4,000 of medical bills under Coverage F, and the guest later sues. The insurer spends \$20,000 defending the suit and settles it for \$90,000. The insured loses two days' wages of \$300 a day attending a deposition at the insurer's request.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Coverage F pays without a finding of fault: $\min(4{,}000,\ 5{,}000) = \$4{,}000$.
> > - Coverage E pays the settlement: $\min(90{,}000,\ 300{,}000) = \$90{,}000$.
> > - Lost earnings are capped at \$250 a day, so the claim expenses are $20{,}000 + 2 \times 250 = \$20{,}500$, paid on top of the limit.
> > - First aid of \$150 is also paid in addition.
> > - The insurer pays $4{,}000 + 90{,}000 + 20{,}500 + 150 = \$114{,}650$, and none of the \$300,000 Coverage E limit is used by the defense.

Damage to property of others pays up to \$1,000 per occurrence, at replacement cost, for property damage to others' property caused by an insured, whether or not the insured is legally liable. It does not pay for damage:

1. Recoverable under Section I.
2. Caused intentionally by an insured aged 13 or older.
3. To property owned by an insured, or by a tenant of an insured or a resident of the household.
4. Arising from a business, from premises the insured owns, rents or controls other than an insured location, or from aircraft, hovercraft, watercraft or motor vehicles, except a recreational off-road vehicle the insured does not own and that need not be registered.

> [!example]- What does damage to property of others pay in each case? {Example}
> (a) The insured borrows a neighbor's laptop, which would cost \$1,400 to replace, and drops it; (b) the insured's 10-year-old son throws a ball through a neighbor's window, \$350 to replace, while playing; (c) the insured's 15-year-old daughter deliberately scratches a classmate's bicycle.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - **(a) \$1,000.** Coverage E excludes property in an insured's care, but this additional coverage pays at replacement cost up to \$1,000, liable or not.
> > - **(b) \$350.** The coverage pays whether or not the child is legally liable, and the exclusion for intentional damage reaches only insureds aged 13 or older.
> > - **(c) Nothing.** The damage was caused intentionally by an insured aged 13 or older.

Loss assessment pays up to \$1,000 for the named insured's share of an assessment by a property owners' association, charged during the policy period, because of bodily injury or property damage not excluded under Section II, or because of liability for an act of an unpaid, elected director, officer or trustee. The \$1,000 applies per accident or per act however many assessments there are, and governmental assessments are not covered.

> [!trap] Paying the household under Coverage F
> Coverage F never pays the named insured or a regular resident of the household, other than a residence employee. A resident child's injury at home is a matter for the family's health insurance, while a guest's is paid under Coverage F without any finding of fault.
