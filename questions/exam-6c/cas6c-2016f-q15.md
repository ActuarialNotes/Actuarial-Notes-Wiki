You are given the following information about a property and casualty insurance company as at December 31, 2015. All amounts are in thousands of dollars (\$000s).

| Investment | Denomination | Market Value (in \$CAD) | Modified Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | \$CAD | 10,000 | 0.0 |
| Bond | \$CAD | 40,000 | 2.0 |
| Bond | \$USD | 9,000 | 4.0 |
| Common Shares | \$CAD | 8,000 | n/a |
| Common Shares | \$USD | 5,000 | n/a |
| Investment Property | \$CAD | 2,000 | n/a |

**Other information:**

- The investments in foreign denominations are not hedged.
- There are no liabilities in foreign denominations.
- There are no other market exposures.
- The company writes only one line of business uniformly throughout the year.

**Discounting Assumptions for Policy Liabilities:**

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Assumed payout pattern at 12 / 24 / 36 months | 10% / 60% / 100% |
| Discount rate, net of management fees | 2.0% |
| Margin for adverse deviation for claims development | 5.0% |
| Margin for adverse deviation for reinsurance recoveries | 0.0% (no reinsurance is purchased) |
| Margin for adverse deviation for interest rate risk | 0.5% |

**Premium Liabilities:**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Unearned premiums | 12,000 |
| Maximum policy acquisition expenses deferrable | 1,248 |

**Undiscounted Claims Liabilities:**

| Accident Year | Paid Losses | Case Reserves | Provision for IBNR and ULAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 and prior | 18,000 | 0 | 0 |
| 2014 | 10,000 | 3,000 | 500 |
| 2015 | 2,000 | 15,000 | 5,000 |

**MCT Risk Charges at Target**

| Risk | Charge |
|---|---|
| Premium liabilities | 20% |
| Unpaid claims | 10% |
| Interest rate risk | 1.25% |
| Foreign exchange risk | 10% |
| Equity risk | 30% |
| Real estate risk – own properties | 10% |
| Real estate risk – investment properties | 20% |

## Part a (5.25 points)

Determine the capital required for interest rate risk as at December 31, 2015.

### Explanation
**Interest-rate sensitive assets**

Market value $= 40{,}000 + 9{,}000 = 49{,}000$

$$\text{Duration} = \frac{40{,}000 \times 2.0 + 9{,}000 \times 4.0}{49{,}000} = 2.367$$

**APV of Claims Liabilities**

Undiscounted unpaid:

- Paid in 2016 $= 3{,}500 + [20{,}000 \times (0.6 - 0.1) / (1 - 0.1)] = 14{,}611$
- Paid in 2017 $= 20{,}000 \times (1 - 0.6) / (1 - 0.1) = 8{,}889$

Assuming payments at half year:

$$PV_{2\%} = \frac{14{,}611}{1.02^{0.5}} + \frac{8{,}889}{1.02^{1.5}} = 23{,}096$$

$$PV_{1.5\%} = \frac{14{,}611}{1.015^{0.5}} + \frac{8{,}889}{1.015^{1.5}} = 23{,}195$$

- PFAD Claims $= 23{,}096 \times 0.05 = 1{,}155$
- PFAD Interest $= 23{,}195 - 23{,}096 = 99$
- PFAD Reinsurance $= 0$
- APV $= 23{,}096 + 1{,}155 + 99 + 0 = 24{,}350$

**Duration of Claims Liabilities**

$$\text{Modified Duration} = \frac{0.5 \times 14{,}611 / 1.02^{0.5} + 1.5 \times 8{,}889 / 1.02^{1.5}}{23{,}096 \times 1.02} = 0.8565$$

**Premium Liabilities**

Premium liabilities $= 12{,}000 - 1{,}248 = 10{,}752$

**Duration of Premium Liabilities**

Interpolated cumulative payment pattern:

- Age 0.2929 $= 10\% + (60\% - 10\%) \times (0.7071 - 0.5) = 20.36\%$
- Age 1.2929 $= 68.28\%$
- Age 2.2929 $= 100\%$

PV:

- Age 0.2929 $= 20.36\% / 1.02^{0.2929} = 20.24\%$
- Age 1.2929 $= 47.93\% / 1.02^{1.2929} = 46.72\%$
- Age 2.2929 $= 31.72\% / 1.02^{2.2929} = 30.31\%$

$$\text{Modified Duration} = \frac{0.2929 \times 20.24\% + 1.2929 \times 46.72\% + 2.2929 \times 30.31\%}{(20.24\% + 46.72\% + 30.31\%) \times 1.02} = 1.369$$

**Interest Rate Risk Margin**

APV of Liability $= 24{,}350 + 10{,}752 = 35{,}102$

$$\text{Duration of Liability} = \frac{24{,}350 \times 0.8565 + 10{,}752 \times 1.369}{24{,}350 + 10{,}752} = 1.0135$$

- $+1.25\%$: $[49{,}000 \times 2.367 \times 1.25\%] - [35{,}102 \times 1.0135 \times 1.25\%] = 1{,}005$
- $-1.25\%$: $[49{,}000 \times 2.367 \times (-1.25\%)] - [35{,}102 \times 1.0135 \times (-1.25\%)] = -1{,}005$

Interest rate risk margin $= \max(1{,}005;\ -1{,}005) = 1{,}005$

Alternatively, measure the duration of the claims liabilities for each accident year from the change in present value when rates move by 1.25%:

**2014 unpaid** $= 3{,}500$

- $PV_{2\%} = 3{,}500 / 1.02^{0.5} = 3{,}466$
- $PV_{3.25\%} = 3{,}500 / 1.0325^{0.5} = 3{,}444$ [if rates rise by 1.25%]
- $PV_{0.75\%} = 3{,}500 / 1.0075^{0.5} = 3{,}487$ [if rates fall by 1.25%]
- Modified duration $= [3{,}487 - 3{,}444] / [2 \times 0.0125 \times 3{,}466] = 0.4962$
- APV $= 3{,}500 / 1.015^{0.5} + 3{,}466 \times 0.05 = 3{,}647$

**2015 unpaid** $= 20{,}000$

$$PV_{2\%} = \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.5 / 0.9}{1.02^{0.5}} + \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.4 / 0.9}{1.02^{1.5}} = 19{,}630$$

$$PV_{3.25\%} = \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.5 / 0.9}{1.0325^{0.5}} + \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.4 / 0.9}{1.0325^{1.5}} = 19{,}407 \quad \text{[if rates rise by 1.25\%]}$$

$$PV_{0.75\%} = \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.5 / 0.9}{1.0075^{0.5}} + \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.4 / 0.9}{1.0075^{1.5}} = 19{,}860 \quad \text{[if rates fall by 1.25\%]}$$

- Modified duration $= [19{,}860 - 19{,}407] / [2 \times 0.0125 \times 19{,}630] = 0.9213$

$$\text{APV} = \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.5 / 0.9}{1.015^{0.5}} + \frac{20{,}000 \times 0.4 / 0.9}{1.015^{1.5}} + 19{,}630 \times 0.05 = 20{,}703$$

Asset and premium liabilities calculated as above:

- $+1.25\%$: $[49{,}000 \times 2.367 \times 1.25\%] - [10{,}752 \times 1.369 \times 1.25\%] - [3{,}647 \times 0.49 \times 1.25\%] - [20{,}703 \times 0.9213 \times 1.25\%] = 1{,}005$
- $-1.25\%$: $[49{,}000 \times 2.367 \times (-1.25\%)] - [10{,}752 \times 1.369 \times (-1.25\%)] - [3{,}647 \times 0.49 \times (-1.25\%)] - [20{,}703 \times 0.9213 \times (-1.25\%)] = -1{,}005$

Interest rate risk margin $= \max(1{,}005;\ -1{,}005) = 1{,}005$

Or, assuming payments at the end of the year:

$$PV_{2\%} = \frac{14{,}611}{1.02^{1}} + \frac{8{,}889}{1.02^{2}} = 22{,}868 \qquad PV_{1.5\%} = \frac{14{,}611}{1.015^{1}} + \frac{8{,}889}{1.015^{2}} = 23{,}023$$

- PFAD Claims $= 22{,}868 \times 0.05 = 1{,}143$
- PFAD Interest $= 23{,}023 - 22{,}868 = 155$
- PFAD Reinsurance $= 0$
- APV $= 22{,}868 + 1{,}143 + 155 + 0 = 24{,}166$

$$\text{Modified Duration} = \frac{1 \times 14{,}611 / 1.02^{1} + 2 \times 8{,}889 / 1.02^{2}}{22{,}868 \times 1.02} = 1.3467$$

Asset and premium liabilities calculated as above:

- $+1.25\%$: $[49{,}000 \times 2.367 \times 1.25\%] - [10{,}752 \times 1.369 \times 1.25\%] - [24{,}166 \times 1.3467 \times 1.25\%] = 859$
- $-1.25\%$: $[49{,}000 \times 2.367 \times (-1.25\%)] - [10{,}752 \times 1.369 \times (-1.25\%)] - [24{,}166 \times 1.3467 \times (-1.25\%)] = -859$

Interest rate risk margin $= \max(859;\ -859) = 859$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate understanding of the market value components and market value durations that feed into the interest rate risk calculation, including the APV and duration of claims liabilities, the premium liabilities and premium liability duration, and the appropriate interest-sensitive assets and corresponding duration.  Candidates were expected to apply concepts to argue that a change to a private employment insurance market would not be viable.

Common mistakes included:

- Including assets that are not interest-rate sensitive (ie. Cash)
- Dividing asset duration by 1.02 and using the resulting factor in the interest rate margin calculation
- Assuming that only Canadian bonds are interest-rate sensitive
- Incorrectly calculating undiscounted future payments (e.g. incorrectly applying the payment pattern)
- Failing to calculate and use modified duration in the interest rate margin calculation
- Incorrectly calculating premium liabilities (e.g. attempting a full calculation as opposed to subtracting maximum DPAE from unearned premiums)
- Incorrect interpolation of payment pattern for premium liabilities

## Part b (1 point)

Determine the capital required for market risk as at December 31, 2015.

### Explanation
- Equity Risk Margin $= [8{,}000 + 5{,}000] \times 30\% = 3{,}900$
- Real Estate Risk Margin $= 2{,}000 \times 20\% = 400$
- Foreign Exchange Risk Margin $= [9{,}000 + 5{,}000] \times 10\% = 1{,}400$
- Interest Rate Risk Margin $= 1{,}005$
- Market Risk Margin $= 3{,}900 + 400 + 1{,}400 + 1{,}005 = 6{,}705$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate understanding of the elements that are included in the calculation of the market risk margin.

If candidates incorrectly calculated the interest rate risk margin in part a., credit was awarded provided that they correctly included the interest rate risk margin in the calculation of the market risk margin.

Common mistakes included:

- Failing to include interest rate risk margin in the calculation of the market risk margin
- Failing to include all of the elements that should be used in the calculation of the market risk margin
- Incorrectly calculating equity risk (e.g. omitting certain components)
- Incorrectly calculating foreign exchange risk (e.g. omitting certain components)
