An insurer's new six-month PAP (ISO PP 00 01 09 18), not a renewal, has been in force for 90 days, and the premium is paid. Which reason allows the insurer to cancel it now, and with how much notice?

- A) The named insured got two speeding tickets last month; 10 days' notice
- B) The insurer has decided to stop writing auto insurance in that county; 20 days' notice
- C) A resident driver's license was suspended last month; 20 days' notice
- D) The named insured moved to a higher-rated territory; 10 days' notice

## Explanation

After a policy has been in effect 60 days, the insurer may cancel only for nonpayment, for a suspension or revocation during the policy period of the license of the named insured, a driver who lives with the named insured or one who customarily uses the covered auto, or for material misrepresentation. Notice is 10 days for nonpayment and 20 days in all other cases. The suspension of a resident driver's license qualifies, with **20 days' notice**. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — traffic tickets are not a ground for cancellation after 60 days, and 10 days' notice applies only to nonpayment or the first 60 days.
- **B** — leaving a market is not a permitted ground mid-term; the insurer can nonrenew at the end of the period with 20 days' notice.
- **D** — a change in garaging lets the insurer adjust the premium, not cancel.
