The Bank of Canada's Monetary Policy Report for October 2026, published October 28, 2026: a quarterly report of the Bank's Governing Council presenting its base-case projection for inflation and growth in the Canadian economy and its assessment of risks. This issue projects inflation in Canada to remain above 3% through much of 2027, materially higher than previously forecast, and then to settle around the 2% target by the middle of 2028; growth is projected to average 2% in 2027 and 2028. The Report is a product of the Governing Council: Tiff Macklem, Carolyn Rogers, Toni Gravelle, Marc-André Gosselin, Nicolas Vincent and Michelle Alexopoulos.

## Overview
- Inflation is projected to remain above 3% through much of 2027 — longer than anticipated in the July Report — then gradually decline and settle around the 2% target by the middle of 2028 as cost pressures fade; see [[Inflation]].
- Higher gasoline and diesel prices combined with increased cost pass-through have pushed up the forecast for inflation; measures of core inflation are still near 2%.
- The Bank has a new macroeconomic model for projection and policy analysis, Prima, which helped analyze how high energy prices affect economic activity and inflation for this Report.
- Growth in GDP is expected to slow in the fourth quarter of 2026, partly as a result of trade tensions, then pick up and average 2% in 2027 and 2028.

## Current conditions
- Economic activity
    - Activity strengthened heading into the summer
    - Growth is expected to slow as new tariffs weigh on demand
- Capacity pressures
    - Employment is unchanged and job seekers face difficulties
    - Other indicators point to some remaining excess capacity
- Inflation
    - High inflation is limited to a few components so far
    - Cost pressures are building
    - Cost pass-through is expected to increase
    - Near-term inflation expectations have risen
- Commodities
    - Energy prices are expected to stay higher for longer
    - Non-energy commodities prices are mixed
- Financial conditions

## Tariff and other assumptions
- Tariff assumptions — tariffs and related trade measures in place or officially announced as at October 22, 2026 (Table 1: the average US tariff rate on Canada, 7.5%; the Canadian tariff rate on the United States, 3.9%)
- Price assumptions for oil and refined products — oil prices in line with the futures curve as at October 15, 2026; Brent assumed to average about US$100 in the fourth quarter of 2026 and settle around US$75 by the end of 2028
- Other assumptions — the Canadian dollar at an average of 71 cents US; the nominal neutral interest rate at the midpoint of its estimated range of 2.25% to 3.25%

## In focus: Refinery margins and the persistence of energy inflation
- Damage to refineries has pushed refinery margins higher
- Higher margins affect inflation through several channels
- Refinery margins will likely decline gradually

## In focus: Factors behind the rise in long-term sovereign bond yields
- Yields on long-term sovereign bonds have risen sharply since the start of the year, reaching their highest levels in more than a decade across many advanced economies.
- The drivers of higher yields differ across countries
- Markets expect higher policy interest rates
- Term premiums are rising for a variety of reasons

## In focus: Using Prima to trace how an energy shock affects inflation
- Prima accounts for the direct effect on prices
- Prima traces how costs move through the production network
- Prima shows how expected costs affect inflation today
- Prima accounts for demand and trade channels
- Prima offers a richer analysis of inflation dynamics
