A driver with several at-fault accidents has been declined by every auto insurer he has approached, but his state requires auto liability insurance. How is he most likely to obtain coverage?

- A) Through the state's assigned risk plan, which places him with an insurer in the state
- B) Through the state guaranty fund, which insures drivers the voluntary market has rejected
- C) From a reinsurer, which accepts the individual risks that primary insurers decline
- D) Through the state's FAIR Plan, which writes liability coverage for high-risk drivers

## Explanation

Every state and the District of Columbia guarantees that auto insurance is available. In an assigned risk plan, the most common system, high-risk drivers are assigned in proportion to the insurers doing business in the state. This is the residual, or shared, market. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Guaranty funds pay the claims of insolvent insurers; they do not insure drivers.
- **C** — A reinsurer accepts risk from insurers, not individual drivers declined by them.
- **D** — FAIR Plans are residual market property plans, for buildings that cannot get voluntary property insurance; they do not write auto liability.
