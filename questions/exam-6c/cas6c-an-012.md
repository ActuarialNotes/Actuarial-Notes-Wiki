A P&C insurer measures a group of one-year contracts, all issued October 1, under the premium allocation approach. It pays directly attributable acquisition cash flows of 300 at issue. It is deciding whether to make the IFRS 17.59(a) election to expense these costs when incurred, or to defer them and amortize them evenly over the coverage period. Its financial year ends December 31.

Determine which of the following statements are true.

- I. Making the election increases the year's insurance service expenses by 225 relative to deferral.
- II. Making the election makes it more likely that the group is classified as onerous at initial recognition.
- III. Under deferral, the December 31 liability for remaining coverage is reduced by the 225 of acquisition cash flows not yet expensed.

- A) I only
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

- **I is true.** With the election, all 300 is expensed in the year of issue. With deferral, the amortization of insurance acquisition cash flows for October to December is 300 × 3/12 = 75. The difference in insurance service expenses is 300 - 75 = 225.
- **II is false.** The CIA's educational note on the LRC (section 5.5) says that under the 59(a) election, acquisition expenses incurred before or at initial recognition are *excluded* from the onerous assessment, so the election "reduces the likelihood of onerous contract classifications", at the cost of front-ending the expense.
- **III is true.** Under the PAA, the LRC equals premiums received less insurance revenue recognized, **less acquisition costs that are yet to be expensed** (the note's section 5.2 and its worked example). The unamortized amount at December 31 is 300 - 75 = 225.

The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — leaves out III, missing that deferred acquisition cash flows sit inside the LRC rather than in a separate deferred acquisition cost asset.
- **B** and **D** — both include II, which reverses the effect the educational note describes.
- **E** — would need all three statements to be true, and II is false.
