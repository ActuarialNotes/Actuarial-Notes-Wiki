# ACIA Module 1
**ACIA Module 1** is one of the two modules the Canadian Institute of Actuaries requires toward the Associate (ACIA) designation.

> [!question]- About the module
>
> The ACIA modules are a core educational requirement toward ACIA designation. They are intended to bridge accredited university education or education obtained via other actuarial organizations with the work environment.
>
> They are administered online via the CIA learning management system.
>
> The modules assume some knowledge of the topics acquired via a recognized university degree or through other Associate-level education. Outcomes that participants are expected to achieve often cover multiple learning objectives, with an emphasis on communications in most tasks.
>
> The modules contain recordings, short readings, and/or quizzes. Each module includes a case study that includes various deliverables such as emails, reports, memorandums, recorded presentations, or spreadsheets. These case studies apply theoretical topics covered in the participant’s university degree or other recognized Associate-level education to practical actuarial scenarios; the content should not be new to the participant, but the application may be.
>
> There is no formal prerequisite for the ACIA modules. Participants in an accredited university will be expected to have completed some actuarial courses prior to completing the first ACIA module, likely in their second year of university.
>
> The second module should be completed when a participant has nearly completed all courses relevant to the ACIA Capstone Exam. It is expected that these participants undertake the second module in the last year of their university program. Participants are responsible for ensuring they have the appropriate knowledge.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- A. Introduction and actuarial communications
> The [[First Portion|first portion]] of the [[Module Introduce|module introduces]] the profession and gives a general context to the participant; it explains the history of the profession, describes the context of actuarial work and the different fields of practice. Communication in an actuarial setting is also discussed: audiences, technical and [[Non-Technical Writing|non-technical writing]], and the impact of [[Cultural Diversity|cultural diversity]] on communication. Finally, different channels of oral and [[Written Business Communication|written business communication]] (e.g., email, letter, memorandum, report, summary, [[Presentation Deck|presentation deck]], messaging, and [[Chat Software|chat software]]) are introduced. Their purpose is explained, and the [[Formatting Rule|formatting rules]] for such communication are also exposed.
>
> **1. What it means to be an actuary in Canada**
>
> 1. Define the word “[[Actuary|actuary]]” and what it means to be an [[Actuary|actuary]] in Canada. Explain why [[Actuary|actuaries]] are professionals.
> 2. Describe historical [[Event|events]] that have impacted the profession in Canada and worldwide.
> 3. Describe [[Today's|today’s]] areas of practice.
> 4. Discuss the future of the actuarial [[Profession|profession]].
>
> **2. Actuaries and the art of communication**
>
> 1. Understand the different audiences (e.g., peer to peer, within the firm, [[External Client|external clients]], customers).
> 2. Understand [[Cultural Diversity|cultural diversity]] and its impact on communication.
> 3. Develop written and [[Oral Communication|oral communication]] intended for audiences within and outside the [[Actuary's Organization|actuary’s organization]] (i.e., match the audience with the purpose and medium of communication).
> 4. [[Establish Difference|Establish differences]] between technical and [[Non-Technical Writing|non-technical writing]].
> 5. Identify the appropriate use of different channels of [[Written Communication|written communication]] (e.g., email, letter, memorandum, report, summary).
> 6. Understand the main characteristics and structure of these channels (i.e., the various parts of a letter, memorandum, report, [[Summary|summary]]).
> 7. [[Compose Email|Compose emails]], letters, memorandums, reports, and summaries.
> 8. Write effective and [[Concise Written Communication|concise written communication]].
> 9. Prepare [[Meaningful Visual Media|meaningful visual media]] (e.g., charts, tables, infographics, videos) and [[Visual Aid|visual aids]] (e.g., slides).
> 10. Identify key principles of [[Effective Public Speaking|effective public speaking]] and appropriate delivery techniques for in-person and virtual settings.

> [!example]- B. The actuarial environment
> The second portion of the [[Module Describe|module describes]] the actuarial environment: the [[Financial System|financial systems]], the [[External Forces Actuary|external forces actuaries]] are facing, the [[Risks Stemming|risks stemming]] from changes in these [[External Force|external forces]]. [[Risk Management|Risk management]], quantification, mitigation, and transfer are explained in detail. [[Emerging Risk|Emerging risks]] are also described. Finally, the participant is exposed to the impact of regulations on the environment.
>
> **1. The context of actuarial work**
>
> 1. Understand how [[Business Work|business works]] in various environments.
> 2. Outline the context of actuarial work and its [[Main Process|main processes]].
> 3. Define the main steps of [[Risk Management|risk management]]: identification, quantification, and mitigation.
> 4. Explain the major sources of [[Risk|risk]] from (changing) [[External Force|external forces]] and their impact on [[Our Financial System|our financial system]].
>
> **2. Financial systems**
>
> 1. Describe [[Financial System|financial systems]] within the national and global context.
> 2. Describe the [[Main Participant|main participants]] in [[Financial Market|financial markets]] and explain their objectives and roles.
> 3. Describe the major factors affecting the development of [[Financial System|financial systems]].
> 4. Explain the [[Main Risk|main risks]] to the stability of national and [[Global Financial System|global financial systems]].
> 5. Describe the operations, [[Governance Structure|governance structure]], legislative framework, [[Capital|capital]], and [[Risk Management|risk management]] of [[Canadian Financial Institution|Canadian financial institutions]] (e.g., [[Insurer|insurance companies]], banks).
> 6. Describe the determinants of [[Interest Rate|interest rates]], price and [[Wage Inflation|wage inflation]], GDP, and unemployment.
>
> **3. External forces**
>
> 1. Define [[External Force|external forces]].
> 2. Categorize the forces (e.g., cultural, demographic, economic, [[Physical|physical]]) and explain how they fit in the actuarial context.
> 3. Explain how changes in [[External Force|external forces]] can lead to [[Risk|risk]].
>
> **4. Risks and risk management**
>
> 1. Define and describe [[Risk Management|risk management]] in an actuarial context.
> 2. Define [[Risk|risk]] types and classify them.
> 3. Explain how [[Risk|risk]] can be transferred to others (e.g., [[Hedging|hedging]], insurance, [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]], [[Specialized Market|specialized markets]], retained versus ceded).
>
> **5. Emerging risks**
>
> 1. Explain how to identify new relevant [[Risk|risks]].
> 2. Describe the impact of [[Climate Change|climate change]] and [[Cyber Insurance|cyber risk]] on actuarial [[Risk|risks]].
> 3. Explain blockchain and its impact on [[Risk Reduction|risk reduction]].
>
> **6. Regulations**
>
> 1. Describe the regulatory context and the impact of [[Regulation|regulations]] on actuarial work.
> 2. Describe the [[International Environment|international environment]] for actuarial work.

> [!example]- C. Actuarial work
> The [[Third Portion|third portion]] of the [[Module Build|module builds]] on the actuarial environment and explains how [[Actuary|actuaries]] work to provide sound solutions. An overview of actuarial work is provided, with a particular emphasis on the distinction between actuarial work and that of other [[Financial Expert|financial experts]]. The actuarial control cycle is introduced, and each component of actuarial work is described. [[Decision-Making|Decision-making]] and problem solving, and ethics and professionalism are also introduced.
>
> **1. Actuarial work**
>
> 1. Explain the difference between the work of an [[Actuary|actuary]] and that of other [[Financial Expert|financial experts]].
>
> **2. The actuarial control cycle and the different steps of actuarial work**
>
> 1. Describe how actuarial solutions are developed to manage [[Risk|risk]].
> 2. Describe how actuarial [[Solution|solutions]] are designed and priced.
> 3. Describe how models are selected and used in actuarial [[Practice|practice]].
> 4. Describe the [[Process|processes]] used to determine assumptions.
> 5. Describe the elements of the process of [[Monitoring|monitoring]] results.
>
> **3. Decision-making and problem solving**
>
> 1. Understand the dynamics of [[Decision-Making|decision-making]] and outline its process.
> 2. Evaluate the effect of [[Firm Structure|firm structure]] and other stakeholders on [[Decision-Making|decision-making]].
> 3. Define the impact of political, legislative, economic, societal, and [[Technological Trend|technological trends]] on [[Business Strategy|business strategy]].
> 4. Define, use, and apply the following: [[Effective Negotiation|effective negotiation]], [[Decision-Making|decision-making]] skills, leadership, [[Strategic Insight|strategic insights]], relationship management.
> 5. Apply a [[Decision-Making|decision-making process]] to [[Case Study|case studies]] and problem solving.
>
> **4. Ethics and professionalism**
>
> 1. Describe ethics and professionalism and how they apply in [[Everyday|everyday]] work.
> 2. Explain the [[Main Principle|main principles]] of actuarial professionalism.
> 3. Understand the importance of checking work and the need to consider [[Peer Review|peer review]].
> 4. Understand how to determine and apply [[Professional Standard|professional standards]] and guidance appropriately, including when an assignment may be governed by [[Professional Standard|professional standards]] of more than one actuarial organization.
> 5. Understand the importance of being qualified to practice and the impacts of providing an opinion or [[Doing Work Outside|doing work outside]] of your domain.
> 6. Describe necessary [[Actuarial Standards of Practice|standards of practice]] and their impact on actuarial work (e.g., [[Data Quality|data quality]], assumptions).
> 7. Understand the principles and importance of diversity, equity, and [[Inclusion|inclusion]].
