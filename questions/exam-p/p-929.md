A random sample of size 3 is drawn without replacement from the integers $\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$. The maximum value in the sample is denoted $M$.

The probability function of $M$ is $P[M=m] = \dfrac{3(m-1)(m-2)}{n(n-1)(n-2)}$ for $m=3,\ldots,n$.

Calculate $\text{Var}(M)$.

- A) $\dfrac{3(n+1)(n-3)}{20}$
- B) $\dfrac{3(n-1)(n-3)}{80}$
- C) $\dfrac{3(n+1)(n-3)}{80}$
- D) $\dfrac{(n+1)(n-1)}{20}$
- E) $\dfrac{(n-1)(n-3)}{20}$

## Explanation

Work with the factorial moments $E[M]$ and $E[M(M+1)]$: they turn each sum into a sum of binomial coefficients. Since $m(m-1)(m-2) = 6\binom{m}{3}$ and $(m+1)m(m-1)(m-2) = 24\binom{m+1}{4}$,

$$
\begin{align*}
E[M] &= \frac{3}{n(n-1)(n-2)}\sum_{m=3}^{n} m(m-1)(m-2) = \frac{18}{n(n-1)(n-2)}\sum_{m=3}^{n}\binom{m}{3} \\
E[M(M+1)] &= \frac{3}{n(n-1)(n-2)}\sum_{m=3}^{n} (m+1)m(m-1)(m-2) = \frac{72}{n(n-1)(n-2)}\sum_{m=3}^{n}\binom{m+1}{4}
\end{align*}
$$

Pascal's rule $\binom{j+1}{k} = \binom{j}{k} + \binom{j}{k-1}$ makes both sums telescope (with $\binom{3}{4} = \binom{4}{5} = 0$):

$$
\begin{align*}
\sum_{m=3}^{n}\binom{m}{3} &= \sum_{m=3}^{n}\left[\binom{m+1}{4}-\binom{m}{4}\right] = \binom{n+1}{4} = \frac{(n+1)n(n-1)(n-2)}{24} \\
\sum_{m=3}^{n}\binom{m+1}{4} &= \sum_{m=3}^{n}\left[\binom{m+2}{5}-\binom{m+1}{5}\right] = \binom{n+2}{5} = \frac{(n+2)(n+1)n(n-1)(n-2)}{120}
\end{align*}
$$

So

$$
\begin{align*}
E[M] &= \frac{18}{n(n-1)(n-2)}\cdot\frac{(n+1)n(n-1)(n-2)}{24} = \frac{3(n+1)}{4} \\
E[M(M+1)] &= \frac{72}{n(n-1)(n-2)}\cdot\frac{(n+2)(n+1)n(n-1)(n-2)}{120} = \frac{3(n+1)(n+2)}{5} \\
E[M^2] &= E[M(M+1)] - E[M] = \frac{3(n+1)\left[4(n+2)-5\right]}{20} = \frac{3(n+1)(4n+3)}{20}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(M) &= E[M^2]-E[M]^2 \\
              &= \frac{3(n+1)(4n+3)}{20}-\frac{9(n+1)^2}{16} \\
              &= \frac{3(n+1)}{80}\left[4(4n+3)-15(n+1)\right] \\
              &= \mathbf{\frac{3(n+1)(n-3)}{80}}
\end{align*}
$$

Check with $n = 4$: $M = 3$ with probability $1/4$ and $M = 4$ with probability $3/4$, so $\text{Var}(M) = \frac{57}{4} - \left(\frac{15}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{3}{16}$, and $\frac{3(5)(1)}{80} = \frac{3}{16}$.
