Given the following information below regarding a series of bonds:

| Bond | Maturity (Years) | Face Value | Annual Coupon |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1.0 | \$1000 | 0% |
| B | 2.0 | \$1000 | 0% |
| C | 3.0 | \$1000 | 3% |
| D | 3.0 | \$1000 | 0% |

- The price of bond A is \$951.23.
- The two-year spot rate is 8%, continuously compounded.
- The price of bond C is \$901.78.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Plot the pure yield curve.

### Explanation
Assume all rates are continuously compounded, as that is how the 2-year spot rate is provided.

- Bond A: $951.23 = 1000e^{-r_1}$, so $r_1 = 5\%$.
- $r_2 = 8\%$ (given).
- Bond C pays coupons of $.03 \times 1000 = 30$: $901.78 = 30e^{-r_1} + 30e^{-r_2 \times 2} + 1030e^{-r_3 \times 3}$, so $3r_3 = .1948$ and $r_3 = 6.49\%$.

Plot the yield against time to maturity: $(1,\ .05)$, $(2,\ .08)$ and $(3,\ .0649)$ — the curve rises from year 1 to year 2, then falls to year 3.

Alternatively, assume all interest rates are annual unless specified otherwise:

- $951.23 = \dfrac{1000}{1 + y_1}$, so $y_1 = .0513$
- $e^{-.08(2)} = \dfrac{1}{(1 + y_2)^2}$, so $y_2 = .0833$
- $901.78 = \dfrac{30}{1.0513} + \dfrac{30}{1.0833^2} + \dfrac{1030}{(1 + y_3)^3}$, so $y_3 = .0671$

and plot $(1,\ .0513)$, $(2,\ .0833)$, $(3,\ .0671)$.

### Examiner Report
Candidate should be able to calculate the rates at 3 different maturities, and present it clearly with a graph, in order to obtain full credit.

Common errors were due to miscalculation. Other candidates confused forward rates with zero rates.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Explain the shape of the pure yield curve plotted in part a. above using a term-structure theory of interest rates.

### Explanation
The yield curve above reflects the expectations theory of term structure. Rates are expected to rise for 2 years, but then decrease after that.

Alternatively: segmentation theory — the yields offered at each point in time (short, medium, long term) are driven by the supply and demand for bonds at that time. There must be a low demand for bonds at $t = 2$, which is why it is offering a higher rate than at times 1 and 3.

### Examiner Report
Candidates should be able to explain the different term structure theories to obtain full credit.

Most  candidates  got  this  part  correctly.  One  of  the  common  mistakes  here  is  the  demand  supply relationship and how it affects the yields. A number of candidates incorrectly thought that a higher demand would correspond to a higher yield.
