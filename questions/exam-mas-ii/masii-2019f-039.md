Dataset Z contains 4 variables and 100 records and has the following correlation matrix.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1.00 & 0.93 & 0.08 & -1.00 \\
0.93 & 1.00 & 0.10 & -0.93 \\
0.08 & 0.10 & 1.00 & -0.08 \\
-1.00 & -0.93 & -0.08 & 1.00
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Determine which of the following plots of cumulative proportional variance is produced by principal components analysis on Dataset Z.

![Five candidate plots of cumulative proportion of variance explained against principal component 1 to 4. Plot A rises in a near-straight line from about 27 to 100. Plot B rises from about 40 through 73 and 90 to 100. Plot C rises from about 58 to 89, reaches 100 at component 3 and stays flat at 100 for component 4. Plot D rises from about 50 to 100 at component 2 and is flat at 100 for components 3 and 4. - A) Plot A
- B) Plot B
- C) Plot C
- D) Plot D
- E) Plot E

## Explanation

The decisive feature is the $-1.00$ in the corner: variable 4 is a perfect negative linear function of variable 1. That makes the correlation matrix **singular**, so one eigenvalue is exactly zero and the matrix has rank 3.

A zero eigenvalue means the fourth principal component explains *no* additional variance. So the cumulative curve must:

- reach **100% at component 3**, and
- be **flat over the final segment** from component 3 to component 4.

Checking the plots against that signature:

- **A, B and E** all keep rising to 100% only at component 4 — they have four non-zero eigenvalues, so they cannot come from a singular matrix.
- **D** is already at 100% by component 2 and flat thereafter, which needs *two* zero eigenvalues (rank 2). But variable 3 is nearly uncorrelated with everything else, so it contributes a substantial eigenvalue of its own; the rank is 3, not 2.
- **C** rises to 100% at component 3 and is flat from 3 to 4 — exactly one zero eigenvalue.

Working the eigenvalues out confirms it: they are approximately 2.92, 0.99, 0.09 and 0, giving cumulative proportions of roughly 73%, 98%, 100%, 100%. The first component absorbs the collinear trio $\{1, 2, 4\}$, the second is essentially variable 3, and the last is empty.

The answer is **C**.
