An electrician wires a new kitchen in a restaurant incorrectly. Months later the faulty wiring starts a fire that damages the kitchen. The restaurant is insured under the BPP with the causes of loss special form. How does the form treat the loss?

- A) The whole loss is excluded, because faulty workmanship caused it
- B) The whole loss is covered, including the cost to redo the faulty work, because fire is a specified cause of loss
- C) Only the wiring is covered, because the fire damage is a consequence of the excluded cause
- D) The fire damage is covered, because a covered cause of loss resulted from the faulty workmanship

## Explanation

The special form excludes faulty, inadequate or defective workmanship, but that exclusion ends with an exception: if an excluded cause results in a covered cause of loss, the form pays for the loss caused by that covered cause. The fire is a covered cause of loss that resulted from the faulty wiring, so the fire damage is covered. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Stops at the exclusion's first sentence and ignores the ensuing-loss exception.
- **B** — The exception pays for loss caused by the resulting fire; it does not turn the faulty workmanship itself into covered damage.
- **C** — Reverses the rule: the excluded cause is the faulty wiring, and the ensuing fire damage is what the exception restores.
