Given the following information:

| Claim ID | Accident Date | 2013 Calendar Year Payments | 2013 Ending Case O/S | 2014 Calendar Year Payments | 2014 Ending Case O/S | 2015 Calendar Year Payments | 2015 Ending Case O/S |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | January 1, 2013 | 80 | 150 | 25 | 100 | 100 | - |
| 2 | June 1, 2013 | 20 | 50 | 25 | 50 | 100 | - |
| 3 | May 1, 2014 | | | 100 | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| 4 | December 15, 2014 | | | 50 | 250 | 150 | 100 |
| 5 | April 1, 2015 | | | | | 50 | 50 |

## Part a (0.75 points)

Construct an accident year cumulative paid claims triangle.

### Explanation
Cumulative paid claims by accident year (months of development):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 100 | 150 | 350 |
| 2014 | 150 | 350 | |
| 2015 | 50 | | |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to build accident year paid claim triangles using transactional claim data.
A common mistake was calculating cumulative case reserves instead of cumulative paids.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Construct an accident year cumulative reported claims triangle.

### Explanation
Reported claims are paid plus case outstanding, accumulated the same way:

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 300 | 300 | 350 |
| 2014 | 450 | 500 | |
| 2015 | 100 | | |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to build accident year reported claim triangles using transactional claim data.
A common mistake was not including all claims for a particular evaluation date.

## Part c (0.5 points)

In 2015 the claims department began paying claims faster without changing the adequacy of case reserves. Produce and briefly discuss a triangle that demonstrates that this action has been successfully executed.

### Explanation
The ratio of cumulative paid to cumulative reported claims:

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 0.333 | 0.500 | 1.000 |
| 2014 | 0.333 | 0.700 | |
| 2015 | 0.500 | | |

The paid-to-reported ratio rises along the latest diagonal (calendar year 2015): 0.500 at 24 months for 2014 against 0.333 a year earlier, and 0.500 at 12 months for 2015 against 0.333 for both prior years. Paid claims are growing faster than reported claims. Since the question states that case reserve adequacy has not changed, the change must come from quicker settlement — which is what the claims department said it did.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to create a paid/reported diagnostic triangle to demonstrate the claims department has been paying claims faster. Further, candidates were expected to comment on how the increased ratio of paid claims to reported claims in the latest diagonal demonstrates faster payments. Full credit was also given to candidates who created a reported/paid diagnostic triangle and commented on the decreasing ratio of reported claims to paid claims in the latest diagonal.
Common mistakes included:

- Creating a diagnostic triangle other than a paid/reported (or reported/paid triangle) such as paid/case, average paid severity or average case outstanding
- Using paid LDFs as a diagnostic
- Neglecting to explain the ratio increase in the latest diagonal of the paid/reported triangle is a result of faster payments.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Recommend and briefly justify a technique for calculating ultimate claims for this dataset, given the change noted in part c. above.

### Explanation
Use the **reported** development technique. The payment pattern has changed, so paid development factors drawn from history no longer describe how this book pays and would overstate ultimate claims. Reported development is unaffected by the payment pattern, and case adequacy has not changed, so the reported factors still hold.

Alternatively, apply the Berquist-Sherman adjustment to the paid triangle to restate it at the new settlement rate, then develop the adjusted paid claims.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to adjust the data and estimation techniques when there is a change in the claims handling process. Candidates were expected to explicitly state a technique that was appropriate for the new claims handling process and give sufficient reasoning why the stated technique will accurately calculate ultimate claims.
For candidates to obtain full credit, they could have selected any technique relying on reported claims and mention that this technique is unaffected by the faster payments. Full Credit was awarded to candidates who selected the expected claims method, the reported B-F method or the Cape Cod method, as long as the candidate explained how these methods were not impacted by the change in settlement rates.
Full credit was also awarded to candidates who selected the Paid Berquist Sherman technique and mentioned this technique will restate or adjust the historical paid triangle for the recent change in settlement rates.
Common mistakes included:

- Insufficient reasoning given for the selected technique
- Incorrect technique given
- For candidates who selected the Paid Berquist Sherman technique, some neglected to discuss the fact that the historical paid triangle needs to be restated to account for the faster payment pattern.
- For candidates who selected a reported claims technique, some only mentioned the case adequacy has not changed. It was important to also state the fact that the reported claims technique is not impacted by changes in settlement rates.
