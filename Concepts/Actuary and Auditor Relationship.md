The **Actuary and Auditor Relationship** is the professional interaction between the actuary who determines amounts in an insurer's financial statements and the [[External Auditor]] who audits those statements. For a Canadian P&C insurer the actuary is the [[Appointed Actuary]], who values the [[Insurance Contract Liabilities|insurance contract liabilities]], and the relationship is governed by the **Joint Policy Statement** (JPS) approved by the Canadian Actuarial Standards Board and the Auditing and Assurance Standards Board, effective for communications initiated on or after 31 March 2023.

Three parties hold separate responsibilities:

| Party | Responsible for |
| :--- | :--- |
| Management | The financial statements |
| Auditor | The audit opinion |
| Actuary | The valuation of the insurance contract liabilities |

The JPS says that using the other professional's work "does not constitute reliance", so neither professional's responsibility is reduced by it.

## Use of each other's work

The *inquiring* professional uses the work of the *responding* professional, and use runs both ways. The actuary is responsible for judging whether data are relevant, sufficient and reliable, and may use the auditor's procedures on data integrity. The auditor may use the actuary's assumptions, methods and data as audit evidence for the liabilities.

The responding professional replies in writing, confirming its qualification and its work. For the actuary the qualification is Fellowship of the [[Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)|CIA]], and for the auditor membership of a professional accounting body. An auditor's response states that its work on the data is not an assurance engagement, so the actuary cannot describe the data as "verified by the auditor".

> [!example]- Can the actuary rely on this audited ledger? {Example}
> The actuary finds a $\$12$ million unexplained difference between claims-system payments and the general ledger. The CFO says the auditors have tested the ledger, so the actuary can rely on the audited figures.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Under the JPS, the actuary may ask the auditor what procedures were performed and on what, and may use that work.
> > - The auditor's response will say its work is not an assurance engagement on the data.
> > - Responsibility for concluding that the data are sufficient and reliable stays with the actuary.
> > - So the actuary must investigate the difference, decide whether it is material to the valuation, and disclose it and its treatment in the [[Appointed Actuary's Report]].

## Matters settled at planning

Communication starts when both engagements are planned, with management's permission for each professional to talk to and share information with the other. The inquiring professional states the intended use of the work and seeks confirmation that the other is appointed, in good standing and working to their professional standards. The two then discuss:

1. The accounting framework and policy choices, against the actuarial valuation choices.
2. [[Materiality]].
3. [[Subsequent Events|Subsequent events]].
4. The timing and dates of their reports.

> [!example]- What should the actuary and auditor settle here? {Example}
> At planning, the auditor tells the Appointed Actuary it intends to use the valuation as audit evidence with a materiality of $\$5$ million. The actuary's valuation materiality is $\$8$ million. The actuary plans to date the report 5 March, and the audit report will be dated 20 March.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Approximations and unexplained differences up to $\$8$ million are acceptable to the actuary but could be material to the audit.
> > - So either the actuary works to a threshold no greater than $\$5$ million for the items the auditor will use, or the auditor plans more procedures of its own.
> > - Events between 5 and 20 March, such as a large claim or a court decision, could affect the liabilities after the actuary has signed.
> > - The two agree how the actuary will consider matters that come to attention up to the date of the actuary's report, and align the dates or agree an update to cover the gap.

## Outside the Joint Policy Statement

The JPS does not cover communications between the auditor and the auditor's own actuary, or between the actuary and an external review actuary ([[Peer Review]]).

The two professionals meet at the audit committee of the board ([[Corporate Governance]]). A disagreement over a judgement, such as the [[Risk Adjustment for Non-Financial Risk|risk adjustment]], a [[IFRS 17 Discount Rates|discount rate]] or [[Risk Transfer|risk transfer]] on a reinsurance contract, belongs there.
