You are building a model to understand student test scores. Your model is as follows:

$$
y_{ij} = \beta_0 + x_{ij}\beta_1 + \tau_i + \epsilon_{ij}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\beta_0 &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu, \sigma_0^2) \\
\beta_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0, \sigma_1^2) \\
\tau_i &\sim \text{Normal}(0, \sigma_2^2) \\
\mu &\sim \text{Normal}(0, \sigma_3^2) \\
\epsilon_{ij} &\sim \text{Normal}(0, \sigma_4^2)
\end{align*}
$$

where student $j$ in classroom $i$ has a final exam score of $y_{ij}$ and a midterm score of $x_{ij}$.

You feel that the classroom data is not dependable and, therefore, you want to shrink the classroom-specific effects toward the overall mean.

Determine how you would adjust the hyperparameters for this model.

- A) Reduce the value of $\sigma_0^2$
- B) Reduce the value of $\sigma_1^2$
- C) Reduce the value of $\sigma_2^2$
- D) Reduce the value of $\sigma_3^2$
- E) Reduce the value of $\sigma_4^2$

## Explanation

$\tau_i$ is the classroom-specific effect, and $\sigma_2^2$ is the variance of its prior. Read that prior as a credibility statement: it says how far a classroom is allowed to depart from the overall mean before the data must work hard to justify it.

Tightening the prior — reducing $\sigma_2^2$ — pulls every $\tau_i$ toward 0, which is exactly shrinking the classroom effects toward the overall mean. In the limit $\sigma_2^2 \to 0$ all $\tau_i = 0$ and the classrooms are pooled completely.

The other hyperparameters govern different quantities:

- $\sigma_0^2$ and $\sigma_3^2$ tighten the global intercept and its hyper-mean, not the classroom departures.
- $\sigma_1^2$ tightens the midterm slope.
- $\sigma_4^2$ is the student-level residual variance — reducing it says the observations are *more* reliable, which pushes the model toward trusting the classroom data more, not less.

Reduce $\sigma_2^2$, so the answer is **C**.
