A warehouse submission says the building is fully sprinklered. In NFPA's data, most sprinkler systems that failed to operate in fires large enough to activate them failed for one reason. Given that finding, which question should the underwriter ask first?

- A) How are the sprinkler valves supervised, so a system shut off is noticed?
- B) How many sprinkler heads are installed per thousand square feet?
- C) Was the system installed by a licensed contractor?
- D) Is the system wet pipe or dry pipe?

## Explanation

In NFPA's 2004–2008 data, sprinklers operated in 91% of fires large enough to activate them. Of the failures to operate, 64% were systems that had been shut off before the fire. Asking how the valves are supervised and the system maintained goes to the most common cause of failure. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Usually only one or two heads are needed to control a fire, and head density doesn't address why systems fail.
- **C** — Installation quality matters, but the data point to systems turned off after installation, not to how they were installed.
- **D** — Wet pipe systems did operate more often than dry pipe ones, but the difference is smaller than the effect of a closed valve.
