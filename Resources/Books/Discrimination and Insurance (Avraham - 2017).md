A chapter by Ronen Avraham asking when it is fair and just for insurers to discriminate between groups on the basis of their actuarial risk. It sets out the confusion in the legal treatment of anti-discrimination norms in insurance, argues that the philosophical literature on discrimination is written at too high a level of abstraction to guide insurance policy, and proposes a theoretical framework to help policy makers apply a fair and just anti-discrimination policy. Written for The Routledge Handbook to Discrimination (ed. Lippert-Rasmussen) and issued as a University of Texas School of Law research paper.

## Introduction
- Example 1: in 2011 the European Court of Justice forbade charging women less than men for life insurance (Test-Achats), a practice not entirely forbidden in many US states.
- Example 2: in the late 1970s and early 1980s the US Supreme Court forbade charging women more than men for employer-provided pension insurance, or paying them smaller monthly benefits (Manhart, Norris), a practice not entirely forbidden in the EU.
