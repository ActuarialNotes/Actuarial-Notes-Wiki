A website requires a five-character password consisting of exactly three distinct characters selected from the 26 upper-case letters of the alphabet and exactly two characters, not necessarily distinct, selected from the ten digits. The password must begin with one of the selected letters.

Calculate the maximum number of unique passwords, in millions, the site will accommodate.

- A) $3.120$
- B) $4.212$
- C) $4.680$
- D) $8.424$
- E) $9.360$

## Explanation

Build the password in stages.

**Choose the letters and the leading one.** Pick 3 distinct letters from 26, then designate which of the three goes first:

$$
\binom{26}{3}(3) = 2600(3) = 7800
$$

**Place the other two letters.** After the first position is filled, four positions remain. The two remaining letters are distinct, and which one goes where matters, so this is a permutation:

$$
{}_4P_2 = 4(3) = 12
$$

That gives $7800 \times 12 = 93{,}600$ ways to place all the letters.

**Fill the two leftover positions with digits.** The digits need not be distinct, so each of the two open positions has 10 choices, independently:

$$
10^2 = 100
$$

Multiplying the stages:

$$
\begin{align*}
93{,}600 \times 100 &= 9{,}360{,}000 \\
                    &= \mathbf{9.360 \text{ million}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 4.680, is half the answer — it comes from treating the two non-leading letters as an unordered pair, $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ instead of ${}_4P_2 = 12$. The letters are distinct, so swapping them produces a different password and the ordering must be counted.
