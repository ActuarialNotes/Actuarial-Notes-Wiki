Given the following information about two companies in different industries:

- ABC is in an industry that is expected to become more competitive.
- XYZ has a business plan that relies on the successful development of a new product.
- One company has Ba-rated debt, and the other B-rated debt.
- B-rated and Ba-rated debt have expected cumulative default rates described in the table below:

| Term (years) | 5 | 10 | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ba | 10% | 20% | 30% |
| B | 30% | 50% | 58% |

## Part a (1 point)

Evaluate which bond rating more accurately describes the expected change in probability of default as a function of time for Company XYZ.

### Explanation
ABC is already established. It is not likely to default in the near future; however, as competition grows, it will be more likely to default. This is reflected in Ba, since the conditional probability of default increases: 10%, then 11.1%, then 12.5% over the three 5-year periods.

XYZ is unlikely to survive the next couple of years if the new product fails. However, if the new product is successful and XYZ survives those years, it is more likely to survive longer. This is reflected in the decreasing conditional default probability of B: 30%, then 28.1% (as printed), then 16%.

**Change in the unconditional probability of default over time:**

| | 5–10 yrs | 10–15 yrs |
|---|---|---|
| A (Ba) | 20 - 10 = 10% | 30 - 20 = 10% |
| B | 50 - 30 = 20% | 58 - 50 = 8% |

Bond rating B more accurately reflects the expected change in probability of default as a function of time for XYZ. Since XYZ requires the successful development of a new product, the earlier years of development are the most critical and should see the greatest change in default probability. Once the company has survived that initial period and the new product is successful, it shouldn't see major changes in default probability. This is best shown by B, since 20% > 8% (while Ba stays at 10% in each period), which may relate better to ABC, being in an industry that is becoming more competitive.

**Hazard rates.** Default intensity given survival:

| Year | 5 | 10 | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ba | 0.1 | 0.111 | 0.125 |
| B | 0.3 | 0.286 | 0.16 |

As seen above, the default intensity $\lambda(t)$ decreases for the B rating, which matches company XYZ. Much depends on the successful development of a new product, which makes the short term risky (70% over 5 years); but if XYZ survives, its chance of default in the next 5 years is as shown above, and the trend goes on for 10–15 years as well. So again, B. ABC's rate goes up, as in a competitive environment.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to recognize that a company that was reliant on a new product would have a period with a high probability of default followed by declining hazard rates if the product is successful.  By far, the most common error was not including any discussion about the declining hazard rate portion of the pattern.
Other errors were generally of the form where candidates argued that the Ba-rated bond with the constant hazard rate was more appropriate.

## Part b (1 point)

Assuming both companies are still in business in 10 years, use the table of default rates to determine which company is expected to have better credit-worthiness.

### Explanation
Hazard rate for year 15 (default in years 10–15, given survival to year 10):

- (ABC) Ba: $\dfrac{0.3 - 0.2}{1 - 0.2} = 12.5\%$
- (XYZ) B: $\dfrac{0.58 - 0.5}{1 - 0.5} = 16\%$

Since the Ba-rated bond (ABC) has the lower hazard rate, ABC is more creditworthy.

### Examiner Report
Most of the errors for part b were using the change in the unconditional probability instead of using the hazard rates to make a decision on which company would have better creditworthiness.
Since the question asked under the condition that both companies survived, the answer needed to use the conditional probability or hazard rates.  Most other deductions were caused by calculation errors.
