Losses are uniformly distributed on the interval $[0, 2000]$. An insurance policy pays the amount of each loss up to a maximum of $m$. The expected value of the claim payment on a random loss is 910.

Calculate $m$.

- A) $910$
- B) $1150$
- C) $1400$
- D) $1600$
- E) $1820$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the loss, with density $1/2000$ on $[0, 2000]$. The payment is $\min(X, m)$: the loss itself below $m$, and the cap $m$ above it. Splitting the expectation at $m$:

$$
\begin{align*}
910 &= \int_0^m \frac{x}{2000}\,dx + \int_m^{2000} \frac{m}{2000}\,dx \\
    &= \frac{m^2}{4000} + \frac{m(2000 - m)}{2000} \\
    &= m - \frac{m^2}{4000}
\end{align*}
$$

Clearing the denominator gives a quadratic:

$$
m^2 - 4000m + 3{,}640{,}000 = 0
$$

whose roots are $m = 1400$ and $m = 2600$. The second exceeds the maximum possible loss of 2000, so

$$
m = \mathbf{1400}
$$

Choice (A), 910, sets the limit equal to the expected payment. A cap always has to sit above the expected payment it produces: capping at 910 would give an expected payment of $910 - 910^2/4000 = 703$, well short of the required 910.
