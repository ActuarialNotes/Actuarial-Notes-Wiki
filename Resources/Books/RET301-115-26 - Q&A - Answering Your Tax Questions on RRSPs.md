A BDO Canada tax article, updated June 24, 2024, that answers common questions on whether and how much to contribute to a Registered Retirement Savings Plan, a Tax-Free Savings Account or the new First Home Savings Account (FHSA). It compares the three accounts, sets out the factors in choosing between them for a first home, retirement or a high-value purchase, and ends with a summary of deadlines and contribution limits and a table comparing the accounts.

## What are the key benefits of an RRSP and a TFSA?
- An [[Registered Retirement Savings Plan|RRSP]] gives a tax deduction for contributions up to certain limits, tax-free accumulation of earnings, and tax only on withdrawal.
- The RRSP annual contribution limit is based on the prior year's earned income, and withdrawals do not adjust it.
- A TFSA gives no deduction, has an annual limit set by statute rather than earned income, and its withdrawals are not taxable and can be re-contributed in a future year.

## Is an FHSA similar to an RRSP or a TFSA?
- The FHSA lets prospective first-time home buyers save tax-free to purchase a home within 15 years; the account holder must be at least 18, a resident of Canada and a first-time home buyer.
- As with an RRSP, contributions are deductible and undeducted contributions can be carried forward, but they must be made within the calendar year to be deductible.
- As with a TFSA, withdrawals to purchase a qualifying home, including income and gains, are not taxable, but contribution room is not restored when withdrawals are made.
- The annual contribution limit is $8,000, the lifetime limit is $40,000, and the most unused room that can be carried forward is $8,000.

## Should I contribute to a TFSA, FHSA, or RRSP?
- Factors named are the funds available, the marginal tax rate now and in the future, and the savings goals.
- Purchase of a first home
    - Funds in an FHSA that is not used to buy a home can be transferred directly to an RRSP with no immediate tax consequences and without reducing unused RRSP deduction room.
    - The Home Buyers' Plan allows a withdrawal of up to $60,000 from an RRSP after April 16, 2024, to be repaid over 15 years.
- Retirement
    - The choice between RRSP and TFSA depends largely on the current marginal tax rate compared with the rate expected in retirement.
- Purchase of a high-value item
    - A TFSA withdrawal restores contribution room at the start of the following calendar year.

## If I'm already a homeowner, can I contribute to my adult child's FHSA?
- A homeowner cannot contribute directly to a child's or grandchild's FHSA, but can gift the funds for them to contribute.

## If I decide to emigrate from Canada, what happens to my FHSA, RRSP and TFSA?
- A withdrawal from an FHSA or RRSP by a non-resident is subject to withholding tax of 25%, unless an FHSA withdrawal is transferred to an RRSP.
- A TFSA may be kept as a non-resident, but contribution room does not accrue for non-resident years and contributions made as a non-resident attract a monthly penalty tax.
