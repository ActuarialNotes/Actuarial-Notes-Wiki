A point $P$ is randomly selected in the Cartesian plane in such a way that the distance from $P$ to the origin has a uniform distribution on the interval $[0, 3]$.

Calculate the variance of the area of the circle that is centered at the origin and passes through $P$.

- A) $9\pi^2/16$
- B) $3\pi^2/4$
- C) $3\pi^2/2$
- D) $36\pi^2/5$
- E) $81\pi^2/5$

## Explanation

Let $R$ be the distance, uniform on $[0,3]$ with density $1/3$. The circle through $P$ has area

$$
A = \pi R^2
$$

The area is a *nonlinear* function of $R$, so its moments must be computed from $R$'s distribution rather than from $E[R]$.

$$
\begin{align*}
E[A] &= \pi E[R^2] = \pi\int_0^3 \frac{r^2}{3}\,dr = \pi\frac{27}{9} = 3\pi \\
E[A^2] &= \pi^2 E[R^4] = \pi^2\int_0^3 \frac{r^4}{3}\,dr = \pi^2\frac{243}{15} = \frac{81\pi^2}{5}
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(A) &= E[A^2] - \bigl(E[A]\bigr)^2 \\
              &= \frac{81\pi^2}{5} - 9\pi^2 \\
              &= \frac{81\pi^2 - 45\pi^2}{5} \\
              &= \mathbf{\frac{36\pi^2}{5}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E) is $E[A^2]$ with the squared mean never subtracted. Note that $E[A] = 3\pi$ is *not* $\pi(E[R])^2 = \pi(1.5)^2 = 2.25\pi$ — computing the area from the mean radius understates it, because $E[R^2] > (E[R])^2$ by exactly $\text{Var}(R)$.
