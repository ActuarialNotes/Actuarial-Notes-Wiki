An insurer's CFO says: "We booked our GAAP loss reserves at the top of the actuary's range so that our statements are conservative, which the conceptual framework encourages." How should this claim be evaluated under FASB Concepts Statement No. 8?

- A) Correct, because prudence is a component of faithful representation
- B) Incorrect, because a deliberate lean conflicts with neutrality
- C) Correct, because conservatism is one of the enhancing characteristics
- D) Incorrect, because estimates cannot be faithfully represented at all

## Explanation

Concepts Statement No. 8 does not include prudence or conservatism as an aspect of faithful representation, because including either would be inconsistent with **neutrality**. A neutral depiction is not slanted to make information received favourably or unfavourably, and the FASB notes that understating assets or overstating liabilities in one period tends to overstate performance in a later one. The answer is **B**.

Conservatism is a concept of statutory accounting, which the NAIC adopts because regulators focus on solvency; it is not part of the GAAP framework.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — this is the older view. The current framework removed prudence from faithful representation.
- **C** — the enhancing characteristics are comparability, verifiability, timeliness and understandability. Conservatism is not among them.
- **D** — an estimate can be faithfully represented if it is described as an estimate, its limits are explained and the process is applied without error.
