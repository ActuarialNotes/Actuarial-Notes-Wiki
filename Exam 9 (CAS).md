# Exam 9

**Risk Management for Actuaries (Exam 9)** is a 4-hour computer-based exam (4.5-hour Pearson VUE appointment, with a 15-minute scheduled break) covering catastrophe modeling and reinsurance pricing, risk and return, financial risk management, and enterprise risk management.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- A. Catastrophe and Reinsurance {25–35%}
> Candidates are expected to be able to interpret the [[Catastrophe Modelling|methods used to model losses]] due to [[Catastrophe Risk|catastrophic events]] for the purpose of generating a [[Catastrophe Expected Loss Cost|catastrophe expected loss cost]]. Candidates are also expected to manage the [[Catastrophe Exposure Management|total exposure]] from [[Catastrophe Risk|catastrophic events]] within an [[Insurance Portfolio|insurance portfolio]].
>
> Candidates will also be familiar with current and historical [[Reinsurance Pricing|methods used to price reinsurance]], with an emphasis on the application of these methods in [[Reinsurance Pricing|pricing]] [[Reinsurance|reinsurance contracts]].
>
> 1. Fit [[Severity Distribution|loss distribution]]-based [[Exposure Curves|exposure curves]].
> 2. Determine the [[Loss Cost|loss cost]] of various [[Types of Reinsurance|types of reinsurance contracts]] (e.g., [[Excess of Loss|excess of loss]], [[Quota Share|quota share]], [[Surplus Share|surplus share]], [[Treaty Reinsurance|treaty]], [[Aggregate Excess of Loss|aggregate excess of loss]], and [[Facultative Reinsurance|facultative]]).
> 3. Determine the effect of [[Reinsurance Contract Provisions|common contract provisions]] (e.g., [[Reinstatements|reinstatements]], [[Loss Corridors|loss corridors]], [[Clash Cover|clash covers]], [[Profit Commission|profit]] and [[Sliding Scale Commissions|sliding scale commissions]]) on the [[Loss Cost|loss cost]] of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance contracts]].
> 4. Evaluate [[Catastrophe Modelling|catastrophe models]].
> 5. Apply [[Catastrophe Modelling|catastrophe models]] to [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]].
>
> **Readings:** Grossi & Kunreuther · Clark · Bernegger

> [!example]- B. Risk and Return {20–30%}
> Candidates can define, compare, select, and apply appropriate [[Risk Measure|risk measures]] to compute a [[Risk Loads|risk-loaded technical premium]]. Candidates can evaluate the [[Economic Value|economic value]] of [[Insurance Cash Flows|insurance cash flows]] reflecting the [[Time Value of Money|time value of money]]. Candidates can explain and apply the [[Insurance Profitability|insurance concepts]] of [[Insurance Margin|margin]], [[Return on Capital|return]], and [[Insurance Leverage|leverage]].
>
> 1. Define and apply [[Risk Taxonomies|risk taxonomies]] and the concept of a [[Risk Measure|risk measure]].
> 2. Calculate and compare [[Financial Risk|financial risk]] using [[Risk Measure|risk measures]].
> 3. Evaluate the [[Economic Value|economic value]] of [[Insurance Cash Flows|insurance cash flows]] reflecting the [[Time Value of Money|time value of money]], including the selection of [[Interest Rate|interest rates]].
> 4. Determine [[Risk Loads|risk loads]] for [[Insurance Pricing|insurance pricing]].
> 5. Assess [[Insurance Profitability|insurance profitability]] by [[Insurance Profitability|decomposition]] (e.g., [[Return on Capital|return on equity]], [[Insurance Margin|margin on premium]], [[Insurance Leverage|leverage]]).
>
> **Readings:** Mildenhall & Major

> [!example]- C. Financial Risk Management (FRM) {20–30%}
> Candidates can describe and explain the need, purpose, design, and execution of insurer [[Financial Risk Management|financial risk management]] ([[Financial Risk Management|FRM]]) and perform the calculations necessary to allocate the [[Cost of Capital|cost of capital]], estimate [[Risk-Adjusted Pricing|risk-adjusted prices]], and evaluate [[Risk-Adjusted Performance|economic performance]] by [[Line of Business|line]] or [[Business Unit|unit]].
>
> 1. Describe and explain the [[Financial Risk|financial risk]] and [[Capital Structure|capital structure]] of [[Insurer|insurers]], the [[Cost of Capital|cost of insurance capital]], and the impact and sources of [[Insurance Market Imperfections|insurance market imperfections]] (e.g., [[Insurance Market Imperfections|informational asymmetries]], [[Insurance Market Imperfections|competition]], [[Insurance Market Imperfections|potential financial distress]]).
> 2. Allocate the [[Cost of Capital|cost]] of [[Risk Capital|risk capital]] to [[Business Unit|business units]] or [[Line of Business|lines of business]].
> 3. Set [[Insurance Pricing|prices]] for [[Insurance Policy|insurance policies]] on a [[Risk-Adjusted Pricing|risk-adjusted basis]].
> 4. Assess [[Risk-Adjusted Performance|risk-adjusted performance]] of [[Business Unit|business units]] or [[Line of Business|lines of business]].
> 5. Estimate and manage [[Interest Rate Risk|interest rate]] and [[Credit Risk|credit risk]] (e.g., [[Credit Risk|default]], [[Prepayment Risk|prepayment]], [[Reinsurance Credit Risk|reinsurance]]).
> 6. Describe and apply the basics of [[Structured Finance|structured finance]].
> 7. Describe and apply [[Securitization|securitization]] and its applications to the management of [[Catastrophe Risk|catastrophe risk]] (e.g., [[CAT Bonds|CAT bonds]], [[Insurance-Linked Securities|insurance-linked securities]]).
>
> **Readings:** Coval, Jurek, and Stafford · Cummins Capital · Cummins CAT Bond · Mildenhall & Major · Panning

> [!example]- D. Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) {15–25%}
> Candidates can define and explain [[Enterprise Risk Management]] ([[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]]), including [[Model Selection|model selection]] and calculations necessary to implement a realistic [[Enterprise Risk Management|program]]. Candidates can describe the relationship between [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]] and the [[Risk|overall risk]] that a [[Business Risk|business]] faces, and the impact of different [[Risk Mitigation|ERM tools]] on [[Business Risk|business risk]] and [[Business Strategy|strategy]].
>
> 1. Describe the [[Enterprise Risk Management|common process of ERM]].
> 2. Determine [[Operational Risk|operational]] and [[Business Risk|strategic risks]].
> 3. Analyze [[Insurance Risk|insurance]] and [[Financial Risk|financial risk]] quantitatively.
> 4. Determine the effect of [[Risk Measure|risk measures]] and [[Risk Modeling|risk modeling]] on [[Business Strategy|strategic management]].
> 5. Select [[Risk Modeling|models]] to manage [[Risk Taxonomies|diverse risks]] and evaluate [[Correlation|correlations]] of [[Risk|risks]].
> 6. Develop [[Risk Mitigation|risk mitigation]] strategies.
> 7. Assess effectiveness of [[Risk Mitigation|risk mitigation strategies]].
>
> **Readings:** Brehm et al
