# Exam GI-301
**GI 301 – Further Topics in General Insurance** is an exam of the Society of Actuaries' Fellowship (FSA) pathway. Its syllabus covers the Mar 2027/Jul 2027 sittings.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Stochastic Reserving {15–25%}
> The candidate will understand how to use [[Stochastic Reserving|stochastic loss development]] models to estimate [[Unpaid Claim Distribution|reserve variability]].
>
> 1. Identify the assumptions underlying the [[Chain Ladder Assumptions|chain ladder estimation]] method.
> 2. Test for the validity of these [[Assumption|assumptions]].
> 3. Identify alternative models that should be considered depending on the results of the [[Test|tests]].
> 4. Estimate the [[Standard Deviation|standard deviation]] of a [[Mack Chain Ladder Model|chain ladder estimator]] of [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]].
> 5. Apply a parametric model of [[Loss Development|loss development]].
> 6. Estimate the [[Standard Deviation|standard deviation]] of a [[Growth Curve|parametric estimator]] of [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]].
> 7. Assess whether a fitted model is acceptable given the [[Data|data]] being modeled.
>
> **Readings:**
> - Outstanding Claims Reserves (Version 1.3a), SOA, 2022, Ch. 3-5 (excluding sections 3.4 & 4.3) (1a-d)
> - Considerations Regarding the Chain Ladder Model, SOA, May 2025 (excluding section 3) (1a-d)
> - Testing the Assumptions of Age-to-Age Factors, Venter G.G., Proceedings of the Casualty Actuarial Society LXXXV, 1998, pp. 812-838 This article may be accessed at www.casact.org through the following navigation: (1a-d)
>     - Publications & Research

>     - Result: Testing the Assumptions of Age-to-Age Factors
> - Stochastic Loss Reserving Using Generalized Linear Models, CAS Monograph Series Number 3, 2016 (1b-d)
>     - Ch. 5: The Bootstrap, section 5.3, pp. 47-51 (start at the paragraph “The bootstrap is…” pp.47)
>     - Ch. 6: Model Validation (excluding section 6.7 and the portion of section 6.4 on pp. 64)
> - LDF Curve Fitting and Stochastic Reserving: A Maximum Likelihood Approach, Clark, D.R., Casualty Actuarial Society Forum, Fall 2003 This article may be accessed at www.casact.org through the following navigation: (1e-g)

>     - Result: LDF Curve-Fitting and Stochastic Reserving: A Maximum Likelihood Approach

> [!example]- Development Analysis for Excess Limits and Layers {5–10%}
> The candidate will understand the considerations in the development of losses for [[Excess Loss Development Factor|excess limits]] and [[Layer of Insurance|layers]].
>
> 1. Estimate [[Ultimate Loss|ultimate claims]] for [[Excess Loss Development Factor|excess limits]] and [[Layer of Insurance|layers]].
> 2. Understand the differences in [[Loss Development|development patterns]] and [[Trend|trends]] for [[Excess Loss Development Factor|excess limits]] and [[Layer of Insurance|layers]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, SOA, 2022 (2a, 2b)
>     - Appendix G: Development Analysis for Excess Limits and Layers

> [!example]- Premium Liabilities {0–5%}
> The candidate will understand the procedure for estimating [[Premium Liability|premium liabilities]].
>
> 1. Understand the purpose of general [[Premium Liability|insurance premium liabilities]].
> 2. Calculate the [[Premium Liability|premium liabilities]] for a general [[Insurer|insurance company]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, SOA, 2022 (3a, 3b)
>     - Ch. 25: Premium Liabilities
>     - Appendix A: Dentist Insurer, section A.3.6
>     - Appendix D: ABC Manufacturing Company/ABCMC Captive, section D.6

> [!example]- Risk Margins for Unpaid Claims {5–10%}
> The candidate will understand the considerations in selecting a [[Risk Margin|risk margin]] for [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]].
>
> 1. Describe a [[Risk Margin|risk margin]] analysis framework.
> 2. Identify the sources of uncertainty underlying an estimate of [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]].
> 3. Describe methods to assess this [[Uncertainty|uncertainty]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - A Framework for Assessing Risk Margins, Marshall, K., Collings, S., Hodson, M., and O’Dowd, C., Institute of Actuaries of Australia 16th General Insurance Seminar, Nov 2008 (4a-c)

> [!example]- Monitoring Results {0–10%}
> The candidate will understand the methods to monitor actual versus expected [[Experience|experience]].
>
> 1. Identify and describe approaches for [[Monitoring|monitoring]] results.
> 2. Prepare a comparison of actual to expected [[Claim|claims]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, SOA, 2022 (5a, 5b)
>     - Ch. 37: Monitoring Results
>     - Appendix E: Public Entity Self-Insurance Pool, sections E.3.1 & E.3.2

> [!example]- Specialized Ratemaking Topics {25–35%}
> The candidate will understand and be able to apply [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]] techniques for the following situations: [[Classification Ratemaking|classification ratemaking]], [[Deductible Rating|deductible options]], [[Increased Limits|increased limit options]], [[Claims Made Coverage|claims-made policies]] and [[Individual Risk Rating|individual risk rating]].
>
> 1. Understand and apply [[Classification Ratemaking|classification ratemaking]] methods.
> 2. Explain the issues and considerations regarding [[Classification Ratemaking|classification ratemaking]].
> 3. Price for [[Deductible Rating|deductible options]] and [[Increased Limits|increased limits]].
> 4. Develop rates for [[Claims Made Coverage|claims-made contracts]].
> 5. Understand and apply techniques for [[Individual Risk Rating|individual risk rating]].
> 6. Estimate the [[Retrospective Premium Reserve|premium asset]] for retrospectively [[Retrospective Rating|rated policies]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, SOA, 2022 (6a, 6b)
>     - Ch. 33: Basic Risk Classification
> - Classification Ratemaking, Minimum Bias and GLMs, SOA, May 2025, section 4 (6a)
> - Actuarial Standards of Practice, No. 12, Risk Classification (for All Practice Areas), Actuarial Standards Board of the American Academy of Actuaries, Last Revised 2011 (excluding Appendix 2) (6b)
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, SOA, 2022
>     - Ch. 34: Actuarial Pricing for Deductibles and Increased Limits (6c)
>     - Appendix D: ABC Manufacturing Company/ABCMC Captive, section D.4 (6c)
>     - Appendix E: Public Entity Self-Insurance Pool, sections E.3.4 & E.3.5 (6c)
>     - Ch. 35: Claims-Made Ratemaking (6d)
>     - Ch. 36: Individual Risk Rating and Funding Allocation for Self-Insurers (excluding section 36.7) (6e)
> - Estimating the Premium Asset on Retrospectively Rated Policies, Teng, M. and Perkins, M., Casualty Actuarial Society, 1996 Proceedings, Vol. LXXXIII, pp. 611-647 (excluding section 5) (6f)
> - This article may be accessed at www.casact.org through the following navigation:

>     - Result: Estimating the Premium Asset on Retrospectively Rated Policies

> [!example]- Reinsurance {15–20%}
> The candidate will understand the fundamentals of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] and demonstrate knowledge of [[Reinsurance Reserving|reinsurance reserving]], the fundamental techniques of [[Reinsurance Pricing|reinsurance pricing]] and [[Risk Transfer Testing|risk transfer testing]] of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance contracts]].
>
> 1. Understand the types of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] and key [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] terms.
> 2. Explain the [[Principal|principal]] functions of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]].
> 3. Analyze and describe the various types of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]].
> 4. Understand the issues encountered when applying [[Loss Development|loss development]] methods to [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]].
> 5. Calculate the price of a [[Reinsurance|reinsurance contract]].
> 6. Apply an aggregate distribution model to a [[Reinsurance Pricing|reinsurance pricing]] scenario.
> 7. Describe considerations involved in [[Reinsurance Pricing|pricing property catastrophe covers]].
> 8. Understand the application of a [[Reinstatements|reinstatement premium]].
> 9. Test for [[Risk Transfer|risk transfer]] in [[Reinsurance|reinsurance contracts]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, SOA, 2022 (7a-c)
>     - Ch. 10: A Reinsurance Primer Including: Errata for Chapter 10 of Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis
> - General Insurance: Considerations in Reinsurance Reserving, SOA, May 2025 (7d)
> - Basics of Reinsurance Pricing, Clark, D.R., Actuarial Study Note, 2014 (7e-h)
> - Risk Transfer Testing of Reinsurance Contracts, Brehm, P. and Ruhm, D., Variance, 2007, Volume 01, Issue 01, CAS, sections 2-5, pp. 11-17 (begin with first complete paragraph on pg. 11) This article may be accessed at www.casact.org through the following navigation: (7i)
>     - Publications & Research
>     - Browse research -> [Author Last Name = Brehm, Year Published = 2007] -> Search
>     - Result: Risk Transfer Testing of Reinsurance Contracts
> - Insurance Risk Transfer and Categorization of Reinsurance Contracts, Gurenko, E., Itigin, A. and Wiechert, R., Policy Research Working Paper No. 6299, World Bank, Dec 2012, pp. 3-21 (end with section III on pp. 21) (7i)
> - Risk Transfer Practice Note, AAA, Nov 2022, section 4 (7i)

> [!example]- Catastrophe Models and Risk Loads {5–10%}
> The candidate will understand [[Catastrophe Model Output|catastrophe modeling output]] and the allocation of [[Catastrophe Risk Load|catastrophe risk loads]] among accounts.
>
> 1. Understand the purpose and development of [[Catastrophe|catastrophe]] models.
> 2. Understand the type of output produced by [[Catastrophe|catastrophe]] models.
> 3. Understand how catastrophe model [[Output|output]] can be used in actuarial tasks.
> 4. Allocate a [[Catastrophe Risk Load|property catastrophe risk load]] among different accounts.
>
> **Readings:**
> - Uses of Catastrophe Model Output, AAA, Jul 2018 (excluding “Florida Commission on Hurricane Loss Projection Methodology” section on pp. 6, 7, 29-30, 35 & all Appendices) (8a-c)
> - An Overview of Catastrophe Modeling Output, SOA, May 2025 (8b, 8c)
> - An Application of Game Theory: Property Catastrophe Risk Load, Mango, D.F., PCAS LXXXV, 1998, CAS This article may be accessed at www.casact.org through the following navigation: (8d)
