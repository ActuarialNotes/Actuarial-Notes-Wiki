## Part a (2.25 points)

Calculate the Table L charges at loss ratios of 0% to 100% in 20% increments.

### Explanation
Expected loss ratios read off the two diagrams:

$$\text{Exp LR for unlimited} = 0.2 \times 0.2 + 0.2 \times 0.4 + 0.2 \times 0.6 + 0.2 \times 0.8 + 0.1 \times 1 = 0.5$$

$$\text{Exp LR for limited} = 0.1 \times 0.2 + 0.3 \times 0.4 + 0.3 \times 0.6 + 0.1 \times 0.8 = 0.4$$

$$\text{LER} = 1 - (0.4 / 0.5) = 0.2$$

Working down from 100%, each charge is the charge at the next higher loss ratio plus the percentage of risks above times the step in $r$:

| Loss Ratio | r (limited LR / unlimited LR) | # risks (limited) | % risks above | Table L Charge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 0 | 2 | 0.8 | 0.68 + 0.8 × (0.4) = 1 |
| 20% | 0.2/0.5 = 0.4 | 1 | 0.7 | 0.4 + 0.7 × (0.4) = 0.68 |
| 40% | 0.4/0.5 = 0.8 | 3 | 0.4 | 0.24 + 0.4 × (0.4) = 0.4 |
| 60% | 1.2 | 3 | 0.1 | 0.2 + 0.1 × (1.6 - 1.2) = 0.24 |
| 80% | 1.6 | 1 | 0 | 0.2 + 0 × (2 - 1.6) = 0.2 |
| 100% | 2 | 0 | 0 | LER = 0.2 |

Alternatively, with $E[x] = .5$, $E[x'] = .4$ and $k = 1 - (E[x'] / E[x]) = 1 - (.4/.5) = .2$:

| Loss Ratio | # at LR | # Over | Double Sum | Partial Charge (n) | $\phi^* = (n)(1 - k) + k$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2 | 8 | 20 | 1 = 20/20 | 1 |
| 20 | 1 | 7 | 12 | .6 = 12/20 | .68 |
| 40 | 3 | 4 | 5 | .25 = 5/20 | .4 |
| 60 | 3 | 1 | 1 | .05 = 1/20 | .24 |
| 80 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 = 0/20 | .2 |
| 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | .2 |

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know how to calculate Table L charges when given limited and unlimited loss ratios.
- Candidates were expected to calculate Table L charges for loss ratios requested.
- When using the % of risks above method, candidates often divided by 8 instead of 10 to get the percentage of risks above the given loss ratio. When calculation errors occurred and their charge at 0 was not equal to 1, the candidate was expected to know to normalize the Table L charges. Some candidates calculated the LER as the difference between the unlimited and limited loss ratios.
- Generally candidates either received full credit on this question or they received very few points. Most candidates performed well on this question.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Describe what the Table L savings at an entry ratio of 0.4 reflects, assuming an expected unlimited loss of \$500,000 and a per accident limit of \$100,000.

### Explanation
$R = .4$, $E = 500{,}000$, per accident limit = 100K.

An entry ratio of .4 corresponds to a hypothetical aggregate limit of 200k ($E$ of 500k × .4). This aggregate limit is on limited losses, limited by the 100k per accident limit. The savings at the entry ratio of .4 describes the amount by which 200,000 on average exceeds the occurrence-limited losses (A + B below).

![Hand-drawn Lee diagram: loss size X on the vertical axis against F(X) on the horizontal, with an unlimited curve F(x) above a limited curve F*(x). A dashed horizontal line at 200K; the region below the dashed line and above the limited curve is hatched and labelled A and B, and the regions C (below the limited curve) and D (above the line between the curves) are left unhatched](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas8-2014-q12-table-l-savings.png)

Alternatively: $R \times E = 0.4 \times 500000 = 200000$. The Table L savings at $r = 0.4$ and $E = 500{,}000$ with a per-accident limit of 100000 is the average amount below 200,000 but above the limited loss curve.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know what the Table L savings means and express it in words.
- Candidates needed to express that the savings is the average difference between the loss amount corresponding to the entry ratio of 0.4 (200,000) and the actual losses limited to the per occurrence limit of 100,000.
- Candidates often didn't accurately describe what the savings actually are and tried to use Table L buzz words to get partial credit.
- Many candidates calculated the savings based on their part a results, while the question never indicated that the scenario in b applied to the data in a.
- Very few candidates received full credit on this part.
- We gave partial credit when candidates calculated the correct savings amount based on their work from part a. We also gave partial credit when they drew a correctly labeled graph that displayed the Table L savings area.
