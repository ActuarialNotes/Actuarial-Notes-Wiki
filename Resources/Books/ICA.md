The federal **[[Insurance Companies Act]]** — the statute governing federally regulated insurers in Canada, and the source of the [[Appointed Actuary]]'s legal duties. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective C5. The syllabus assigns specified sections of Chapter 47, updated to 12 December 2017:

> **165(1), 165(2), 203, 331(1), 331(2), 331(4), 346, 357–370, 464, 465, 476–478, 517, 581, 625–632, 641, 664, 665, 667(1), 667(2) and 674.**

## What the assigned sections cover

| Sections | Subject |
|---|---|
| **165(1)–(2)** | **Duties of directors** — the board's responsibility to manage or supervise the management of the company's business and affairs, and the specific duties it must discharge (including appointing the actuary and the auditor, and establishing the required committees) |
| **203** | Financial statements and their approval |
| **331(1), (2), (4)** | Adequacy of capital and liquidity — the requirement to maintain adequate capital and adequate and appropriate forms of liquidity, and the Superintendent's power to direct an increase |
| **346** | Related provisions on the company's financial position |
| **357–370** | **The actuary of the company** — appointment by the directors, qualifications and disqualifications, revocation, the **valuation of policy liabilities in accordance with [[Accepted Actuarial Practice\|generally accepted actuarial practice]]**, the report to the directors, the requirement to meet with the directors or the audit committee, the **[[Duty to Report\|duty to report]]** to the officers and directors — and to the Superintendent where they do not act — on any matter having material adverse effects on the company's financial condition, the right of access to records, and the **qualified privilege** protecting a statement made in good faith |
| **464–465** | The **auditor**, and the relationship between the auditor and the actuary |
| **476–478** | Further audit and reporting provisions |

## Why the statute is on the syllabus

Everything else in Domain C's professional-responsibility material derives its force from here. [[CIA CSOP|Standards of Practice]] bind the actuary professionally, but the Act is what makes "accepted actuarial practice" a **legal** requirement and what makes the appointed actuary a statutory officer rather than an employee with a title. Three points are examined repeatedly:

- **The board appoints the actuary**, not management — which is what gives the role its independence.
- **The duty to report escalates.** The actuary reports to the officers and directors; if they do not take suitable action, the actuary reports to the **Superintendent**. Reporting is protected by qualified privilege.
- **The Superintendent's powers** are supervisory and preventive, exercised well before insolvency; a formal winding-up order under the *Winding-Up and Restructuring Act* is what triggers [[PACICC]].
