The size $X$, in thousands, of a randomly selected claim on a commercial property policy has density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{5 - x}{8}, & 1 < x < 5 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

The insurer sends every claim larger than a threshold $t$ for special review, and sets $t$ so that 10% of claims are sent.

Calculate $t$.

- A) 1.21
- B) 1.74
- C) 3.74
- D) 4.20
- E) 4.60

## Explanation

Ten percent of claims exceed $t$, so $P(X > t) = 0.10$ and $F(t) = 0.90$: the threshold is the 90th percentile of $X$. Integrate the density from the bottom of its support, $x = 1$, to get the distribution function:

$$
\begin{align*}
F(t) &= \int_1^{t} \frac{5 - x}{8}\,dx \\
     &= \left[-\frac{(5 - x)^2}{16}\right]_1^{t} \\
     &= 1 - \frac{(5 - t)^2}{16}, \qquad 1 < t < 5
\end{align*}
$$

Set it equal to 0.90 and take the root inside the support:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 - \frac{(5 - t)^2}{16} &= 0.90 \\
(5 - t)^2 &= 1.6 \\
t &= 5 - \sqrt{1.6} \\
  &= \mathbf{3.74}
\end{align*}
$$

Check: $P(X > t) = (5 - t)^2 / 16 = 1.6 / 16 = 0.10$. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** (1.21) solves $F(t) = 0.10$, giving the 10th percentile: below it lie the 10% of *smallest* claims, not the 10% that exceed it.
- **B** (1.74) integrates from 0 instead of from 1, where the support begins, solving $\int_0^{t} \frac{5 - x}{8}\,dx = 0.90$. That counts an extra $\tfrac{9}{16}$ of area on $(0, 1)$, where the density is actually zero, so the cumulative probability reaches 0.90 far too early.
- **D** (4.20) solves $f(t) = 0.10$, treating the density's height as a probability. A probability for a continuous random variable is an area under $f$, not its value at a point.
- **E** (4.60) is the 90th percentile of a uniform distribution on $(1, 5)$, $t = 1 + 0.9 \times 4$. The density falls from $\tfrac{1}{2}$ at $x = 1$ to 0 at $x = 5$, so large claims are rarer and the percentile sits lower.
