During a survey of a machine shop insured for general liability and workers compensation, the risk control representative finds that the shop has added a welding operation that wasn't on its application. Which other functions most need this finding?

- A) Claims, to reopen closed files, and marketing, to promote welding coverage
- B) Marketing, to raise the producer's commission, and premium audit, to cancel the audit
- C) Claims alone, because only a loss can show whether the welding is hazardous
- D) Underwriting, to re-evaluate the account, and premium audit, to classify the payroll

## Explanation

Risk control gives underwriters the information they need to accept, modify or decline an account, and a new operation means the account should be re-evaluated, the monitoring step of the underwriting process. It also shares what it learns about operations with premium auditing, which must assign the welders' payroll to the right classification at audit. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Closed claims aren't affected by a new operation, and promoting coverage isn't the issue.
- **B** — Commission has nothing to do with the finding, and the audit is more important, not less, when operations change.
- **C** — Waiting for a loss defeats the purpose of a survey.
