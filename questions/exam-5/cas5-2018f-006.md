Two methods of deriving expense provisions in ratemaking include the premium-based projection method and the exposure-based projection method.

## Part a (1 point)

For each method, briefly describe how both fixed and variable expenses are treated.

### Explanation

Both methods begin the same way: each expense category (commissions, taxes/licenses/fees, general, other acquisition) is split into fixed and variable components, and the variable piece is handled identically in the two methods.

**Premium-based projection method**

- *Variable expenses:* divided by premium — written premium if the expense is incurred when the policy is written (e.g. commissions), earned premium if incurred over the policy period — to produce a variable expense ratio.
- *Fixed expenses:* also divided by premium, producing a fixed expense **ratio to premium**. If fixed expenses and premium are trending at different rates, a trend adjustment can be applied to that ratio.

**Exposure-based projection method**

- *Variable expenses:* treated exactly as in the premium-based method — divided by written or earned premium to get a variable expense ratio.
- *Fixed expenses:* divided by **exposures** (or policy count) — written exposures if incurred at policy inception, earned exposures if incurred over the policy period — producing an average fixed expense **per exposure** in dollars. A fixed expense trend can be applied to project that average dollar amount to the future level.

In both methods the ratios are computed for each historical year and a selection representative of future expense levels is made.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to explain how fixed and variable expenses are treated under the two common methodologies, distinguishing fixed and variable expenses from total expenses.

Common mistakes included not mentioning the separation of total expense into fixed and variable buckets, and not identifying the use of exposures or policy count for the fixed expense portion of the exposure-based method.

## Part b (0.5 point)

Briefly describe one shortcoming (or distortion) of each method.

### Explanation

**Premium-based projection method.** Because fixed expenses are expressed as a percentage of premium, a rate change during or after the experience period distorts the historical fixed expense ratios — the ratio falls when rates rise even though the underlying dollar expense has not changed. It also means high-premium policyholders are charged a proportionally larger share of costs that are genuinely fixed, which is not appropriate.

**Exposure-based projection method.** The average fixed expense per exposure is distorted by economies of scale in a changing book — a growing or shrinking book changes the average fixed cost per exposure independently of the true cost level. It can also be distorted if exposures trend at a different rate than expenses, or where fixed expenses genuinely vary by risk characteristic (new business typically costs more to write than renewal business).

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to understand and describe a potential shortcoming or distortion of each method.

Common mistakes included, for the premium-based method, stating the need for on-level premium without explaining why; and, for the exposure-based method, simply stating that exposures must be clearly defined, are not readily available, are difficult to estimate, or are subject to change.
