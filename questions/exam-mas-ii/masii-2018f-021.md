In the table below, $p_i$ represents the true distribution of events and $q_i$ represents a model's estimates of $p_i$.

| $i$ | $p_i$ | $q_i$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.2 | 0.1 |
| 2 | 0.3 | 0.2 |
| 3 | 0.5 | 0.7 |

Calculate the Kullback-Leibler divergence.

- A) Less than or equal to 0.090
- B) Greater than 0.090 but less than or equal to 0.095
- C) Greater than 0.095 but less than or equal to 0.100
- D) Greater than 0.100 but less than or equal to 0.105
- E) Greater than 0.105

## Explanation

The Kullback-Leibler divergence of the model $q$ from the true distribution $p$ is the expected log-ratio of the two, with the expectation taken under the **true** distribution:

$$
D_{KL}(p, q) = \sum_i p_i \ln\frac{p_i}{q_i}
$$

It measures the extra uncertainty introduced by using $q$ to describe events that actually follow $p$, and it is the distance that information criteria such as AIC and WAIC set out to estimate.

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{KL}(p, q) &= 0.2\ln\frac{0.2}{0.1} + 0.3\ln\frac{0.3}{0.2} + 0.5\ln\frac{0.5}{0.7} \\
&= 0.2(0.6931) + 0.3(0.4055) + 0.5(-0.3365) \\
&= 0.1386 + 0.1216 - 0.1682 \\
&= 0.0920
\end{align*}
$$

The order matters: weighting by $q$ instead, $\sum_i q_i \ln(q_i/p_i) = 0.0851$, would land in (A).

This falls in $(0.090, 0.095]$, so the answer is **B**.
