A subcontractor's written contract assumes the project owner's tort liability for injuries arising from the subcontractor's work and also its cost of defense. An injured worker of another trade sues both, and the subcontractor's CGL insurer defends the subcontractor. Which fact, on its own, means the insurer need **not** defend the owner as a supplementary payment?

- A) The owner's answer to the complaint blames the subcontractor alone, while the subcontractor blames the owner
- B) The suit seeks damages that exceed the subcontractor's each occurrence limit
- C) The owner is not shown on the subcontractor's policy as an additional insured
- D) The owner is a corporation rather than an individual

## Explanation

The insurer defends an indemnitee named in the same suit only if all the conditions in paragraph 2 are met. One is that the allegations and what the insurer knows show no conflict between the interests of the insured and the indemnitee. Each blaming the other is such a conflict. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — the size of the claim doesn't remove the obligation; it ends only when the limit has been used up in judgments or settlements.
- **C** — this defense obligation rests on the insured contract, not on additional insured status; the indemnitee need not be an insured.
- **D** — nothing in the conditions turns on the indemnitee's form of organization.
