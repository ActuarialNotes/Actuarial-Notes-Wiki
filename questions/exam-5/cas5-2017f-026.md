Given the following information:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Paid Claims (\$000) as of 12 months | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 750 | 1,125 | 1,350 | 1,485 |
| 2014 | 2,000 | 3,000 | 3,600 | |
| 2015 | 2,500 | 3,750 | | |
| 2016 | 3,000 | | | |

| Accident Year | Calendar Year Paid ULAE (\$000) |
|---|---|
| 2013 | 220 |
| 2014 | 220 |
| 2015 | 330 |

- Case reserves at December 31, 2016 = \$3,500,000.
- IBNR reserves at December 31, 2016 = \$1,000,000.
- The four-year weighted average ULAE to loss ratio = 10%.
- No business was written prior to 2013.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Estimate the unpaid ULAE using the classical technique.

### Answer
\$275,000

### Explanation
The classical (paid-to-paid) technique assumes ULAE is incurred half at the *opening* of a claim and half over its *life*. So for claims already reported and open, half the ULAE effort has already been spent — only the case reserves get 50% weight — while claims not yet reported still require the full effort, so IBNR gets 100% weight:

$$\text{Unpaid ULAE} = \text{ULAE ratio} \times \left(0.50 \times \text{case reserves} + 1.00 \times \text{IBNR}\right)$$

Using the given four-year weighted average ratio of 10%:

$$\text{Unpaid ULAE} = 0.10 \times \left(0.50 \times \$3{,}500{,}000 + \$1{,}000{,}000\right) = 0.10 \times \$2{,}750{,}000 = \$275{,}000$$

Selecting a lower ratio is also defensible on this data, since the calendar year ratios (computed in part b) are falling: at 9%, the estimate would be $0.09 \times \$2{,}750{,}000 = \$247{,}500$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to estimate unpaid ULAE with the classical technique, demonstrating that the ratio is applied to 50% of case reserves and 100% of IBNR reserves. The given four-year average 10% paid-to-paid ratio was expected, but credit was also awarded for calculating and selecting an appropriate ratio from the data.

Common errors were calculation mistakes and selecting an inappropriate ULAE ratio.

## Part b (1 points)

Calculate the paid ULAE to paid claims ratio for calendar year 2016.

### Answer
8.3%

### Explanation
Calendar year 2016 paid ULAE is not given directly — it has to be backed out of the four-year weighted average ratio. That requires calendar year paid claims, which are the sum of the *incremental* payments along each diagonal of the accident year triangle:

$$\text{CY paid claims} = \sum_{AY} \left( \text{cumulative}_{AY, m} - \text{cumulative}_{AY, m-12} \right)$$

| CY | Incremental payments | CY paid claims |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 750 | 750 |
| 2014 | $(1{,}125 - 750) + 2{,}000$ | 2,375 |
| 2015 | $(1{,}350 - 1{,}125) + (3{,}000 - 2{,}000) + 2{,}500$ | 3,725 |
| 2016 | $(1{,}485 - 1{,}350) + (3{,}600 - 3{,}000) + (3{,}750 - 2{,}500) + 3{,}000$ | 4,985 |
| **Total** | | **11,835** |

Reading the paid claims by accident year instead of by calendar year is the classic trap here: the denominator of a paid-to-paid ratio must be the claims paid *during* that calendar year, matching the ULAE actually paid out that year.

The four-year *weighted* average ratio is total ULAE over total claims, so total paid ULAE across 2013-2016 is:

$$0.10 \times 11{,}835 = 1{,}183.5$$

Subtracting the three known years leaves calendar year 2016:

$$\text{CY 2016 paid ULAE} = 1{,}183.5 - 220 - 220 - 330 = 413.5$$

$$\text{CY 2016 paid ULAE to paid claims ratio} = \frac{413.5}{4{,}985} = 8.3\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to determine the calendar year 2016 paid ULAE to paid claims ratio from the information provided, recognizing that the four-year weighted average ratio is what supplies the missing 2016 ULAE.

Common errors included confusing accident year paid claims with calendar year paid claims, not realizing the four-year ratio was needed to solve for 2016, and developing losses or ULAE to ultimate.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Assess the reasonableness of the unpaid ULAE estimate from part a. above.

### Explanation
The part a estimate is **too high**. The paid-to-paid ratios by calendar year are falling steadily:

| CY | Paid ULAE | CY paid claims | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 220 | 750 | 29.3% |
| 2014 | 220 | 2,375 | 9.3% |
| 2015 | 330 | 3,725 | 8.9% |
| 2016 | 413.5 | 4,985 | 8.3% |

The 10% four-year weighted average is pulled up by 2013, the company's first year of writing, when a small book of paid claims was carried by a full year's claims-department overhead — a start-up ratio that is not representative of a going concern. Every year since has come in below 10%, and the trend is still downward as the book grows and fixed claim-handling costs are spread over more paid claims.

Applying a 10% ratio to future unpaid claims therefore overstates unpaid ULAE. A ratio selected from recent experience — a weighted average of 2014-2016, roughly 8.7%, or simply the latest year's 8.3% — would give a more appropriate estimate. This also flags that the classical technique's steady-state assumption is violated here: the book is growing rapidly, which is precisely why the historical ratio does not carry forward.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to recognize that the ULAE ratios were declining over time and to conclude that the part a result was not appropriate given that shift in the paid-to-paid ratios.

Common errors included not referencing the changing paid-to-paid ratios at all; asserting that an assumption of the classical method was violated — for example that the growing book breaks the steady-state assumption — without explaining why that leads to an unreasonable estimate; and giving inadequate justification for the assessment, such as saying the result looks reasonable because it is in line with the average.
