The random variable $Y$ follows a Poisson distribution with mean parameter $\lambda$. Six posterior samples for $\lambda$ are provided in the table below:

| $i$ | $\lambda_i$ |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 1 |
| 4 | 3 |
| 5 | 4 |
| 6 | 2 |

The following additional information is provided:

- The first realized observation from $Y$, $y_1$, is 3
- The contribution from $y_1$ to the effective number of parameters ($p_{WAIC}$) is 0.2019

Calculate the contribution to WAIC from $y_1$.

- A) Less than 1.05
- B) Greater than or equal to 1.05 but less than 2.05
- C) Greater than or equal to 2.05 but less than 3.05
- D) Greater than or equal to 3.05 but less than 4.05
- E) Greater than or equal to 4.05

## Explanation

Each observation contributes to WAIC through its log pointwise predictive density, penalised by its share of the effective number of parameters:

$$
\text{WAIC}_i = -2\left(\text{lppd}_i - p_{WAIC,i}\right), \qquad \text{lppd}_i = \ln\left(\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S} p(y_i \mid \lambda_s)\right)
$$

The likelihood of $y_1 = 3$ under a Poisson with mean $\lambda$ is $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^3/3!$. For the sampled values:

$$
\begin{align*}
p(3 \mid \lambda = 1) &= e^{-1}(1)/6 = 0.06131 \\
p(3 \mid \lambda = 2) &= e^{-2}(8)/6 = 0.18045 \\
p(3 \mid \lambda = 3) &= e^{-3}(27)/6 = 0.22404 \\
p(3 \mid \lambda = 4) &= e^{-4}(64)/6 = 0.19537
\end{align*}
$$

Average over the six samples ($\lambda = 2, 3, 1, 3, 4, 2$), then take the log:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{1}{6}\sum_{s=1}^{6} p(3 \mid \lambda_s) &= \frac{0.18045 + 0.22404 + 0.06131 + 0.22404 + 0.19537 + 0.18045}{6} \\
&= \frac{1.06566}{6} \\
&= 0.17761 \\
\text{lppd}_1 &= \ln 0.17761 \\
&= -1.7282
\end{align*}
$$

(The given $p_{WAIC,1} = 0.2019$ is the variance of the six log-likelihoods, computed with divisor 6.) Then

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{WAIC}_1 &= -2(-1.7282 - 0.2019) \\
&= 3.860
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[3.05, 4.05)$, so the answer is **D**.
