For a certain product, the time until failure is modeled by a distribution with density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
ce^{-cx}, & x > 0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $c$ is a constant. The median time until failure is 10 years.

The product has a warranty that provides a payment of 35 if failure occurs in the first 5 years, 25 if failure occurs after 5 years, but within 7.5 years, and 0 if failure occurs after 7.5 years.

Calculate the expected payment under the warranty.

- A) $13.06$
- B) $17.13$
- C) $20.84$
- D) $24.58$
- E) $27.56$

## Explanation

The density is exponential with rate $c$. The median condition gives

$$
\int_0^{10} ce^{-cx}\,dx = 1 - e^{-10c} = 0.5 \implies c = \frac{\ln 2}{10} = 0.069315
$$

The payment takes two nonzero values, so the expectation is a two-term sum:

$$
E[\text{payment}] = 35\,F(5) + 25\left[F(7.5) - F(5)\right]
$$

with $e^{-5c} = 0.70711$ and $e^{-7.5c} = 0.5946$:

$$
\begin{align*}
&= 35\left(1 - 0.70711\right) + 25\left(0.70711 - 0.5946\right) \\
&= 35(0.29289) + 25(0.11251) \\
&= 10.25 + 2.81 \\
&= \mathbf{13.06}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 17.13, treats the median of 10 as the *mean*, taking $c = 0.1$ instead of $0.069315$. For an exponential the mean exceeds the median by a factor of $1/\ln 2 = 1.443$, so the true mean here is 14.43 years — failures are slower than the mistaken rate implies, and the warranty pays less.
