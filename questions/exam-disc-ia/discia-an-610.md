A gift shop insures its business personal property under the BPP with the causes of loss special form, and the declarations show no higher special limits. Burglars steal, in one night: fur garments worth \$4,000; jewelry worth \$3,100, every item worth more than \$100; watches worth \$900, every one worth \$100 or less; lottery tickets held for sale worth \$1,000; and other gifts worth \$2,000. Ignoring the deductible, how much does the special form pay?

- A) \$7,250
- B) \$8,150
- C) \$8,900
- D) \$11,000

## Explanation

Theft is covered under the special form, subject to its special limits per occurrence of theft: \$2,500 for furs, \$2,500 for jewelry and watches (not applying to items worth \$100 or less each), and \$250 for stamps and tickets, including lottery tickets held for sale.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid} &= \min(4{,}000,\ 2{,}500) + \min(3{,}100,\ 2{,}500) + 900 \\
&\quad + \min(1{,}000,\ 250) + 2{,}000 \\
&= 8{,}150
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — \$7,250 puts the \$900 of watches under the jewelry limit, but the limit does not apply to jewelry and watches worth \$100 or less per item.
- **C** — \$8,900 forgets the \$250 limit on lottery tickets.
- **D** — \$11,000 applies no special limits at all.
