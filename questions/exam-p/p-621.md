The time between automobile accidents at a particularly dangerous intersection is exponentially distributed with mean 15 days.

There have been no accidents in the previous 10 days.

Calculate the probability that the next accident will occur more than 5 days from now, but less than 25 days from now.

- A) $0.27$
- B) $0.37$
- C) $0.47$
- D) $0.53$
- E) $0.63$

## Explanation

The exponential distribution is memoryless: the 10 accident-free days already elapsed tell you nothing about the wait still to come. So the waiting time from *now* is again exponential with mean 15, and

$$
\begin{align*}
P[15 < X < 35 \mid X > 10] &= P[5 < X < 25] \\
                           &= P[X < 25] - P[X < 5] \\
                           &= \left(1 - e^{-25/15}\right) - \left(1 - e^{-5/15}\right) \\
                           &= e^{-5/15} - e^{-25/15} \\
                           &= 0.71653 - 0.18888 \\
                           &= \mathbf{0.5277}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.27, keeps the window measured from the original starting point rather than from now: $P[15 < X < 35] = e^{-1} - e^{-35/15} = 0.2709$. That is the unconditional probability of an interval placed 10 days further out, and it is the answer only if you forget to condition — the memoryless property says to slide the window back by the 10 days already survived, not to leave it where it sits.
