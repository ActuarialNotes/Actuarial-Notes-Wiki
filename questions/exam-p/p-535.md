A policyholder experiences one accident under a health insurance policy and one accident under a dental insurance policy. The health insurance policy reimburses 80% of losses. Losses under this policy have variance 40,000. The dental insurance policy reimburses 90% of losses. Losses under this policy have variance 10,000.

The policyholder's two losses are independent.

Calculate the variance of the policyholder's total unreimbursed loss under these policies.

- A) $1{,}700$
- B) $9{,}000$
- C) $16{,}300$
- D) $33{,}700$
- E) $50{,}000$

## Explanation

If a policy reimburses 80%, the policyholder bears the other 20%. So the total unreimbursed loss is

$$
U = 0.2X + 0.1Y
$$

where $X$ and $Y$ are the health and dental losses.

The losses are independent, so the variances add — and each coefficient enters **squared**:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(U) &= (0.2)^2\text{Var}(X) + (0.1)^2\text{Var}(Y) \\
              &= 0.04(40{,}000) + 0.01(10{,}000) \\
              &= 1600 + 100 \\
              &= \mathbf{1700}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 16,300, uses the reimbursement percentages 0.8 and 0.9 rather than the retained 0.2 and 0.1 — reading which side of the split the question asks about is the first hazard. Choice (E), 50,000, is the sum of the two gross variances, with no coinsurance factor at all. Squaring the small retentions is what makes the answer two orders of magnitude below that.
