Given the following information:

| Accident Year | Selected Ultimate Claims as of June 30, 2017 | Reported Claims as of June 30, 2017 | Reported Claims as of December 31, 2017 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 3,220 | 3,126 | 3,088 |
| 2015 | 4,229 | 3,248 | 3,796 |
| 2016 | 4,845 | 2,018 | 2,900 |
| 2017 | 5,101 | 697 | 1,565 |

**Selected Reported Claims Age-to-Age Factors**

| 6-12 | 12-18 | 18-24 | 24-30 | 30-36 | 36-42 | 42-48 | 48-Ult |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.870 | 1.630 | 1.430 | 1.290 | 1.170 | 1.080 | 1.030 | 1.005 |

## Part a (1.25 points)

Compare the actual versus expected claims reported between June 30, 2017 and December 31, 2017 for each accident year.

### Explanation
The factors are **semi-annual**. At June 30, 2017 the accident years are aged 42 (2014), 30 (2015), 18 (2016) and 6 (2017) months, and each moves on six months by December 31, 2017. The expected percentage reported at an age is 1 ÷ the age-to-ultimate factor:

| Age | 6 | 18 | 24 | 30 | 36 | 42 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Age-to-ultimate | 7.3547 | 2.4129 | 1.6873 | 1.3080 | 1.1180 | 1.0352 | 1.0050 |
| % reported | 13.60% | 41.44% | 59.26% | 76.45% | 89.45% | 96.60% | 99.50% |

(and 12 months: 3.9330, 25.43%). Expected emergence over the half-year is the selected ultimate × the change in expected % reported; actual emergence is the change in reported claims:

| AY | Ultimate | % reported, 6/30 → 12/31 | Expected | Actual | Actual - expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 3,220 | 96.60% → 99.50% | 93.3 | 3,088 - 3,126 = -38 | -131.3 |
| 2015 | 4,229 | 76.45% → 89.45% | 549.6 | 3,796 - 3,248 = 548 | -1.6 |
| 2016 | 4,845 | 41.44% → 59.26% | 863.4 | 2,900 - 2,018 = 882 | +18.6 |
| 2017 | 5,101 | 13.60% → 25.43% | 603.4 | 1,565 - 697 = 868 | +264.6 |

Accident years 2015 and 2016 emerged almost exactly as expected. Accident year 2014 emerged far below expected — reported claims actually fell — and accident year 2017 emerged well above expected (about 44% more than expected).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate actual reported claims between two dates and understand the relation between chain ladder development factors and reporting patterns in order to calculate the expected claims emergence between. They were also expected to compare the actual emergence to the expected emergence and give reasons why the actual emergence may deviate from the expected emergence.

Candidates were expected to the compute the actual and expected claims reported in the given time period and compare the two, either numerically or descriptively.

Common errors included:

- Not realizing that the given development factors were semi-annual and applying them as annual factors when calculating the expected emergence.
- Using the age-to-age factors as age-to-ultimate factors.
- Calculating cumulative expected claims instead of incremental expected claims in the given time period.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two scenarios that would result in reported claims emerging lower than expected.

### Explanation
- **Case reserves weakened:** if the company starts setting case reserves less adequately, reported claims (paid + case) grow more slowly than the historical pattern assumes.
- **Slower reporting:** a shift in the mix toward a longer-tailed line, or a delay in recording claims (for example a backlog in the claims department), means claims are reported later than the pattern expects.
- **Favourable experience:** fewer or smaller claims than expected (lower frequency or severity, or large salvage and subrogation recoveries) also make emergence come in low.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide two reasons that would result in reported claims emerging lower than expected and to explain their reasoning.

Common errors included:

- Simply stating "change in mix of business" without additional explanation. An appropriate answer would have been: a change in the mix of business towards a longer tailed line of business leading to claims being reported later than expected.
- Restating the question as an answer: "claims coming in lower".
- Confusing reported claims emerging lower than expected and claims emerging higher than expected.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two scenarios that would result in reported claims emerging higher than expected.

### Explanation
- **Case reserves strengthened:** raising the adequacy of case reserves increases reported claims faster than the historical pattern assumes.
- **Faster reporting:** a shift toward a shorter-tailed line, or a process change that records claims sooner, brings claims in earlier than expected.
- **Adverse experience:** a large or catastrophe loss, or higher-than-expected frequency or severity (for example from inflation), makes reported claims emerge above expectation.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide two reasons that would result in reported claims emerging higher than expected and to explain their reasoning.

Common errors included:

- Simply stating "change in mix of business" without additional explanation. An appropriate answer would have been: a change in the mix of business towards a shorter tailed line of business leading to claims being reported faster than expected.
- Making statements about paid claims and the case reserves without considering the fact that the two are linked.
- Confusing reported claims emerging lower than expected and claims emerging higher than expected.
