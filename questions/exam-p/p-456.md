In a group of ten patients, three have high blood pressure, six have normal blood pressure, and one has low blood pressure.

Four of these ten patients are randomly selected without replacement.

Calculate the probability that exactly two of these four patients have normal blood pressure.

- A) $0.058$
- B) $0.071$
- C) $0.300$
- D) $0.346$
- E) $0.429$

## Explanation

Only one distinction matters for this question: normal blood pressure versus not. The three high and one low patients collapse into a single "not normal" group of four, so the population is 6 normal and 4 other.

Four patients are drawn without replacement, so the count of normal patients is hypergeometric:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{exactly 2 normal}] &= \frac{\binom{6}{2}\binom{4}{2}}{\binom{10}{4}} \\
                           &= \frac{(15)(6)}{210} \\
                           &= \frac{90}{210} \\
                           &= \mathbf{0.42857}
\end{align*}
$$

Splitting the "other" group into its high and low parts would need a three-category multivariate hypergeometric sum and gives the same answer — collapsing the irrelevant distinction first saves that work. Choice (D), 0.346, is the binomial $\binom{4}{2}(0.6)^2(0.4)^2 = 0.3456$ — what you get by treating the four draws as independent. With a population of only ten, sampling without replacement changes the answer materially.
