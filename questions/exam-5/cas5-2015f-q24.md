Given the following data as of December 31, 2014:

Paid Claims Only ($000) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 1,000 | 1,100 | 1,157 | 1,178 |
| 2012 | 1,500 | 1,650 | 1,733 | |
| 2013 | 2,000 | 2,200 | | |
| 2014 | 2,500 | | | |

Paid ALAE ($000) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 100 | 220 | 347 | 424 |
| 2012 | 150 | 330 | 520 | |
| 2013 | 200 | 440 | | |
| 2014 | 375 | | | |

| Accident Year | Selected Ultimate Claims Only ($000) |
|---|---|
| 2011 | 1,178 |
| 2012 | 1,768 |
| 2013 | 2,356 |
| 2014 | 2,945 |

- Assume that no development occurs after 48 months.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate ultimate ALAE using the multiplicative development technique applied to the ratio of paid ALAE-to-paid claims only for accident years 2012, 2013, and 2014.

### Explanation
Form the ratio of paid ALAE to paid claims only at each evaluation:

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 10.0% | 20.0% | 30.0% | 36.0% |
| 2012 | 10.0% | 20.0% | 30.0% | |
| 2013 | 10.0% | 20.0% | | |
| 2014 | 15.0% | | | |

Multiplicative age-to-age factors on that ratio triangle:

| AY | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 2.0 | 1.5 | 1.2 |
| 2012 | 2.0 | 1.5 | |
| 2013 | 2.0 | | |

| | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ATA | 2.0 | 1.5 | 1.2 | 1.0 |
| ATU | 3.6 | 1.8 | 1.2 | 1.0 |

Develop each year's ratio to ultimate and apply it to that year's selected ultimate claims only:

| AY | Ultimate ALAE/Loss Ratio | Ultimate ALAE |
|---|---|---|
| 2012 | $30.0\% \times 1.2 = 36.0\%$ | $1{,}768 \times 36.0\% = 636.50$ |
| 2013 | $20.0\% \times 1.8 = 36.0\%$ | $2{,}356 \times 36.0\% = 848.20$ |
| 2014 | $15.0\% \times 3.6 = 54.0\%$ | $2{,}945 \times 54.0\% = 1{,}590.30$ |

Alternatively, a candidate may note that AY 2014's ultimate ratio of $54\%$ is far above the historical $36\%$ — driven entirely by the single $15\%$ observation at 12 months against $10\%$ in every prior year — judge 2014 an outlier and select $36\%$ instead, giving ultimate ALAE of $2{,}945 \times 36.0\% = 1{,}060$.

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to know how to use paid ALAE and paid loss triangles to calculate paid ALAE to paid loss ratio triangles.
The candidate was expected to know how to calculate multiplicative development factors from paid ALAE to paid loss triangles and apply those factors to determine ultimate ALAE to loss ratios by accident year.
The candidate was expected to know how to apply ultimate ALAE to loss ratios to ultimate loss to determine estimates of ultimate ALAE by Accident Year.
Common errors included calculating ultimate ALAE for only AY 2014, applying ultimate ALAE/loss ratio to paid loss rather than to ultimate loss, failing to use the development data from AY 2011, and developing paid ALAE rather than the paid ALAE/paid loss ratio. In order to receive full credit for a response in which the candidate chose an ultimate ALAE/loss ratio for AY 2014 that differed from the ratio resulting from the multiplicative method, the candidate needed to provide justification for this decision.
Most candidates received full credit for part a.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Calculate ultimate ALAE using the additive alternative approach to the technique in part a. above for accident years 2012, 2013, and 2014.

### Explanation
The additive alternative develops the *difference* in the ratio rather than its multiple:

| AY | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 10.0 | 10.0 | 6.0 |
| 2012 | 10.0 | 10.0 | |
| 2013 | 10.0 | | |

| | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ATA | 10.0 | 10.0 | 6.0 | 0.0 |
| ATU | 26.0 | 16.0 | 6.0 | 0.0 |

| AY | Ultimate ALAE Ratio | Ultimate ALAE |
|---|---|---|
| 2012 | $30.0\% + 6.0\% = 36.0\%$ | $1{,}768 \times 36.0\% = 636.50$ |
| 2013 | $20.0\% + 16.0\% = 36.0\%$ | $2{,}356 \times 36.0\% = 848.20$ |
| 2014 | $15.0\% + 26.0\% = 41.0\%$ | $2{,}945 \times 41.0\% = 1{,}207.50$ |

As in part a, a candidate treating 2014's $15\%$ as noise could instead select $36\%$ and report $1{,}060$.

### Examiner Report
The candidate is expected to know how to calculate additive development factors from paid ALAE to paid loss triangles and apply those factors to determine ultimate ALAE to loss ratios by Accident Year.
The candidate is expected to know how to apply ultimate ALAE to loss ratios to ultimate loss to determine estimates of ultimate ALAE by Accident Year.
Common errors included calculating ultimate ALAE for only AY 2014, applying ultimate ALAE/loss ratio to paid loss rather than to ultimate loss, failing to use the development data from AY 2011, developing paid ALAE rather than the paid ALAE/paid loss ratio, and multiplying instead of adding the calculated ATA factors. In order to receive full credit for a response in which the candidate chose an ultimate ALAE/loss ratio for AY 2014 that differed from the ratio resulting from the additive method, the candidate needed to provide justification for this decision.

Most candidates received full credit for part b.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Select and justify a reasonable estimate of ultimate ALAE for accident year 2014 based on the estimates calculated in parts a. and b. above.

### Explanation
Multiple selections were accepted provided the reasoning was stated; the grading of this part followed from whatever the candidate did in parts a and b. Representative positions:

- **Select the additive estimate of $\$1{,}207{,}450$.** The multiplicative age-to-ultimate factor for AY 2014 is $3.6$, which is highly leveraged at 12 months; the additive approach is more stable at early maturities.
- **Select the multiplicative estimate of $\$1{,}590{,}300$.** If the rise in AY 2014's paid-to-paid ratio is genuine and not an artifact of the claim settlement rate, the multiplicative approach is the more responsive of the two.
- **Select $36\%$, giving $\$1{,}060$k.** AY 2014 sits at $15\%$ at 12 months against $10\%$ in all prior years and is immature; treating that as noise and using the stable historical ratio is defensible.

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to be able to compare the advantages and disadvantages of multiplicative and additive development techniques.
Multiple answers were accepted, and since many candidates exercised judgment when selecting ultimate ALAE/loss ratios in parts a and b, part c was graded based on consideration of the candidate's response in parts a and b.
Common errors included not stating which method the candidate selected and not appropriately and adequately justifying the selection.
Most candidates received full credit in part c.
