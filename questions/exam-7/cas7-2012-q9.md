## Part a (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two advantages that non-parametric smoothing models for claims run-off pattern estimates have over models based on parametric curves.

### Explanation
1. Not as rigid a fit, so they can fit the data better, using a blending of two well-known models.
2. Parametric curves may end up showing trends that aren't in the data when extrapolating, or showing non-intuitive increasing development. Non-parametric models can control this.

Alternatively:

There are fewer parameters required — parametric models have been critiqued for being over-parameterized. (The same sample added that smoothing models allow extrapolation beyond the end of the data; the examiners did not credit extrapolation, as it is not unique to non-parametric smoothing models.)

### Examiner Report
For the a. part, a common answer that did not receive credit was the ability to extrapolate the data as extrapolation is not an unique characteristic/advantage of non-parametric smoothing models.
A common incomplete response for the b. part stated that theta was the smoothing parameter, but failed to identify a tradeoff aspect between goodness of fit and smoothing.  Stating that theta "credibility weighted" two curves was also not a valid response.
Credit was given in the c. part if the response explained that the two extremes were unsmoothed and fully smoothed.  The two extremes resulted in reliance on the empirical data and a smoothed parametric curve was also a common valid response.
Credit for the d. part was only given for stating the Chain Ladder or Hoerl/Gamma curve.
Responses that did not mention the extreme values or note which curve went to each extreme received credit, but responses that matched the extreme with the wrong curve did not.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Describe the role of the $\theta$ parameter in the incremental claim predictor:

$$\eta_{ij} = s_{\theta_i}(i) + s_{\theta_j}(\log(j)) + s_{\theta_j}(j)$$

### Explanation
$\theta$ is the level of smoothing that takes place, trading off between a rigid fit and the chain ladder method.

Alternatively:

The $\theta$ parameter works as a smoothing parameter between the analytic result (say an over-dispersed Poisson, resulting in chain ladder estimates) and the Hoerl curve. The model works to fit and smooth simultaneously, which keeps the output from being too jumpy.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe the fit of the model from part b. above when $\theta$ is at each extreme.

### Explanation
When $\theta = 0$ there is no smoothing and the fit is to the data itself. When $\theta = \infty$ the model is completely smoothed and linear.

Alternatively:

When $\theta$ is small, more dependence is on the emerging data; when $\theta$ is large, more dependence is on the parametric model.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Identify the resulting models when $\theta$ is at each of its extremes.

### Explanation
When θ = 0, this is the chain ladder method. When θ = ∞, this is the Hoerl Curve.

Alternatively:

When θ = 0 ⇒ chain ladder method

When θ → ∞ ⇒ Hoerl curve
