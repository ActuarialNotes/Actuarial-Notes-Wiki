The payment $X$, in thousands, that an insurer makes on a randomly selected claim has distribution function

$$
F(x) = \begin{cases}
0, & x < 0 \\
0.2 + 0.1x, & 0 \le x < 2 \\
0.5 + 0.1x, & 2 \le x < 5 \\
1, & x \ge 5
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate $P(X \ge 1 \mid X \le 2)$. Give your answer as a decimal to two decimal places.

## Explanation

Read the distribution function before using it. It jumps by 0.2 at $x = 0$ (from 0 to 0.2) and by 0.3 at $x = 2$ (from $F(2^-) = 0.4$ to $F(2) = 0.7$), and rises with slope 0.1 in between. So $X$ is a mixed random variable with

$$P(X = 0) = 0.2, \qquad P(X = 2) = 0.3.$$

Both events include $x = 2$, so the jump there must be counted:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(X \le 2) &= F(2) \\
           &= 0.7 \\[4pt]
P(1 \le X \le 2) &= F(2) - F(1^-) \\
                 &= 0.7 - 0.3 \\
                 &= 0.4
\end{align*}
$$

($F$ is continuous at 1, so $F(1^-) = F(1) = 0.3$.)

$$
\begin{align*}
P(X \ge 1 \mid X \le 2) &= \frac{0.4}{0.7} \\
                        &= 0.5714
\end{align*}
$$

To two decimal places, the answer is **0.57**.

**Common wrong entries**

- **0.10** is $F(2^-) - F(1)$, unconditioned and without the point mass at 2: the probability of $X$ lying in $[1, 2)$.
- **0.14** leaves the mass at 2 out of the numerator but keeps it in the denominator, 0.1/0.7.
- **0.25** reads $F(2)$ as the left-hand value 0.4 in both places, 0.1/0.4, as if $X$ were continuous at 2.
- **0.40** is $P(1 \le X \le 2)$, the numerator alone; the condition $X \le 2$ was not divided out.
