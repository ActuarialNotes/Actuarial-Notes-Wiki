An actuary is comparing the predictions from two different Bayesian models, $q$ and $r$. Model $q$ has 4 effective parameters and model $r$ has 5 effective parameters. Below are the posterior likelihoods from the two models:

| Posterior Prediction | Posterior Likelihood from $q$ | Posterior Likelihood from $r$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 100 |
| 2 | 150 | 175 |
| 3 | 175 | 200 |
| 4 | 125 | 125 |

The actuary decides to calculate Deviance and AIC and uses the most appropriate measure to identify the better model considering the effect of overfitting.

Identify which of the following statements is correct.

- A) Model $q$ has a better AIC, so it is a better model
- B) Model $r$ has a better Deviance, so it is a better model
- C) Model $q$ has a better AIC and Deviance, so it is a better model
- D) Model $r$ has a better AIC and Deviance, so it is a better model
- E) Neither Deviance nor AIC measures overfitting, so the analysis is not conclusive

## Explanation

**Deviance** is $-2$ times the log pointwise predictive density:

$$
\begin{align*}
D_q &= -2\big[\ln 100 + \ln 150 + \ln 175 + \ln 125\big] \\
    &= -2(19.609) \\
    &= -39.22 \\
D_r &= -2\big[\ln 100 + \ln 175 + \ln 200 + \ln 125\big] \\
    &= -2(19.897) \\
    &= -39.79
\end{align*}
$$

Lower is better, so **model $r$ has the better deviance** — unsurprisingly, since it is the more flexible model.

**AIC** adds a penalty of twice the effective number of parameters:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{AIC}_q &= -39.22 + 2(4) = -31.22 \\
\text{AIC}_r &= -39.79 + 2(5) = -29.79
\end{align*}
$$

Now **model $q$ wins**: $r$'s extra effective parameter costs 2 but buys only 0.57 in deviance.

The question asks for the measure appropriate to *overfitting*, and that is AIC — deviance measures in-sample fit only and always favours the more complex model, which is precisely the overfitting trap. This rules out (B), (E) and the "both" claims in (C) and (D).

Model $q$ has the better AIC, so the answer is **A**.
