A legal update on amendments to the Canada Labour Code that would require federally regulated employers to insure their long-term disability plans. Written by Tate E. McLeod of Norton Rose Fulbright Canada LLP and dated 13 June 2013, it explains what the amendments do, why they were introduced and how federally regulated employers should prepare.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam GH-201C (SOA)|Exam GH-201C]] — GH201-660-25: Self-Funded Disability Plans: Time Running Out on Federally Regulated Employers; outcomes 3b.

## What is happening?
- Bill C-38, the Jobs, Growth and Long-term Prosperity Act, amended Part III of the Canada Labour Code; the changes were expected to come into effect in the spring or summer of 2014
- Once in force, the amendments remove the discretion federally regulated private sector employers have to [[Self-Insurance Funding|self-insure]] their employees’ long-term disability (LTD) plans, and require them to obtain insurance coverage for any LTD plan they offer
- Employers that self-insure or rely on an Administrative Services Only (ASO) plan will have to engage a licensed insurance company to administer and insure LTD benefits
- The article expects an enormous impact on certain employers in banking, transportation and telecommunications, as well as other federally regulated industries

## Why are these changes being introduced?
- Legislative debates over Bill C-38 show that the amendments were driven by public policy concerns
- It points to the 350 disabled Nortel employees whose LTD coverage was lost in the Nortel bankruptcy because Nortel’s self-funded LTD plan was underfunded by approximately $50 to $75 million
- There is little regulation of uninsured LTD plans: no requirement to set aside adequate reserves for future liabilities or to keep funds in trust to protect them from creditors on bankruptcy, whereas the insurance industry is highly regulated
- The aim of the amendments is to ensure that employees who receive LTD benefits keep their financial security if their employer goes bankrupt with an underfunded plan

## What should federally regulated employers do to prepare?
- The amendments indicate that some federally regulated employers may be exempt, but the regulations governing the exceptions had not been issued
- All federally regulated employers with self-funded LTD plans must take immediate steps to prepare for the possibility that an insured plan will soon be required
- Once the exemptions are published, employers should consult legal counsel to determine whether they fit the criteria
