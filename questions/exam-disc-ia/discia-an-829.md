A highway contractor will replace a culvert 20 feet from a freight railroad's tracks. The railroad requires a policy in its own name protecting it from liability arising from the contractor's work at the job site. Which ISO general liability coverage form fits, and who is its named insured?

- A) The Railroad Protective Liability Coverage Form (CG 00 35), with the railroad as named insured
- B) The Owners and Contractors Protective Liability Coverage Form (CG 00 09), with the contractor as named insured
- C) The contractor's CGL (CG 00 01), whose insured contract definition covers its promise to indemnify the railroad
- D) The Products/Completed Operations Liability Coverage Form (CG 00 37), with the railroad as named insured

## Explanation

Railroad protective liability insurance protects a railroad from liability it incurs because of the work of contractors on or near its right-of-way. The railroad is the named insured; the contractor buys the policy and is named on the declarations but is not an insured. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — the OCP form protects a project owner or contractor that hires a designated contractor, and the designated contractor who buys it is not its named insured.
- **C** — the CGL's definition of insured contract carves out an indemnity of a railroad for construction or demolition within 50 feet of railroad property, so the contractor's CGL would not cover that promise.
- **D** — this form insures the products-completed operations hazard of the insured's own products and completed work, not a railroad's liability for ongoing work.
