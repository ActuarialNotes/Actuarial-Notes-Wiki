Given the following information as of December 31, 2018:

| Accident Year | Earned Premium (\$000) | Incremental Paid Loss at 12 Months (\$000) | Incremental Paid Loss at 24 Months (\$000) | Incremental Paid Loss at 36 Months (\$000) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 800 | 320 | 220 | 80 |
| 2017 | 600 | 300 | 200 | |
| 2018 | 400 | 280 | | |

- Assume there is no loss development beyond 36 months.

## Part a (2 points)

Calculate the total Neuhaus loss ratio claims reserve estimate.

### Explanation
Neuhaus credibility-weights the chain ladder (individual) and BF (collective) reserves using $z = ELR \times p_i$.

Incremental loss ratios, ELR and payout factors:

$$m_1 = \frac{320 + 300 + 280}{800 + 600 + 400} = 0.5 \qquad m_2 = \frac{220 + 200}{800 + 600} = 0.3 \qquad m_3 = \frac{80}{800} = 0.1$$

$$ELR = \sum m_i = 0.90$$

$$p_1 = \frac{m_1}{ELR} = 0.556 \qquad p_2 = \frac{m_1 + m_2}{ELR} = 0.889 \qquad p_3 = 1$$

Example calculations for AY 2017:

$$\text{CL Reserve} = \frac{300 + 200}{0.889} - (300 + 200) = 62.43$$

$$\text{BF Reserve} = 600 \times 0.90 \times (1 - 0.889) = 59.94$$

$$z = 0.90 \times 0.889 = 0.80$$

$$\text{Neuhaus Reserve} = 0.80 \times 62.43 + (1 - 0.8) \times 59.94 = 61.932$$

| AY | CL Reserve | BF Reserve | $z$ | Neuhaus Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 0 | 0 | 0.90 | 0 |
| 2017 | 62.43 | 59.94 | 0.80 | 61.932 |
| 2018 | 223.60 | 159.84 | 0.50 | 191.720 |
| Total | | | | **253.652** (\$000) |

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to know how to estimate reserves according to the Neuhaus method.
Common mistakes included:

- Not using cumulative paid losses in the 2017 year when estimating individual reserves.
- Only calculating reserves for a single year (2017 or 2018)
- Calculating a non-zero reserve for the 2016 accident year
- When estimating reserves for either the collective or individual method, neglecting to multiply by q (the loss ratio reserve factor)
- Deriving payout factors and/or individual reserve estimates based on chain ladder LDF method
- Calculating reserves based on the expected loss ratio method rather than the BF reserve as the collective reserve estimate
- Misinterpreting the claims triangle as cumulative instead of incremental losses

## Part b (0.5 points)

Describe why the Neuhaus method may not be appropriate for the data in the table above.

### Explanation
Any of the following:

- Neuhaus assumes a constant ELR for all accident years, but as the book shrinks the incremental loss ratios at 12 months are increasing: 0.4 (2016), 0.5 (2017), 0.7 (2018).
- Neuhaus uses a common loss ratio for all years to compute the credibility factor $z$ (and to estimate $R_{coll}$), but the loss ratio appears to change substantially year over year as earned premium declines.
- An expected loss ratio method presumes a consistent loss ratio and/or exposure base. Premiums have dropped 50% in 2 years while losses have not dropped substantially, so loss ratios appear to be increasing — which has the effect of giving high credibility to a shrinking line.
- Neuhaus works for stable business, which is not the case here: premium is decreasing from 2016 to 2018, which may indicate a change in mix of business, so a constant loss ratio cannot be assumed.
- The decline in premium may mean historical premium and losses are not representative of current and future premium and loss, so the overall ELR calculated from the data may not be appropriate for all years.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify patterns in the premium or loss and their fit to the Neuhaus model assumptions.  A variety of responses were accepted in regard to tying the observation back to the Neuhaus method, including responses where candidates commented on either how the Neuhaus method works or underlying assumptions of the method.
Common mistakes included:

- Not specifically commenting on the trends evident in the data.
- Not tying the trends in the data back to the Neuhaus method.
