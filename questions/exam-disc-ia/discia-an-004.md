For a U.S. property-casualty insurer, which basis is the starting point for computing its federal taxable income?

- A) GAAP net income, adjusted for permanent differences
- B) IFRS 17 profit or loss, adjusted to the tax law
- C) Management's internal economic-basis results
- D) Statutory income, adjusted as the tax law requires

## Explanation

For insurance companies the starting point for taxable income is income determined under statutory accounting, which is then adjusted under the tax laws and regulations. The adjustments specific to insurers mostly accelerate income, chiefly through discounting loss reserves and reducing the unearned premium that may be deducted. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — GAAP serves investors and lenders; it is not the tax starting point for an insurer.
- **B** — IFRS is used outside the U.S. and by U.S. subsidiaries of IFRS filers; it is not the U.S. tax basis.
- **C** — management's internal figures serve its own decisions and follow no external rule the IRS relies on.
