The following data shows the experience of a merit rating plan for a specific state.

| Number of Accident-Free Years | Earned Car Years | Earned Premium (\$000) | Number of Incurred Claims |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 or More | 250,000 | 250,000 | 1,200 |
| 2 | 300,000 | 100,000 | 625 |
| 1 | 25,000 | 100,000 | 750 |
| 0 | 12,000 | 150,000 | 1,500 |
| Total | 587,000 | 600,000 | 4,075 |

The base rate is \$1,000 per exposure. No other rating variables are applicable.

## Part a (0.5 points)

The typical exposure base used to develop the merit rating plan is earned premium. Briefly discuss two assumptions in selecting this exposure base.

### Explanation
The 2 assumptions made are:

1. High frequency territories are also high premium territories
2. Territorial differentials are proper / adequate

### Examiner Report
Most candidates were awarded full credit. No common errors were identified.

## Part b (1.5 points)

Calculate the ratio of credibility for an exposure with two or more years accident-free experience to one or more years accident-free experience.

### Explanation
Frequencies (claims per unit of earned premium):

- Freq 2 or more = (1,200 + 625)/(250,000 + 100,000) = 0.0052
- Freq 1 or more = (1,200 + 625 + 750)/(250,000 + 100,000 + 100,000) = 0.0057
- Freq total = 4,075/600,000 = 0.0068

Mod factors:

- Mod 2 or more = 0.0052/0.0068 = 0.7677
- Mod 1 or more = 0.0057/0.0068 = 0.8425

Credibility factors:

- Cred 2 or more = 1 - Mod 2 or more = 1 - 0.7677 = 0.2323
- Cred 1 or more = 1 - Mod 1 or more = 1 - 0.8425 = 0.1575

$$\text{Ratio} = 0.2323/0.1575 = 1.4750$$

### Examiner Report
Most candidates were awarded full credit. The most common error was using earned-car years in the calculation of the frequency instead of earned premium.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Calculate the premium for an exposure that is accident free for two or more years.

### Explanation
$$\text{Premium} = \text{Base rate} \times \text{Mod} = 1{,}000 \times 0.7677 = \$767.7$$

### Examiner Report
Most candidates did well on this question. The two most common errors were:

- Using something other than the base rate
- Calculating premium for the sum of all earned-car years
