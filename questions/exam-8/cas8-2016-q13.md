A risk is written using a retrospective rating plan with the following characteristics:

| Characteristic | Value |
|---|---|
| Standard Premium | \$10,000,000 |
| Expected Loss Ratio | 60% |
| Loss Ratio at Maximum Premium | 80% |
| Loss Ratio at Minimum Premium | 20% |
| Loss Conversion Factor | 1.085 |
| Provision for Losses and Total Expenses Exclusive of Taxes | 0.97 |

The following Lee Diagram depicts actual experience from a sample of similarly-sized risks and similar to the risk in question:

![Lee diagram titled Unlimited Loss: loss ratio (0 to 1.2) on the vertical axis against percent of risks (10% to 100%) on the horizontal axis, ten risks of 10% each in increasing order with loss ratios 0, 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 0.8, 1.0, 1.0 and 1.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas8-2016-q13-lee-diagram.png)

## Part a (1.25 points)

Determine the converted insurance charge for this plan.

### Explanation
In the chart, each bar is 10% of risks:

$$E = 0.2(0.1) + 0.4(0.1) + 0.6(0.1) + 0.8(0.2) + 1(0.2) + 1.2(0.1) = 0.6$$

From the chart, the insurance charge at the maximum loss ratio (0.8) and the insurance savings at the minimum loss ratio (0.2), each as a ratio to $E$:

$$\phi(r_G) = \frac{0.2 \times (1 - 0.8) + 0.1 \times (1.2 - 0.8)}{0.6} = 0.1333 \qquad \psi(r_H) = \frac{0.2 \times 0.2}{0.6} = 0.0667$$

$$\text{Converted charge} = cI = 1.085 \times \big(\phi(r_G) - \psi(r_H)\big) \times E = 1.085 \times (0.1333 - 0.0667) \times (0.6 \times 10{,}000{,}000) = \$434{,}000$$

Alternatively, work in units of standard premium without dividing by $E$: $E\phi(r_G) = 0.2(1 - 0.8) + 0.1(1.2 - 0.8) = 0.08$ and $E\psi(r_H) = 0.2 \times 0.2 = 0.04$, so $cI = 1.085 \times (0.08 - 0.04) = 0.0434$ of standard premium, or \$434,000 in dollars.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the insurance charge and insurance savings, and then convert that into a Net Converted Insurance Charge.
Common mistakes include:
-       Not dividing the insurance charge and savings by the expected losses, E
-       Failing to calculate and include the insurance savings.  If a candidate did not calculate the Net insurance charge in Part A, but properly included the insurance savings as part of the derivation of Part B, full credit was given.

## Part b (1.25 points)

The insured's actual ultimate losses are \$8,700,000 and the final retrospective premium is \$12,500,000. Determine the tax multiplier that was used in the rating of this plan.

### Explanation
Basic premium, with $e + E = 0.97$:

$$b = e - (c - 1)E + cI = (0.97 - 0.6) \times 10{,}000{,}000 - (1.085 - 1) \times 6{,}000{,}000 + 434{,}000 = 3{,}624{,}000$$

Retrospective premium is $R = (b + cL) \times T$. The actual loss ratio is $8{,}700{,}000 / 10{,}000{,}000 = 0.87 > 0.80$, so use the 80% loss ratio at the maximum premium, i.e. $L = 8{,}000{,}000$:

$$R = 12{,}500{,}000 = (3{,}624{,}000 + 1.085 \times 8{,}000{,}000) \times T \quad\Rightarrow\quad T = 1.0159$$

Alternatively, from the Table M balance equations, with the maximum premium $G = 12{,}500{,}000 / 10{,}000{,}000 = 1.25$ as a ratio to standard premium:

$$\phi(r_H) - \phi(r_G) = 0.733 - 0.133 = 0.6 = \frac{0.97T - H}{1.085 \times 0.6 \times T} \qquad r_G - r_H = 1.0 = \frac{1.25 - H}{1.085 \times 0.6 \times T}$$

$$0.5794T = 1.25 - 0.6510T \quad\Rightarrow\quad 1.25 = 1.2304T \quad\Rightarrow\quad T = 1.0159$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use the answer from Part a, along with the basic premium equation and retrospective premium equation to determine the tax multiplier.
Common mistakes include:
-       Not recognizing that the Provision for Losses and Total Expenses Exclusive of Taxes (0.97)
represents e+E
-       Using the actual ultimate losses of 8,700,000 instead of the losses at the maximum premium of 8,000,000
