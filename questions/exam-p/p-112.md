The figure below shows the cumulative distribution function of a random variable, $X$.

*SOA's figure is a graph of $F(x)$ against $x$; it is transcribed here as a table of the graph's pieces, left to right.*

| $x$ | $F(x)$ on the graph |
| :--- | :--- |
| $x < 0$ | $0$: horizontal, ending in an open circle at $(0, 0)$ |
| $0 \le x \le 2$ | $0.5$: horizontal, from a filled dot at $(0, 0.5)$ to a filled dot at $(2, 0.5)$ |
| $2 \le x \le 3$ | a straight line from $(2, 0.5)$ up to a filled dot at $(3, 1.0)$ |
| $x \ge 3$ | $1.0$: horizontal from $(3, 1.0)$ onward |

Calculate $E(X)$.

- A) 0.00
- B) 0.50
- C) 1.00
- D) 1.25
- E) 2.50

## Explanation

This is a mixed distribution. The graph jumps from $0$ to $0.5$ at $x=0$ and is flat on either side of the jump, so there is a discrete point mass at $x=0$ with probability $0.5$.

From $x=2$ to $x=3$, the distribution is continuous. The density function is the derivative of the CDF in this region, which is the slope of the straight line from $(2, 0.5)$ to $(3, 1.0)$:
$$f(x) = \frac{1 - 0.5}{3 - 2} = 0.5, \quad 2 \le x < 3$$

The expected value is the sum of the discrete part and the continuous part:
$$E[X] = 0 \cdot P(X=0) + \int_{2}^{3} x f(x) dx$$
$$E[X] = 0(0.5) + \int_{2}^{3} 0.5x dx$$
$$
\begin{align*}
E[X] &= 0 + 0.5 \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_2^3 \\
     &= 0.5 \left( \frac{9}{2} - \frac{4}{2} \right) \\
     &= 0.5(2.5) \\
     &= 1.25
\end{align*}
$$
