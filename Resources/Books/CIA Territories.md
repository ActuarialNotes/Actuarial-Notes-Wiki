The CIA's March 2024 paper on **[[Territorial Rating|territory]] as a rating variable**. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective A1.

## Contents

- **1. Overview**
  - *1.1 Considerations in determining territorial boundaries* — the tension between homogeneity (a territory should group similar risks) and [[Credibility|credibility]] (it must be big enough to have signal), plus contiguity, stability over time, and boundaries a consumer can recognise.
  - *1.2 Considerations in determining territory relativities* — beyond expected loss cost: dislocation from the current rate, competitive position, regulatory constraints, and the interaction with other rating variables already in the model.
- **2. Technical modelling considerations** — spatial smoothing and clustering, handling sparse cells, and separating the territory signal from variables correlated with it.
- **3. Lifecycle** — territories drift as population, road networks and building stock change; the paper treats definition and relativity review as a recurring exercise, not a one-off.
- **4. Third-party data and models** — geodemographic data and vendor territory schemes, and the actuary's responsibility for a model built by someone else (see [[CIA Reliance]] and [[CIA Models]]).
- **5. Additional considerations on using postal codes to define territories** — postal codes are a delivery construct, not a risk construct: they are re-drawn by Canada Post, vary enormously in size, and a Forward Sortation Area can straddle very different risks.
- **6. Fairness considerations** — where territory becomes a proxy for socioeconomic or ethnic characteristics, and therefore where indirect discrimination arises (see [[CIA Bias]]).

## Why it is on the syllabus

Territory is the classic Canadian [[Risk Classification Restrictions|restricted variable]] problem: it is one of the most predictive variables available and one of the most politically contested. Ontario constrains territorial definition changes in a rate filing (see [[FSCO Tech Notes]] §17), and the [[Unfair Discrimination|unfair discrimination]] argument against territory is a proxy argument, not a direct one.
