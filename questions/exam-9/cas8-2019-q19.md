An insurance company is deciding between three reinsurance treaty options from the same reinsurance company based on the following output from a catastrophe model:

![Insurance Company's Retained Loss by Return Period: return period (0 to 250 years) against retained loss (600 to 1,000, in millions of dollars) for four curves. Gross (no reinsurance) is the lowest and rightmost curve: about a 25-year return period at 750, 50 years at 800, 100 years at about 860 and 250 years at about 930. Option 1 lies to the left of gross with a similar shape: about 25 years at 675, 50 years at about 725, 100 years at about 775 and 250 years at about 840. Option 2 runs just above the gross curve to about 750 (about 30 years), then rises steeply: about 115 years at 800 and 250 years at about 825. Option 3 starts near gross, crosses Option 1 around 725 to 750, and rises most steeply, reaching 250 years at about 820](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas8-2019-q19-retained-loss-by-return-period.png)

| Insurance Company | Gross (No Reinsurance) | Option 1 | Option 2 | Option 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retained AAL | \$500 million | \$450 million | \$486 million | \$488 million |
| Coefficient of Variation of Retained Loss | 25% | 25% | 24% | 22% |
| Coefficient of Variation of Ceded Loss | n/a | 25% | 76% | 169% |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the probability that the insurance company retains more than \$750 million of loss on a gross basis.

### Explanation
Reading the gross curve, a retained loss of \$750M or more has a return period of 25 years, so

$$\Pr[\text{Retained} > 750\text{M}] = \frac{1}{25} = 4\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to read return period from the graph and calculate the probability that the insurance company retains losses above a certain point.
Common mistakes included:

- Not identifying a return period from the graph
- Incorrectly calculating the probability from the return period.

## Part b (0.75 points)

One of the options represents a Quota Share reinsurance treaty. Justify which of the three options it is, and state the Quota Share percentage.

### Explanation
Option 1 is the Quota Share treaty. In a quota share, losses are shared in the same proportion across all risks, so the coefficient of variation is the same for gross, retained and ceded loss — 25% for Option 1. The shape of its retained loss curve is identical to the gross curve, just reduced by 10%: at the 1-in-25 year return period,

$$\text{QS\%} = 1 - \frac{675}{750} = 10\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify the quota share treaty from the graph and calculate the quota share percentage.
Most candidates were able to identify which option was the quota share treaty, but many of them did not state the quota share percentage. If candidates listed data points from the curve and used that to show the AAL or ceded % are identical for option 1, credit was given.
Common mistakes included:

- Not providing the quota share percentage
- Stating that Option 2 is the quota share because the sum of ceded and retained CV equals 1
- Stating that Option 3 is the quota share because the shape of retained loss is the same as gross loss.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Describe a reason why the reinsurance premium for Option 3 may be higher compared to Option 1.

### Explanation
- The coefficient of variation is much higher for the ceded portion of losses for option 3 (CV = 169%) compared to that of Option 1 (CV = 25%)
- This means that there is a lot more uncertainty in the amounts that the reinsurance treaty will cover. This requires a higher risk load for the reinsurance premiums

Alternatively:

- Option 3 picks up the volatile part in the tail, compare to Quota Share option 1
- Reinsurer needs more capital to support losses that are riskier/more volatile. The profit margin will be higher.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to justify why the reinsurance premium may be higher for one treaty compared to another.
Common mistakes include:

- Not providing justification on why volatility/tail risk coverage would lead to higher reinsurance premiums
- Claiming that Option 3 is more expensive because it has higher AAL.
