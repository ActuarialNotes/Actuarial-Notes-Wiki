An actuary uses classical credibility to develop full credibility standards for a private passenger auto indication.

Given the following information for State A:

| Coverage | Exposures 2015 | Exposures 2016 | Exposures 2017 | Claim Counts 2015 | Claim Counts 2016 | Claim Counts 2017 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bodily Injury | 20,000 | 24,000 | 22,000 | 1,020 | 1,100 | 950 |
| Collision | 18,000 | 21,000 | 19,000 | 1,700 | 2,100 | 1,975 |

|  |  |
|---|---|
| 1,082 | Claim counts standard for full credibility |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the number of exposures needed for full credibility for each coverage.

### Explanation
The exposure standard is the claim-count standard divided by the expected claim frequency. Selecting each coverage's three-year frequency:

- Bodily Injury: $\dfrac{1{,}020 + 1{,}100 + 950}{20{,}000 + 24{,}000 + 22{,}000} = \dfrac{3{,}070}{66{,}000} = 0.04652$
- Collision: $\dfrac{1{,}700 + 2{,}100 + 1{,}975}{18{,}000 + 21{,}000 + 19{,}000} = \dfrac{5{,}775}{58{,}000} = 0.09957$

$$\text{Bodily Injury: } \frac{1{,}082}{0.04652} = 23{,}261 \text{ exposures} \qquad \text{Collision: } \frac{1{,}082}{0.09957} = 10{,}867 \text{ exposures}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how to calculate an exposure credibility standard using classical credibility, understand the advantages and disadvantages of certain aspects of classical credibility, and understand what makes a good compliment of credibility.

Candidates were expected to understand how to calculate a full credibility exposure standard using classical credibility. Additionally, candidates were expected to make a selection for expected frequency to use in the calculation.

Common errors included:

- Combining the calculation for bodily injury and collision. The question specifically asked for exposures needed for full credibility for each coverage.
- Calculating full credibility standard based on the frequency of each year without making a selection.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Briefly explain why an actuary may prefer using an exposure standard for full credibility over a claim standard.

### Explanation
Exposures are known (or can be projected) before the experience period happens, while the number of claims is not known until the period has developed. An exposure standard lets the actuary know in advance how much business is needed for full credibility, and it is simpler to apply because the credibility of a review does not depend on a claim count that is still developing.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the potential advantages of using an exposure standard over a claim standard.

Common errors included:

- Stating that credibility would be higher since there are more exposures than claims. The credibility standard will also be higher for exposure, so this is not true.
- Stating that exposure is preferred for lines of business with low frequency because the claim count may never reach full credibility. For these lines, the exposure standard would be similarly high.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe one advantage and one disadvantage of using classical credibility.

### Explanation
- *Advantage:* classical (limited fluctuation) credibility is the most commonly used and accepted approach, and it is easy to compute — it needs only the claim count (or exposures) and the chosen standard, data that is readily available.
- *Disadvantage:* it rests on simplifying assumptions — claim counts follow a Poisson distribution and severity is constant — and the full credibility standard depends on an arbitrary choice of the probability and range ($p$ and $k$); it also ignores how accurate the complement is, so it may give too much or too little weight to the experience.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the advantages and disadvantages of classical credibility.

Common errors included:

- Stating advantages/disadvantages that were general to credibility, not specific to classical credibility, such as it smooths indications by weighting experience with credibility standard.
- Stating incorrect assumptions about classical credibility, such as losses are assumed to follow a normal distribution. In classical credibility, losses have constant severity and Poisson frequency.
- Vague responses such as "inaccurate" or "biased", without any further explanation. Any method could be biased or inaccurate.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Propose a complement of credibility for the indication analysis and briefly evaluate the proposed complement.

### Explanation
A reasonable complement for a State A private passenger auto indication is the **countrywide (or larger regional) indication** for the same coverage, applied to State A's current rates.

- It is **based on more data**, so it has low process variance and is **readily available** and **easy to compute** from the company's own data.
- It is **not independent** of State A — State A's experience is part of the countrywide data — and it may be **biased** if State A's loss costs, mix of business or legal environment differ from the countrywide average.

(Another accepted complement is trended present rates, adjusted for any difference between the last indicated and implemented change.)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to propose a reasonable complement of credibility and discuss the advantages/disadvantages of that complement.

Common errors included:

- Not providing any discussion of the advantages/disadvantages of the selected compliment.
- Stating incorrect assumptions about classical credibility, such as losses are assumed to follow a normal distribution. In classical credibility, losses have constant severity and Poisson frequency.
- Vague responses such as "inaccurate" or "biased", without any further explanation. Any method could be biased or inaccurate.
