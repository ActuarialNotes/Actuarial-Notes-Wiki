## II. Large insurance exposures

**The Gross Underwriting Limit Policy (GUWP)** must be consistent with the insurer's **[[Risk Appetite|risk appetite framework]]**, and must:

- **define what constitutes a Single Insurance Exposure** by class of insurance (exposures may be aggregated across coverages and classes);
- **establish limits by class** on the gross insurance risk the insurer will accept in respect of a maximum loss on a Single Insurance Exposure; and
- be **reviewed by senior management at least annually**.

The maximum loss on a Single Insurance Exposure is measured using relevant, reasonable and supportable information, **without regard to the probability of the loss event occurring**, and using **forward-looking approaches — not solely past losses**. That instruction is the heart of the guideline: this is an exposure limit, not an expected-loss calculation.

By class, a Single Insurance Exposure aggregates:

| Class | Aggregation |
|---|---|
| **Property** | In-force exposures at a **single location**, including exposures subject to that location |
| **Credit** | In-force exposures to any one **buyer or group of connected buyers** |
| **Surety** | In-force bonds to any one **contractor or group of connected contractors** |
| **Title** | In-force exposures related to the legal title for a **single location** |

**The Insurance Exposure Limit.** At no time should an insurer's **Net Retention plus its Largest Net Counterparty [[Unregistered Reinsurance|Unregistered Reinsurance]] Exposure**, arising from a maximum loss on a Single Insurance Exposure, exceed:

- **100% of Total [[Capital Available]]** where an entity in the insurer's control chain is a widely held company and/or a regulated financial institution;
- **25% of Total Capital Available** otherwise;
- **100% of Net Assets Available** for a foreign branch.

Adding unregistered reinsurance to net retention is the mechanism that matters: ceding to an unregistered reinsurer moves the exposure but does not remove it from the limit, because the insurer keeps the counterparty risk.

## III. Investment concentration

The parallel constraint on the asset side — exposure to a single [[Concentration Risk|counterparty or issuer]] as a share of capital.
