In a group of 3000 medical insurance policyholders, 1100 have a high resting heart rate, and 1900 have a low or normal resting heart rate. Of the policyholders with a high resting heart rate, 60 were treated for a stroke this year. Of the policyholders with a low or normal resting heart rate, 28 were treated for a stroke this year.

Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen policyholder from the group has a low or normal resting heart rate, given that this policyholder was treated for a stroke this year.

- A) $0.009$
- B) $0.015$
- C) $0.318$
- D) $0.467$
- E) $0.633$

## Explanation

Let $H$ and $L$ be the two heart-rate groups and $S$ the event of stroke treatment. The counts give

$$
\begin{align*}
P[H] &= \frac{1100}{3000}, \qquad P[L] = \frac{1900}{3000} \\
P[S \mid H] &= \frac{60}{1100}, \qquad P[S \mid L] = \frac{28}{1900}
\end{align*}
$$

Applying Bayes' theorem:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[L \mid S] &= \frac{P[S \mid L]P[L]}{P[S \mid L]P[L] + P[S \mid H]P[H]} \\
            &= \frac{\frac{28}{1900}\cdot\frac{1900}{3000}}{\frac{28}{1900}\cdot\frac{1900}{3000} + \frac{60}{1100}\cdot\frac{1100}{3000}} \\
            &= \frac{28}{28 + 60} \\
            &= \mathbf{0.31818}
\end{align*}
$$

The group sizes cancel completely, leaving just the raw counts of stroke patients — 28 low-or-normal against 60 high, out of 88 in total. That shortcut is available whenever the conditional probabilities are themselves given as counts over the same group sizes: the answer is simply the share of the 88 treated policyholders who are in group $L$.

Choice (A) is the joint probability $P[S \cap L] = 28/3000 \approx 0.009$; choice (B) is $P[S \mid L] = 28/1900 \approx 0.015$, the conditioning read in the wrong direction; and choice (E) is the prior $P[L] = 1900/3000 \approx 0.633$, which ignores the stroke information.
