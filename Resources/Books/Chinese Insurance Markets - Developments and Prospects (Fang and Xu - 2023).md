An NBER working paper by Hanming Fang and Xian Xu reviewing the development of the insurance industry in China. Prepared as a chapter for the Handbook of Insurance, edited by Georges Dionne, it gives a comprehensive discussion of China's regulatory framework, major insurance segments, market structure, InsurTech and social insurance, and of the challenges and prospects for the future development of the Chinese insurance market. Dated May 2023, revised November 2023.

## 3 Regulatory Framework in China
- A country's insurance governance consists of the relevant laws and regulations and the insurance regulatory authority; the section describes the evolution of both in China after 1979.
- 3.1 China's Insurance Laws and Rules
    - 3.1.1 Insurance Law
        - The milestones are the Maritime Law (1992), the Insurance Law of the People's Republic of China (June 30, 1995), the first formal basic insurance law, and its amendments of 2002 (to fulfil China's WTO commitments), 2009, 2014 and 2015.
    - 3.1.2 Other Laws
        - The Company Law, the Tax Law and the Civil Code also apply to insurance companies.
    - 3.1.3 Key Rules and Regulations
        - The rules and regulations issued under the insurance laws include the State Council's regulations on foreign-owned insurers (2001), on compulsory motor vehicle traffic accident liability insurance (2006) and on agricultural insurance, its "Ten National Regulations" (2006) and "Ten New National Regulations" (2014), and the CIRC's and CBIRC's rules covering nearly all aspects of the insurance market.
- 3.2 Insurance Regulatory Authority
    - 3.2.1 China Insurance Regulatory Commission (CIRC)
        - The People's Bank of China oversaw insurance until November 1998, when the CIRC was established; in March 2018 it was combined with the China Banking Regulatory Commission into the CBIRC.
    - 3.2.2 China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (CBIRC)
        - The CBIRC supervised banking and insurance in a unified manner from 2018 until it was abolished in February 2023.
    - 3.2.3 National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA)
        - Established in March 2023, the NFRA subsumes the CBIRC's responsibilities, the PBOC's supervision of financial groups and certain functions of the China Securities Regulatory Commission, overseeing the financial industry except the securities sector.
    - 3.2.4 People's Bank of China
    - 3.2.5 China Insurance Industry Association
        - Established in 2001, the CIIA is a national self-regulatory organization of the insurance industry.
- 3.3 Key Regulatory Issues
    - Following the IAIS core principles, the Chinese regulators have established three pillars of regulation: market behavior, solvency and corporate governance.
    - 3.3.1 Market Behavior Regulation
        - The NFRA's market behavior rules cover the prohibition of unfair competition, consumer protection, risk management, disclosure requirements and anti-money laundering; regulation and supervision differ considerably between regions.
    - 3.3.2 Solvency Regulation
        - The CIRC's 2003 Solvency Regulation Rules set a minimum solvency ratio of 100% and minimum paid-up capital, and a system of solvency reports and inspections.
        - The Risk-Oriented Solvency System (C-ROSS), fully implemented in 2016, changed supervision from scale-oriented to risk-oriented, with core and comprehensive solvency adequacy ratios and a risk rating.
        - The CBIRC's Solvency Regulation Rules (II) of December 2021 improve the measurement of interest rate risk and the capital standards for long-term equity investments.
    - 3.3.3 Corporate Governance Regulations
        - The CIRC's 2006 guiding opinions on insurers' governance structure were superseded by the CBIRC's June 2021 Corporate Governance Rules of Banking and Insurance Institutions, which unify the regimes for insurers and banks; among their provisions, the chairman of the board may not also be the institution's president.

- 5.3 Market Imperfections
    - 5.3.1 Issues of the Insurance Market Structure
        - The direct insurance market is highly concentrated, the top 5 insurers in both life and nonlife holding over 50% combined; the reinsurance market is seriously lagging; and foreign insurers face licensing, distribution, regulatory, language and cultural barriers.
    - 5.3.2 Lack of Insurance Product Varieties
        - Most insurers offer similar products, quickly imitating a popular one, so many products are homogenous.
    - 5.3.3 Problems with the Management of Insurance Assets
        - The lack of medium- and long-term investment products with stable returns exposes the industry, especially life insurers, to asset-liability mismatch risk, and poor asset-market liquidity to liquidity risk.
    - 5.3.4 Lack of Insurance Literacy
        - Commercial insurance ceased in China from 1959 for 20 years, so awareness of risk and insurance is lower; misleading sales and delayed claims payments have contributed to consumers' distrust.
