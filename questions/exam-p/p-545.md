The total amount of all hurricane claims filed with a company in a one-year time period is modeled by a random variable, $X$, with density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{1{,}000{,}000}e^{-x/1{,}000{,}000}, & \text{for } x > 0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate, to the nearest 100,000, the difference between the 15th and 95th percentiles of the total amount of hurricane claims.

- A) $1{,}800{,}000$
- B) $1{,}900{,}000$
- C) $2{,}700{,}000$
- D) $2{,}800{,}000$
- E) $3{,}000{,}000$

## Explanation

The density is exponential with mean $\theta = 1{,}000{,}000$, so $F(x) = 1 - e^{-x/\theta}$ and the $100q$th percentile is

$$
x_q = -\theta\ln(1-q)
$$

Evaluate at both percentiles:

$$
\begin{align*}
x_{0.15} &= -1{,}000{,}000\ln(0.85) = 162{,}519 \\
x_{0.95} &= -1{,}000{,}000\ln(0.05) = 2{,}995{,}732
\end{align*}
$$

The difference is

$$
\begin{align*}
x_{0.95} - x_{0.15} &= 2{,}995{,}732 - 162{,}519 \\
                    &= 2{,}833{,}213
\end{align*}
$$

which to the nearest 100,000 is $\mathbf{2{,}800{,}000}$.

Choice (E), 3,000,000, is the 95th percentile alone, rounded — the 15th percentile is small enough (about 0.16 of a mean) that omitting it still lands near an answer choice. The exponential's long right tail is what puts the 95th percentile at nearly three times the mean while the 15th sits well below it.
