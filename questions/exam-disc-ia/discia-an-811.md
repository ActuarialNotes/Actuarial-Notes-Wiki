A display collapses in a garden center during the policy period. The CGL has a \$5,000 Medical Expense limit. Within a year of the accident, the center's insurer receives these reasonable medical expenses: customer 1, \$3,200; customer 2, \$7,500; and a self-employed electrician the center had hired to rewire the lighting, \$4,000. Customer 1 also reports another \$1,000 of expenses 13 months after the accident. What does Coverage C pay?

- A) \$8,200
- B) \$9,200
- C) \$10,700
- D) \$12,200

## Explanation

Coverage C pays reasonable medical expenses regardless of fault, up to the Medical Expense limit per person, for expenses incurred and reported within one year of the accident. Exclusion b removes a person hired to do work for an insured.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid} &= \min(3{,}200,\ 5{,}000) + \min(7{,}500,\ 5{,}000) \\
&= 3{,}200 + 5{,}000 \\
&= 8{,}200
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** adds customer 1's late \$1,000, which was reported after the one-year window.
- **C** pays customer 2 in full, ignoring the per-person limit.
- **D** also pays the hired electrician (\$8,200 + \$4,000), whom exclusion b removes.
