Given the following quarterly exposure information:

| Calendar Year and Quarter | Written Exposures | Earned Exposures |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 Q1 | 100 | 5.00 |
| 2017 Q2 | 450 | 247.50 |
| 2017 Q3 | 400 | 427.50 |
| 2017 Q4 | 100 | 52.50 |
| 2018 Q1 | 125 | 53.75 |
| 2018 Q2 | 550 | 528.75 |
| 2018 Q3 | 475 | 562.50 |
| 2018 Q4 | 30 | 59.00 |

- The company started writing business on January 1, 2017.
- The company stops writing business on December 31, 2018.
- The quarterly earnings pattern was set by analyzing historical experience across the industry and is not uniform.
- All policies are annual.
- All policies are written on the first day of the quarter.
- There are no policy cancellations and no mid-term adjustments.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the 2017 policy year earned exposures as of March 31, 2018.

### Answer
780

### Explanation
**Step 1 — recover the earning pattern.** The pattern is seasonal: an in-force annual policy earns a fixed fraction of its exposure in each *calendar* quarter. Because all policies are written on the first day of a quarter and run twelve months, the exposure earned in any quarter is

$$\text{Earned}_q = (\text{exposures in force during } q) \times s_q$$

Working forward from the first quarter, in which only the 100 exposures written in 2017 Q1 are in force:

$$s_1 = \frac{5.00}{100} = 0.05$$

In 2017 Q2 the 100 exposures from Q1 and the 450 written in Q2 are both in force, so

$$s_2 = \frac{247.50}{100 + 450} = \frac{247.50}{550} = 0.45$$

Continuing gives $s_3 = 427.50 / 950 = 0.45$ and $s_4 = 52.50 / 1{,}050 = 0.05$. The seasonal pattern is therefore

$$(s_1, s_2, s_3, s_4) = (5\%,\ 45\%,\ 45\%,\ 5\%), \qquad \sum s_q = 1$$

This reproduces all four 2018 quarters as a check — e.g. in 2018 Q2 the 2017 Q1 and Q2 policies have expired, leaving $400 + 100 + 125 + 550 = 1{,}175$ in force, and $1{,}175 \times 0.45 = 528.75$.

**Step 2 — earn each 2017 cohort to March 31, 2018.** Policy year 2017 is the business written during 2017. Each cohort is earned over the calendar quarters it is in force, up to 3/31/2018:

| Written | Exposures | Quarters earned by 3/31/2018 | Fraction earned | Earned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 Q1 | 100 | Q1–Q4 of 2017 (expired) | $1.00$ | 100 |
| 2017 Q2 | 450 | $s_2 + s_3 + s_4 + s_1$ | $1.00$ | 450 |
| 2017 Q3 | 400 | $s_3 + s_4 + s_1$ | $0.45 + 0.05 + 0.05 = 0.55$ | 220 |
| 2017 Q4 | 100 | $s_4 + s_1$ | $0.05 + 0.05 = 0.10$ | 10 |

$$\text{PY2017 earned as of 3/31/2018} = 100 + 450 + 220 + 10 = 780$$

Alternatively, work from the calendar-year figures: sum the earned exposures from 2017 Q1 through 2018 Q1 and strip out the piece belonging to policy year 2018:

$$(5 + 247.5 + 427.5 + 52.5 + 53.75) - 0.05(125) = 786.25 - 6.25 = 780$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of the determination of, and the relationships among, written, earned and in-force exposures, and to derive the quarterly earning pattern from the written and earned exposures given. The exposures were not earned evenly, and this is where candidates struggled — many assumed a uniform pattern and carried that assumption through the whole question. For this part the non-uniform pattern had to be recognized to get the amount earned in the first quarter right. Common mistakes were calculating an incorrect earnings pattern and assuming an even earning pattern instead of the actual one.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Calculate the in-force exposures as of May 31, 2018.

### Answer
1175

### Explanation
In-force exposures are simply the exposures on policies that have incepted and not yet expired on the valuation date — no earning pattern is involved. Each annual policy written on the first day of a quarter expires twelve months later:

| Written | Exposures | Policy period | In force 5/31/2018? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 Q1 | 100 | 1/1/2017 – 12/31/2017 | No — expired |
| 2017 Q2 | 450 | 4/1/2017 – 3/31/2018 | No — expired |
| 2017 Q3 | 400 | 7/1/2017 – 6/30/2018 | Yes |
| 2017 Q4 | 100 | 10/1/2017 – 9/30/2018 | Yes |
| 2018 Q1 | 125 | 1/1/2018 – 12/31/2018 | Yes |
| 2018 Q2 | 550 | 4/1/2018 – 3/31/2019 | Yes |
| 2018 Q3 | 475 | 7/1/2018 – 6/30/2019 | No — not yet written |

$$400 + 100 + 125 + 550 = 1{,}175$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the in-force exposure from the written exposures provided. A common mistake was adding the wrong quarters together — typically by including a cohort that had already expired or omitting one still in force.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Calculate the calendar year 2018 unearned exposures.

### Answer
293.5

### Explanation
Unearned exposures at 12/31/2018 are the written exposures on policies still in force, times the fraction of each policy not yet earned. Only the 2018 cohorts remain unexpired — every 2017 policy has expired by 9/30/2018:

| Written | Exposures | Earned by 12/31/2018 | Unearned fraction | Unearned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 Q1 | 125 | $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 + s_4 = 1.00$ | $0$ | 0 |
| 2018 Q2 | 550 | $s_2 + s_3 + s_4 = 0.95$ | $0.05$ | 27.5 |
| 2018 Q3 | 475 | $s_3 + s_4 = 0.50$ | $0.50$ | 237.5 |
| 2018 Q4 | 30 | $s_4 = 0.05$ | $0.95$ | 28.5 |

$$27.5 + 237.5 + 28.5 = 293.5$$

Alternatively, since the company wrote nothing before 2017, cumulative written less cumulative earned gives the same result:

$$2{,}230 - 1{,}936.5 = 293.5$$

Or roll the prior unearned balance forward — unearned at 12/31/2017 was 317.5, and calendar year 2018 earned 1,204.0 against 1,180 written:

$$317.5 - (1{,}204.0 - 1{,}180) = 317.5 - 24 = 293.5$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the calendar year 2018 unearned exposures. Common mistakes included assuming a uniform earning pattern, failing to account for the unearned written exposures carried over from 2017, and applying the earning pattern to the earned exposures rather than to the written exposures.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Calculate the calendar year 2019 quarter 1 earned exposures.

### Answer
52.75

### Explanation
The company stopped writing on December 31, 2018, so nothing new incepts in 2019 Q1 and the only exposures earning are those still in force. The 2018 Q1 policies expired 12/31/2018; the remaining 2018 cohorts are still running:

$$\text{In force in 2019 Q1} = 550 + 475 + 30 = 1{,}055$$

A first calendar quarter earns $s_1 = 5\%$, so

$$1{,}055 \times 0.05 = 52.75$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the earned exposures for the quarter immediately after writing ceased, using the correct earnings pattern. Common mistakes included using an even earnings pattern and using the wrong exposure base — applying the seasonal factor to unearned exposures rather than to the in-force exposures.
