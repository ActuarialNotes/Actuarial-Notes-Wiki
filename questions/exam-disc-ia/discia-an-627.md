After a covered fire, a shop owner adds invented stock to the inventory submitted with the claim, intending to collect more. Which commercial property condition addresses this, and with what effect?

- A) Control of property, which reduces the claim by the value of the invented stock
- B) Liberalization, which lets the insurer revise the coverage part to exclude the stock
- C) Legal action against us, which bars the owner from suing the insurer for two years
- D) Concealment, misrepresentation or fraud, which makes the coverage part void

## Explanation

The concealment, misrepresentation or fraud condition makes the coverage part void in any case of fraud by the named insured relating to it, and if any insured intentionally conceals or misrepresents a material fact about a claim. Inventing stock in a claim is such a misrepresentation. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Control of property protects the insured against acts of others beyond its control; it says nothing about the insured's own fraud.
- **B** — Liberalization automatically extends a broadened coverage to the policy; it is not a way to cut coverage.
- **C** — The legal action condition requires compliance with the policy and a suit within two years after the loss; it is not a penalty for fraud.
