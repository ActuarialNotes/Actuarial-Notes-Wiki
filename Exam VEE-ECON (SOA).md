# VEE Economics

**VEE Economics** is one of the *Validation by Educational Experience* (VEE) requirements, and both bodies ask for it. It is required for the [[Associate of the Society of Actuaries (ASA)|ASA]] and for the [[Associate of the Casualty Actuarial Society (ACAS)|ACAS]], whose VEE topics the CAS set together with the Canadian Institute of Actuaries and the SOA. A VEE is not an exam. It is credit for university coursework, or for an approved alternative, that the VEE Administration Committee has approved for the topic. A candidate applies for it once they have passed two SOA, CAS or CIA actuarial exams. A CAS candidate may claim it directly through the CAS or through the SOA. Credit obtained through the CAS may not be recognized by other actuarial organizations, such as the SOA.

> [!question]- About the requirement
>
> Typically, the VEE requirement for economics will be met if a candidate has completed two economics courses, one course covering microeconomics and the other covering macroeconomics. If distinct courses are used, the application should indicate which are for the microeconomics topic and which are for the macroeconomics topic. Most of the topics listed below should be covered.
>
> The topics are those of the SOA's *Guidelines for Approval of VEE Courses and Educational Experiences*, effective November 1, 2024, the guidelines the CAS's VEE page also points to. Credit can't be claimed for part of a topic: the CAS notes that a microeconomics course may not be submitted alone, but must come with an approved macroeconomics course on the same application.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Microeconomics
> 1. Explain the concept of [[Utility|utility]] and how [[Utility Maximization|rational utility]] maximizing agencies make consumption choices.
> 2. Explain the [[Elasticity|elasticity]] of supply and demand and the effects on a market of the different levels of elasticity.
> 3. Explain the interaction between [[Supply and Demand|supply and demand]] and the way in which [[Market Equilibrium|equilibrium market prices]] are achieved.
> 4. Explain various [[Pricing Strategy|pricing strategies]] that can be used by firms.
> 5. Explain the core economic concepts involved in choices made by businesses with respect to short-run and [[Long-Run Investment|long-run investment]] and [[Production Choice|production choices]].
> 6. Explain [[Competitive Market|competitive markets]] and how they operate.
> 7. Explain profitability in markets with [[Imperfect Competition|imperfect competition]].

> [!example]- Macroeconomics
> 1. Explain basic macroeconomic measures (e.g., [[Gross Domestic Product|GDP]]) used to compare the economies of countries.
> 2. Describe the structure of [[Public Finance|public finances]] for an [[Macroeconomics|industrialized country]].
> 3. Explain the effect of [[Fiscal Policy|fiscal]] and [[Monetary Policy|monetary policy]] on the economy, including the effect on [[Financial Market|financial markets]].
> 4. Explain the role of [[International Trade|international trade]], [[Exchange Rate|exchange rates]] and the [[Balance of Payments|balance of payments]] in the economy.
> 5. Explain the effect of [[Savings and Consumption|savings and consumption rates]] on the economy.
> 6. Explain the major factors affecting the level of [[Interest Rate|interest rates]], the rate of [[Inflation|inflation]], the [[Exchange Rate|exchange rate]], the level of employment and the rate of growth for an [[Macroeconomics|industrialized country]].
> 7. Describe the function of [[Money|money]] in the economy.
> 8. Explain the relationship between [[Money|money]] and [[Interest Rate|interest rates]].
> 9. Explain how [[Macroeconomic Policy|macroeconomic policies]] affect businesses.
