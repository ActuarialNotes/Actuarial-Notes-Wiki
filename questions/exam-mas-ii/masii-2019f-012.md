You are performing $F$-tests to test linear hypotheses about multiple fixed effects in a Linear Mixed Model. You use the Kenward-Roger method to approximate the denominator degrees of freedom. You are given the following statements about the Kenward-Roger adjustment:

I. This adjustment is necessary because the variance of the fixed effects is biased downward due to the estimation of the variance-covariance matrix.
II. This adjustment accounts for the presence of random effects and correlated residuals in the Linear Mixed Model.
III. In general, the adjustment is more critical for data sets with larger sample sizes than for smaller sample sizes.

Determine which of the proceeding statements are true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I is true.** The usual estimated covariance matrix of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}$ treats $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ as if it were the true covariance parameter, ignoring the extra uncertainty from having estimated it. That understates the variance of the fixed effects, so standard errors come out too small and tests are anti-conservative. Kenward-Roger inflates the covariance matrix to correct exactly this.

**II is true.** The adjustment is designed for the linear mixed model setting — its degrees-of-freedom approximation is what allows an $F$-test when observations are neither independent nor equally weighted, which is precisely the situation created by random effects and correlated residuals.

**III is false.** The correction matters most when the sample is **small**. As the sample grows the downward bias becomes negligible and the Kenward-Roger denominator degrees of freedom converge to the naive ones. The statement has the direction backwards.

Statements I and II are true, which is not one of options (A) through (D), so the answer is **E**.
