A newly hired actuary becomes the Appointed Actuary of a Quebec-chartered property and casualty insurer. She has previously worked only for federally regulated insurers, where she followed OSFI's guidance to the Appointed Actuary and the CIA's educational notes.

Which statement best describes how the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) affects her valuation and financial condition testing (FCT) work?

- A) The AMF issues its own annual guides for her liabilities report and her FCT, which she follows alongside the CIA's guidance
- B) The AMF regulates only securities markets, so OSFI's instructions alone govern her actuarial reports
- C) The AMF guides replace the CIA's guidance, so the CIA's FCT educational notes no longer apply to her work
- D) The AMF has no role until the insurer applies for a federal charter under the Insurance Companies Act
- E) The AMF reviews only market conduct, so it sets no requirements for the content of actuarial reports

## Explanation

The CIA's *2024 Guidance to the Appointed Actuary and Valuation Actuaries of P&C Insurers* (section 6.2) says the AMF issues specific guides to the actuaries of **Quebec-chartered** insurers for both the valuation of insurance contract liabilities and the FCT. The liabilities-report guide is updated annually, sets out the report's expected content and layout, and mandates supplementary tables. The FCT guide says actuaries follow the CIA guidance on FCT, *with some additional expectations from the AMF*. The answer is **A**: the AMF adds requirements, and the CIA's guidance still applies.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — the AMF is Quebec's integrated regulator, and it is the regulator of Quebec-chartered insurers. The P&C return instructions list it beside OSFI as a *primary regulator*, the body to which the external auditor's report is addressed. It does not regulate securities alone.
- **C** — the AMF's FCT guide builds on the CIA's FCT guidance rather than replacing it. Accepted actuarial practice in Canada still rests on the CIA's standards.
- **D** — a Quebec-chartered insurer is supervised by the AMF *because* it is provincially chartered. A federal charter would move prudential supervision to OSFI, not bring in the AMF.
- **E** — the AMF's guides govern the content and layout of the actuary's liabilities report and its FCT, which are prudential matters, so it does far more than review market conduct.
