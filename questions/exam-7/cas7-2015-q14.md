Given the following information:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Basic premium factor | 0.20 |
| Loss conversion factor | 1.20 |
| Tax multiplier | 1.05 |
| Loss capping ratio (first adjustment) | 85% |
| Incremental loss capping ratio (second adjustment) | 60% |
| Expected loss ratio | 75% |
| Percent of loss emerged at first adjustment | 80% |

## Part a (1.25 points)

Calculate the premium development to loss development (PDLD) ratios for the first and second premium adjustments.

### Explanation
First adjustment:

$$\text{PDLD}_1 = \left[\frac{BP}{S \cdot \text{ELR} \cdot \%L_1} + \frac{CL}{L} \cdot \text{LCF}\right] \cdot TM = \left[\frac{0.2}{0.75 \times 0.8} + 0.85 \times 1.2\right] \times 1.05 = 1.421$$

Second adjustment (incremental capped loss ratio × LCF × TM):

$$\text{PDLD}_2 = 0.6 \times 1.2 \times 1.05 = 0.756$$

### Examiner Report
A majority of the candidates had a good understanding of this part.

- The candidate was expected to know how to calculate the "Premium Development" to "Loss Development" ratio at different points in the life-cycle of a retro rated policy.
- The candidate was expected to understand the data provided in the question, use the appropriate formulae, and compute the PDLD ratios at the first and second adjustment period correctly.  Full credit was given if the candidates went straight to the computation without the intermediate step of writing down the formulae.
- A few candidates were unable to link the appropriate meaning to the values provided in the question and used them incorrectly.
- There were very few arithmetic errors.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly explain two benefits of using the retrospective rating formula method over using historical data when calculating the PDLD ratio.

### Explanation
- The formula reflects the pricing parameters of the plans currently being sold; policy terms may have changed since the historical policies were written, so the formula better reflects current conditions.
- PDLD ratios from the formula are more stable than empirical ones — historical patterns can be extremely volatile, making development selections difficult.

### Examiner Report
This part was more challenging than part a.

- The candidate was expected to provide a brief description of two benefits of using a formula based approach to calculate PDLD ratios as against using an empirical "loss reported data history" as the basis.
- The candidate should have summarized the potential for a "responsive" method that facilitates the inclusion of the latest pricing mechanisms used in selling the policies and secondly the reduction in the volatility of the estimated ratios, when the process is driven by the "formula" method as against the "historical data" method.
- Many candidates were careless about the wording of their answers and did not relate the underlying factors in a correct and coherent manner.
Many candidates overlooked the fact that a time element was inevitable before the final closure of a retro treaty.

## Part c (0.75 points)

Assuming all claims are greater than the plan minimum, explain how a push to settle small claims faster would impact PDLD ratios.

### Explanation
- Small claims fall within the plan limitations (they are not capped by the per-occurrence limit), so settling them faster increases losses and capped losses by the same amount in the early periods: the ratio of capped to total loss moves toward one and the **early PDLD ratios increase**.
- Later loss emergence then comes mostly from large claims, whose development is likely outside the plan parameters (it hits the cap), so the **PDLD ratios for later adjustments decrease**.
- In short, the PDLD pattern starts steeper and flattens faster.
