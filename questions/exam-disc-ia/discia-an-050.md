A customer wins a \$1,250,000 compensatory judgment against the insured for an injury on its premises. The insured's CGL policy has a \$1,000,000 each occurrence limit, and the aggregate is not an issue. The insurer spent \$200,000 defending the suit. Ignoring interest and court costs, how much does the insurer pay in total?

- A) \$1,000,000
- B) \$1,200,000
- C) \$1,250,000
- D) \$1,450,000

## Explanation

Damages are paid up to the each occurrence limit. The expenses the insurer incurs in defending a suit are supplementary payments, which do not reduce the limits of insurance.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Insurer pays} &= \min(1{,}250{,}000,\ 1{,}000{,}000) + 200{,}000 \\
&= 1{,}200{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The insured bears the \$250,000 of the judgment above the limit. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — treats defense costs as eroding the limit.
- **C** — pays the whole judgment, ignoring the limit, and leaves out the defense costs.
- **D** — pays the whole judgment plus defense, ignoring the limit.
