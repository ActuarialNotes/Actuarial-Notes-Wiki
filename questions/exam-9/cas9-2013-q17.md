Consider the following assumptions for a new policy:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Expected losses | \$100,000 |
| Expenses | \$20,000 |
| Policyholder supplied funds as a ratio to premium | 43.6% |
| Tax rate | 35% |
| Before-tax return on invested assets | 6% |
| Target return on equity | 8% |
| Surplus-to-premium ratio | 2.0 |
| Surplus-to-equity ratio | 1.0 |

## Part a (1 point)

Using the calendar year return on equity method, calculate the indicated price for the policy.

### Explanation
ROE = [U·P + i·(PHSF%·P + S)]·(1 - tax%)/E

8% = [(1 - CR)·P + .06·(43.6%·P + 2·P)]·(1 - 35%)/E

(8%·E/P)/.65 = 8%·2/.65 = 1 - CR + .06·2.436

CR = (Loss + Expense)/Premium = 120,000/Premium

CR = 1 - 8%·2/.65 + .06·2.436 = 0.900

Premium = 120,000/0.900 = \$133,333

Alternatively:

$$U = \frac{1}{1 - t_u}\left[r \cdot \frac{QSR}{PSR} - i_{AFIT}\left(PHSF + \frac{1}{PSR}\right)\right]$$

$$U = \frac{1}{1 - .35}\left[.08\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}}\right) - .06(1 - .35)\left(.436 + \frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right]$$

U = 0.1

$$P = \frac{L + F_X}{1 - V - U} = \frac{100{,}000 + 20{,}000}{1 - .1} = \$133{,}333$$

Alternatively:

$$U = \frac{1}{1 - Tax}\left[ROE \times \frac{E/S}{P/S} - I_{Aft} \times \left(PHSF + \frac{1}{P/S}\right)\right]$$

$$= \frac{1}{-.35}\left[.08 \times \frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}} - .06(1 - 35) \times (.436 + 2)\right] \text{ (as printed)}$$

$= .09999$

Price = (Losses + Expenses)/(1 - U)

$= \dfrac{100k + 20k}{1 - .0999}$

$= 133{,}332$

Alternatively:

$$U = \frac{1}{1 - t}\left(r\left[\frac{Eg}{Prem}\right] - I_{af}\left[PHSF + \frac{slur}{prem}\right]\right) \text{ (Eg, slur as printed)}$$

I after tax = .06(1 - .35) = 39% (as printed)

$$U = \frac{1}{1 - .35}[.16 - .039(2.436)] = 10\%$$

$$P = \frac{100{,}000 + 20{,}000}{1 - .10} = 133{,}333$$

Alternatively:

$$ROE = \frac{UW\ profit + II - taxes}{Equity}$$

$$8\% = \frac{([P - 100k - 20k] + 6\%(.436P + S)) \times (1 - t)}{Equity}$$

S:P = 2 → S = 2P

S:Eq = 1 → S = Equity

$$8\% = \frac{(1 - .35)[P - 120K + .06(2.436P)]}{2P} \qquad .08 = \frac{-7800}{2P} + \frac{.745P}{2P} = .3725 - \frac{3900}{P} \qquad P = \$133{,}332$$

(the 7800 and 3900 are as printed).

Alternatively:

Iafit = 0.06(1 - 0.35) = 0.039; E/p = S/p · E/s = 2·1 = 2

$$U = \frac{r\left(\frac{e}{p}\right) - i_{afit}\left(PHSF + \frac{s}{p}\right)}{1 - t_u}$$

$$U = \frac{0.08(2) - 0.039(0.436 + 2)}{1 - 0.35} = 0.100$$

$$P = \frac{100{,}000 + 20{,}000}{1 - 0.1000}$$ → I assume all expenses are fixed

P = 133,333.33

### Examiner Report
Candidates who listed out the correct formula, plug in the right number, and accurately calculate the final answer for P will receive full credit for part a. They may use formula in either model solution #1 or #2.

Common errors:

- Many candidates did not lay out the formula; rather they directly plug in the numbers. 0.25 point was deducted if the candidate failed to write out one or more formula.
- Some candidates express figures in \$1000s without specifically noting it on the paper with a "K" or "in \$1000s", as a result they get final answer of P = \$133.33, rather than \$133,333. 0.25 point was taken out in this situation. This deduction was necessary because the problem asked for a premium number and there is a big difference in the context of the problem between a premium of \$133.33 and \$133,333.
- Many candidates did not correctly use after-tax return on invested assets, rather they used the before tax interest rate.
- Many candidates miscalculated the ratio of QSR / PSR, and/or 1/PSR as used in the underwriting profit provision formula.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two ways that risk is reflected in the calendar year return on equity method.

### Explanation
1. Risk can be reflected in the selection of the surplus requirement
2. Alternatively we can adjust for risk by selection a different target return.

Alternatively:

The target return on equity, r, reflects the riskiness of the investment for the shareholders; The Premium to Surplus ratio, PSR, reflects the amount of surplus required to support the riskiness of the written business.

Alternatively:

It is reflected in the – selection of the target return on equity; this will be based on the risk of policy or line. – Selection of the leverage ratio (premium to surplus) or allocated surplus- this is based on the risk of the line.

Alternatively:

By selecting target return on equity; By selecting surplus to premium ratio.

Alternatively:

Uses benchmark leverage ratios to ensure an amount of equity that reflects risk inherent to the policy. Selected target ROE is set high enough to ensure an adequate return for the risk.

Alternatively:

Based on the selected target return on equity, (ROE) higher ROE = more conservative = can absorb more risk

Lower ROE = less conservative- can not absorb as much risk

Based on the allocated surplus, Higher surplus- more cushion to absorb risk. Lower surplus = Less Cushion to absorb risk

### Examiner Report
Those who pointed out the two key items – selection of target ROE and selection of allocated surplus or leverage ratio received full credit for part b. Missing either item will result in a 0.25 point deduction.

Common errors:

- Some candidates layout the pros and cons of the CY ROE method (i.e. how results can be distorted by using calendar year data) as there were similar questions asked in prior exams, however, this was not exactly what this question is asking for.
- Note that the question asks one to "describe two ways that risk is reflected in the Calendar Year Return on Equity method", but not to "identify which component of CY ROE formula contains risk or indicates risk". The model answer gives a couple examples "via the selection of target ROE and via the selection of allocated surplus", both of which are items that can be judgmentally selected based on your contemplation of the risk level. In other words, the question is looking for "measures/ways" CY ROE method allows user to adjust for risk, but not simply a list of risks the user faces.

Some candidates identified underwriting risk, investment risk, asset risk, and etc. They also explained what these risks are or why they exist. Although those risks mentioned are relevant to the insurance business, they are not "ways" with which user can use CY ROE method to reflect risks. Therefore, no credit was given.
