A weather satellite is sending a binary code of 0s and 1s describing a tropical storm. Suppose 70% of the message being sent is 0s and there is an 80% chance of any particular 0 or 1 being received correctly. (If a 1 is sent and incorrectly received, it is received as a 0, and vice versa.)

Calculate the probability that a 0 was sent, given that a 1 was received.

- A) $0.16$
- B) $0.34$
- C) $0.37$
- D) $0.63$
- E) $0.90$

## Explanation

The priors are $P[\text{0 sent}] = 0.7$ and $P[\text{1 sent}] = 0.3$. A 1 is received either because a 0 was sent and garbled, or because a 1 was sent and came through:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{1 received} \mid \text{0 sent}] &= 0.2 \\
P[\text{1 received} \mid \text{1 sent}] &= 0.8
\end{align*}
$$

Apply Bayes' theorem:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{0 sent} \mid \text{1 received}] &= \frac{(0.7)(0.2)}{(0.7)(0.2) + (0.3)(0.8)} \\
                                        &= \frac{0.14}{0.14 + 0.24} \\
                                        &= \frac{0.14}{0.38} \\
                                        &= \frac{7}{19} = \mathbf{0.368}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.16, is close to the numerator 0.14, the joint probability without the division. The answer is high for a channel that is 80% reliable precisely because 0s are more than twice as common as 1s: even a rare transmission error on the plentiful 0s produces a substantial share of the received 1s.
