A February 2016 study by the Providence Center for Outcomes Research & Education (CORE), completed in partnership with Enterprise Community Partners, that explores the link between affordable housing and health care through several national health reform metrics. It uses Medicaid claims data to measure changes in health care cost and use, and survey data to examine access and quality, for residents of 145 housing properties of three types: family housing, permanent supportive housing, and housing for seniors and people with disabilities. It also considers the impact of integrated services within housing.

## Executive Summary
- Key findings: total Medicaid expenditures declined by 12%, outpatient primary care utilization increased 20% in the year after moving in, ED use fell by 18%, and many residents reported better access to care and better quality of care after move-in.
- The presence of health services was a driver of lower costs and ED use, despite low awareness among residents.
