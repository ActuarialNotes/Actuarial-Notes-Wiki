This year, a dental insurance company reimburses up to two fillings and one root canal. A policyholder's number of fillings and number of root canals are independent and Poisson distributed with means 1 and 0.3, respectively.

Determine the probability that the policyholder has no unreimbursed fillings and no unreimbursed root canals this year.

- A) $(0.5e^{-1})(0.3e^{-0.3})$
- B) $(2.5e^{-1})(1.3e^{-0.3})$
- C) $1 - (2.5e^{-1})(1.3e^{-0.3})$
- D) $(1 - 2.5e^{-1})(1 - 1.3e^{-0.3})$
- E) $1 - (1 - 2.5e^{-1})(1 - 1.3e^{-0.3})$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the number of fillings and $Y$ the number of root canals. Nothing goes unreimbursed exactly when $X \le 2$ and $Y \le 1$.

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \le 2] &= e^{-1}\left[\frac{1^0}{0!} + \frac{1^1}{1!} + \frac{1^2}{2!}\right] = 2.5e^{-1} \\
P[Y \le 1] &= e^{-0.3}\left[\frac{0.3^0}{0!} + \frac{0.3^1}{1!}\right] = 1.3e^{-0.3}
\end{align*}
$$

The two counts are independent, so the joint probability is the product:

$$
P[X \le 2]\,P[Y \le 1] = \mathbf{(2.5e^{-1})(1.3e^{-0.3})}
$$

Numerically that is $(0.9197)(0.9631) = 0.8857$.

Choice (D) takes the complement inside each factor: $1 - 2.5e^{-1} = P[X \ge 3]$ and $1 - 1.3e^{-0.3} = P[Y \ge 2]$, so (D) is the probability of having unreimbursed fillings *and* unreimbursed root canals — the opposite event in both coordinates, worth only 0.003.
