A computer manufacturer collects data on how long it takes before its computers fail. The time to fail, in years, follows an exponential distribution. Twenty percent of its computers fail within two years.

The probability a randomly selected computer fails before time $t$, in years, is 0.80.

Calculate $t$.

- A) $3.6$
- B) $7.2$
- C) $8.0$
- D) $14.4$
- E) $16.0$

## Explanation

Let $\beta$ be the mean of the failure time $X$, so that $P[X \leq x] = 1 - e^{-x/\beta}$. The mean is not given, so recover it from the 20% figure first:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.2 &= P[X \leq 2] = 1 - e^{-2/\beta} \\
e^{-2/\beta} &= 0.8 \\
\beta &= \frac{-2}{\ln 0.8} \\
      &= 8.96284
\end{align*}
$$

Now solve for the time by which 80% have failed:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.8 &= 1 - e^{-t/8.96284} \\
0.2 &= e^{-t/8.96284} \\
t   &= -8.96284\ln(0.2) \\
    &= \mathbf{14.42513}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 8.0, is $4 \times 2$ — scaling the two years by the ratio of the probabilities, as though failure accumulated linearly in time. It does not: the exponential's failure probability rises quickly at first and then flattens, so reaching 80% takes far longer than four times as long as reaching 20%. Note also that $t = 14.4$ exceeds the mean 8.96, as it must, since more than half the computers have failed by then.
