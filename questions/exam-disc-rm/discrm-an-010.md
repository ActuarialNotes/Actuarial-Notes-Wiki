A parcel delivery company's vans are dented in parking lots dozens of times a year, and each repair costs a few hundred dollars. Using the traditional risk management matrix, which treatment best fits this exposure?

- A) Retention combined with loss control
- B) Avoidance of the exposure
- C) Transfer by insurance with no deductible
- D) Duplication of the delivery fleet

## Explanation

The exposure is **high in frequency and low in severity**. The matrix points to **retention with loss control**: the losses are predictable enough to budget, and reducing their frequency, for example through driver training or parking procedures, pays off because they happen so often. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Avoidance fits exposures that are both frequent and severe. Giving up the vans would mean giving up the business.
- **C** — Insuring frequent small losses costs more in premium and insurer expense than the losses themselves; insurance suits low-frequency, high-severity exposures.
- **D** — Duplication keeps spares for use after a loss, which addresses severe interruptions, not frequent minor dents.
