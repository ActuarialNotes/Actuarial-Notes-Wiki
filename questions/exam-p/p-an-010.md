A disease affects 30% of the population. A diagnostic test returns positive for 80% of those who have the disease and positive for 20% of those who do not. If a randomly selected person tests positive, what is the probability they actually have the disease?

- A) 0.24
- B) 0.48
- C) 0.63
- D) 0.80
- E) 0.37

## Explanation

Let $D$ = has the disease and $+$ = tests positive. We are given $P(D) = 0.30$, $P(+ \mid D) = 0.80$ and $P(+ \mid D^c) = 0.20$.

$D$ and $D^c$ partition the population, so the law of total probability gives the overall positive rate:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(+) &= P(+ \mid D)\,P(D) + P(+ \mid D^c)\,P(D^c) \\
     &= 0.80(0.30) + 0.20(0.70) \\
     &= 0.24 + 0.14 = 0.38
\end{align*}
$$

Then Bayes' theorem:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(D \mid +) &= \frac{P(+ \mid D)\,P(D)}{P(+)} \\
             &= \frac{0.24}{0.38} \\
             &= 0.6316 \approx \mathbf{0.63}
\end{align*}
$$
