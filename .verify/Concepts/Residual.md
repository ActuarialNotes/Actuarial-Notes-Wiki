## [F-001] Externally studentized t degrees of freedom given without defining p
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-10-04T20:47Z/8861
- date: 2026-10-05
- severity: minor
- status: open
- locus: Linear regression, second bullet ('follows a t distribution with n - p - 2 degrees of freedom')
- claim: The externally studentized residual, using sigma-hat_(i), 'follows a t distribution with n - p - 2 degrees of freedom'. The page never says what p counts.
- evidence: Shalizi, CMU 36-401 Fall 2015, Lecture 20 'Outliers and Influential Points', eqs. (32)-(34), PDF pp.12-13: t_i = e_i / (sigma-hat_(-i) sqrt(1 - H_ii)) ~ t_{n-p-2}, with p the number of predictors (the intercept is the '+1' in the p + 1 coordinates of eq. 37) - the convention of ISLR, which tests on n - p - 1 df (eq. 3.24, p.77) and gives average leverage (p + 1)/n (p.99). Penn State STAT 501 Lesson 11 §11.4 states the same residual follows a t distribution with (n - 1) - p df, 'p = the number of regression parameters including the intercept', which is also how Dobson & Barnett use p. Both give 16 df for n = 20 with two predictors plus an intercept; the page's n - p - 2 read with p = 3 coefficients gives 15. Correct under ISLR's convention, off by one under the other MAS-I reading's.
- source_rank: 3
- proposed_action: Define p (number of predictors, excluding the intercept, as in ISLR) or write the df as n - (number of coefficients) - 1.
- applied: false
- fingerprint: 4b66b1b892a9

## [F-002] Poisson deviance residuals presented as the ones to check against Normal quantiles
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-10-04T20:47Z/8861
- date: 2026-10-05
- severity: minor
- status: open
- locus: Example 'Pearson and Deviance Residuals for Claim Counts', closing paragraph (and GLM bullet 'usual choice for a QQ plot')
- claim: For an individual-policy Poisson frequency GLM: 'The deviance residuals pull the two toward symmetry, which is why they are the ones plotted against Normal quantiles.'
- evidence: Goldburd, Khare, Tevet & Guller, GLMs for Insurance Rating (CAS Monograph 5, 2nd ed.), §6.3.1 pp.67-69 (PDF 77-79): deviance residuals are approximately normal 'if the assumed GLM distribution is correct', illustrated with a gamma severity model's q-q plot, but 'For discrete distributions (such as Poisson or negative binomial) ... the deviance residuals will likely not follow a normal distribution ... This makes deviance residuals less useful for assessing the appropriateness of such distributions'; it points to randomized quantile residuals or binned working residuals instead, and says the problem fades only for highly aggregated data. Rodriguez, GLM notes ch.3 §3.8.1, PDF p.47, says the same of Pearson residuals (approximately normal with grouped data, 'not the case with individual data'). The example's arithmetic is right (recomputed: r^P = 1.643 / -1.095, d = 1.898 / 2.4, r^D = 1.378 / -1.549); the defect is the conclusion drawn for individual Poisson counts.
- source_rank: 3
- proposed_action: Add the source's caveat: for individual-level discrete (Poisson) data deviance residuals cluster and are not close to Normal, so the Normal QQ check suits continuous responses (e.g. gamma) or aggregated counts. Authoring - needs a human.
- applied: false
- fingerprint: 296fe3be2d16

## [F-003] LMM: covariances R and V belong to the errors, not to the estimated residuals
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-10-04T20:47Z/8861
- date: 2026-10-05
- severity: minor
- status: open
- locus: Linear mixed models paragraph; example 'The Residual Covariance Implied by a Random Intercept'
- claim: Conditional residuals y - X beta-hat - Z b-hat 'estimate epsilon and have covariance R'; marginal residuals y - X beta-hat 'have covariance V = Z D Z^T + R'.
- evidence: Loy & Hofmann, 'HLMdiag', J. Stat. Softw. 56(5) (2014), §1.1 p.3 and §2 p.4: the model gives y_i ~ N(X_i beta, sigma^2 V_i), V_i = I + Z_i D Z_i^T (D scaled by sigma^2), and the residual types are defined on the true parameters - conditional eps_i = y_i - X_i beta - Z_i b_i, marginal zeta_i = y_i - X_i beta = Z_i b_i + eps_i. R and V are the covariances of eps and zeta. With beta estimated they are not - the distinction the page itself draws for OLS (Var(e_i) = sigma^2 (1 - h_ii); Shalizi Lecture 20 eq. 28). Rank-5 check: random intercept, sigma_b^2 = 40, sigma^2 = 160, intercept-only X, m policyholders x 3 years, GLS beta-hat: Var(y - X beta-hat) = V - X (X^T V^-1 X)^-1 X^T has within-policyholder diagonal 181.33 and off-diagonal 21.33 for m = 5 (correlation 0.118, not 0.20), approaching 200 / 40 only as m grows (m = 50: 198.13 / 38.13). The example's V_i = 40J + 160I and correlation 0.20 are right as the model-implied covariance of the marginal errors (Rodriguez, Models for Longitudinal and Clustered Data, PDF p.4: cor(Y_ij, Y_ij') = sigma_a^2 / (sigma_a^2 + sigma_e^2)).
- source_rank: 3
- proposed_action: Attribute R and V to the errors eps and Zb + eps (the model-implied covariance structure) and say the fitted residuals only approximate it. Authoring - needs a human.
- applied: false
- fingerprint: 969b7e1b3949
