While reviewing a wholesaler's balance sheet to identify risks, a risk manager notes a large balance of accounts receivable owed by a handful of retail customers. Which risk does this item most directly point to, and in which category?

- A) Credit risk, a financial risk
- B) Property damage risk, a hazard risk
- C) Product liability risk, a hazard risk
- D) Reputational risk, a strategic risk

## Explanation

Each asset on the balance sheet can be lost. Receivables are money owed by customers, so the risk is that a customer fails to pay: **credit risk**, which is a **financial risk**. Concentration in a few customers makes it larger. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Property damage applies to physical assets such as warehouses and inventory, not amounts owed.
- **C** — Product liability arises from goods that injure someone; the receivables say nothing about that.
- **D** — Reputation is a strategic risk, but a receivable balance points to whether customers pay, not to the firm's reputation.
