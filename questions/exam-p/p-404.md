The joint probability function of $X$ and $Y$ is

$$
p(x,y) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{24 - 7x - 3y}{126}, & \text{for } x = 0, 1, 2 \text{ and } y = 0, 1, 2 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate $\text{Var}(Y)$.

- A) $0.56$
- B) $0.65$
- C) $0.75$
- D) $0.80$
- E) $0.87$

## Explanation

Only the marginal distribution of $Y$ is needed, so sum the joint function over $x$ for each value of $y$:

$$
\begin{align*}
p_Y(0) &= \frac{24 + 17 + 10}{126} = \frac{51}{126} \\
p_Y(1) &= \frac{21 + 14 + 7}{126} = \frac{42}{126} \\
p_Y(2) &= \frac{18 + 11 + 4}{126} = \frac{33}{126}
\end{align*}
$$

These add to $126/126 = 1$, confirming the normalizing constant.

Now the two moments:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y] &= 0\left(\frac{51}{126}\right) + 1\left(\frac{42}{126}\right) + 2\left(\frac{33}{126}\right) \\
     &= \frac{108}{126}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y^2] &= 0^2\left(\frac{51}{126}\right) + 1^2\left(\frac{42}{126}\right) + 2^2\left(\frac{33}{126}\right) \\
       &= \frac{174}{126}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(Y) &= \frac{174}{126} - \left(\frac{108}{126}\right)^2 \\
              &= \mathbf{0.64626}
\end{align*}
$$

Marginalizing first is what keeps this a one-variable calculation — no assumption about independence of $X$ and $Y$ is needed or valid. Marginalizing the wrong variable is the trap: summing over $y$ instead gives $p_X(x) = (63 - 21x)/126$, and then $\text{Var}(X) = 1 - (84/126)^2 = 0.5556$, which is choice (A).
