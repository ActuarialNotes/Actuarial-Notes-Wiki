Given the following data evaluated as of December 31, 2018:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Paid Claims (\$000) as of 12 months | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,200 | 2,325 | 2,900 | 3,100 |
| 2016 | 1,800 | 3,300 | 4,100 | |
| 2017 | 1,500 | 2,800 | | |
| 2018 | 1,700 | | | |

| Accident Year | Case Outstanding (\$000) as of 12 months | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,500 | 800 | 400 | 160 |
| 2016 | 2,000 | 1,150 | 575 | |
| 2017 | 1,750 | 975 | | |
| 2018 | 2,200 | | | |

| Value | Item |
|---|---|
| 1.15 | 48-Ult paid claim to prior case outstanding development factor |

- There is no paid or reported development beyond 60 months.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Estimate unpaid claims for accident year 2018 as of December 31, 2018 using a case outstanding development technique.

### Answer
Approximately \$3,094 thousand

### Explanation
The case outstanding development technique projects unpaid claims directly from the case reserve, using two sets of ratios: how case outstanding runs off from one maturity to the next, and how much is *paid* in each period relative to the **prior** period's case outstanding.

**Step 1 — incremental paid claims.** Difference the cumulative paid triangle:

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,200 | 1,125 | 575 | 200 |
| 2016 | 1,800 | 1,500 | 800 | |
| 2017 | 1,500 | 1,300 | | |
| 2018 | 1,700 | | | |

**Step 2 — case outstanding to prior case outstanding.**

| Ratio | Observations | Selected |
|---|---|---|
| 12–24 | $800/1{,}500 = 0.533$, $1{,}150/2{,}000 = 0.575$, $975/1{,}750 = 0.557$ | $0.5552$ |
| 24–36 | $400/800 = 0.500$, $575/1{,}150 = 0.500$ | $0.5000$ |
| 36–48 | $160/400 = 0.400$ | $0.4000$ |

**Step 3 — incremental paid to prior case outstanding.** Note the denominator is the case outstanding at the *start* of the period, not the current one:

| Ratio | Observations | Selected |
|---|---|---|
| 12–24 | $1{,}125/1{,}500 = 0.750$, $1{,}500/2{,}000 = 0.750$, $1{,}300/1{,}750 = 0.743$ | $0.7476$ |
| 24–36 | $575/800 = 0.719$, $800/1{,}150 = 0.696$ | $0.7072$ |
| 36–48 | $200/400 = 0.500$ | $0.5000$ |
| 48–Ult | given | $1.1500$ |

**Step 4 — project accident year 2018 forward from its case outstanding of 2,200.** At each step, the payment comes from the case outstanding standing at the beginning of the period, and the case outstanding then runs off to the next maturity:

| From | Case Outstanding | Incremental Paid | Next Case Outstanding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 → 24 | 2,200.00 | $2{,}200 \times 0.7476 = 1{,}644.76$ | $2{,}200 \times 0.5552 = 1{,}221.35$ |
| 24 → 36 | 1,221.35 | $1{,}221.35 \times 0.7072 = 863.74$ | $1{,}221.35 \times 0.5000 = 610.67$ |
| 36 → 48 | 610.67 | $610.67 \times 0.5000 = 305.34$ | $610.67 \times 0.4000 = 244.27$ |
| 48 → Ult | 244.27 | $244.27 \times 1.15 = 280.91$ | — |

The unpaid claims estimate is the sum of all the *future* payments (the 1,700 already paid at 12 months is excluded):

$$1{,}644.76 + 863.74 + 305.34 + 280.91 = \$3{,}094.75 \text{ thousand}$$

**Cross-check via the alternative case outstanding method.** Completing accident year 2015 to ultimate ($1.15 \times 160 + 3{,}100 = 3{,}284$) and building 12-to-ultimate factors on paid and on reported claims gives $CDF_{paid} = 2.6491$ and $CDF_{rep} = 1.2194$. The case outstanding development factor is then

$$\frac{(CDF_{rep} - 1) \times CDF_{paid}}{CDF_{paid} - CDF_{rep}} + 1 = 1.4065$$

$$\text{Unpaid} = 2{,}200 \times 1.4065 = \$3{,}094.25 \text{ thousand}$$

The two approaches agree to within rounding, so the answer is approximately **\$3,094 thousand**.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the mechanics and assumptions of the case outstanding method and apply either version of it to the data. Common mistakes included calculating ultimate claims rather than unpaid claims; omitting the 48-to-ultimate paid-on-prior-case factor or applying it incorrectly; using a method other than one of the two case outstanding methods; using cumulative paid claims and cumulative development factors instead of incremental paid claims and ratios; and using the current year's case outstanding rather than the prior year's when forming the paid-on-prior-case ratios.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Briefly describe a scenario where it would be appropriate to use the case outstanding development technique.

### Explanation
The technique is appropriate where there is little or no **pure IBNR**, since it projects everything from the case reserve on claims already reported and so cannot pick up claims that have not yet been reported. For example:

- A **short-tailed line of business** — property, for instance — in which essentially all claims are reported within the first development period, so the case outstanding at the valuation date already covers nearly the whole population of claims.
- A **claims-made policy**, where coverage is triggered by the report date, so by construction there is no pure IBNR at the end of the policy period.
- A **self-insured entity** whose data is limited to case outstanding, with no paid or reported triangle of its own; it can apply the case outstanding development factors implied by industry paid and reported patterns to its own case reserves.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to give a scenario in which the case outstanding method would be appropriate or preferred over other methods. Common mistakes included listing an *assumption* of the method rather than a scenario in which it applies, and describing scenarios in which the method would not be appropriate.
