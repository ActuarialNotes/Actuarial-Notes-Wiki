**HO-3 Liability Exclusions** are the Section II exclusions of the ISO Homeowners 3 – Special Form (HO 00 03, 05 11 edition). They remove from [[HO-3 Liability Coverages|personal liability (Coverage E) and medical payments to others (Coverage F)]] the exposures that belong on other policies or are uninsurable. The form groups them as:

1. A. Motor vehicle liability, both coverages.
2. B. Watercraft liability, both coverages.
3. C. Aircraft liability and D. hovercraft liability, both coverages.
4. E. Exclusions that apply to both coverages: intended injury, business, professional services and the rest.
5. F. Exclusions for Coverage E only.
6. G. Exclusions for Coverage F only.

Exclusions A to D, and E.4 (uninsured premises), do not apply to bodily injury to a residence employee arising out of and in the course of employment by an insured.

## Motor vehicles, aircraft and hovercraft

Both coverages exclude liability arising from the ownership, maintenance, use, loading, entrustment or supervision of others' use of a "motor vehicle", a self-propelled land or amphibious vehicle, or a trailer being towed or carried by one. The exclusion applies to a motor vehicle that is registered for public roads, or required to be registered where the occurrence happens, or that is being raced, rented to others, used to carry people or cargo for a charge, or used in a business (except a golf cart on a golfing facility). Even then the only motor vehicles covered are ones:

1. In dead storage on an insured location.
2. Used solely to service a residence.
3. Designed to assist the handicapped, and in that use or parked on an insured location.
4. Designed for off-road recreational use and either not owned by an insured, or owned and on an insured location (the residence premises and most other insured locations), or off it only if a battery-powered children's toy that can't exceed 5 mph.
5. An owned golf cart for up to four people that can't exceed 25 mph, while on a golfing facility or inside a private residential community, including its roads where carts may legally travel.

Aircraft liability and hovercraft liability are not covered at all. Model or hobby aircraft not designed to carry people or cargo are not aircraft.

> [!example]- Does Section II cover each injury? {Example}
> (a) The insured's teenage daughter, driving the family's registered car, hits a cyclist; (b) the insured, mowing his lawn with his riding mower, throws a stone that injures a neighbor; (c) the insured's son crashes the family's own ATV into a hiker on a friend's farm.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - **(a) Excluded.** The car is a registered motor vehicle, so the family's auto policy responds ([[PAP Liability Coverage]]).
> > - **(b) Covered.** The mower is an unregistered motor vehicle used solely to service a residence.
> > - **(c) Excluded.** An owned off-road recreational vehicle is covered only on an insured location, and the friend's farm isn't one. A non-owned one would be covered anywhere it need not be registered.

## Watercraft

Watercraft liability is excluded if the watercraft is being raced (except sailing vessels and predicted log cruises), rented to others, used to carry people or cargo for a charge, or used in a business. Otherwise coverage applies only if the watercraft:

1. Is stored.
2. Is a sailboat under 26 feet, or 26 feet or more and not owned by or rented to an insured.
3. Has an inboard or inboard-outdrive engine (including a water jet pump) of 50 horsepower or less not owned by an insured, or more than 50 not owned by or rented to an insured.
4. Has outboard motors of 25 total horsepower or less, more than 25 not owned by an insured, more than 25 owned and acquired during the policy period, or more than 25 owned before the period if declared at inception or reported in writing within 45 days of acquisition.

> [!example]- Does Section II cover the swimmer and the dock worker? {Example}
> (a) The insured, driving a borrowed jet ski powered by a 110-horsepower water jet pump, injures a swimmer; (b) the insured's own 22-foot sailboat strikes a dock worker.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - **(a) Covered.** An inboard water jet engine over 50 horsepower is covered if it is not owned by or rented to an insured, and this one is borrowed.
> > - **(b) Covered.** A sailing vessel under 26 feet is covered even when owned.

> [!trap] Treating inboard and outboard engines alike
> An inboard engine over 50 horsepower is covered only if an insured neither owns nor rents the boat, so a borrowed one is covered and a rented one isn't. An outboard over 25 horsepower is covered whenever an insured doesn't own it, rented or not.

## Exclusions for both coverages

Both coverages exclude:

1. Bodily injury or property damage expected or intended by an insured, even if of a different kind or degree, or to a different person or property, than intended, except injury from reasonable force used to protect persons or property.
2. Business: injury arising from a "business" conducted from an insured location or engaged in by an insured.
3. Professional services.
4. Premises an insured owns or rents, or rents to others, that are not an insured location.
5. War.
6. Communicable disease transmitted by an insured.
7. Sexual molestation, corporal punishment, or physical or mental abuse.
8. Use, sale, manufacture, delivery or possession of a controlled substance, except legitimate use of prescribed drugs.

A business is a trade, profession or occupation, or any other activity for money, except activities for which no insured received more than \$2,000 in the 12 months before the policy period, volunteer work, home day care traded without pay, and home day care for a relative. The exclusion does not apply to renting an insured location occasionally as a residence, renting part of it as a residence (unless a single-family unit takes more than two roomers or boarders), or renting part as an office, school, studio or private garage; nor to an insured under 21 in a part-time or occasional self-employed business with no employees.

> [!example]- Does Coverage E cover each claim? {Example}
> (a) The insured runs a paid day care for four neighbors' children, earning \$15,000 a year, and a child is injured; (b) the insured's 16-year-old son mows lawns for pay with no employees, and a customer's child is hit by a stone from his mower; (c) the insured punches a party guest in anger; (d) the insured's spouse trips on a toy and sues the insured; (e) a visiting friend is injured by the insured's dog.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - **(a) Excluded.** The paid home day care is a "business" because more than \$2,000 was received, and none of the exceptions fits.
> > - **(b) Covered.** An insured under 21 in a part-time, self-employed business with no employees is an exception to the business exclusion.
> > - **(c) Excluded.** The injury was expected or intended by an insured. Had the insured used reasonable force to protect someone, the exclusion would not apply.
> > - **(d) Excluded.** Bodily injury to the named insured or a resident relative is excluded from Coverage E, and Coverage F excludes residents too.
> > - **(e) Covered.** No exclusion applies to a guest injured by the insured's animal.

## Exclusions for Coverage E only

Coverage E alone excludes:

1. Liability for a loss assessment against the named insured as an association member (other than the loss assessment additional coverage), and liability under a contract, except written contracts that directly relate to the ownership, maintenance or use of an insured location or where the named insured assumed others' liability before the occurrence.
2. Property damage to property owned by an insured, including the cost of repairing it to prevent injury to others.
3. Property damage to property rented to, occupied or used by, or in the care of an insured, except damage caused by fire, smoke or explosion.
4. Bodily injury to anyone eligible for benefits an insured provides or must provide under a workers compensation, non-occupational disability or occupational disease law.
5. Injury for which an insured is, or would be but for exhaustion, an insured under a nuclear energy liability policy.
6. Bodily injury to the named insured or a resident relative, ward or student insured, including a claim against an insured to repay or share damages someone else owes for injuring an insured.

> [!example]- What does Section II pay for the damage to this rented cabin and rug? {Example}
> The insured rents a lake cabin for a week (an insured location, as premises occasionally rented for non-business use). He accidentally starts a kitchen fire that does \$40,000 of damage to the cabin, and separately spills paint on the owner's \$2,000 rug. Coverage E has a \$300,000 limit.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Coverage E excludes property damage to property rented to or in the care of an insured, except damage caused by fire, smoke or explosion. Being an insured location does not change that.
> > - The \$40,000 of fire damage is covered under Coverage E.
> > - The rug is excluded from Coverage E because the cause was not fire, smoke or explosion, but the damage to property of others additional coverage pays its replacement cost up to \$1,000 per occurrence, so it pays \$1,000.

## Exclusions for Coverage F only

Coverage F alone excludes bodily injury:

1. To a residence employee that happens off the insured location and not in the course of employment.
2. To anyone eligible for workers compensation, non-occupational disability or occupational disease benefits.
3. From nuclear reaction, radiation or radioactive contamination.
4. To anyone other than a residence employee who regularly resides on any part of the insured location.

Coverage F does not have the Coverage E-only exclusions, so contract liability and damage to owned property are irrelevant to it.
