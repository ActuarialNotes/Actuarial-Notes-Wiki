An insurer faces the incurred loss scenarios in the table below. Only one scenario can occur in the coming year. The insurer uses VaR (99%) as the capital requirement.

| Scenario | Probability | Water Loss | Wind Loss | Total Loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.50 | 0 M | 0 M | 0 M |
| 2 | 0.25 | 20 M | 5 M | 25 M |
| 3 | 0.15 | 30 M | 20 M | 50 M |
| 4 | 0.09 | 30 M | 45 M | 75 M |
| 5 | 0.01 | 25 M | 75 M | 100 M |

## Part a (2.75 points)

Allocate the capital to each peril using capital allocation by percentile layer.

### Explanation
VaR 99% = 75M, so the capital held is 75M. It is made of three 25M layers (0–25M, 25–50M and 50–75M, written 25x0, 25x25 and 25x50). Each layer is allocated to the scenarios whose loss reaches it, in proportion to their probability (the denominators .5, .25 and .10 below), giving each scenario's capital:

| Scenario | 25x0 | 25x25 | 25x50 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | (.25/.5)(25) = 12.5 | 0 | 0 | 12.5 |
| 3 | (.15/.5)(25) = 7.5 | (.15/.25)(25) = 15 | 0 | 22.5 |
| 4 | (.09/.5)(25) = 4.5 | 9 | 22.5 | 36 |
| 5 | 0.5 | 1 | 2.5 | 4 |
| Total |  |  |  | 75 |

Each scenario's capital is then split between the perils in proportion to that scenario's losses:

$$\text{Water} = \frac{20}{25}(12.5) + \frac{30}{50}(22.5) + \frac{30}{75}(36) + \frac{25}{100}(4) = 38.9M$$

$$\text{Wind} = \frac{5}{25}(12.5) + \frac{20}{50}(22.5) + \frac{45}{75}(36) + \frac{75}{100}(4) = 36.1M$$

Alternatively:

Allocate each layer to scenario and peril separately (scenarios that do not reach a layer receive none of it):

| Scenario | 0–25M Layer | Water | Wind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | (.25/.5)(25) = 12.5M | (20/25)(12.5) = 10 | (5/25)(12.5) = 2.5 |
| 3 | (.15/.5)(25) = 7.5M | 4.5 | 3 |
| 4 | (.09/.5)(25) = 4.5M | 1.8 | 2.7 |
| 5 | (.01/.5)(25) = .5 | .125 | .375 |

| Scenario | 25–50M Layer | Water | Wind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | (.15/.25)(25) = 15M | 9 | 6 |
| 4 | (.09/.25)(25) = 9M | 3.6 | 5.4 |
| 5 | (.01/.25)(25) = 1 | .25 | .75 |

| Scenario | 50–75M Layer | Water | Wind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | (.09/.10)(25) = 22.5M | 9 | 13.5 |
| 5 | (.01/.10)(25) = 2.5 | .625 | 1.875 |

Total allocation: Water $= 16.425 + 12.85 + 9.625 = 38.9M$; Wind $= 8.575 + 12.15 + 15.375 = 36.1M$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the VaR and then use the capital allocation by percentile layer approach to allocate the required capital (VaR) to water and wind perils. Candidates were expected to recognize the three different layers to be allocated, apply the correct weights to allocate the losses within the layers to the 5 scenarios to assign the appropriate amount of capital to each scenario. Finally, candidates were expected to allocate the capital for each scenario to peril and then sum across scenarios to get the total capital allocated to each peril.

Common errors include:

- Incorrectly using \$100M as the VaR
- Excluding scenario 5 from contributing to any loss layer
- Misunderstanding what the layers represented
- Including an extra layer above the 99% VaR

## Part b (0.5 points)

Without performing any calculations, explain how switching to coTVaR(99%) would impact the capital allocated to each peril.

### Explanation
Allocation using coTVaR will focus on tail events, which are dominated by the wind peril. More capital will be allocated to wind and less capital will be allocated to water.

Alternatively:

TVaR means that total capital will increase. Wind makes up a greater proportion of the worst-case scenarios, so wind would get a larger proportion allocated to it.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to directionally explain how switching the capital allocation methodology from percentile layer approach to co-TVaR approach would impact the allocation to peril.

Despite the fact that this wasn't mentioned in the question, candidates that explicitly assumed that the total capital requirements would increase were not penalized for making this assumption.
Common errors include:

- Failing to recognize that coTVaR ONLY considers losses that contribute to the tail (TVaR) of the aggregate distribution.
- Calculate the coTVaR percentages incorrectly by considering only scenario 5 as opposed to scenarios 4 and 5.
