In a given year, the number of accidents an auto insurance policyholder experiences is Poisson distributed with mean 0.10. The numbers of accidents by year are independent.

Calculate the probability that a policyholder's first accident occurs during the sixth year of the policy, given that the policyholder experiences no accidents during the first two years of the policy.

- A) $0.0009$
- B) $0.0577$
- C) $0.0705$
- D) $0.0729$
- E) $0.3297$

## Explanation

For each year, the probability of no accidents is

$$
q = P[N = 0] = e^{-0.10} = 0.90484
$$

so the probability of at least one accident in a year is $1 - q = 0.09516$.

Years are independent, so conditioning on the first two being accident-free simply removes them from consideration. What remains is that years 3, 4 and 5 must be clear and year 6 must have an accident:

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= q^3(1-q) \\
  &= (0.90484)^3(0.09516) \\
  &= (0.74082)(0.09516) \\
  &= \mathbf{0.0705}
\end{align*}
$$

The count of clear years is 3, not 5 — the conditioning discards years 1 and 2 rather than requiring them. Using $q^5(1-q)$ instead gives 0.0577, which is choice (B), and is the answer to the *unconditional* version of the question.
