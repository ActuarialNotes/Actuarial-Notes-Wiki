A group of students will take Exam 1. Those who pass Exam 1 will take Exam 2. Those who also pass Exam 2 will take Exam 3. For the initial group, the probability of passing Exam 1 is 0.80, the probability of passing Exams 1 and 2 is 0.48, and the probability of passing Exams 1, 2, and 3 is 0.36. A student who fails an exam stops taking exams.

Calculate the probability that a student of this group passes Exam 2, given that the student does not pass all three exams.

- A) $0.12$
- B) $0.19$
- C) $0.25$
- D) $0.64$
- E) $0.75$

## Explanation

The sequential structure means "passes Exam 2" already implies passing Exam 1, so the given 0.48 *is* the probability of passing Exam 2.

**Denominator.** Not passing all three is the complement of the 0.36:

$$
P[\text{not all three}] = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64
$$

**Numerator.** The intersection is passing Exam 2 but not Exam 3. Since everyone who passes all three also passed Exam 2, that group is nested inside the 0.48:

$$
P[\text{pass 2, not all three}] = 0.48 - 0.36 = 0.12
$$

Dividing:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{pass 2} \mid \text{not all three}] &= \frac{0.12}{0.64} \\
                                           &= \mathbf{0.1875}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.12, is the numerator alone. The nesting is what makes this straightforward: because the exams are taken in sequence, each event contains the next, so every intersection is just a difference of the given cumulative probabilities.
