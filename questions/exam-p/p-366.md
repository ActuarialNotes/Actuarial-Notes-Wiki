A mover transports ten identical boxes with fragile contents. The contents of seven of these boxes all stay intact after the move.

The mover randomly chooses five different boxes from the ten to inspect.

Calculate the probability that the contents of exactly three of these five boxes are all intact.

- A) $0.042$
- B) $0.083$
- C) $0.139$
- D) $0.417$
- E) $0.700$

## Explanation

The boxes are sampled **without replacement** from a finite population split into 7 intact and 3 damaged, so the count of intact boxes among the five inspected is hypergeometric, not binomial.

Choosing exactly 3 intact boxes means choosing 3 of the 7 intact and the remaining 2 from the 3 damaged:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{exactly 3 intact}] &= \frac{\binom{7}{3}\binom{3}{2}}{\binom{10}{5}} \\
                           &= \frac{(35)(3)}{252} \\
                           &= \frac{105}{252} \\
                           &= \mathbf{0.41667}
\end{align*}
$$

Treating the draws as independent with $p = 0.7$ gives the binomial value $\binom{5}{3}(0.7)^3(0.3)^2 = 0.309$, which is not among the choices — sampling without replacement from a population this small changes the answer materially. Choice (E) is the population proportion 0.7 itself.
