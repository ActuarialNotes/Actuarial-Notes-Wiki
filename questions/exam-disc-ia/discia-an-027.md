A homeowners policy excludes damage caused by "surface water" without defining the term. After a mudslide set off by a rainstorm, the insurer and the insured read the word differently, and the court finds both readings reasonable. Which characteristic of insurance contracts most directly leads the court to adopt the insured's reading?

- A) The policy is aleatory, so doubt favours the party who paid premium
- B) The policy is unilateral, so only the insurer's promises are read
- C) The policy is made in utmost good faith, so the insurer must disclose
- D) The policy is a contract of adhesion, so ambiguity goes against the drafter

## Explanation

An insurance policy is a contract of adhesion: the insurer drafts it and the buyer accepts or rejects it without bargaining over the wording. Courts therefore construe ambiguous terms against the drafter (*contra proferentem*), a rule especially important in insurance because of the generalized nature of many policy terms. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — aleatory describes the unequal, chance-dependent exchange of value; it is not a rule for reading ambiguous words.
- **B** — unilateral means only the insurer makes legally enforceable promises; it does not tell a court how to read an exclusion.
- **C** — utmost good faith concerns disclosure of facts by both parties, not interpretation of the insurer's wording.
