A ceding insurer's contract with a reinsurer does not, despite its form, transfer both underwriting risk and timing risk. Under SSAP No. 62R, how does the ceding insurer account for it?

- A) Record ceded premium and reduce loss reserves for expected recoveries
- B) Record the net consideration as a deposit; do not reduce loss reserves
- C) Record ceded premium, but take credit for recoveries only when paid
- D) Expense the net consideration as an underwriting expense when paid

## Explanation

A contract that fails risk transfer is accounted for as a deposit: the net consideration paid, premium less commissions, is recorded as a deposit asset, adjusted through interest income using the effective yield, and no deduction is made from the loss and LAE reserves. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — this is reinsurance accounting, which requires risk transfer; without it, no reinsurance credit is recorded.
- **C** — there is no ceded premium at all under deposit accounting.
- **D** — the payment is a deposit to be recovered, not an expense.
