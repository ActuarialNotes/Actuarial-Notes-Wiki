A furniture store's employee, driving the store's truck on a delivery route, negligently rear-ends a car. The same day, an independent courier the store hired to deliver a lamp, who drives her own van and sets her own methods, negligently hits a cyclist. Is the store liable for either accident under respondeat superior?

- A) Liable for the employee's accident, not liable for the courier's
- B) Liable for both, because both drivers were delivering the store's goods
- C) Liable for neither, because the store itself was not negligent
- D) Liable for the courier's accident only, because she was hired for that delivery

## Explanation

Respondeat superior holds an employer or principal liable for torts its employees or agents commit within the scope of the employment or agency, even though the employer itself was not at fault. It applies to employees but **not to independent contractors**. The employee was on his delivery route, so the store is liable for that accident; the courier is an independent contractor.

- **B** extends respondeat superior to an independent contractor, which it does not reach.
- **C** misses the point of vicarious liability: the party held liable need not be at fault itself.
- **D** reverses the rule.
