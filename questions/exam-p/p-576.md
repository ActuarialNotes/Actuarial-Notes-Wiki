A light bulb's lifetime in months is uniformly distributed on $[0, 40]$.

Let $a$ be a positive real number less than 30. The probability that the light bulb fails within 30 months, given that it is working after $a$ months, equals 0.60.

Calculate the value of $a$.

- A) $6$
- B) $12$
- C) $15$
- D) $16$
- E) $18$

## Explanation

Conditioning on survival past $a$ restricts the distribution to $[a, 40]$, which has length $40 - a$. Within that range, failing before month 30 covers the sub-interval $(a, 30)$ of length $30 - a$.

For a uniform distribution these are simple length ratios:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.60 &= P[X < 30 \mid X > a] = \frac{30 - a}{40 - a}
\end{align*}
$$

Solving:

$$
\begin{align*}
30 - a &= 0.6(40 - a) \\
30 - a &= 24 - 0.6a \\
6 &= 0.4a \\
a &= \mathbf{15}
\end{align*}
$$

Checking: given survival past 15 months, the remaining lifetime is uniform on $[15, 40]$, and failing by 30 covers 15 of those 25 months — exactly 0.60.

Note the unconditional $P[X < 30] = 30/40 = 0.75$ exceeds 0.60, which is why a positive $a$ exists: conditioning on survival past $a$ *lowers* the failure probability, because a uniform lifetime is not memoryless.
