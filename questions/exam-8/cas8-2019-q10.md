An actuary has created new models to calculate the experience modification factor for two lines of business.

Given the following:

- Line of business 1 (currently does not use experience rating)

![Line of Business 1 by Quintiles: the standard loss ratio of the proposal (left axis) and the experience modification factor of the proposal (right axis), both from 0 to 1.800, by quintile 1 to 5. The standard loss ratio rises gently from about 0.79 in quintile 1 to about 0.81, 1.00 and 1.03, then about 1.28 in quintile 5; the experience modification factor rises more steeply, from about 0.42 in quintile 1 to about 0.62, 1.00 and 1.20, then about 1.65 in quintile 5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas8-2019-q10-line-of-business-1.png)

- Line of business 2 (currently uses experience rating)

![Line of Business 2 by Quintiles: standard loss ratio (0.500 to 0.750) by quintile 1 to 5. The current plan's standard loss ratio stays in a narrow band, about 0.655, 0.675, 0.645, 0.700 and 0.660; the proposal's rises steadily, from about 0.590 in quintile 1 to about 0.635, 0.640 and 0.680, then about 0.735 in quintile 5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas8-2019-q10-line-of-business-2.png)

| Line of Business 2 | Efficiency Test |
|---|---|
| Current Plan | 0.0346 |
| Proposed Plan | 0.1184 |

For both lines of business, assume that the cost of implementation is negligible.

## Part a (0.5 points)

For line of business 1, evaluate whether the new experience modification should be implemented.

### Explanation
Yes — implement it. The standard loss ratio is the manual loss ratio divided by the mod, so the manual loss ratio can be read off the graph as $\text{MLR} = \text{SLR} \times \text{Mod}$:

| Quintile | SLR | Mod | MLR = SLR × Mod |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.79 | 0.42 | 0.33 |
| 2 | 0.81 | 0.62 | 0.50 |
| 3 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 4 | 1.03 | 1.20 | 1.24 |
| 5 | 1.28 | 1.65 | 2.11 |

- The manual loss ratios have a strong positive trend, so the mod is good at identifying risk differences.
- The standard loss ratios are much flatter than the mod, so the mod partially corrects for those differences. There is still a small positive trend — the plan does not give enough credibility — but it is better than the current plan with no experience rating, under which worse-than-average risks would be even more underpriced and the company could be adversely selected against.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to be able to identify/calculate the Manual LR from the graph provided and compare it to the Standard LR. Two of the points must have been made in order to receive full credit.
A common mistake was stating that the plan should not be implemented since the Standard LR was not flat.

## Part b (0.5 points)

For line of business 1, describe a way to improve the results of the experience modification.

### Explanation
Not enough credibility is given to actual experience because of increasing standard loss ratios, so give more credibility to actual experience

Alternatively:

Give more weight to actual experience, this will reduce premium for low mods and increase for high mods.  The final product should be standard LR that do not have a trend

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to be able to identity that not enough credibility was assigned to the loss data since there as an increasing trend in the Standard LR. Full credit was awarded for one explanation of how to improve the model and for an explanation on why the model needs to be improved, or an additional reason or if both reasons on how to improve the model were provided.

If candidate wrote about the credibility in Part A and did not mention everything in Part B, the responses in Part A was considered for Part B since this was a common mistake.
Simply stating that the credibility needed to be adjusted was not an acceptable response.

## Part c (0.5 points)

For line of business 2, evaluate whether the proposed plan should be used.

### Explanation
No, do not use.  Current standard LR is flatter than proposed SLR.  Also efficiency test shows same conclusion.  Current<proposed.  Current is better at accounting for experience.

Alternatively:

Do not use proposed plan.  Standard loss ratios should be close to constant (supports current)
and prefer a lower efficiency statistic (supports current)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to be able to compare quintile charts as well as efficiency test statistics.
