A CBC Explains article by Mouhamad Rachini, posted December 12, 2023, on the Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP), a $13-billion insurance program that will start covering most basic dentistry costs for about nine million eligible Canadians, according to the federal government. It answers questions about the plan as announced. It is the SOA's study note GH201-719-25 (formerly GHVR-719-24).

## Who pays for what under the CDCP?
- The CDCP helps cover various oral health-care services, with the focus on those deemed medically necessary by an oral health-care professional.
- Covered services include preventive, diagnostic, restorative, endodontic, prosthodontic, periodontal and oral surgery services; teeth whitening, implants and mouth guards are not covered.
- Applicants with household incomes below $70,000 do not pay participating providers; for incomes of $70,000 to $79,999 the government covers 60 percent of service fees, and for $80,000 to $89,999 the co-pay is 60 percent.

## Who is eligible?
- Applicants must have an annual adjusted family net income under $90,000 and no access to other dental insurance.
- Applicants must be a Canadian resident for tax purposes and have filed an income tax return for the previous year.
- Applicants are reassessed every year to confirm they still meet the eligibility requirements.

## Is the cut-off income before or after taxes?
- The cut-off is adjusted family net income, not family net income: the family's net income less income from universal child care benefits and registered disability savings plans, plus any repaid amounts of those.
- The adjusted amount determines both the $90,000 threshold and the co-pay.

## Can I use CDCP with my employer's plan?
- Canadians with access to dental insurance, through their or a family member's employer, a pension from a previous employer, or a plan bought individually or through an insurance or benefits company, are not eligible to apply.
- Applicants with dental benefits through a provincial, territorial or federal government program can still apply if they meet all the eligibility criteria.

## Can I use my current dentist, denturist or dental hygienist?
- Oral health providers can enrol voluntarily as participating CDCP providers with Sun Life, which administers the program, and submit claims to Sun Life directly; users pay any co-pay out of pocket to the provider.

## If I'm eligible, when can I apply?
- Applications first open in December 2023, by phone, to eligible seniors aged 87 and older, with other age groups phased in.
- Seniors aged 77 to 86 can apply from January 2024, those 72 to 76 in February and those 70 to 71 in March; seniors aged 65 to 69 from May 2024, when applications switch from telephone to online.
- People with valid disability tax credit certificates and children under 18 can apply from June 2024; eligible low- and middle-income Canadians aged 18 to 65 from 2025.
