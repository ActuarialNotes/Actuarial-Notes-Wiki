An actuary in the United States receives a claims file from a third-party administrator to build a severity model. Determine which of the following statements about the actuary's obligations under the Actuarial Standards of Practice are true.

- I. ASOP No. 23 requires the actuary to audit the administrator's records for accuracy and completeness before using the file.
- II. The accuracy of the file is the administrator's responsibility, but the actuary still reviews the data and discloses the reliance on the administrator.
- III. Where the actuary departs from a standard, the departure is allowed only if the actuary discloses it and the reason.

- A) I only
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

- **I is false.** ASOP No. 23 asks for a review, an examination of the obvious characteristics of the data for reasonableness and consistency, which is less detailed than an audit. The review is documented and the data's limitations disclosed, but no audit is required.
- **II is true.** Under ASOP No. 23 the actuary may rely on data supplied by others, whose accuracy is their responsibility, but still reviews the data and discloses the reliance.
- **III is true.** The ASOPs use "should" for what is normally required and allow a deviation only where the actuary discloses it and the reason, as ASOP No. 41 requires.

II and III are true, so the answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A**, **B** and **C** each accept I, which turns the review ASOP No. 23 asks for into an audit.
- **E** would need all three statements true, or none.
