In a large population of patients, 20% have cancer. Of those who have cancer, 8% have stage IV cancer.

Patients are tested one at a time, at random, until five patients with stage IV cancer are found.

Let $N$ represent the number of patients tested. Let $C$ represent the number of patients tested who have cancer.

Determine the probability function, $p_{N,C}(n,c)$, for integers $n$ and $c$ such that $5 \leq c \leq n$.

- A) $p_{N,C}(n,c) = \dfrac{(n-1)!}{(n-c)!(c-5)!4!}(0.8)^{n-c}(0.184)^{c-5}(0.016)^5$
- B) $p_{N,C}(n,c) = \dfrac{(n-1)!}{(n-c)!(c-5)!4!}(0.8)^{n-c}(0.12)^{c-5}(0.08)^5$
- C) $p_{N,C}(n,c) = \dfrac{n!}{(n-c)!(c-5)!5!}(0.8)^{n-c}(0.184)^{c-5}(0.016)^5$
- D) $p_{N,C}(n,c) = \dfrac{(n-5)!}{(n-c)!(c-5)!}(0.8)^{n-c}(0.184)^{c-5}(0.016)^5$
- E) $p_{N,C}(n,c) = \dfrac{(n-1)!}{(n-c-1)!(c-4)!4!}(0.8)^{n-c-1}(0.12)^{c-4}(0.08)^5$

## Explanation

Each patient falls into exactly one of three categories, with probabilities

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{no cancer:} \quad & 1 - 0.2 = 0.8 \\
\text{cancer, not stage IV:} \quad & 0.2(1 - 0.08) = 0.184 \\
\text{stage IV:} \quad & 0.2(0.08) = 0.016
\end{align*}
$$

Testing stops at the fifth stage IV patient, so the $n$th patient is **fixed** to be stage IV. That leaves the first $n-1$ patients to supply

- $n - c$ with no cancer,
- $c - 5$ with cancer but not stage IV,
- $4$ with stage IV.

Those counts sum to $n-1$, as they must.

Multiplying the independent per-patient probabilities gives $(0.8)^{n-c}(0.184)^{c-5}(0.016)^5$, where the fifth factor of $0.016$ is the final patient. The number of orderings of the first $n-1$ patients is the multinomial coefficient

$$
\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-c)!\,(c-5)!\,4!}
$$

so

$$
p_{N,C}(n,c) = \mathbf{\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-c)!(c-5)!4!}(0.8)^{n-c}(0.184)^{c-5}(0.016)^5}
$$

Choice (C) uses $n!$ and $5!$, which permutes the last patient as though its position were free — but the stopping rule pins it. Choice (B) uses 0.12 and 0.08 in place of 0.184 and 0.016: the 8% is *conditional* on having cancer, so it must be multiplied by 0.2 before it can be used as an unconditional category probability.
