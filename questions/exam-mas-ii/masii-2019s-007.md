You are given:

- Claims follow a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda$.
- The prior distribution of $\lambda$ follows gamma distribution with $\alpha = 4$ and $\theta = 0.06$.
- Claims are independent and identically distributed between risks.

In Year 1, the following claim experience was observed for a portfolio:

| Cohort | Risks | Claim Frequency per Risk |
|--------|-------|--------------------------|
| X | 500 | 0.12 |
| Y | 300 | 0.20 |
| Z | 200 | 0.15 |
| Total | 1,000 | 0.15 |

In Year 2, the portfolio has the same number of risks.

Calculate the expected number of claims for the portfolio in Year 2 using the Bayesian approach.

- A) Less than 153
- B) At least 153, but less than 158
- C) At least 158, but less than 163
- D) At least 163, but less than 168
- E) At least 168

## Explanation

Claims are i.i.d. across risks, so every risk in the portfolio shares the same $\lambda$ and the cohort split carries no extra information — update the prior with the portfolio total.

Year 1 exposure is $n = 1{,}000$ risks and the observed claim count is

$$
1{,}000 \times 0.15 = 150 \text{ claims}.
$$

The gamma is conjugate to the Poisson. With $\theta$ as the scale parameter, the posterior is gamma with

$$
\begin{align*}
\alpha^* &= \alpha + \sum x_i = 4 + 150 = 154 \\
\frac{1}{\theta^*} &= \frac{1}{\theta} + n = \frac{1}{0.06} + 1{,}000 = 1016.67
\end{align*}
$$

The posterior (and hence predictive) mean claim frequency per risk is

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\lambda \mid \text{data}] &= \alpha^*\theta^* \\
                            &= \frac{154}{1016.67} \\
                            &= 0.15147
\end{align*}
$$

With the same 1,000 risks in Year 2,

$$
1{,}000 \times 0.15147 = 151.5 \text{ claims}.
$$

This is less than 153, so the answer is **A**.
