## What is Solvency II?
- The Solvency II Directive is a harmonised framework aimed at ensuring there is a single market, using a single set of rules for insurance services, and its requirements apply to (re)insurance companies and EU insurance groups operating in the EU.
- It encompasses requirements surrounding risk sensitive solvency, governance and risk management, and public disclosures.

## How and why did Solvency II come about?
- It is an EU directive adopted in 2009 that replaced the 14 insurance and reinsurance directives previously known as Solvency I, and its overall objective is to protect (re)insurers' policyholders and provide for a stable financial system.

## What does Solvency II equivalence mean?
- Equivalence for third countries means a non-EU jurisdiction has an insurance regulatory regime that achieves the same outcomes as those determined under the Solvency II framework; EIOPA is responsible for the technical assessment and the European Commission for the equivalence decision.
- It enables (re)insurers and insurance groups from third countries to conduct business in the EU as though they were EU-domiciled.
