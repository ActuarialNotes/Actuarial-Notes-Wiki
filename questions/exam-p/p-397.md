The combined results of employee satisfaction surveys taken at each of Store A and Store B are given in the following table, in which satisfaction is ranked from 0 to 5.

| Satisfaction | Combined Frequencies over Stores A and B |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 9 |
| 1 | 6 |
| 2 | 12 |
| 3 | 6 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 9 |

Among only the employees of Store A, the frequency of each response is at least 4. Store A has three modes, each with a frequency of 8.

Calculate the largest possible number of modes for Store B.

- A) $0$
- B) $1$
- C) $2$
- D) $3$
- E) $5$

## Explanation

Store B's frequency at each satisfaction level is the combined frequency minus Store A's, so pin down Store A first.

**Where Store A's three modes must sit.** Store A's modal frequency is 8, so no level of A exceeds 8. A level whose combined frequency is 6 cannot give A a frequency of 8, so the modes can only be at levels 0, 2 and 5 — the three levels with combined frequencies of 9, 12 and 9. That is exactly three levels, so all three are modes, with A having 8 at each.

Store B's frequencies there are the leftovers:

$$
9 - 8 = 1, \qquad 12 - 8 = 4, \qquad 9 - 8 = 1
$$

**The remaining levels.** At levels 1, 3 and 4 the combined frequency is 6, and Store A has at least 4 at every level, so Store A holds 4, 5 or 6 there. Store B therefore has **at most 2** at each of those levels.

**Count B's modes.** Store B's frequencies are 1 at level 0, at most 2 at level 1, 4 at level 2, at most 2 at levels 3 and 4, and 1 at level 5. The single largest value is the 4 at level 2, and nothing else can reach it. So Store B has exactly

$$
\mathbf{1} \text{ mode}
$$

Choice (D), 3, mirrors Store A's three modes; the constraint that makes B's count differ is the floor of 4 on every one of A's frequencies, which caps B at 2 wherever the combined total is only 6.
