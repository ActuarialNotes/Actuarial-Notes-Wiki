A state legislator argues that insurance buyers can protect themselves by shopping around, so insurance needs no more regulation than any other product. State insurance regulators answer that regulation exists to protect consumers. What do they identify as the most important protection it provides?

- A) Holding premiums as low as competition allows
- B) Making sure insurers stay solvent so they can pay claims
- C) Guaranteeing every applicant can buy coverage
- D) Shielding licensed insurers from out-of-state competition

## Explanation

In the NAIC's account, state regulation began for one overriding reason, to protect consumers, and its most important consumer protection is to make sure insurers **remain solvent** so they can meet their obligations to pay claims. Beyond that, states supervise sales and marketing practices and policy terms so consumers are treated fairly.

- **A** is not the goal: rates must also not be inadequate, because a rate too low to cover costs threatens solvency.
- **C** overstates regulation. Availability is a concern in some lines, but no regulator guarantees coverage to every applicant.
- **D** is the opposite of the consumer purpose regulators describe.
