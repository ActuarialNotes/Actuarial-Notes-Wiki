Which description best fits the traditional risk management function, before the move to enterprise risk management?

- A) A chief risk officer on the executive team oversees all of the firm's risks as one portfolio
- B) Each department manages its own financial and strategic risks, and no one buys insurance
- C) The board manages the firm's risks directly by setting its risk appetite and capital targets
- D) A risk manager, often reporting to the treasurer, buys insurance and runs loss control

## Explanation

Risk management grew out of corporate insurance buying. The traditional risk manager was responsible for specific **pure risks**, mainly by buying insurance and running loss control to keep employees and property safe, and typically reported to the treasurer. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — A chief risk officer overseeing hazard, financial, operational and strategic risks together is the enterprise risk management model that replaced the traditional one.
- **B** — Insurance buying was the core of the traditional function, not something nobody did.
- **C** — Setting a risk appetite for the whole enterprise is part of modern ERM governance, not the traditional role.
