Given the following information for an insurance company as of December 31, 2016:

**Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 68,600 | 87,800 | 100,000 |
| 2015 | 72,800 | 91,500 | |
| 2016 | 55,900 | | |

**Cumulative Reported Claim Counts as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 80 | 95 | 100 |
| 2015 | 85 | 99 | |
| 2016 | 87 | | |

- There is no development after 36 months for reported claims or reported claim counts.
- A new law limiting claimant benefits came into effect on January 1, 2016 and is applicable to accidents occurring on or after January 1, 2016. The expected impact is a 25% reduction in claim severity.
- There is no loss trend.

Estimate ultimate claims for accident year 2016 as of December 31, 2016 using a frequency-severity technique.

### Answer
Ultimate claims for accident year 2016 $\approx$ 80,755 (\$000), i.e. about \$80.8 million.

### Explanation
The method develops **counts** and **severity** separately and multiplies the two ultimates. The law change is what makes the question interesting: it applies by *accident* date, so accident year 2016 is already at the reduced benefit level while 2014 and 2015 are not.

**Step 1 — develop reported claim counts to ultimate.** The law limits benefits, not the number of accidents, so the count triangle needs no adjustment.

| Accident Year | 12–24 | 24–36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | $95/80 = 1.1875$ | $100/95 = 1.0526$ |
| 2015 | $99/85 = 1.1647$ | |
| **Selected (straight average)** | **1.1761** | **1.0526** |

With no development after 36 months the tail is 1.000, so

$$\text{CDF}_{12} = 1.1761 \times 1.0526 = 1.2380$$

$$N_{2016}^{\text{ult}} = 87 \times 1.2380 = 107.7 \text{ claims}$$

**Step 2 — build a severity triangle on a common benefit level.** Raw severity is reported claims divided by reported counts; accident years 2014 and 2015 are then restated to post-reform terms by **multiplying by 0.75** (not dividing by 1.25 — the stated impact is a 25% reduction).

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | $68{,}600/80 \times 0.75 = 643.13$ | $87{,}800/95 \times 0.75 = 693.16$ | $100{,}000/100 \times 0.75 = 750.00$ |
| 2015 | $72{,}800/85 \times 0.75 = 642.35$ | $91{,}500/99 \times 0.75 = 693.18$ | |
| 2016 | $55{,}900/87 = 642.53$ | | |

Accident year 2016 is **not** adjusted — its claims already reflect the new law. With no loss trend, the restated severities line up neatly across accident years at each maturity, which is the check that the adjustment was applied to the right years.

**Step 3 — develop severity to ultimate.**

| Accident Year | 12–24 | 24–36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | $693.16/643.13 = 1.078$ | $750.00/693.16 = 1.082$ |
| 2015 | $693.18/642.35 = 1.079$ | |
| **Selected** | **1.078** | **1.082** |

$$\text{CDF}_{12} = 1.078 \times 1.082 = 1.1669$$

$$S_{2016}^{\text{ult}} = 642.53 \times 1.1669 = 749.8 \ (\$000)$$

**Step 4 — combine.**

$$\text{Ultimate}_{2016} = 107.7 \times 749.8 = \mathbf{80{,}755} \ (\$000)$$

A useful observation: because a development factor is a *ratio* of two severities from the same accident year, the 0.75 adjustment cancels out of it. The severity factors are 1.078 and 1.082 whether or not the older years are restated. What the adjustment does matter for is the level: accident year 2016's own base severity of 642.53 is already post-reform, so it must be developed with factors from a consistently restated triangle rather than reduced a second time.

**Alternatively:** the same answer follows from an incremental frequency-severity calculation. Incremental counts for 2016 are $87 \times 0.176 = 15.3$ in the 12–24 period and $(87 + 15.3) \times 0.053 = 5.4$ in the 24–36 period; the restated incremental severities average $(960 + 1{,}002)/2 = 981$ and $1{,}830$ respectively, giving

$$\text{Ultimate}_{2016} = 55{,}900 + 15.3 \times 981 + 5.4 \times 1{,}830 = 80{,}790 \ (\$000)$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to run a frequency-severity method — developing claim counts and severities to ultimate separately and multiplying — or, equivalently, an incremental method using incremental counts and incremental severities. The crux was handling the benefit reform: it applies by accident date, so the pre-reform accident years must be restated by multiplying by 0.75 while accident year 2016 is left alone.

Common errors centred on that adjustment: treating it as applying by report date, applying it to accident year 2016 (the year that already reflects it), and dividing by 1.25 instead of multiplying by 0.75. Others averaged the ultimate counts across accident years instead of developing 2016's own counts; developed aggregate reported claims with a loss development method and then divided by developed counts to back into a severity; or attempted the incremental method with cumulative rather than incremental severities.
