The loss, $X$, subject to reimbursement under an insurance policy, has a distribution with density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\left(\dfrac{1}{\beta}\right)e^{-\frac{(x-d)}{\beta}}, & \text{for } x \ge d \\[6pt]
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $d$ is the deductible, and $\beta$ is a positive constant.

Determine the 10th percentile of $X$.

- A) $\beta \ln\left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right)$
- B) $\beta \ln\left(\dfrac{10}{9}\right)$
- C) $\beta \ln\left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right) + d$
- D) $\beta \ln\left(\dfrac{10}{9}\right) + d$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right)$

## Explanation

This is an exponential with mean $\beta$ shifted to start at $d$, so its cdf carries the same shift:

$$
F(x) = 1 - e^{-\frac{(x-d)}{\beta}}, \qquad x \ge d
$$

Let $p$ be the 10th percentile, so $F(p) = 0.10$:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 - e^{-\frac{(p-d)}{\beta}} &= 0.10 \\
e^{-\frac{(p-d)}{\beta}} &= 0.90 \\
\frac{-(p - d)}{\beta} &= \ln 0.90 \\
\frac{p - d}{\beta} &= \ln\frac{10}{9}
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
p = \mathbf{\beta \ln\frac{10}{9} + d}
$$

Choice (B) drops the $+d$. The support begins at $d$, so every percentile of $X$ must be at least $d$ — an answer without that term would put the 10th percentile below the smallest possible loss. Choice (C) keeps the shift but uses $\ln(11/10)$, which comes from writing $-\ln(1.10)$ in place of $-\ln(0.90)$; the two are close in value but only $0.90$ is the survival probability at the 10th percentile.
