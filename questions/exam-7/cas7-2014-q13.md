Describe four aspects of reinsurance loss reserving that make it somewhat more difficult than primary loss reserving.

### Explanation
Any four of the following, each with a brief description (the textbook lists seven):

- **Report lags are longer** — a claim is not reported to the reinsurer until it reaches a size threshold, and it passes through the cedant's processing, possibly an intermediary, and the reinsurer's own system, all of which add delay.
- **Reporting patterns are not homogeneous** — reinsurance covers many lines at different attachment points, under a wide variety of contract types (treaty, excess, quota share) and terms (loss corridors, treatment of ALAE), so homogeneous exposure groups are hard to form.
- **Industry statistics are not very helpful** — the Schedule P "excess casualty" line mixes many lines of business and attachment points and is inconsistent across companies.
- **Persistent upward development** — primary companies tend to under-reserve LAE, and severity trends affect excess layers more.
- **Less knowledge of the exposures** — the reinsurer often receives only summary loss and premium reports, without the underlying detail the cedant has.
- **IT and coding issues** — the variety of reinsurance data is hard to code, and what needs coding changes faster than the systems are updated.
- **High reserve-to-surplus ratio** — reinsurers' reserves are large relative to surplus, and it may be hard to convince management of the reserves required.

### Examiner Report
The question was worded nearly identically to Learning Objective A11, "Compare and contrast reinsurance and primary reserving procedures." In addition, the text included for this section specifically listed 7 items and explanations.
The question requested candidates to briefly describe four ways that reinsurance reserving is more challenging than primary reserving.  Approximately 65% of candidates received full credit, and for the most part provided model solutions very similar to the items listed in the textbook.
Candidates were expected to provide four items, and as detailed above the majority of candidates received full credit.  Candidates were expected not only to simply list the item, but also provide a brief description of how or why it is relevant.
In general, when points were deducted it was due to either: (a) showing less than four items; (b)
candidate only providing a brief overview of the item and not providing an additional description;
or (c) candidate providing two items and descriptions that were essentially the same.
