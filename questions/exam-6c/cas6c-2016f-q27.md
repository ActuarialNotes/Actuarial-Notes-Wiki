A federally licensed Canadian insurer is replacing its current Appointed Actuary (AA). The following information is available:

- Associate of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries (ACIA)
- Fellow of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries (FCIA)

| Candidate | Designation | Information available about the Candidate |
|---|---|---|
| A | ACIA | has been working as a reserving actuary for twenty years |
| B | FCIA | has been working as a reserving actuary in the company's subsidiary in the U.S. for the past ten years |
| C | FCIA | has been working in a Canadian consulting firm and acts as AA for five Canadian insurers for the past fifteen years |
| D | FCIA | has been working as CEO of the company for three years |
| E | FCIA | has been working as a pricing actuary of the company for twelve years |

## Part a (1.25 points)

For each of the above candidates, explain whether they are eligible to serve as the AA.

### Explanation
- A: No, because not an FCIA
- B: No, because no relevant experience in Canada – need 3 of 6 last years in Canada
- C: Yes
- D: Assuming experience in valuation he could be but only temporarily with approval of OSFI.
- E: No, because need experience in valuation – need 1 of 6 last years in Canada

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to explain the various requirements to serve as an AA, identifying why each candidate did not qualify. For candidate C, no explanation was required.

Common mistakes included:

- For candidate D, did not state that the CEO can be AA if they receive permission from OSFI instead simply stating no because they are CEO.
- Misstating the year requirements (for example, 2 of past 6 years of Canadian work instead of 3 of past 6 years).

## Part b (0.75 points)

Upon appointment of the new AA, identify three disclosures that must be made in the company's upcoming AA report.

### Explanation
Any three of the following:

- Date of appointment of new AA
- Date of resignation of previous AA
- Date OSFI was notified of the appointment
- List of AA's qualifications
- Confirmation of communication with the previous AA regarding the reason for their resignation or revocation

### Examiner Report
Candidates expected to identify disclosures specific to the appointment of a new AA.

Common mistakes included:

- Identifying one or more AA disclosures that are not specific to a new AA appointment (e.g.
compensation of the AA)
- Stating that OSFI needs to know the reason for the resignation/revocation of the previous AA, when in fact they only require confirmation that the new AA had communication with the previous one with it.
- Date of OSFI "approval" of position
- Name of the new AA

## Part c (1 point)

Briefly describe four roles or duties of the AA.

### Explanation
Any four of the following:

- Produce AA report
- Value policy liabilities per actuarial standards of practice
- DCAT: produce DCAT to assess financial condition of the company and meet with senior management / board to report.
- Report to CEO/CFO any event that could have material adverse impact on the company's financial condition and seek rectification in given timeline. If no action taken, needs to send report to Superintendant and notify senior management they have done so.
- The AA is required in each financial year to meet with and report to the directors or chief agent on the company's financial position.
- Report to directors on fairness of policies regarding participating policyholder policies
- Report whether a dividend/bonus is in accordance with a policy regaring participating policyholder dividends.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate understanding of the main roles of the AA.

Common mistakes included:

- Overly brief descriptions
- Speaking to the participating dividend policy, or dividends/investment allocation for participating policies without actually identifying that it is specific to participating policies.
The AA is not required to report on the fairness of dividends paid by the company to shareholders
- Stating that the AA must report "any" adverse findings to the board

## Part d (0.5 points)

Briefly describe whether an insurance company's provision for policy liabilities shown in the Annual Return may be different from the AA's estimated policy liabilities.

### Explanation
Yes they could be different but company's provision cannot be less than AA's estimate. AA will also need to provide explanation on material differences.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate understanding that the company can carry any reported reserve in the Annual Report as long as it is larger than the AA's estimate.
A common mistake was to provide one of the many possible reasons why a company may allow it to differ without providing the requirement that it must be greater than or equal to the AA's estimate.

## Part e (0.75 points)

Identify three components in the calculation of premium liabilities.

### Explanation
Any three of the following:

- Expected losses & LAE in relation to unearned premium / in-force policies
- Expenses required to service the in-force policies (maintenance costs)
- Contingent commissions
- Premium Deficiency
- DPAE
- Unearned Premium
- Expected reinsurance costs
- Premium adjustments for swing rated policies / retro-rated policies

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate understanding of the components underlying the calculation of premium liabilities. Possible components from the OSFI or Premium Liabilities readings were accepted, with many candidates electing to provide items from the latter.

Common mistakes included:

- Stating "expected losses" without specifying that they are in relation to the unearned premium or in-force policies.
- Providing very detailed elements of the calculation within each component

## Part f (0.75 points)

Briefly describe OSFI's three objectives for requiring peer review of the work of the AA.

### Explanation
- Provide guidance to AA and provide education
- Help OSFI in its assessment of the safety and soundness of the insurer
- Provide confidence in the work of the AA to the public, insurers and regulator

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate that they understand OSFI's primary objectives for peer review.
A common mistake was stating that one objective is verifying that the AA followed accepted actuarial practice; this did not receive full credit as it is considered to be only one component of the insurer safety & soundness objective.
