In a series of decisions in 1978, commonly referred to as the Trilogy, the Supreme Court of Canada established a cap on non-pecuniary damages.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Briefly describe three reasons to justify the introduction of the cap on non-pecuniary damages.

### Explanation
(3 of the following)

- Awards for non-pecuniary damages are limitless
- No amount of money can provide true restitution
- Very big awards increase the social burden
- Limitless losses leads to extravagant payment of claims
- Economic damages will be fully compensated
- No cap could lead to extremely high premiums so only rich could afford it
- Cap helps to keep the cost low which lead to availability in the market
- Ensure predictability and stability of awards

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide reasons to support the introduction of the capping on non-pecuniary damages.

There were no common errors on this part.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Briefly describe three situations to which the cap does not apply.

### Explanation
- Sexual assault
- Defamation
- Negligence causing financial loss

Alternatively:

- Sexual abuse
- Defamation
- Young vs Bella where the prof thought the paper was a confession to child abuse and defamed the plaintiff

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to briefly describe situations to which the cap does not apply.
A common error was to provide only two reasons instead of three.
