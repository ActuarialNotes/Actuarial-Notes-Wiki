## Part a (0.5 points)

Explain whether the GLM output supports including territory as a rating variable.

### Explanation
**Yes — the output supports including territory.** Three features of the diagnostic point the same way:

- **The chi-square percentage is 0.1%**, far below the conventional 5% threshold. Territory is highly statistically significant: the chance of seeing this much variation in relativity across territories if territory had no real effect is negligible.
- **The standard error bands are narrow for most territories**, so the individual relativities are estimated precisely rather than being artefacts of thin data.
- **There is substantial lift across the levels**, with a clear upward progression from about 0.35 in territory 1 to about 1.42 in territory 9. The relativities are not only different from one another but ordered and widely spread, so rating by territory genuinely differentiates expected cost.

One caveat worth noting: **territory 4** carries only about 10,000 policies and correspondingly wide error bands, and its indicated relativity is out of line with the surrounding trend. That level would benefit from being grouped with a neighbouring territory or from spatial smoothing — but it does not undermine the case for the variable as a whole.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Briefly describe three benefits of using multivariate methods over univariate methods for classification ratemaking.

### Explanation
Any three distinct benefits:

- **They account for exposure correlation.** Multivariate methods consider all rating variables simultaneously, so the effect attributed to one variable is measured holding the others constant. A univariate method assigns to each variable any differences actually caused by correlated variables, double-counting the same signal.
- **They can capture interactions between rating variables.** Where the effect of one variable depends on the level of another, a multivariate model can represent that directly; a univariate analysis has no way to express it.
- **They produce model diagnostics.** Standard errors, chi-square statistics and residual measures come out of the fitting process, giving an objective basis for judging whether a variable belongs in the model and how reliably each level is estimated — as the chi-square percentage and error bands did in part a.
- **They separate signal from noise.** The fitting process is designed to estimate the systematic effect rather than to reproduce random fluctuation in the data, so the resulting relativities are more stable.

Common errors included:

- Not providing 3 distinct reasons.  For example, if the candidate referenced correcting for exposure correlation and distributional bias as two separate reasons, credit was only given for one of those responses.
- Only providing 2 responses

## Part c (0.25 points)

Briefly describe how spatial smoothing can be used to improve territory relativity estimates.

### Explanation
Territory definitions are often granular enough that an individual territory holds too little data for a credible estimate — territory 4 above, with roughly 10,000 policies and very wide error bands, is exactly that case.

**Spatial smoothing credibility-weights a territory's own experience with the experience of nearby territories**, on the reasoning that geographically close areas share risk characteristics. Two common forms are **distance-based** smoothing, where the weight given to another territory declines as its distance from the subject territory increases, and **adjacency-based** smoothing, which draws on territories sharing a border.

The effect is to borrow information from neighbours, increasing the effective volume of data behind each estimate. That narrows the confidence intervals and produces relativities that vary smoothly across the map rather than jumping erratically between sparsely populated territories.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to discuss how spatial smoothing uses information from nearby territories to improve the territory relativity estimates.

Common errors included:

- No response.  Candidates left part c blank more frequently than the other parts.
- Discussing boundary redefinition or clustering rather than spatial smoothing
