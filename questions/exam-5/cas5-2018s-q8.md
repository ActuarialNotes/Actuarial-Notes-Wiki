**Cumulative Reported Loss + ALAE (\$000s) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,000 | 1,167.5 | 1,250 |
| 2016 | 1,100 | 1,285 |  |
| 2017 | 1,050 |  |  |

| Calendar Year | Earned Premium (\$000s) |
|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,900 |
| 2016 | 2,085 |
| 2017 | 2,100 |

|  |  |
|---|---|
| 2% | Annual loss and ALAE trend |
| 3% | Annual premium trend |
| 10% | Fixed Expense Ratio |
| 30% | Variable Expense Ratio |
| 5% | Profit and Contingencies Provision |
| 7% | ULAE Provision (as % of Loss and ALAE) |
| +4% | Rate change effective July 1, 2016 (only rate change in the past three years) |

|  |  |
|---|---|
| 1.067 | 36 to ultimate reported claim development factor |

- All policies are annual.
- Exposures are written evenly throughout each calendar year.
- Rates will be in effect for one year.
- The historical experience is fully credible.

## Part a (4.25 points)

Calculate the ultimate losses and ALAE for each accident year using the Cape Cod technique incorporating rate change and trend.

### Explanation
**Development.** Volume-weighted age-to-age factors, with the given 36-to-ultimate factor:

$$\text{12–24: } \frac{1{,}167.5 + 1{,}285}{1{,}000 + 1{,}100} = 1.1679 \qquad \text{24–36: } \frac{1{,}250}{1{,}167.5} = 1.0707 \qquad \text{36–ult: } 1.067$$

Cumulative factors: AY 2015 1.0670; AY 2016 1.0707 × 1.067 = 1.1424; AY 2017 1.1679 × 1.1424 = 1.3342.

**On-level factors.** The +4% change took effect July 1, 2016 on annual policies written evenly. By the parallelogram method the share of each calendar year's earned premium at the new rate is 0% in 2015, $\tfrac{1}{2}\times 0.5^2 = 12.5\%$ in 2016 and 87.5% in 2017:

$$\text{OLF}_{2015} = 1.04 \qquad \text{OLF}_{2016} = \frac{1.04}{0.875 + 0.125 \times 1.04} = 1.0348 \qquad \text{OLF}_{2017} = \frac{1.04}{0.125 + 0.875 \times 1.04} = 1.0048$$

**Common level.** Bring every year to accident year 2017: losses are trended at 2% from each accident year (2 years for 2015, 1 for 2016), and on-level premium at 3% from each calendar year's average written date (2 years for 2015, 1 for 2016).

| AY | Reported | Trended reported | On-level EP trended | CDF | Used-up premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,250 | 1,250 × 1.02² = 1,300.5 | 1,900 × 1.04 × 1.03² = 2,096.3 | 1.0670 | 1,964.7 |
| 2016 | 1,285 | 1,285 × 1.02 = 1,310.7 | 2,085 × 1.0348 × 1.03 = 2,222.3 | 1.1424 | 1,945.3 |
| 2017 | 1,050 | 1,050.0 | 2,100 × 1.0048 = 2,110.1 | 1.3342 | 1,581.6 |
| **Total** | | **3,661.2** | | | **5,491.7** |

Used-up premium is trended on-level premium ÷ CDF. The Cape Cod expected claim ratio at the 2017 level is

$$\text{ELR} = \frac{3{,}661.2}{5{,}491.7} = 0.6667$$

**Back to each year's level.** Applied to each year's actual earned premium, the ratio is de-trended and taken back off current rate level:

$$\text{ELR}_{AY} = 0.6667 \times \text{OLF}_{AY} \times \frac{1.03^{t}}{1.02^{t}}: \qquad 0.7070 \ (2015), \quad 0.6967 \ (2016), \quad 0.6699 \ (2017)$$

**Ultimate.** Ultimate = (untrended) reported + expected unreported:

| AY | Expected claims | % unreported $= 1 - 1/\text{CDF}$ | Unreported | Ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 0.7070 × 1,900 = 1,343.3 | 6.28% | 84.4 | 1,334.4 |
| 2016 | 0.6967 × 2,085 = 1,452.5 | 12.47% | 181.1 | 1,466.1 |
| 2017 | 0.6699 × 2,100 = 1,406.8 | 25.05% | 352.4 | 1,402.4 |

Ultimate loss and ALAE (\$000s): 1,334.4 for 2015, 1,466.1 for 2016 and 1,402.4 for 2017.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use the Cape Cod technique to calculate ultimate losses and incorporate the result into development of an overall indicated rate change.

Candidates were expected to determine the appropriate trend period to be applied to earned premium and reported losses. In addition, candidates needed to incorporate on-leveling to determine used up premium and the expected claim ratio within the Cape Cod method.

Candidates were also expected to apply de-trending to the expected claim ratio in development of the ultimate claims by year.

Alternatively, the candidate could have brought all premiums to the average rate level for 2017
rather than current rate level through the on-leveling calculation.

Common errors included:

- Incorrectly calculating the on-level factors.
- Not using the appropriate trend period for losses and premium or using no trending at all.
- Using trended, developed losses in the expected claim ratio calculation instead of trended losses.
- Not incorporate trend or on-leveling when determining the expected claims ratio by year.
- For candidates that did not incorporate net trend within the expected claims ratio by year calculation, multiplying the expected claim ratio by on-level earned premium or trended on-level earned premium.
- Including ULAE within the calculation.
- In developing the final ultimate claims, adding trended reported losses to the unreported losses instead of adding untrended reported losses.

## Part b (2.25 points)

Calculate the indicated rate change for policies effective January 1, 2019 using the latest three accident years of experience.

### Explanation
Policies effective January 1, 2019, written for one year, have an average written date of July 1, 2019 and an average accident date of January 1, 2020. Losses are trended from each accident year's average accident date (July 1) and premium from each calendar year's average written date (January 1):

| AY | Ultimate | Loss trend | Trended ultimate | On-level EP | Premium trend | Trended on-level EP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 1,334.4 | 1.02^4.5 = 1.0932 | 1,458.7 | 1,976.0 | 1.03^4.5 = 1.1423 | 2,257.1 |
| 2016 | 1,466.1 | 1.02^3.5 = 1.0718 | 1,571.3 | 2,157.6 | 1.03^3.5 = 1.1090 | 2,392.8 |
| 2017 | 1,402.4 | 1.02^2.5 = 1.0508 | 1,473.5 | 2,110.1 | 1.03^2.5 = 1.0767 | 2,272.0 |
| **Total** | | | **4,503.5** | | | **6,921.9** |

Loading ULAE (7% of loss and ALAE):

$$\text{Projected loss ratio} = \frac{4{,}503.5 \times 1.07}{6{,}921.9} = 0.6962$$

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{0.6962 + 0.10}{1 - 0.30 - 0.05} - 1 = \frac{0.7962}{0.65} - 1 = +22.5\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to trend premium and ultimate losses developed in part a. to calculate an overall rate indication.

Common errors included:

- Not using the appropriate trend period for losses and premium or using the same trend period as determined in part a.
