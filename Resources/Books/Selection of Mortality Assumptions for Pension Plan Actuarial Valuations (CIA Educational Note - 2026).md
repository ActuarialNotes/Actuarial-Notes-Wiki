A Canadian Institute of Actuaries educational note, dated April 21, 2026 (document 226049), prepared by the Committee on Pension Plan Financial Reporting and approved for distribution by the Actuarial Guidance Council on April 14, 2026. It says that the actuary applies judgment in selecting a best estimate mortality assumption for the pension plan under review, that no single standard assumption applies to all plans, and that the selection has two components: a base mortality table and a future mortality improvement scale.

- Process

## 1 Introduction
- The best estimate has two key components: the base mortality table (current mortality rates applicable to the plan) and the future mortality improvement scale.

## 2 Current levels of mortality
- The actuary first determines the best estimate of current mortality levels, considering the plan's own experience and its credibility, similar plans, populations with similar longevity, published mortality studies and member or plan characteristics correlated with mortality.
- The process typically involves four steps: assess the plan's credibility; analyze mortality experience; select an unadjusted table, typically from published studies; and adjust for plan membership characteristics.
- 2.1 Credibility
    - Distinguishes very large plans (typically more than 10,000 retirees), mid-size plans (1,000 to 10,000), small plans (for example, 100 or more retirees) and very small plans.
- 2.2 Analysis of mortality experience
    - Benefit amount versus number of lives
    - Effect of base year
    - Basis of comparison
    - Evolution of workforce longevity characteristics
- 2.3 Published mortality studies
    - The 2024 CIA CPM study: 10 tables each weighted by amount and by lives (20 in all), with separate tables for retirees and surviving spouses and Combined, Light and Heavy variants.
    - The 2014 CIA CPM study: CPM2014, CPM2014Publ and CPM2014Priv.
    - Previous studies: UP94, GAM94 and RP-2000 with Scale AA.
    - New information
    - Relevance of certain mortality studies

## 3 Adjustments for plan membership characteristics
- Published studies indicate higher mortality for blue-collar workers, individuals receiving smaller pensions and surviving spouses or disabled pensioners.
- Where credible plan experience exists it is preferable to rely on that experience rather than on adjustments derived from published studies.
- 3.1 Collar type, industry, pension size, sector and other indicators
    - Collar type
    - Industry
    - Pension size
    - Private and public sector
    - Other member-level socio-economic indicators
    - Emerging experience
    - Graduation of rates

## 4 Adjustment for future improvements in mortality
- A best estimate of future improvement typically has three components: a short-term improvement rate, an ultimate long-term rate and a transition period and pattern between them.
- Three common practices: two-dimensional generational mortality scales, one-dimensional generational mortality scales, and static mortality scales with a fixed projection period.
- 4.1 Considerations in selecting future mortality improvement assumptions
    - Long-term improvement framework
    - Recency of historical analysis
    - Population basis
    - Scale construction
    - Ultimate improvement rate
    - MI-2024, MI-2017, CPM-B or internally developed assumptions may each be reasonable.
- 4.2 Application of mortality improvement to base mortality

## 5 Special situations
- 5.1 Pre-retirement mortality
- 5.2 Disabled life mortality
- 5.3 Surviving spouse mortality

## 6 Sensitivity
- Sensitivity may be illustrated by setting life expectancy one year higher than assumed, by an age setback, or by a percentage reduction to mortality rates such as 10 per cent.

## 7 Application
- 7.1 Use of pension mortality assumptions for other purposes
- 7.2 New experience studies
- 7.3 Applicability
