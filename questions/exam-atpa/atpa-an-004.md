An actuary in the United States is building a predictive pricing model for a personal auto book. Match each task in the project to the Actuarial Standard of Practice (ASOP) whose subject it is. An option may be used more than once or not at all.

## Part a (1 point)
The extract's earned premium cannot be reconciled to the financial statements, and the actuary must decide whether the data can be used and what to disclose about them.

- A) ASOP No. 12
- B) ASOP No. 13
- C) ASOP No. 23
- D) ASOP No. 41
- E) ASOP No. 56

### Answer
C

### Explanation
ASOP No. 23 covers data quality: selecting and reviewing the data, deciding how to use them, and disclosing their significant limitations. **A**, **B**, **D** and **E** cover risk classification, trending, communications and modeling.

## Part b (1 point)
The actuary chooses which characteristics will group policyholders into rating classes.

- A) ASOP No. 12
- B) ASOP No. 13
- C) ASOP No. 23
- D) ASOP No. 41
- E) ASOP No. 56

### Answer
A

### Explanation
Grouping risks into classes is risk classification, the subject of ASOP No. 12. ASOP No. 56 (**E**) governs building and using the model itself, not the choice of classes it rates by.

## Part c (1 point)
The actuary builds the predictive model and decides how it will be validated before use.

- A) ASOP No. 12
- B) ASOP No. 13
- C) ASOP No. 23
- D) ASOP No. 41
- E) ASOP No. 56

### Answer
E

### Explanation
ASOP No. 56 covers modeling. **C** would apply to the data the model is fitted on, and **A** to the classes it produces, but the model itself falls under No. 56.

## Part d (1 point)
The actuary's report departs from what a standard calls for and says so, giving the reason.

- A) ASOP No. 12
- B) ASOP No. 13
- C) ASOP No. 23
- D) ASOP No. 41
- E) ASOP No. 56

### Answer
D

### Explanation
The ASOPs allow a deviation only where the actuary discloses it and the reason, as ASOP No. 41 on actuarial communications requires. The standard being departed from may be any of the others, but the disclosure in the report is a communication.

## Part e (1 point)
Historical loss costs are projected forward to the period the new rates will be in effect.

- A) ASOP No. 12
- B) ASOP No. 13
- C) ASOP No. 23
- D) ASOP No. 41
- E) ASOP No. 56

### Answer
B

### Explanation
Projecting losses to a future period is trending, the subject of ASOP No. 13. **E** is tempting because the projection feeds the model, but trending procedures have a standard of their own.
