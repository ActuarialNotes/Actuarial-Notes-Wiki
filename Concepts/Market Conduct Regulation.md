![[Media/Figures/Market_Conduct_Regulation.svg|340]]

**Market Conduct Regulation** is provincial supervision of how insurers behave toward customers. It is the counterpart to [[Solvency Regulation]], and it is what a provincial [[Superintendent of Insurance]] spends most of its time on.

Its organising principle across Canada is the **fair treatment of customers**, set out in joint [[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators|CCIR]]/CISRO guidance covering the whole product life cycle: design, distribution, disclosure, claims and complaints. It covers:

1. **Sales and advertising**, and product suitability.
2. **Claims handling**, the highest-stakes area. An insurer owes a [[Duty of Good Faith|duty of good faith]] in handling a claim, and a breach is actionable in court as well as before the regulator ([[Bad Faith Damages]]).
3. **Complaint resolution**, escalated outside the insurer to the General Insurance OmbudService.
4. **Privacy.**
5. **Distribution conduct**: the licensing and conduct of agents and adjusters, disclosure of compensation and conflicts, and the [[Take-All-Comers Rule|take-all-comers]] obligation in regulated auto markets.

Insurers file an annual **Market Conduct Annual Statement** with provincial regulators, reporting complaint counts, claims-denial rates and distribution data, which regulators use to target examinations. It is a CCIR product, distinct from OSFI's prudential [[Canadian Annual Return]]. The industry's self-regulatory layer sits alongside, in the [[Insurance Bureau of Canada]]'s [[Code of Consumer Rights and Responsibilities]].

Market conduct increasingly reaches actuarial work. Whether a rating variable is fair ([[Unfair Discrimination]], [[Bias in Actuarial Practice]]) and whether an algorithm can be explained to the customer are now conduct questions as well as pricing questions.

> [!example]- Is a rating factor that charges more to policyholders who shop around less a conduct problem or a pricing problem? {Example}
> - It is both, and the conduct dimension is the binding one.
> - Actuarially, the factor, built from renewal-response data, measures price elasticity rather than expected cost, so two policyholders with identical risk pay different premiums for the same coverage.
> - In conduct terms, it charges on the basis of a customer's inattention. Canadian regulators have taken the position that rates must be justified by risk and expense, so the variable is not permitted in filings.
> - Under the fair treatment of customers, it is worse than a technical filing defect, because it targets the customers least able to protect themselves.
> - Supporting a filing whose classification plan is not risk-based conflicts with the [[Standards of Practice]] and with the actuary's duty to the public interest.

> [!trap] Confusing the conduct statement with the prudential return
> The Market Conduct Annual Statement goes to provincial regulators and reports how customers are treated. The [[Canadian Annual Return]] goes to OSFI and reports financial condition.
