Four men at a wedding party throw their hats into a big box. Later, each of them randomly selects a hat from the box and places it on his head.

Calculate the probability that none of the four men has his own hat on his head.

- A) $0.042$
- B) $0.250$
- C) $0.333$
- D) $0.375$
- E) $0.500$

## Explanation

Label the men and their hats $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$. A random assignment is a permutation of the four hats, and there are $4! = 24$ of them, all equally likely. The event wanted is that no man draws his own hat — a **derangement**.

Count the complement instead: at least one man gets his own hat. Inclusion–exclusion over which men are matched gives

$$
\begin{align*}
\#\{\text{at least one match}\} &= \binom{4}{1}3! - \binom{4}{2}2! + \binom{4}{3}1! - \binom{4}{4}0! \\
                                &= 24 - 12 + 4 - 1 \\
                                &= 15
\end{align*}
$$

Each term counts the permutations fixing a chosen set of men, with the alternating signs correcting for the overlaps. So

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{no man gets his own hat}] &= \frac{24 - 15}{24} \\
                                  &= \frac{9}{24} \\
                                  &= \mathbf{0.375}
\end{align*}
$$

The nine derangements can also be listed directly, which is a practical check at this size. Choice (A), 0.042, is $1/24$ — the probability that *everyone* gets his own hat. Choice (E) is the intuition that a hat is "as likely as not" to be wrong; in fact the derangement probability of $n$ items tends to $e^{-1} \approx 0.368$ as $n$ grows, and 0.375 is already close to that limit.
