At year end an insurer estimates that its retrospectively rated workers compensation policies will produce \$250,000 of return premium for the expired part of their terms. How does SSAP No. 66 have it record the estimate?

- A) As part of the change in unearned premium, through written or earned premium
- B) As loss adjustment expense, because the adjustment depends on the losses
- C) Not at all until the retrospective premium is calculated and billed
- D) As a direct charge to surplus that bypasses the statutory income statement

## Explanation

Retrospective premium adjustments are estimated for the expired portion of the policy period and are an immediate adjustment to premium. For a property-casualty insurer, accrued return retrospective premium is recorded as part of the change in unearned premium, with the entry made either to written premium or as an adjustment to earned premium. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — the adjustment is premium, though its amount depends on the insured's losses; it is not a claim expense.
- **C** — SSAP No. 66 requires the adjustment to be estimated from experience to date, not deferred until billing.
- **D** — the adjustment flows through premium in the income statement, not directly to surplus.
