A Frequently Asked Questions paper, dated December 2021, prepared by the Tax Work Group of the Life Practice Council of the American Academy of Actuaries. It discusses the calculation of tax reserves and the reporting of tax reserve changes under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) passed at the end of 2017, covering the provisions of the tax code that govern the calculation of tax reserves under IRC § 807(c)(1)-(6) and what constitutes a change in tax reserves.

## Introduction
- This is the second FAQ paper prepared by the Academy's Tax Work Group, following a first paper released in early 2018 on life insurance tax reserve methods and assumptions under IRC § 807(d) and (f) as they existed at the end of 2017.
- The paper also provides the Tax Work Group's comments on those tax reserve assumptions that affect contract tax compliance and thus policyholder taxation under the definition of life insurance rules (IRC § 7702) and the modified endowment contract rules (IRC § 7702A).
- The paper is not an interpretation of actuarial standards of practice and does not constitute tax advice.

## General Background on the Calculation of Tax Reserves Under IRC § 807(c)
- The tax reserves included in IRC § 807(c) are life insurance reserves; unearned premiums and unpaid losses; amounts discounted at interest necessary to satisfy obligations not involving life, accident or health contingencies; dividend accumulations and other amounts held at interest; premiums received in advance and liabilities for premium deposit funds; and reasonable special contingency reserves under group term life or group accident and health contracts.
- The tax reserve for any contract is not to be less than the net surrender value of the contract, and may not exceed the statutory reserve for that contract (the "stat cap").
- The TCJA introduced two major changes: tax reserves are now based on an NAIC-prescribed reserve method as of the valuation date rather than at issue, and tax reserves are no longer required to be calculated using the prescribed interest rate and mortality tables.
