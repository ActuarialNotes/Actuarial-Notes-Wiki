Given the following accident year and report year information as of December 31, 2016:

**Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 120 | 200 | 276 |
| 2015 | 120 | 200 | |
| 2016 | 60 | | |

**Incremental Reported Claim Counts as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 60 | 10 | 3 |
| 2015 | 60 | 10 | |
| 2016 | 60 | | |

**Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Report Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 120 | 180 | 240 |
| 2015 | 140 | 210 | |
| 2016 | 86 | | |

- No claims are reported beyond 36 months.
- Accident year 36-to-ultimate development factor = 1.06.
- No claims occurred prior to January 1, 2014.

Calculate the claims incurred but not yet reported (IBNYR) in total for all years as of December 31, 2016.

### Answer
IBNYR $\approx$ 39.4 (\$000), i.e. about \$39,400.

### Explanation
The whole question turns on one identity and on what each aggregation of the data can see:

$$\text{IBNR} = \text{IBNER} + \text{IBNYR}$$

- An **accident year** cohort is fixed by *occurrence* date, so it keeps growing for two reasons: claims already known develop (IBNER) **and** claims that occurred in the year are still being reported (IBNYR). Developing the accident year triangle to ultimate therefore captures **total IBNR**.
- A **report year** cohort is fixed by *report* date. No new claims can ever enter report year 2015 — by construction every claim in it was reported during 2015. All of its development is on claims already known, so developing the report year triangle to ultimate captures **IBNER only**.

Hence

$$\text{IBNYR} = \text{AY ultimate} - \text{RY ultimate}$$

**Step 1 — develop the accident year triangle.** The given 36-to-ultimate factor of 1.06 must be applied.

| Accident Year | 12–24 | 24–36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | $200/120 = 1.667$ | $276/200 = 1.380$ |
| 2015 | $200/120 = 1.667$ | |
| **Selected** | **1.667** | **1.380** |

| | 12–ult | 24–ult | 36–ult |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative factor | $1.667 \times 1.4628 = 2.4380$ | $1.380 \times 1.06 = 1.4628$ | 1.0600 |

| Accident Year | Reported | CDF | Ultimate (\$000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 276 | 1.0600 | 292.56 |
| 2015 | 200 | 1.4628 | 292.56 |
| 2016 | 60 | 2.4380 | 146.28 |
| **Total** | **536** | | **731.40** |

**Step 2 — develop the report year triangle.** No tail is applied here: the accident year tail exists because accident year 2014 was still developing past 36 months, but report year 2014 is complete at 240 and no report year tail is given.

| Report Year | 12–24 | 24–36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | $180/120 = 1.500$ | $240/180 = 1.3333$ |
| 2015 | $210/140 = 1.500$ | |
| **Selected** | **1.500** | **1.3333** |

| | 12–ult | 24–ult | 36–ult |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative factor | $1.500 \times 1.3333 = 2.0000$ | 1.3333 | 1.0000 |

| Report Year | Reported | CDF | Ultimate (\$000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 240 | 1.0000 | 240.00 |
| 2015 | 210 | 1.3333 | 280.00 |
| 2016 | 86 | 2.0000 | 172.00 |
| **Total** | **536** | | **692.00** |

**Step 3 — take the difference.**

$$\text{IBNYR} = 731.40 - 692.00 = \mathbf{39.40}\ (\$000)$$

The two triangles reconcile as a check: reported to date is 536 either way, so

$$\text{IBNR} = 731.40 - 536 = 195.40, \qquad \text{IBNER} = 692.00 - 536 = 156.00,$$

$$\text{IBNYR} = 195.40 - 156.00 = 39.40 \ \checkmark$$

**Alternatively:** the incremental claim count triangle supports a frequency-severity attack on the same quantity. Cumulating the counts gives 60, 70, 73 for 2014; 60, 70 for 2015; 60 for 2016, so the count factors are $70/60 = 1.1667$ and $73/70 = 1.0429$, and every year projects to 73 ultimate counts (219 in total) against 203 reported — 16 claims not yet reported. Using accident year 2014's ultimate severity, $292.56/73 = 4.008$ (\$000), as the selection (2016's raw severity of $60/60 = 1.0$ is far too green),

$$\text{IBNYR} \approx 16 \times 4.008 = 64.1\ (\$000)$$

The examiner's report accepted this route as well, provided the count projection was right and the selected severity was developed to ultimate.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to build both the accident year and the report year triangles, select development factors, calculate cumulative development factors, project both sets of ultimates, and then apply the relationship among IBNR, IBNER and IBNYR to isolate the pure late-reporting piece. A frequency-severity route was also accepted, provided the ultimate count projection was correct and the selected severity was itself developed to ultimate.

Common errors included stopping at IBNR or IBNER rather than IBNYR; omitting the given 1.06 tail from the accident year cumulative factors; applying a tail to the report year factors, where none belongs; and, on the frequency-severity route, failing to develop the severity to ultimate.
