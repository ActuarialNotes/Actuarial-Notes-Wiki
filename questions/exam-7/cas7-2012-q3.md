Given the following information as of December 31, 2011:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Incurred Losses (\$000) as of 24 Months | Cumulative Incurred Losses (\$000) as of 36 Months |
|---|---|---|
| 2004 | 2,000 | 2,600 |
| 2005 | 4,000 | 6,000 |
| 2006 | 6,500 | 11,700 |
| 2007 | 6,000 | 10,000 |
| 2008 | 3,600 | 6,600 |
| 2009 | 7,600 | 11,000 |
| 2010 | 5,000 | |

## Part a (2 points)

Using a volume-weighted average to calculate the overall age-to-age factor, create a plot of weighted residuals following Mack's methodology.

### Explanation
Volume-weighted 24–36 age-to-age factor (AY 2004–2009):

$$f = \frac{\sum C_{i,36}}{\sum C_{i,24}} = \frac{2{,}600 + 6{,}000 + \dots + 11{,}000}{2{,}000 + 4{,}000 + \dots + 7{,}600} = \frac{47{,}900}{29{,}700} = 1.613$$

For the volume-weighted average, Mack's weighted residual is

$$r_i = \frac{C_{i,36} - C_{i,24}\, f}{\sqrt{C_{i,24}}}, \qquad \text{e.g. AY 2004: } \frac{2{,}600 - 2{,}000 \times 1.613}{\sqrt{2{,}000}} = -14$$

| AY | $C_{i,24}$ | $C_{i,36}$ | Expected $C_{i,24} \times 1.613$ | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2004 | 2,000 | 2,600 | 3,226 | -14 |
| 2005 | 4,000 | 6,000 | 6,452 | -7.15 |
| 2006 | 6,500 | 11,700 | 10,485 | 15.07 |
| 2007 | 6,000 | 10,000 | 9,678 | 4.16 |
| 2008 | 3,600 | 6,600 | 5,807 | 13.22 |
| 2009 | 7,600 | 11,000 | 12,259 | -14.44 |

Plot each residual (y-axis) against the 24-month incurred loss $C_{i,24}$ (x-axis). The six points fall at roughly -14 (2,000), -7 (4,000), +13 (3,600), +4 (6,000), +15 (6,500) and -14 (7,600): some above and some below zero across the whole range of $C_{i,24}$.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Based on the residual plot, assess whether the variance assumption underlying the chain ladder method has been met.

### Explanation
- Mack's variance assumption is that $\text{Var}(C_{i,k+1} \mid C_{i,k})$ is proportional to $C_{i,k}$; if it holds, the weighted residuals should be randomly scattered around zero with no pattern as $C_{i,24}$ increases.
- Here the points are fairly randomly scattered around 0 and do not appear to be increasing or decreasing with $C_{i,24}$.
- So it appears the variance assumption has been met.

### Examiner Report
Most candidates knew how to calculate the weighted LDF.  There were a number of variations in the form of the residual calculation.  Partial credit was given if the residual numerator had Actual – Expected.  Many candidates did not use the square root of the 24 month actuals in the denominator.  The graph was accepted if the plot was of residuals vs. loss @ 24 months, even if residuals were calculated incorrectly.  For the assessment of the variance assumption, candidates frequently lost at least partial credit for not fully assessing and explaining the reasoning behind their answer.  Candidates are encouraged to state what the assumption is and how the graph did or did not meet the criteria.
