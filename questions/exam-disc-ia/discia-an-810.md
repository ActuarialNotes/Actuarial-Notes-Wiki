A department store's CGL has a \$1,000,000 Personal and Advertising Injury limit and a \$2,000,000 general aggregate, of which \$500,000 has already been paid this year on a premises bodily injury claim. Its security staff then wrongly detain two shoppers in separate incidents. One recovers \$1,250,000 and the other \$600,000. What does Coverage B pay in total?

- A) \$1,000,000
- B) \$1,500,000
- C) \$1,600,000
- D) \$1,850,000

## Explanation

False detention is offense (a). The Personal and Advertising Injury limit is the most paid for all personal and advertising injury sustained by any one person or organization, and Coverage B payments are subject to the general aggregate, which the earlier premises claim has already reduced to \$1,500,000.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Capped} &= \min(1{,}250{,}000,\ 1{,}000{,}000) + \min(600{,}000,\ 1{,}000{,}000) \\
&= 1{,}600{,}000 \\
\text{Paid} &= \min(1{,}600{,}000,\ 2{,}000{,}000 - 500{,}000) \\
&= 1{,}500{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats the Personal and Advertising Injury limit as one cap on all Coverage B claims together; it applies per person or organization.
- **C** applies the per-person limit but ignores the general aggregate, which Coverage A premises claims and Coverage B claims share.
- **D** pays both claims in full, ignoring both the per-person limit and the aggregate.
