Toby purchased a 20-year par value bond with semiannual coupons of 40 and a redemption value of 1100. The bond can be called at 1200 on any coupon date prior to maturity, starting at the end of year 15.

Calculate the maximum price of the bond to guarantee that Toby will earn an annual nominal interest rate of at least 6% convertible semiannually.

- A) 1251
- B) 1262
- C) 1278
- D) 1286
- E) 1295

## Explanation

The yield is $j = 3\%$ per half-year. Among the call dates, compare the coupon with the call price: $40/1200 = 3.33\% > 3\%$, so for early redemption the **earliest** call date (end of year 15) gives the lowest price: each period the call is deferred adds a coupon of 40 that exceeds the $0.03 \times 1200 = 36$ the yield requires on the call price, so the price at a call date rises with the date. That earliest call must then be compared with redemption at maturity, where the bond pays 1100 rather than 1200.

**If called at end of year 15 (30 periods):**

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= 40a_{\overline{30}|0.03} + 1200v_{0.03}^{30} \\
  &= 1278.40
\end{align*}
$$

**If held to maturity (40 periods):**

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= 40a_{\overline{40}|0.03} + 1100v_{0.03}^{40} \\
  &= 1261.80
\end{align*}
$$

The smaller value is the maximum price that guarantees at least a 6% nominal yield whatever date the bond is redeemed on: $\mathbf{1261.80}$. (With callable bonds, the most a buyer will pay is the smallest price over the possible redemption dates; paying more may not earn the desired yield.)
