A regulator finds that an insurer, once, took 20 days to acknowledge a claim through an adjuster's oversight. Under the NAIC Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act, when does such an act become an improper claims practice?

- A) Whenever it happens, because each late acknowledgment is a separate violation of the act
- B) Only when the claimant files a lawsuit against the insurer under the act
- C) When it is flagrant, or frequent enough to be a general business practice
- D) Only when the insurer's case reserve on the claim turns out to be inadequate

## Explanation

The model act makes a listed act an improper claims practice when it is committed flagrantly and in conscious disregard of the act, or with such frequency that it indicates a general business practice. One late acknowledgment through oversight is neither, though the same delay across many files would be. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Treats every isolated act as a violation, which the act's own standard doesn't.
- **B** — The model act creates no private cause of action; it is enforced by the commissioner.
- **D** — Reserve adequacy has nothing to do with the act's standard.
