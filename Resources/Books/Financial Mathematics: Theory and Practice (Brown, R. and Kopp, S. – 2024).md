A financial mathematics textbook for mathematics and actuarial students with a grounding in first-year calculus. Its preface describes a generic approach to financial mathematics across a wide range of transactions — annuities, mortgages, personal loans and bonds — with a limited treatment of advanced topics such as duration and immunization. Every example is solved from first principles; financial calculators and spreadsheets are used where appropriate, and the audience is students who plan to write the actuarial examinations.

## 2 Equivalence Equations
- 2.1 [[Equation of Value|Equations of Value]]
    - Every sum of money should carry the date on which it falls due; at a compound interest rate $i$, $X$ due on a given date is equivalent to $Y$ due $n$ periods later if $Y = X(1+i)^n$.
    - Equivalence at compound interest is transitive, and a sum of dated values due on different dates has no meaning until each is replaced by its equivalent value on one date.
    - An equation of value states that the dated values of two sets of payments are equal on a common focal date (the comparison or valuation date).
- 2.2 Determining the Rate and Time
    - Given $S$, $P$ and $n$, the rate follows from $S = P(1+i)^n$ as $i = (S/P)^{1/n} - 1$.
