## The problem the note addresses

Under the pre-2023 Canadian model the appointed actuary set essentially every input to the policy liabilities. IFRS 17 hands several of them to **management**: the [[Level of Aggregation|level of aggregation]], the [[Contract Boundary|contract boundary]], the [[Premium Allocation Approach|PAA]] election, the [[Coverage Units|coverage unit]] basis, and the accounting policies generally. But the [[Insurance Companies Act]] still requires the appointed actuary to give an [[Statement of Actuarial Opinion|opinion]] on the amount of the [[Insurance Contract Liabilities|insurance contract liabilities]]. So: what does the actuary do about a number that depends on a decision the actuary did not make?

## The answer

- **What has not changed**: the statutory appointment, the [[Duty to Report|duty to report]] to [[OSFI]], the [[FCT|financial condition testing]] obligation, and the requirement that the valuation be in accordance with [[Accepted Actuarial Practice|accepted actuarial practice]].
- **Taking responsibility versus relying.** For each item set by others the actuary decides whether to **take responsibility** for it — which requires understanding it and satisfying themself it is reasonable — or to **rely** on it with disclosure. The choice is item by item, and it drives the wording of the opinion.
- **Reporting with reservation.** Where the actuary cannot take responsibility and cannot accept the item, §2.2 sets out the reservation — the mechanism that keeps the opinion honest rather than forcing an all-or-nothing signature.
- **The opinion** (§3) — the form it takes under IFRS 17, and how reliance and reservation appear in it.
- **§4** covers work outside IFRS 17's scope.
- The **case studies** work through concrete disagreements between actuary and management. They are the part exam questions are built from.
