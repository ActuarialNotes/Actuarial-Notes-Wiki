The following table gives the joint probability function of two random variables $X$ and $Y$:

| $Y$ \ $X$ | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1/6 | 0 | 1/2 |
| 1 | 0 | 1/3 | 0 |

Calculate $\text{Cov}(X,Y)$.

- A) $-1/9$
- B) $0$
- C) $1/18$
- D) $1/9$
- E) $2/9$

## Explanation

Compute the three moments the covariance formula needs. Only cells with nonzero probability contribute.

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X] &= 0\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right) + 1\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) + 2\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \tfrac{4}{3} \\
E[Y] &= 1\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) = \tfrac{1}{3}
\end{align*}
$$

For $E[XY]$, the product is nonzero only where both $x$ and $y$ are nonzero — a single cell, $(x,y) = (1,1)$:

$$
E[XY] = 1(1)\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) = \tfrac{1}{3}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Cov}(X,Y) &= E[XY] - E[X]E[Y] \\
                &= \frac{1}{3} - \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \\
                &= \frac{3 - 4}{9} \\
                &= \mathbf{-\frac{1}{9}}
\end{align*}
$$

The negative sign makes sense from the table's shape: $Y = 1$ occurs only when $X = 1$, while the larger value $X = 2$ always pairs with $Y = 0$. Choice (B), 0, is the trap of assuming that a table with many zero cells implies independence — here the zeros create dependence rather than remove it.
