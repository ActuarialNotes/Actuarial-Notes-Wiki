A grain dealer asks an insurer to cover its losses if grain prices fall before it sells its inventory. Which characteristic of an ideally insurable loss exposure does this exposure fail most clearly?

- A) A large number of similar exposure units
- B) Losses that are definite and measurable
- C) Pure risk
- D) An economically feasible premium

## Explanation

An ideally insurable exposure involves pure risk, the chance of loss or no loss. A change in market price is speculative risk: the same exposure that can produce a loss can also produce a gain. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — many dealers hold similar inventories, so a pool of exposure units exists.
- **B** — a price fall is definite in time and measurable in amount.
- **D** — the exposure's main problem is not cost; speculative risk is not insured even when the loss is measurable.
