A business owner's aircraft policy has a pilot warranty requiring a current medical certificate. His certificate expired two weeks before he flew to a client meeting. During the flight the engine fails from a manufacturing defect, and the aircraft is destroyed in the forced landing. His health played no part in the accident. Is the hull loss covered?

- A) No, because the pilot did not meet the warranty when he flew
- B) Yes, because the loss was caused by the engine defect and not by his health
- C) Yes, but only at actual cash value rather than the agreed value
- D) Yes, but only once the manufacturer has been shown not to be liable

## Explanation

When a pilot who does not meet the pilot warranty is flying, there is no coverage, **whether or not the shortfall caused the loss**. The expired medical certificate breaches the warranty, so the hull loss is not covered.

- **B** applies a causation test the warranty does not have. Arguing the loss would have happened anyway does not restore coverage.
- **C** confuses the settlement basis. Hull coverage on a covered total loss pays the agreed value less the deductible, but here nothing is owed.
- **D** invents a condition. The insurer's possible recovery from the manufacturer does not decide whether the policy covers the loss.
