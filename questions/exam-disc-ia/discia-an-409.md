A named insured has two personal auto policies with the same insurer, one with a \$100,000 each person bodily injury limit and the other with \$300,000. Both apply to an accident in which he seriously injures one person, whose damages are \$500,000. What is the most the insurer will pay for this person's damages under the two policies together?

- A) \$100,000
- B) \$300,000
- C) \$400,000
- D) \$500,000

## Explanation

Part F's "two or more auto policies" provision caps the insurer's total liability under all the policies it issued to the insured at the **highest applicable limit under any one policy**:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid} &= \min\big(500{,}000,\ \max(100{,}000,\ 300{,}000)\big) \\
&= 300{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** uses the lower limit, as if only the first policy applied.
- **C** stacks the two limits, which is exactly what the provision prevents.
- **D** pays the damages in full, ignoring the limits.
