Given the following information about bonds with coupons paid annually:

| Bond | Bond Par Value | Time to Maturity (in years) | Annual Coupon Rate | Bond Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$100 | 1.0 | 0% | \$95.12 |
| 2 | \$100 | 2.0 | 0% | Unknown |
| 3 | \$100 | 3.0 | 0% | \$78.66 |
| 4 | \$100 | 3.0 | 5% | \$91.72 |

Company A and Company B enter into a forward rate agreement with the following attributes:

- Company A will lend money to Company B in two years for a term of one year.
- The principal amount of this loan is \$8,000,000.
- The annual interest rate for this loan is 12% compounded annually.

Calculate the value of the forward rate agreement to Company B.

### Explanation
The problem can be logically split into two parts: calculating the forward rate from $t = 2$ to $t = 3$, then calculating the value of the forward rate agreement.

**1. The forward rate.** The rates from the zero-coupon bonds:

- Bond 1: $r_1 = (100/95.12) - 1 = .0513$, or continuous: $r_1 = \ln(100/95.12) = .050031$
- Bond 3: $r_3 = (100/78.66)^{1/3} - 1 = .0833$, or continuous: $r_3 = \ln(100/78.66)/3 = .080012$

Using Bond 4, set up an equation to find $r_2$:

$$91.72 = \frac{5}{1.0513} + \frac{5}{(1 + r_2)^2} + \frac{105}{1.0833^3} \Rightarrow r_2 = .0695$$

Or it could be worked directly with discount factors, $91.72 = 5(.9512) + 5D_2 + 105(.7866)$, where $D_2 = .8742$, or inverted $(1 + r_2)^2 = 1.1439$, so $r_2 = .0695$. Or continuous: $91.72 = 5/e^{.05} + 5/e^{2r_2} + 105/e^{.08 \times 3}$, so $r_2 = .067575$.

The forward rate at $t = 3$ is $f_3 = \dfrac{(1 + r_3)^3}{(1 + r_2)^2} - 1$:

- $f_3 = 1.0833^3/1.0695^2 - 1 = .111436$, or 11.14%
- $f_3 = .8742/.7866 - 1 = .1113654$, or 11.14%
- $f_3 = e^{.08 \times 3}/e^{.06757 \times 2} - 1 = .110555$, or 11.06%

Rounding in the inputs moves the result:

- $1 + r_1 = 1.0513$, $1 + r_3 = 1.0833$ give $1 + r_2 = 1.0695$ and $f_3 = 11.14\%$
- $1 + r_1 = 1.051$, $1 + r_3 = 1.083$ give $1 + r_2 = 1.078$ and $f_3 = 9.31\%$
- Continuous: $r_1 = .0500$, $r_3 = .0800$, $r_2 = .0675746$, $f_3 = .110555$ or 11.06%; depending on how one rounds $r_2$, $f_3$ could round to 11.05% or 11.07%

**2. The value of the forward rate agreement to Company B.**

$$V_{FRA} = L\,(R_K - R_F)\,(T_2 - T_1)\,e^{-R_2 T_2}$$

(as printed), applied to Company B as

$$V_{FRA} = 8{,}000{,}000 \times (f_3 - .1200) \times (3 - 2) \times \text{Discount Factor}$$

There are three discount factors that were commonly used: $(1.0833)^{-3}$ or 0.7866, which are roughly equivalent, or discounting by the continuous rate, $e^{-(.08 \times 3)}$. A few candidates discounted by the forward rate (1.1114) and then by $(1.0695)^2$, which is equivalent to $(1.0833)^3$.

Alternatively, after calculating $D_2 = .8742$, value the two legs directly (the result is in dollars):

$$V_{FRA} = 8M \times .8742 - 8M \times 1.12 \times .7866 = -54{,}336$$

or, without calculating $f_3$:

$$V_{FRA} = \frac{8M}{(1.0695)^2} - \frac{8M \times 1.12}{(1.0833)^3} = -54{,}336$$

### Examiner Report
- The candidate was expected to know how to get the Forward Rate at t=3 using the Bootstrapping Method, and then use that information to Calculate the value of the Forward Rate Agreement.
- Candidates generally received full credit, with the exception of minor deductions for calculating errors, or expressing the FRA in terms of Company A, rather than Company B.
- Some candidates did not discount by a correct rate, or discounted for two years rather than three. Some used $\exp(.0833 \times 3)$ where .0833 is not a continuous rate in the problem given.
- A few candidates misinterpreted the listed Bond 4 as a 4-year Bond, rather than a 3-year Bond, then could not solve properly for $r_2$.
- A few candidates calculated $f_2$ rather than $f_3$ and used $f_2$ in the FRA equation.
