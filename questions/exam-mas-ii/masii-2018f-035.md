A linear regression is modeled with the following structure:

$$
\begin{align*}
Y_i &\sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_i) \\
\ln(\lambda_i) &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_{1i} + \beta_2 X_{2i} \\
\beta_0 &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ 5) \\
\beta_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ 2) \\
\beta_2 &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ 2)
\end{align*}
$$

100 samples from four posterior distributions are placed in order from smallest to largest. Each posterior is independently ordered. The $i^{\text{th}}$ row corresponds to the $i^{\text{th}}$ smallest sample.

| $i$ | $\beta_0$ | $\beta_1$ | $\beta_2$ | $\beta_1 + \beta_2$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | -5.59 | -2.13 | -2.86 | -4.83 |
| 2 | -4.89 | -1.22 | -2.40 | -3.75 |
| 3 | -4.68 | -1.13 | -2.37 | -3.65 |
| 4 | -4.54 | -1.07 | -1.92 | -3.16 |
| 5 | -4.28 | -1.06 | -1.89 | -2.97 |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 96 | 5.08 | 1.23 | 1.64 | 3.15 |
| 97 | 5.38 | 1.30 | 1.66 | 3.19 |
| 98 | 5.79 | 1.37 | 1.86 | 3.26 |
| 99 | 5.80 | 1.39 | 2.12 | 3.36 |
| 100 | 6.32 | 1.63 | 2.14 | 3.67 |

You are told that the $98^{\text{th}}$ posterior interval for $\beta_0$ is $[-4.89,\ 5.80]$.

Determine the upper bound of the $94^{\text{th}}$ posterior interval for $\beta_1 + \beta_2$.

- A) Less than or equal to 3.00
- B) Greater than 3.00 but less than or equal to 3.20
- C) Greater than 3.20 but less than or equal to 3.40
- D) Greater than 3.40 but less than or equal to 3.60
- E) Greater than 3.60

## Explanation

A posterior interval read from $n$ ordered samples cuts off an equal number of samples in each tail. The $\beta_0$ interval shows the convention. With 100 samples, a 98% interval leaves $1\%$ of 100, or 1 sample, below and 1 above, so it runs from the $2^{\text{nd}}$ to the $99^{\text{th}}$ smallest sample: $[-4.89,\ 5.80]$, as given.

A 94% interval leaves $3\%$ of 100, or 3 samples, in each tail, so it runs from the $4^{\text{th}}$ to the $97^{\text{th}}$ smallest sample. The upper bound for $\beta_1 + \beta_2$ is read from its own column:

$$
97^{\text{th}} \text{ smallest of } \beta_1 + \beta_2 = 3.19
$$

Don't add the $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ columns. Each column is ordered independently, so the $97^{\text{th}}$ values of $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ come from different posterior draws, and $1.30 + 1.66 = 2.96$ is not a quantile of the sum. The sum has to be formed draw by draw and then sorted, and that is the last column.

$3.19$ falls in $(3.00,\ 3.20]$, so the answer is **B**.
