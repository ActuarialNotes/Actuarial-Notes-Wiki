A random variable, $X$, denotes the number of airplanes landing each hour at a certain airport. $X$ is modeled by a Poisson distribution. On average, 20 airplanes are expected to land each hour.

Calculate $E(X^2) + E(X)$.

- A) $40$
- B) $60$
- C) $420$
- D) $440$
- E) $820$

## Explanation

For a Poisson distribution the mean and variance are both $\lambda = 20$. The second moment follows from the variance identity:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= \text{Var}(X) + \bigl(E[X]\bigr)^2 \\
       &= 20 + 400 \\
       &= 420
\end{align*}
$$

Adding the mean:

$$
\begin{align*}
E(X^2) + E(X) &= 420 + 20 \\
              &= \mathbf{440}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 420, is $E[X^2]$ alone. Choice (E), 820, uses $E[X^2] = 800$, which comes from doubling $\lambda^2$ instead of adding the variance to it. The whole question rests on the Poisson's defining coincidence — mean equals variance — which is what makes $E[X^2]$ computable from $\lambda$ alone.
