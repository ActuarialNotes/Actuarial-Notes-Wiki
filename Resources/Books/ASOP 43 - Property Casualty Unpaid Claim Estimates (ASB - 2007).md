The standard governing the estimation of **[[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]]** for property/casualty coverages. On the [[Exam 5 (CAS)|Exam 5]] syllabus for objectives A4 and B2, and the source of the vocabulary examiners expect when a question asks for an "actuarial central estimate." Adopted June 2007.

## Section 1 — Purpose, Scope, and Effective Date

- Applies to actuaries estimating unpaid claims for P&C coverages, including the obligations of **self-insurers and other risk-retaining entities** (see [[Self-Insured Retention]])
- Applies whether the estimate is a point estimate, a range, or a probability distribution

## Section 2 — Definitions

- **Actuarial central estimate** — an estimate representing an expected value over the range of reasonably possible outcomes. It is *not* defined as a mean, median, or mode, and is not required to be any particular statistic
- **Unpaid claim estimate** — the actuary's estimate of the obligation for future payment resulting from claims due to past events
- **Method** — a systematic procedure for estimating unpaid claims; **model** — a mathematical representation; **assumption** — a judgment about a future or uncertain quantity
- **Coverage**, **claim**, and **measurement date** (the date at which the obligation is valued)

## Section 3 — Analysis of Issues and Recommended Practices

### 3.2 Purpose or Use of the Unpaid Claim Estimate

- The intended use (financial reporting, [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]], funding, collateral) shapes the estimate's basis — for example, whether it is [[Gross Losses|gross]] or [[Net Losses|net]] of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]], discounted, or inclusive of [[Allocated Loss Adjustment Expense|ALAE]] and [[Unallocated Loss Adjustment Expenses ULAE|ULAE]]

### 3.3 Constraints on the Analysis

- Time, data availability, and resource limits may constrain the work; if the constraints prevent an adequate analysis, the actuary should disclose this

### 3.4 Scope of the Unpaid Claim Estimate

- Explicitly identify the claims covered, the components included (case, [[IBNR]], ALAE, ULAE, [[Salvage and Subrogation|salvage and subrogation]]), and the [[Net Losses|net/gross]] basis

### 3.6 Unpaid Claim Estimate Analysis

- **Method and model selection** — consider multiple methods and whether the assumptions underlying each are appropriate for the data (see [[Chain Ladder Method]], [[Bornhuetter-Ferguson Method]], [[Cape Cod Method]], [[Expected Loss Method]], [[Frequency-Severity Method]], [[Berquist-Sherman Method]])
- **Data** — evaluate for reasonableness and consistency; consider [[Homogeneity|homogeneity]] and [[Credibility|credibility]] in segmentation
- **Changing conditions** — adjust for operational and environmental changes ([[Case Adequacy|case adequacy]], [[Settlement Rate|settlement rates]], [[Mix of Business|mix of business]], [[Rate Level Change|rate level]], [[Inflation|inflation]], legal environment)
- **External conditions and external data** — consider [[External Information in Reserving|industry and benchmark information]] where internal experience is thin
- **Uncertainty** — consider the sources of uncertainty, though a quantitative variability estimate is not required unless it is within the scope

### 3.7 Recoverability

- Consider whether [[Reinsurance Recovery|reinsurance]] and other recoveries are collectible when the estimate is presented net

### 3.8 Presentation

- A point estimate, a range, or a distribution may each be appropriate; the basis must be stated

### 3.9 Reasonableness

- Apply diagnostics — loss ratios, [[Frequency|frequencies]], [[Severity|severities]], implied development — to test the result (see [[Reserve Adequacy]])

### 3.11 Documentation

- Document the analysis sufficiently for another actuary to evaluate the work

## Section 4 — Communications and Disclosures

- Disclose the intended purpose, the measurement date, the scope and basis of the estimate, whether it is an actuarial central estimate, material assumptions and changes from the prior analysis, and any reliance on others (see [[Reserve Communication]], [[Stakeholder Reporting]])
