Given the following information:

| Territory | Current Premium (\$000) | Current Territory Factor | Indicated Territory Factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 90 | 0.80 | 0.70 |
| 2 | 300 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 3 | 260 | 1.15 | 1.10 |
| Total | 650 | | |

Management requires achieving the following objectives with the upcoming rate change:

- Target an overall rate level increase of 10%.
- Revise territorial relativities to the indicated relativity, while capping the overall rate impact to any territory at 13%.
- Territory 2 remains the base territory.

Calculate the territorial relativities that will be implemented with the rate change.

### Answer
Territory 1: 0.711; Territory 2: 1.000; Territory 3: 1.118

### Explanation
**Step 1 — the change in each territory's relativity.**

$$T_1: \frac{0.70}{0.80} = 0.8750, \qquad T_2: \frac{1.00}{1.00} = 1.0000, \qquad T_3: \frac{1.10}{1.15} = 0.9565$$

**Step 2 — the off-balance.** Moving to the indicated relativities lowers the average rate level on its own, so a base rate correction is needed to still land on +10%. The premium-weighted average of those changes is

$$\frac{90(0.8750) + 300(1.0000) + 260(0.9565)}{650} = \frac{627.45}{650} = 0.9653$$

$$\text{Off-balance factor} = \frac{1}{0.9653} = 1.0359$$

**Step 3 — uncapped total change by territory**, combining the relativity change, the off-balance correction and the 10% target:

| Territory | Calculation | Total change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $0.8750 \times 1.0359 \times 1.10$ | $0.9971 \Rightarrow -0.29\%$ |
| 2 | $1.0000 \times 1.0359 \times 1.10$ | $1.1395 \Rightarrow +13.95\%$ |
| 3 | $0.9565 \times 1.0359 \times 1.10$ | $1.0900 \Rightarrow +9.00\%$ |

These weight back to exactly +10% overall, confirming the off-balance is right. But **territory 2 breaches the 13% cap**.

**Step 4 — apply the cap and redistribute the shortfall.** Holding territory 2 to +13% leaves premium short of the target:

$$\text{Shortfall} = 300(1.1395) - 300(1.13) = 341.86 - 339.00 = 2.86$$

That 2.86 must be recovered from the uncapped territories, whose combined premium at the uncapped changes is $89.74 + 283.40 = 373.14$:

$$\text{Redistribution factor} = 1 + \frac{2.86}{373.14} = 1.00767$$

| Territory | New premium | Capped total change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $90(0.9971)(1.00767) = 90.43$ | $90.43/90 = 1.0047$ |
| 2 | $300(1.13) = 339.00$ | $1.1300$ |
| 3 | $260(1.0900)(1.00767) = 285.57$ | $285.57/260 = 1.0984$ |
| **Total** | **715.00** | $= 650 \times 1.10$ ✓ |

**Step 5 — convert back to relativities.** Apply each territory's capped change to its *current* factor, then rebase so territory 2 equals 1.00:

| Territory | Capped factor | Rebased to $T_2$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1.0047 \times 0.80 = 0.8038$ | $0.8038 / 1.13 = \mathbf{0.711}$ |
| 2 | $1.1300 \times 1.00 = 1.1300$ | $\mathbf{1.000}$ |
| 3 | $1.0984 \times 1.15 = 1.2631$ | $1.2631 / 1.13 = \mathbf{1.118}$ |

Equivalently, work directly from the indicated relativities: the base rate absorbs $1.13 / 1.1395 = 0.992$ of the capping, so the residual adjustment to the non-base territories is $1.00767 / 0.992 = 1.016$, giving $0.70 \times 1.016 = 0.711$ and $1.10 \times 1.016 = 1.118$.

Note the implemented relativities do **not** equal the indicated 0.70 and 1.10: capping territory 2 forces some of its shortfall onto the other two, which shows up as relativities slightly above indicated.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to develop uncapped relativities (or premiums) for each territory and determine the adjustments needed to achieve an overall +10% rate change without any territory exceeding a +13% total adjustment, then convert those adjustments into final territorial relativities.

Common errors included inaccurate weighting of the initial relativity changes; failing to adjust the territorial relativities by the 0.992 base rate factor reflecting the capping in territory 2; and applying the total indicated changes to the wrong relativities in the final step.
