The lifetime (in years) of a car is a random variable with probability density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
0.0625e^{-0.0625x}, & \text{for } x > 0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the probability that the car's lifetime is less than 20 years, given that the car's lifetime is at least five years.

- A) $0.445$
- B) $0.522$
- C) $0.608$
- D) $0.832$
- E) $0.975$

## Explanation

The density is exponential with rate $0.0625 = 1/16$, so the mean is 16 years and

$$
P[X < x] = 1 - e^{-x/16}
$$

Apply the definition of conditional probability. The event $\{X < 20\} \cap \{X > 5\}$ is the interval $5 < X < 20$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X < 20 \mid X > 5] &= \frac{P[5 < X < 20]}{P[X > 5]} \\
                     &= \frac{\left(1 - e^{-20/16}\right) - \left(1 - e^{-5/16}\right)}{e^{-5/16}} \\
                     &= \frac{e^{-5/16} - e^{-20/16}}{e^{-5/16}} \\
                     &= 1 - e^{-15/16} \\
                     &= \mathbf{0.60839}
\end{align*}
$$

The last line is the exponential distribution's **memoryless** property in action: given survival past 5 years, the remaining lifetime is exponential with the same mean 16, so the question collapses to the probability of failing within the next 15 years.

Choice (A), 0.445, is the numerator $P[5 < X < 20]$ with the division left undone. Ignoring the conditioning altogether gives the unconditional $P[X < 20] = 1 - e^{-1.25} = 0.713$, which is not offered. Conditioning *lowers* the probability here: a car known to have lasted 5 years has only a 15-year window left in which to fail before year 20, rather than the full 20.
