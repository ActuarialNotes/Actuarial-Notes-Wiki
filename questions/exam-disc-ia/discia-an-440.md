A named insured's PAP (ISO PP 00 01 09 18) carries collision coverage with a \$1,000 deductible and other than collision coverage with a \$250 deductible. Driving at night, she swerves to avoid a deer, misses it, and hits a tree, causing \$3,000 of damage to her car, whose actual cash value is \$15,000. How much does Part D pay?

- A) \$0
- B) \$2,000
- C) \$2,750
- D) \$3,000

## Explanation

Collision is the upset of the auto or its impact with another vehicle or object. Contact with a bird or animal is listed as other than collision, but the car never touched the deer; it hit a tree, so the loss is **collision**, and the collision deductible applies:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid} &= \min(15{,}000,\ 3{,}000) - 1{,}000 \\
&= 2{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats swerving to avoid an animal as uncovered; the impact with the tree is a covered collision.
- **C** classifies the loss as contact with an animal and applies the \$250 other than collision deductible.
- **D** forgets the deductible.
