| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |

| 2016 | 560 | 532 | |

**Cumulative Reported Loss + ALAE (\$) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 7,200,000 | 8,208,000 | 8,850,600 |
| 2016 | 8,120,000 | 9,256,800 | |
| 2017 | 9,145,000 | | |

| | |
|---|---|
| \$98,000 | Expected reinsurance recoveries |
| \$318,000 | Cost of reinsurance (expected ceded premium) |
| 3% | Expected annual exposure increase |

| Calendar Year | Earned Exposures |
|---|---|
| 2015 | 14,000 |
| 2016 | 15,000 |
| 2017 | 17,000 |

| | |
|---|---|
| 5% | ULAE provision as a percent of loss and ALAE |
| 3% | Annual pure premium trend |
| \$21 | Projected fixed expenses per exposure |
| 15% | Variable expense ratio |
| 10% | Profit provision |
| 2% | Contingency provision |
| \$950 | On-leveled and projected earned premium per exposure |

- Exposures are written evenly throughout each year.
- All policies are annual.
- There is no loss development or claim count development beyond 36 months.
- The reinsurance contract has a 12 month term length and an effective date of January 1, 2019.
- Rates are to be in effect for one year.
- Rate revision is planned to be effective April 1, 2019.

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the projected net reinsurance cost per exposure using a 12-month term for the reinsurance contract.

### Answer

\$12.20 per exposure

### Explanation

The net cost of reinsurance is the ceded premium less the expected recoveries:

$$318{,}000 - 98{,}000 = 220{,}000$$

The contract is effective January 1, 2019 for 12 months, so it covers calendar year 2019 with an average date of July 1, 2019. The latest exposure year is CY 2017, average date July 1, 2017 — a 2-year projection at the 3% annual exposure trend:

$$17{,}000 \times (1.03)^2 = 18{,}035$$

$$\text{Net reinsurance cost per exposure} = \frac{220{,}000}{18{,}035} = \$12.20$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to calculate the net cost of reinsurance and project the latest year's exposures forward to the period covered by the reinsurance contract.

Common mistakes included calculating the projection period for the exposure trend incorrectly, omitting the expected reinsurance recoveries from the net cost, and using exposures summed over multiple accident years rather than the latest year's exposures as the base for the projection.

## Part b (2 points)

Calculate the ultimate losses and ALAE for each accident year using an appropriate frequency-severity technique.

### Answer

AY 2015: \$8,850,600; AY 2016: \$9,978,830; AY 2017: \$11,238,473

### Explanation

**Step 1 — Build the severity triangle** (reported loss + ALAE ÷ reported counts):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 15,000 | 18,000 | 19,800 |
| 2016 | 14,500 | 17,400 | |
| 2017 | 15,500 | | |

**Step 2 — Severity age-to-age factors**

12–24: $18{,}000/15{,}000 = 1.20$ and $17{,}400/14{,}500 = 1.20$, so select **1.20**.
24–36: $19{,}800/18{,}000 = 1.10$, so select **1.10**. No development after 36 months, so 36–ult = 1.00.

Severity CDFs to ultimate: 12 months $= 1.20 \times 1.10 = 1.32$; 24 months $= 1.10$; 36 months $= 1.00$.

**Step 3 — Claim count age-to-age factors**

12–24: $456/480 = 0.95$ and $532/560 = 0.95$, so select **0.95** (counts develop downward as claims close without payment).
24–36: $447/456 = 0.98$. 36–ult = 1.00.

Count CDFs to ultimate: 12 months $= 0.95 \times 0.98 = 0.931$; 24 months $= 0.98$; 36 months $= 1.00$.

**Step 4 — Ultimate counts and severities**

| AY | Reported Count | Count CDF | Ultimate Count | Reported Severity | Severity CDF | Ultimate Severity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 447 | 1.000 | 447 | 19,800 | 1.00 | 19,800 |
| 2016 | 532 | 0.980 | 521 | 17,400 | 1.10 | 19,140 |
| 2017 | 590 | 0.931 | 549 | 15,500 | 1.32 | 20,460 |

**Step 5 — Ultimate loss and ALAE** = ultimate count × ultimate severity:

$$\text{AY 2015} = 447 \times 19{,}800 = 8{,}850{,}600$$

$$\text{AY 2016} = 9{,}256{,}800 \times 1.10 \times 0.98 = 9{,}978{,}830$$

$$\text{AY 2017} = 9{,}145{,}000 \times 1.32 \times 0.931 = 11{,}238{,}473$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to use a frequency-severity technique to develop claim counts and severities to ultimate for each accident year, and to combine those results into ultimate loss and ALAE.

Common mistakes included using the chain ladder method on the loss triangle rather than a frequency-severity technique, and using a frequency-severity technique involving trend without applying the appropriate trend to all components of the technique.

## Part c (1.75 points)

Calculate the projected pure premium per exposure using even weights across the three accident years.

### Answer

\$729 per exposure (including ULAE)

### Explanation

Rates are effective April 1, 2019 for one year, and all policies are annual. The average written date is October 1, 2019, so the average loss date is one half policy term later:

$$\text{Average loss date} = 10/1/2019 + 6 \text{ months} = 4/1/2020$$

Trend periods run from the July 1 midpoint of each accident year to April 1, 2020: 4.75, 3.75 and 2.75 years.

| AY | Earned Exposures (1) | Ultimate Loss (2) | Trend (3) | Trended PP $= \frac{(2)}{(1)} \times (3)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 14,000 | 8,850,600 | $1.03^{4.75} = 1.1507$ | 727 |
| 2016 | 15,000 | 9,978,830 | $1.03^{3.75} = 1.1172$ | 743 |
| 2017 | 17,000 | 11,238,473 | $1.03^{2.75} = 1.0847$ | 717 |

Equal weights across the three years:

$$\text{Projected pure premium} = \tfrac{1}{3}(727 + 743 + 717) = 729$$

Note the exposures are **not** trended — the exposure trend belongs in the reinsurance cost projection of Part a, not in the historical pure premium. ULAE at 5% may be applied here ($729 \times 1.05 = 765$) or carried into Part d; credit was given either way.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to calculate the loss trend period for each accident year, trend the losses, apply the ULAE factor, and determine the projected pure premium. Credit was given for omitting ULAE here if it was correctly included in Part d.

Common mistakes included computing the pure premium from losses and exposures summed across accident years rather than applying equal weights to each year's pure premium, and trending the exposures used in the pure premium calculation.

## Part d (1 point)

Calculate the indicated rate change.

### Answer

+15.2%

### Explanation

Using the pure premium method, the indicated average premium per exposure is

$$\text{Indicated Premium} = \frac{PP \times (1 + \text{ULAE}) + \text{Fixed Expense} + \text{Net Reins. Cost}}{1 - V - Q_{\text{profit}} - Q_{\text{cont}}}$$

$$= \frac{729 \times 1.05 + 21 + 12.20}{1 - 0.15 - 0.10 - 0.02} = \frac{765.45 + 21 + 12.20}{0.73} = \frac{798.65}{0.73} = 1{,}094$$

Comparing with the on-leveled and projected earned premium per exposure of \$950:

$$\text{Indicated Rate Change} = \frac{1{,}094}{950} - 1 = 15.2\%$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to calculate the indicated premium and the indicated rate level change.

Common mistakes included omitting the net reinsurance cost per exposure from the indicated premium, and omitting or incorrectly including the contingency provision in the denominator.
