![[A Comparison of Residential Flood Insurance Markets in 25 Countries (Atreya et al. - 2015) - Cover.svg]]

A white paper reviewing homeowners' flood insurance in 25 countries. It compares the roles of the public and private sectors, design choices (required or voluntary insurance, bundled with other coverage or standalone, how premiums are set), market penetration, the links between insurance and mitigation efforts, and post-disaster public relief, and concludes that the best design depends on the specificities and culture of each country. A working draft, revised June 28, 2015.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6I (CAS)|Exam 6I]] — objective A2; pp. 1–14.

## 1 A Growing Interest in Flood Insurance
- Recent large-scale floods have prompted national debates about who should pay for flood catastrophes, the roles of the public and private sectors, and the affordability of risk-based [[Flood Insurance|flood insurance]].
- The paper categorises each country's design choices without judging which solution is better: public vs. private roles; voluntary vs. mandatory insurance; bundled vs. single policies; risk-based vs. subsidised premiums; economic incentives linking insurance with risk reduction; and federal disaster relief.
- The 25 countries: Austria, Australia, Belgium, Canada, Czech Republic, Germany, Finland, France, Hungary, Iceland, Indonesia, Japan, Mexico, Morocco, Nepal, Netherlands, Norway, Peru, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Spain, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States (Figure 1).
