## Part a (2.25 points)

Calculate the expected loss costs for the policy using the Insurance Charge Reflecting Loss Limitation (ICRLL) procedure.

### Explanation
$$\text{Expected Unlimited Loss} = 1.5M \times 0.75 = 1.125M \qquad \text{LER} = \frac{0.21}{0.75} = 0.28$$

The ICRLL adjustment factor and the adjusted expected loss used to pick the expected loss group:

$$F = \frac{1 + 0.8 \times \text{LER}}{1 - \text{LER}} = 1.7 \qquad \text{Adj Loss} = 1.125M \times 1.7 \times 1.1 = 2.10375M \;\rightarrow\; \text{ELG } 24$$

$$\text{Expected Limited Loss} = 1.125M \times (1 - \text{LER}) = 810{,}000 \qquad \text{Expected Excess Loss} = 1.125M \times \text{LER} = 315{,}000$$

$$\text{Entry Ratio} = \frac{1M}{810{,}000} = 1.234 \;\rightarrow\; \text{Charge} = 0.1706 \text{ (from the table)}$$

$$\text{Expected Loss Cost} = \text{Charge} \times \text{Exp Lim Loss} + \text{Expected Excess Loss} = 0.1706 \times 810{,}000 + 315{,}000 = 453{,}186$$

As a percentage of standard premium: $453{,}186 / 1.5M = 0.3021$.

## Part b (1 point)

Explain why the ICRLL procedure produces reasonably accurate insurance charges.

### Explanation
The LUGS adjustment shifts the aggregate loss distribution used to estimate the insurance charge to a loss distribution associated with a larger (less skewed) loss distribution. This less skewed distribution approximates the limited loss distribution that is really acting on the risk being rated due to the presence of the per-loss limit. This leads to insurance charges that are smaller than would be indicated in the absence of a loss limit, reflecting the removal of the overlap between the "loss limitation" and the aggregate loss limit.

Alternatively: the per-occurrence limit (or a deductible in this case) reduces the losses that count toward the aggregate limit. If one pulled an insurance charge without adjusting losses, there would be overlap between the losses eliminated by the deductible and the losses implicitly eliminated by the aggregate limit. To reduce the overlap, an expected loss group with more stable losses (resulting in a lower charge) is selected by finding an equivalent ELG based on the losses eliminated and the state hazard group relativity. Because the lower ELG produces lower charges for the aggregate limit, this helps to eliminate the overlap and provide accurate charges.
