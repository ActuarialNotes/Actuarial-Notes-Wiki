The **Code of Consumer Rights and Responsibilities** is the [[Insurance Bureau of Canada]]'s voluntary industry code setting out what a home, auto or business insurance customer is entitled to expect from an insurer, and what the customer owes in return. It is self-regulation: adopted by IBC member insurers, not enacted by any legislature, and enforceable only through the regulator's [[Market Conduct Regulation|market conduct]] powers if a Superintendent chooses to treat a breach as evidence.

| The consumer has a right to | The consumer is responsible for |
| :--- | :--- |
| Be informed about coverage, price and the claims process in plain language | Asking questions and understanding the coverage bought |
| Professional and prompt service | Providing complete and accurate information |
| Privacy of personal information | Informing the insurer of material changes in risk |
| Prompt and fair claims handling, with reasons if a claim is denied | Paying premiums when due |
| Complain, and have the complaint escalated internally and then to the General Insurance OmbudService | Reporting claims promptly and honestly |

The Code mirrors the insurance contract's own logic, the insurer's [[Duty of Good Faith|duty of good faith]] and the insured's duty of full disclosure, but expresses it as service expectations rather than as conditions of coverage.

## Limits of a voluntary code

A voluntary code has no statutory remedy, applies only to members, is drafted by the industry it governs and does not create a cause of action. Its defenders argue it raises the floor faster than legislation can. Its critics argue it is unenforceable where it matters.

> [!example]- A policyholder's claim is denied without written reasons, which the Code entitled them to. What can they do? {Example}
> - The Code itself gives them nothing to sue on, because it creates no cause of action.
> - They can use the insurer's internal complaints process, which the Code and the fair treatment guidance both require the insurer to maintain.
> - They can take the dispute to the General Insurance OmbudService, an independent dispute-resolution service whose recommendations are non-binding on the consumer and generally followed by insurers.
> - They can complain to the provincial regulator. A pattern of denials without reasons is evidence of a conduct failure, so the Code matters here indirectly.
> - They can go to court over the coverage itself and, if the denial was handled in bad faith, for [[Bad Faith Damages|bad faith damages]].
> - The Code is a statement of expectations that feeds enforcement elsewhere, not a remedy in itself.

## Fair treatment of customers

The Code sits inside the wider fair treatment of customers framework from the [[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators|CCIR]] and CISRO, which is backed by regulatory expectation. The direction of travel has been from voluntary code toward supervised outcome.
