Two claims offices handle the same line. Office X had 800 claims reported, closed 760 of them and paid \$3,800,000 on the closed claims. Office Y had 900 reported, closed 630 and paid \$2,520,000. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A) Y handles claims better, because its average paid per closed claim is the lower of the two
- B) X handles claims better, because it paid more in total on the claims it closed
- C) The offices perform alike, because their average payments differ by only \$1,000 a claim
- D) Y's average may rise as its open claims close, so no one measure settles it

## Explanation

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Closure}_X &= \frac{760}{800} \\
&= 95\% \\
\text{Closure}_Y &= \frac{630}{900} \\
&= 70\% \\
\text{Average}_X &= \frac{3{,}800{,}000}{760} \\
&= 5{,}000 \\
\text{Average}_Y &= \frac{2{,}520{,}000}{630} \\
&= 4{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

Y's average paid is lower, but it has closed only 70% of its claims. If its open claims are the larger and harder ones, its average will rise as they close. No single measure settles which office performs better; a claims audit of the open files would. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Reads the average in isolation, ignoring that Y has left 30% of its claims open.
- **B** — Total paid reflects how many claims were closed, not how well they were handled.
- **C** — Ignores the large difference in closure rates.
