On average, a certain word processing software program has a fatal crash once in every 50 instances of saving a document.

The instances of fatal crashes, while saving, are independent from one another.

Calculate the probability that the second fatal crash, while saving, occurs on the fourth instance of saving a document.

- A) $0.00038$
- B) $0.00115$
- C) $0.00230$
- D) $0.01882$
- E) $0.02000$

## Explanation

Each save crashes with probability $p = 1/50 = 0.02$, independently.

For the **second** crash to land on the fourth save, two things must hold: the fourth save crashes, and exactly one of the first three saves crashed. That is the negative binomial pattern.

The position of the single earlier crash can be any of the first three saves, giving $\binom{3}{1} = 3$ arrangements. Each arrangement has two crashes and two clean saves:

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \binom{3}{1}(0.02)^2(0.98)^2 \\
  &= 3(0.0004)(0.9604) \\
  &= \mathbf{0.001152}
\end{align*}
$$

The binomial coefficient counts only the first three trials, not all four — the fourth save's outcome is fixed by the stopping condition. Using $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ instead would give 0.00230, which is choice (C): that would be the probability of exactly two crashes *somewhere* in four saves, a different event that does not require the run to end on the fourth.
