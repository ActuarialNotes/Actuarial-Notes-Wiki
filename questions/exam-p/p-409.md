The amount of a loss under a fire insurance policy is continuous and has cumulative distribution function

$$
F(x) = \begin{cases}
0, & \text{for } x < 0 \\
c\left(\dfrac{x}{15}\right)^{4/3}, & \text{for } 0 \leq x \leq 10 \\
1, & \text{for } x > 10,
\end{cases}
$$

where $c$ is a positive constant.

The insurer reimburses each loss up to a maximum amount $m$. The probability that a given loss is partially reimbursed is 0.56.

Calculate $m$.

- A) $5.40$
- B) $6.47$
- C) $7.03$
- D) $7.80$
- E) $8.10$

## Explanation

Losses never exceed 10, so the distribution function must already equal 1 there. That pins down $c$:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 = F(10) &= c\left(\frac{10}{15}\right)^{4/3} \\
c &= \left(\frac{15}{10}\right)^{4/3}
\end{align*}
$$

A loss is **partially** reimbursed exactly when it exceeds the cap $m$ — a loss at or below $m$ is paid in full, not partially. So

$$
\begin{align*}
0.56 &= P[X > m] = 1 - F(m) \\
     &= 1 - \left(\frac{15}{10}\right)^{4/3}\left(\frac{m}{15}\right)^{4/3} \\
     &= 1 - \left(\frac{m}{10}\right)^{4/3}
\end{align*}
$$

The two constants collapse neatly: the distribution function is just $(x/10)^{4/3}$ once $c$ is substituted. Solving,

$$
\begin{align*}
\left(\frac{m}{10}\right)^{4/3} &= 0.44 \\
m &= 10(0.44)^{3/4} \\
  &= \mathbf{5.40}
\end{align*}
$$

The interpretive step is what this question tests. Reading "partially reimbursed" as $P[X \leq m] = 0.56$ instead gives $m = 10(0.56)^{3/4} = 6.47$, which is choice (B) — a loss below the cap is reimbursed *fully*, so that reading has the inequality backwards.
