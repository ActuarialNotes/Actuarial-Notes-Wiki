In a certain year, an insurance company's profit is modeled by a normal distribution with mean 6.72. The 80th percentile of the profit is 8.40.

Calculate the 90th percentile of the insurance company's profit in the year.

- A) $8.61$
- B) $8.96$
- C) $9.28$
- D) $9.45$
- E) $12.80$

## Explanation

The standard deviation is not given, so recover it from the 80th percentile first. Writing $X$ for the profit and $\sigma$ for its standard deviation,

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X < 8.4] &= 0.80 \\
P\left[Z < \frac{8.4 - 6.72}{\sigma}\right] &= 0.80
\end{align*}
$$

The 80th percentile of the standard normal is $z_{0.80} = 0.84162$, so

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{8.4 - 6.72}{\sigma} &= 0.84162 \\
\sigma &= \frac{1.68}{0.84162} \\
       &= 2
\end{align*}
$$

Now run the same relation forward at the 90th percentile, where $z_{0.90} = 1.28155$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{P - 6.72}{2} &= 1.28155 \\
P &= 6.72 + 2(1.28155) \\
  &= \mathbf{9.28}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 9.45, is $8.40 \times \tfrac{90}{80}$ — percentiles of a normal distribution are not proportional to their probabilities. Choice (E) drops the mean from the first step, taking $\sigma = 8.4/0.84162 = 9.98$ and then reporting $\sigma z_{0.90} = 12.79$. The relation that *is* linear is between the percentile and its $z$-score, which is what the two-step solution exploits.
