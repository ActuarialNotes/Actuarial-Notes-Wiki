From a collection of eight keys, Door A can only be opened with one key and Door B can only be opened with one other key.

Calculate the probability that among four randomly selected keys, exactly three keys will open neither door.

- A) $0.03$
- B) $0.11$
- C) $0.29$
- D) $0.42$
- E) $0.57$

## Explanation

Two of the eight keys open a door; the other six open neither. Four keys are drawn without replacement, so the count of useless keys is hypergeometric.

Exactly three useless keys means the fourth key is one of the two door keys:

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \frac{\binom{6}{3}\binom{2}{1}}{\binom{8}{4}} \\
  &= \frac{(20)(2)}{70} \\
  &= \frac{40}{70} \\
  &= \mathbf{0.5714}
\end{align*}
$$

The two factors must use up exactly four keys, which is the check that the split was read correctly. Choice (C), 0.29, drops the $\binom{2}{1}$ factor: $\binom{6}{3}/\binom{8}{4} = 0.286$ counts only one way to fill the fourth slot when either door key would do. Choice (D), 0.42, is the binomial $\binom{4}{3}(6/8)^3(2/8)$ — treating the four draws as independent, which they are not with only eight keys.
