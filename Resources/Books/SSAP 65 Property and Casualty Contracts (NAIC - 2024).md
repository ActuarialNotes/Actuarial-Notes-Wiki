Statement of Statutory Accounting Principles No. 65 of the NAIC's Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual. It establishes statutory accounting principles for property and casualty insurance contracts, covering claims-made policies, discounting, structured settlements, policies with long coverage periods, high deductible policies, asbestos and environmental exposures, excess statutory reserves and policyholder dividends.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objectives C1–C2; NAIC Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual, 2025, SSAP No. 65, paragraphs 1-46, available in Appendix IV of the COPLFR P&C Practice Note (as the content outline states it)

## Scope of Statement
- The statement establishes statutory accounting principles for property and casualty insurance contracts; topics it does not cover follow the more general statutory accounting guidance.
- Title insurance, mortgage guaranty insurance and financial guaranty insurance are outside its scope and are addressed in SSAP Nos. 57, 58 and 60.

## Summary Conclusion
- Claims-Made Policies
    - A claims-made policy may cover only claims reported within the policy dates that occur after a specified retroactive date, which eliminates duplicate coverage when converting from occurrence coverage.
- Discounting
    - Except for fixed and reasonably determinable payments, such as workers' compensation tabular indemnity reserves and long-term disability claims, property and casualty [[Loss Reserve Discounting|loss reserves]] are not discounted, and no loss adjustment expense reserves are discounted.
    - Tabular reserves are indemnity reserves calculated using discounts determined with reference to actuarial tables that incorporate interest and contingencies such as mortality, remarriage, inflation or recovery from disability.
- Structured Settlements
    - Structured settlements are periodic fixed payments to a claimant for a determinable period or for life, frequently funded by an annuity the reporting entity purchases; the accounting depends on whether the entity or the claimant is the payee.
- Policies with Coverage Periods Equal to or in Excess of Thirteen Months
    - These are single premium or fixed premium contracts, such as home warranty and mechanical breakdown policies, that generally are not subject to cancellation or premium modification by the reporting entity.
- High Deductible Policies
    - High deductible plans, particularly workers' compensation, differ from self insurance coupled with an excess of loss policy because state laws generally require the reporting entity to fund the deductible and periodically review the insured's financial viability.
- Asbestos and Environmental Exposures
- Excess Statutory Reserve
    - The statement eliminates the requirement to record excess statutory reserves, which do not meet the definition of a liability in SSAP No. 5R.
- Policyholder Dividends
    - Dividends to policyholders become liabilities when declared by the board of directors; incurred policyholder dividends are reported in the statement of income.
