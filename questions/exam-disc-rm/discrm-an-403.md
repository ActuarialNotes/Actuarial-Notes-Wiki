An employee of a bottling plant loses a hand in a machine. The plant's NCCI workers compensation and employers liability policy pays his statutory benefits. He then sues the machine's manufacturer, which sues the plant, claiming the plant removed a safety guard. Which part of the policy responds to the manufacturer's suit against the plant?

- A) Part One, because the injury arose out of and in the course of the employee's employment
- B) Part Three, because the suit against the plant is brought by someone other than the employee
- C) No part, because liability the plant owes to a third party is excluded as contractual liability
- D) Part Two, which covers the employer's liability to a third party over an employee's injury

## Explanation

Part One pays only the benefits the workers compensation law requires. **Part Two, employers liability**, pays damages the employer is legally obligated to pay because of bodily injury to an employee in the course of employment, and it expressly includes damages for which the employer is liable to a third party that is sued over the employee's injury and claims against the employer. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Part One has already paid the statutory benefits; a tort claim for damages is outside it.
- **B** — Part Three extends coverage to other states listed on the information page; it has nothing to do with who sues.
- **C** — The contractual liability exclusion removes liability the employer *assumed under a contract*; the manufacturer claims the plant was at fault, not that the plant agreed to bear the loss.
