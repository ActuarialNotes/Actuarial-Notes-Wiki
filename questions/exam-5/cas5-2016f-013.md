An insurance company is considering updating its territorial relativities given the following information:

| Territory | Number of Exposures | Trended and Ultimate Incurred Losses & ALAE | Current Territorial Relativity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30,000 | \$3,000,000 | 1.100 |
| 2 | 50,000 | \$4,000,000 | 1.000 |
| 3 | 25,000 | \$1,500,000 | 0.850 |

- The base territory remains the same.
- Exposures are homogeneous within each territory.
- The full credibility standard = 45,000 exposures.
- Partial credibility is determined by the square root rule.
- Complement of credibility is equal to normalized current territorial relativities.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate the credibility weighted territorial relativities using the pure premium approach.

### Answer
Territory 1: 1.226. Territory 2: 1.000. Territory 3: 0.780.

### Explanation
Everything in this calculation has to be kept on a **common basis**. The indicated relativities come out of the data normalized to the exposure-weighted average; the current relativities are stated relative to the base territory. Mixing the two — credibility-weighting a normalized indication against a raw current relativity — is the error the examiners called out, so the current relativities are normalized first and the finished result is rebased to the base territory at the end.

**Step 1 — pure premiums and indicated relativities.**

$$\text{PP}_{\text{total}} = \frac{3{,}000{,}000 + 4{,}000{,}000 + 1{,}500{,}000}{30{,}000 + 50{,}000 + 25{,}000} = \frac{8{,}500{,}000}{105{,}000} = 80.95$$

| Territory | Pure premium | Indicated relativity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $3{,}000{,}000/30{,}000 = 100.00$ | $100.00/80.95 = 1.235$ |
| 2 | $4{,}000{,}000/50{,}000 = 80.00$ | $80.00/80.95 = 0.988$ |
| 3 | $1{,}500{,}000/25{,}000 = 60.00$ | $60.00/80.95 = 0.741$ |

**Step 2 — partial credibility by the square root rule**, capped at 1.000:

$$Z = \min\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{45{,}000}},\ 1\right)$$

| Territory | Exposures | $Z$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30,000 | $\sqrt{30{,}000/45{,}000} = 0.816$ |
| 2 | 50,000 | $\sqrt{50{,}000/45{,}000} = 1.054 \rightarrow 1.000$ |
| 3 | 25,000 | $\sqrt{25{,}000/45{,}000} = 0.745$ |

**Step 3 — normalize the current relativities.** The exposure-weighted average current relativity is

$$\overline{R}_{\text{curr}} = \frac{30{,}000(1.100) + 50{,}000(1.000) + 25{,}000(0.850)}{105{,}000} = \frac{104{,}250}{105{,}000} = 0.993$$

| Territory | Current relativity | Normalized current relativity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.100 | $1.100/0.993 = 1.108$ |
| 2 | 1.000 | $1.000/0.993 = 1.007$ |
| 3 | 0.850 | $0.850/0.993 = 0.856$ |

**Step 4 — credibility-weight**, normalized indication against normalized complement:

$$R^{cw} = Z\,R^{ind} + (1-Z)\,R^{norm}_{\text{curr}}$$

| Territory | Calculation | $R^{cw}$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $0.816(1.235) + 0.184(1.108)$ | 1.212 |
| 2 | $1.000(0.988) + 0.000(1.007)$ | 0.988 |
| 3 | $0.745(0.741) + 0.255(0.856)$ | 0.770 |

**Step 5 — rebase to the base territory.** Territory 2 carries a current relativity of 1.000, so it is the base and the base territory does not change. Divide through by Territory 2's credibility-weighted relativity:

| Territory | Calculation | Relativity at base territory |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1.212/0.988$ | **1.226** |
| 2 | $0.988/0.988$ | **1.000** |
| 3 | $0.770/0.988$ | **0.780** |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate territorial relativities by the pure premium approach: pure premium by territory, partial credibility from the square root rule, credibility-weighted relativities against the normalized current relativities as the complement, and a final rebasing of the result to the base territory.

Common mistakes included miscalculating the normalized current relativities, and credibility-weighting indicated pure premium relativities balanced to the base territory against normalized current relativities balanced to the average — mixing two different bases inside one weighted average.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Determine the percent change by territory, assuming the indicated relativities are to be adopted and no overall premium change is desired.

### Answer
Territory 1: +9.4%. Territory 2: -1.9%. Territory 3: -10.0%.

### Explanation
Changing the relativities alone does not leave premium where it was — the new set collects more than the old set — so the base rate has to absorb an **off-balance** that pulls the overall change back to zero.

**Step 1 — indicated change by territory**, new relativity over current relativity:

| Territory | Calculation | Indicated change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1.226/1.100 - 1$ | +11.5% |
| 2 | $1.000/1.000 - 1$ | 0.0% |
| 3 | $0.780/0.850 - 1$ | -8.3% |

**Step 2 — the overall effect of adopting them.** Premium is proportional to exposures times relativity, so the weights are **premium** weights (exposures $\times$ current relativity), not exposures alone:

$$\frac{\sum n_i R^{new}_i}{\sum n_i R^{curr}_i} = \frac{30{,}000(1.226) + 50{,}000(1.000) + 25{,}000(0.780)}{30{,}000(1.100) + 50{,}000(1.000) + 25{,}000(0.850)} = \frac{106{,}280}{104{,}250} = 1.019$$

Adopting the new relativities would raise premium 1.9%. The same 1.9% falls out of premium-weighting the individual changes:

$$\frac{33{,}000(0.115) + 50{,}000(0.000) + 21{,}250(-0.083)}{104{,}250} = 1.9\%$$

**Step 3 — offset the base rate and restate the changes.**

$$\text{Offset} = \frac{1}{1.019} = 0.981$$

| Territory | Calculation | Change with off-balance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1.115/1.019 - 1$ | **+9.4%** |
| 2 | $1.000/1.019 - 1$ | **-1.9%** |
| 3 | $0.917/1.019 - 1$ | **-10.0%** |

Check: the new relativities after the offset are $1.100(1.094) = 1.203$, $1.000(0.981) = 0.981$ and $0.850(0.900) = 0.765$, and $30{,}000(1.203) + 50{,}000(0.981) + 25{,}000(0.765) = 104{,}250$ — exactly the current premium basis, so the change is revenue neutral.

*Alternatively*, the report's other accepted sample takes the off-balance as the **exposure**-weighted average of the territorial changes, $[30{,}000(11.5\%) + 50{,}000(0\%) + 25{,}000(-8.3\%)]/105{,}000 = 1.3\%$, an offset of $1/1.013 = 0.987$, and final changes of $+10.0\%$, $-1.3\%$ and $-9.5\%$. It is the same mechanism with lighter weights; because it ignores the current relativity in the weighting, it leaves about 0.6% of premium on the table rather than landing exactly on revenue neutrality.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the indicated factor change by territory, use those results to determine the base rate offset needed for a revenue-neutral overall change, and convert back to a final percentage change by territory.

Common mistakes included skipping the offset entirely and simply dividing the part a relativities by the current relativities; calculating the offset from either the current relativities or the indicated relativities alone rather than both; and computing the offset but never converting the result back into a percentage change by territory.

## Part c (0.75 points)

Briefly discuss three reasons why proposed rate changes might deviate from indicated rate changes.

### Explanation
Three reasons, each tying the departure to a constraint on what can actually be filed rather than to a defect in the indication:

- **Regulatory constraints.** Regulators may cap the size of an increase (or a decrease) a filing may take at once, or may disallow a rating variable outright, so the proposed change is capped below the indication.
- **Marketing and competitive pressure.** A large increase invites non-renewal and loss of market share, and a large decrease may leave rates below competitors' for no gain, so the insurer dampens the change and takes it over several filings.
- **Operational constraints.** Systems may not support a new rating variable or a new relativity structure, so the proposal is limited to what can be implemented and administered.

Other answers accepted: judgment applied to a volatile line where the data is thin and the raw indication swings implausibly; a long-term pricing view that accepts inadequate rates on new business because renewal business is more profitable (lifetime value); and a decision to close the gap through tighter underwriting guidelines instead of rate.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to state, in general terms, why a proposed change might deviate from the indicated change — regulatory, competitive/marketing, operational or judgmental constraints on what the insurer will file. Three distinct reasons were needed for full credit, and the list of acceptable answers was not exhaustive, provided each was adequately supported.

A common mistake was explaining why *actual* performance could differ from indicated performance, which is a question about the accuracy of the indication rather than about the decision to depart from it.
