A personal auto insurer orders a consumer report on an applicant and, based partly on the credit information in it, places her in a rating tier with a higher premium. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, which of the following must the insurer do?

- A) Give her the reporting agency's name and say the agency did not make the decision
- B) Obtain her written consent before ordering the report for underwriting
- C) Report the rating decision to the state insurance commissioner within 72 hours
- D) Offer her the premium she would have paid if credit had not been used

## Explanation

Charging more for insurance because of information in a consumer report is an **adverse action** under the FCRA (15 U.S.C. §1681a(k)). Section 1681m(a) then requires the insurer to notify the consumer, disclose the credit score used, give the reporting agency's name, address and telephone number with a statement that the agency did not make the decision, and tell her of her rights to a free copy of the report within 60 days and to dispute its accuracy. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — underwriting insurance involving the consumer is itself a permissible purpose for obtaining a report (§1681b(a)(3)(C)); the FCRA does not require her written consent for it.
- **C** — a 72-hour notice to the commissioner is the Data Security Model Law's rule for cybersecurity events, not a rating decision.
- **D** — re-rating without credit is an exception some state credit laws grant for extraordinary life circumstances; it is not an FCRA requirement.
