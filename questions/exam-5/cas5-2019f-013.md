Given the following information:

| Policy Limit | Claims | % of Claims at Policy Limit |
|---|---|---|
| \$50,000 | 145 | 100% |
| \$100,000 | 550 | 60% |
| \$200,000 | 875 | 40% |

- All claim payments are either 50% of the policy limit or 100% of the policy limit.
- \$50,000 is the basic limit.

Calculate the indicated increased limit factor for the \$200,000 limit.

### Answer
2.646

### Explanation
**Step 1 — recover the claim sizes.** Every payment is either half the limit or the full limit, so each policy-limit group splits into two known claim sizes:

| Policy Limit | Claims at 100% of limit | Claims at 50% of limit |
|---|---|---|
| \$50,000 | $145 \times 100\% = 145$ at \$50,000 | $0$ at \$25,000 |
| \$100,000 | $550 \times 60\% = 330$ at \$100,000 | $550 \times 40\% = 220$ at \$50,000 |
| \$200,000 | $875 \times 40\% = 350$ at \$200,000 | $875 \times 60\% = 525$ at \$100,000 |

**Step 2 — build the limited average severity layer by layer.** The ILF is

$$ILF(200{,}000) = \frac{LAS(200{,}000)}{LAS(50{,}000)}, \qquad LAS(200{,}000) = LAS(0\text{–}50k) + LAS(50k\ xs\ 50k) + LAS(100k\ xs\ 100k)$$

The key restriction is **censoring**: a policy with a \$50,000 limit can never produce a loss above \$50,000, so its data says nothing about higher layers and must be excluded from them. Each layer uses every policy limit **at or above the top of that layer**, and no others.

**Layer \$0–\$50,000** — all three groups are usable, and every claim in the table is at least \$50,000, so each contributes the full \$50,000:

$$LAS(0\text{–}50k) = \frac{(145 + 550 + 875)(50{,}000)}{145 + 550 + 875} = \$50{,}000$$

**Layer \$50,000 excess of \$50,000** — only the \$100,000 and \$200,000 policies (1,425 claims). Contribution is $\min(\text{loss}, 100{,}000) - 50{,}000$:

- \$100,000 policies: the 330 claims at \$100,000 contribute \$50,000 each; the 220 at \$50,000 contribute \$0.
- \$200,000 policies: all 875 claims reach at least \$100,000, so every one contributes the full \$50,000.

$$LAS(50k\ xs\ 50k) = \frac{(330 + 875)(50{,}000)}{550 + 875} = \frac{60{,}250{,}000}{1{,}425} = \$42{,}280.70$$

**Layer \$100,000 excess of \$100,000** — only the \$200,000 policies (875 claims). Contribution is $\min(\text{loss}, 200{,}000) - 100{,}000$:

- 350 claims at \$200,000 contribute \$100,000 each; the 525 at \$100,000 contribute \$0.

$$LAS(100k\ xs\ 100k) = \frac{350(100{,}000)}{875} = \$40{,}000$$

**Step 3 — combine.**

$$LAS(200{,}000) = 50{,}000 + 42{,}280.70 + 40{,}000 = \$132{,}280.70$$

$$ILF(200{,}000) = \frac{132{,}280.70}{50{,}000} = 2.646$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to work with the censored data, using as much data as was available at each limit, to calculate limited average severities and from them the increased limit factor. The claim sizes had to be split correctly for each set of policies, and three separate LAS amounts had to be calculated — each using data from every policy limit equal to or above that layer — then combined using the probability that losses exceed the limit.

Common mistakes included not using data from multiple policy limits for each LAS where it was available; calculating $LAS(150k\ xs\ 50k)$ as a single layer instead of splitting it; using censored data from policy limits below the layer being measured; and double-counting the probability terms.
