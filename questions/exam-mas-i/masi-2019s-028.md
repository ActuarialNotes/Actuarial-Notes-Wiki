Determine which one of the following statements about ridge regression is false.

- A) As the tuning parameter $\lambda \to \infty$, the coefficients tend to zero.
- B) The ridge regression coefficients can be calculated by determining the coefficients $\hat{\beta}_1^R, \hat{\beta}_2^R, \ldots, \hat{\beta}_p^R$ that minimize: $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_j x_{ij}\right)^2 + \lambda\sum_{i=1}^{p}\beta_j^2$
- C) Unlike standard least squares coefficients, ridge regression coefficients are not scale equivariant.
- D) The shrinkage penalty is applied to all the coefficient estimates except for the intercept.
- E) Ridge regression shrinks the coefficient estimates, which has the benefit of reducing the bias.

## Explanation

Ridge regression trades bias for variance: shrinking the coefficients makes the fit less flexible, which lowers variance at the cost of *increased* bias.

**A is true.** As $\lambda \to \infty$ the penalty $\lambda\sum_j \beta_j^2$ dominates the objective, and the coefficient estimates are driven toward zero.

**B describes the ridge objective.** Ridge coefficients minimize the residual sum of squares plus the penalty, $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j x_{ij}\right)^2 + \lambda\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2$, which is what the statement intends.

**C is true.** Least squares coefficients are scale equivariant: multiplying $X_j$ by a constant $c$ divides $\hat{\beta}_j$ by $c$ and leaves $X_j\hat{\beta}_j$ unchanged. Because the ridge penalty acts on the size of the coefficients, rescaling a predictor changes the ridge fit itself — which is why predictors are standardized first.

**D is true.** The penalty sum runs over $\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_p$ only; the intercept, which just measures the mean response when the predictors are zero, is not shrunk.

**E is false.** Shrinkage *increases* bias. Its benefit is the reduction in variance, which improves test error when that reduction outweighs the added bias.

The false statement is **E**.

*Note: the CAS answer key accepted both **B** and **E** for this question.* B was also credited because the formula as printed has index errors — the inner sum runs from $j = 1$ to $n$ rather than $p$, and the penalty is summed over $i$ while its term is $\beta_j^2$ — so, read literally, it is not the ridge objective.
