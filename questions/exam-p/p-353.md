A community college provides life insurance to its employees. The amount of insurance, $X$, of a randomly selected employee is modeled by a distribution with density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{8}{x^3}, & \text{for } x > 2 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $X$ is measured in tens of thousands.

Calculate the probability that an employee is insured for no more than 30,000, given that the employee is insured for at least 25,000.

- A) $0.20$
- B) $0.31$
- C) $0.44$
- D) $0.64$
- E) $0.69$

## Explanation

Because $X$ is in tens of thousands, 30,000 is $x = 3$ and 25,000 is $x = 2.5$.

Integrate the density to get the cumulative distribution function:

$$
\begin{align*}
F(x) &= \int_2^x 8t^{-3}\,dt \\
     &= -4t^{-2}\Big|_2^x \\
     &= 1 - 4x^{-2}
\end{align*}
$$

Evaluate it at the two points:

$$
\begin{align*}
F(3)   &= 1 - \frac{4}{9} = 0.5556 \\
F(2.5) &= 1 - \frac{4}{6.25} = 0.3600
\end{align*}
$$

Now apply the definition of conditional probability. The event $\{X \leq 3\} \cap \{X \geq 2.5\}$ is just $\{2.5 \leq X \leq 3\}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \leq 3 \mid X \geq 2.5] &= \frac{F(3) - F(2.5)}{1 - F(2.5)} \\
                            &= \frac{0.5556 - 0.3600}{1 - 0.3600} \\
                            &= \frac{0.1956}{0.6400} \\
                            &= \mathbf{0.3056}
\end{align*}
$$

which rounds to 0.31. Choice (D) is the unconditional $P[X \leq 3] = 0.5556$ mistaken for the answer, or the $0.64$ denominator read off on its own — the conditioning is what the question is testing, so the division by $P[X \geq 2.5]$ cannot be skipped.
