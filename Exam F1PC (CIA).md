# Exam F1PC
**F1PC – Pricing** is an exam of the property and casualty track of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Fellowship (FCIA) pathway.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The aim of this exam is for candidates to develop the following skills:
>
> - Understand the main principles and techniques of pricing that are relevant to P&C insurance (including reinsurance), including techniques to estimate the key components of a technical price.
>
> - Apply these principles and techniques within the context of P&C insurance.
>
> - Understand how pricing links to wider business processes (e.g., business planning, estimating unpaid claims, and capital setting.
>
> - Create and evaluate hypothetical scenarios, including using judgement to assess the implications of possible actions and to develop appropriate proposals or recommendations relating to pricing P&C insurance products.
>
> Exams are administered as an open-book, four-hour exam requiring analysis in the context of a problem and submission of written responses to specific questions. Candidates will complete the exam through the CIA’s learning management system. The ranges of weights (expressed in parentheses) attached to the examination topics further below apply to most of exams administered. Candidates should also recognize that questions often cover multiple learning objectives, including communication.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Principal terms {0%}
>
> 1. Define the [[Principal|principal]] terms used in pricing P&C [[Insurance Policy|insurance products]]. (A1)

> [!example]- Regulations and applicable standards {15%}
>
> 1. Apply relevant legislation and regulations applicable to actuarial pricing, including [[Automobile Insurance Legislation|automobile insurance legislation]] in Ontario and Alberta, [[Rate Filing|rate filing]] requirements, and [[Residual Market Mechanism|residual market mechanisms]]. (A3)
> 2. Apply actuarial [[Professional Responsibilities of the Actuary|professionalism requirements]] applicable to pricing analyses. (B3)

> [!example]- Data {10%}
>
> 1. Differentiate the types of data (including [[Claim|claims]] and [[Expenses|expenses]]) and information required for pricing (including analyses of aggregate [[On-Leveling|rate level]], classification, and [[Large Account|large account]]), their sources, and main uses.(A4)
> 2. Apply the concepts of [[Homogeneity|homogeneity]] and [[Credibility|credibility]] in selecting data for pricing analyses. (B3)
> 3. Understand the implications to data requirements of different characteristics of insurance (e.g., [[Long Tail|long tail]] vs. [[Short Tail|short tail]], [[Low Frequency-high Severity|low frequency/high severity]] vs. [[High Frequency-low Severity Line|high frequency/low severity lines]] of business). (B2)
> 4. Evaluate the influence of the [[Insurer's Internal Environment|insurer’s internal environment]] (such as changes in operations or information technology) on data used for pricing analyses. (B5)
> 5. Evaluate the influence of [[External Environment|external environments]] (such as economic and judicial decisions and environmental changes) on data used for pricing analyses. (B5)
> 6. Understand the possible causes of [[Data Error|data errors]] and the effects of [[Inadequate Data|inadequate data]] and information. (B2)
> 7. Create appropriate [[Data-Validation Process|data-validation processes]]. (C6)

> [!example]- Catastrophe modelling {5%}
>
> 1. Describe the practical considerations involved in [[Catastrophe Modelling|catastrophe modelling]]. (C2)
> 2. Describe a catastrophe model, including the basic structure, key perils modelled, components (including [[Event Hazard|event hazard]], [[Exposure Base|exposure]], vulnerability, and financial analysis) and outputs (including [[Event Distribution|event distributions]] and simulations), and their uses. (B2)
> 3. Apply outputs from the [[Catastrophe-Modelling Process|catastrophe-modelling process]] to the [[Overall Rate Level Indication|overall rate level indication]]. (C3)

> [!example]- Pricing – overall rate level indication {25%}
>
> 1. Calculate and evaluate the [[Overall Rate Level Indication|overall rate level indication]] using the following techniques: (C5)
>    - a. [[Loss Ratio|Loss ratio]];
>    - b. [[Pure Premium|Pure premium]];
>    - c. [[Frequency Severity|Frequency severity]]; and
>    - d. [[Loss Curve|Loss curve]].
> 2. For each of the above techniques:
>    - a. Understand the assumptions and mechanics, including the application of [[Credibility|credibility]]. (C2)
>    - b. Analyze assumption changes and determine their influence on the results of the [[Overall Rate Level Indication|overall rate level indication]]. (C4)
>    - c. Evaluate the technique with varying [[Data Quality|data quality]] and completeness, and in different situations. (D5)
>    - d. Describe the strengths and limitations of each technique and the impact of these limitations on results. (D2)
>    - e. Select the appropriate technique and assumptions given a specific use case. (D5)
> 3. Define and calculate various components of a [[Technical Premium|technical premium]], including: (C3)
>    - a. Expected [[Claim|claims]] and adjustment [[Expenses|expenses]];
>    - b. [[Expense Loading|Expense loading]](s);
>    - c. [[Reinsurance Cost|Reinsurance costs]] and benefits;
>    - d. [[Profit Margin|Profit margin]];
>    - e. [[Investment Return|Investment return]]; and
>    - f. [[Regulatory Capital|Capital requirements]] and [[Return on Capital|return on capital]].
> 4. Create [[Experience Investigation|experience investigations]] and evaluate the results considering: (D6)
>    - a. [[Data Inaccuracy|Data inaccuracy]], scarcity, or incompleteness;
>    - b. [[Varied Exposure|Varied exposure]] measures;
>    - c. [[Risk Relativity|Risk relativities]];
>    - d. [[Seasonality]] of [[Claim|claims]];
>    - e. [[Net Margin|Net margin]] changes;
>    - f. [[Large Loss|Large losses]] and [[Catastrophe Event|catastrophe events]]; and
>    - g. Analysis by peril or type of [[Claim|claim]].
> 5. [[Adapt Pricing|Adapt pricing]] techniques to allow for [[Frequency|frequency]] and [[Severity Distribution|severity distributions]]. (C3)
>    - a. Reflect the differences between short-tail vs. [[Long-Tail Business|long-tail business]] and occurrence vs. [[Claims Made Coverage|claims-made coverages]] in pricing analyses (C3).
> 6. Respond to practical considerations, including operational and [[Regulatory Constraint|regulatory constraints]], that influence [[Insurance Pricing|insurance pricing]]. (C5)
> 7. Evaluate how the pricing of [[Competitor Products Influence|competitor products influences]] price setting. (B5)

> [!example]- Pricing – segmentation {25%}
>
> 1. Understand how the concepts of segmentation, [[Adverse Selection|adverse selection]], [[Product Hierarchy|product hierarchy]], and [[Regulations Influence Pricing|regulations influence pricing]]. (B2)
> 2. Demonstrate the need to price for different levels of [[Risk|risk]] and the consequences for [[Portfolio Performance|portfolio performance]] if [[No Risk-Based Pricing|no risk-based pricing]] is employed. (C3)
> 3. Apply and evaluate different pricing techniques for segmentation analysis, including: (C5)
>    - a. Simple tabular analysis.
>    - b. [[Generalized Linear Model|Generalized linear models]] ([[Generalized Linear Model|GLMs]]); and
>    - c. Machine-learning models and [[Neural Network|neural networks]].
> 4. Describe [[Data Mining|data mining]] and non-parametric approaches. (C2)
> 5. For each of the techniques in 6.3:
>    - a. Understand the assumptions and mechanics of the technique, including the application of [[Credibility|credibility]] and off-balance considerations. (C2)
>    - b. Validate the underlying data and the output of the model or technique. (C5)
>    - c. Evaluate the appropriateness and limitations of various segmentation techniques for a given use case, including the application of a credibility-weighting technique. (D5)
> 6. For [[Generalized Linear Model|GLMs]] and [[Multivariate Modelling|multivariate modelling]]:
>    - a. Apply [[Generalized Linear Model|GLMs]] to the rating of [[Personal-Lines Business|personal-lines business]] and [[Small Commercial Risk|small commercial risks]]; (C3)
>    - b. Understand the different types of multivariate models; (C2)
>    - c. Apply multivariate models in pricing; (C3)
>    - d. Select the appropriate [[Error Structure|error structures]] and [[Link Function|link functions]]; (C4)
>    - e. Analyze [[Residual Chart|residual charts]], [[Parameter Estimate|parameter estimates]], and other [[Generalized Linear Model|GLM]] and multivariate [[Model Output|model output]]; and (C4)
>    - f. Analyze and [[Correct Error|correct errors]] for given [[Generalized Linear Model|GLM]] and multivariate [[Model Output|model outputs]]. (C4)
> 7. Apply calculations relating to a range of [[Pricing Matter|pricing matters]], including:
>    - a. Allowance for excesses and [[Deductible|deductibles]]; (C3)
>    - b. Treatment of [[Granular Rating|granular rating]] factors such as geography, vehicle characteristics, and occupations; and (C3)
>    - c. Treatment of [[Natural Hazard|natural hazards]] and catastrophes. (C3)
> 8. Adopt a pricing technique to allow for government-mandated or [[Monitored Rate|monitored rates]]. (C3)
> 9. Recognize and allow for the key features that relate to [[Long-Tail Business Pricing|long-tail business pricing]], including:
>    - a. [[Linkage]] with [[Unpaid Claims|outstanding claims]] analysis; (C3)
>    - b. [[Exposure Base|Exposure]] analysis; (C3)
>    - c. Setting and application of economic assumptions; and (C3)
>    - d. Other [[Product-Specific Feature|product-specific features]]. (C3)

> [!example]- Pricing – large account pricing {5%}
>
> 1. Describe the particular considerations when [[Pricing Large Commercial Risk|pricing large commercial risks]], including considerations related to [[Credibility|credibility]]. (B2)
> 2. Describe alternative approaches to [[Pricing Large Commercial Risk|pricing large commercial risks]]. (A2)
> 3. Apply appropriate pricing techniques for [[Large Account|large accounts]]. (C3)
> 4. Evaluate the appropriateness and limitations of various pricing techniques for a given scenario, product, or set of data. ([[D5]])
> 5. Perform and evaluate calculations relating to a range of [[Pricing Matter|pricing matters]], including:
>    - a. Allowance for aggregate [[Deductible|deductibles]] and limits; (C5)
>    - b. Allowance for [[Loss-Responsive Policy Condition|loss-responsive policy conditions]]; (C5)
>    - c. Treatment of [[Self-Insured Retention|self-insured retentions]]; (C5)
>    - d. [[Claims-sharing Arrangement|Claims-sharing arrangements]]; and (C5)
>    - e. Incorporation of [[Underwriting View|underwriting views]] in a [[Rigorous Pricing|rigorous pricing]] framework. (C5)

> [!example]- Pricing – reinsurance {10%}
>
> 1. Describe the similarities and differences between [[Pricing Direct|pricing direct]] and [[Reinsurance Business|reinsurance business]]. (B2)
> 2. Calculate appropriate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for each of the following [[Types of Reinsurance|types of reinsurance]]:
>    - a. [[Proportional Reinsurance|Proportional reinsurance]]; (C3)
>    - b. [[Non-proportional Reinsurance|Non-proportional reinsurance]]; (C3)
>    - c. [[Property Catastrophe Reinsurance|Property catastrophe reinsurance]]; and (C3)
>    - d. [[Stop Loss|Stop loss]]. (C3)
> 3. Describe the data required to determine appropriate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for each of the above [[Types of Reinsurance|types of reinsurance]]. (A2)

> [!example]- Portfolio analytics {5%}
>
> 1. Create a [[Portfolio Analytics|portfolio analytics]] framework for a P&C [[Insurer|insurer]]. (C6)
> 2. Create framework(s) to monitor: (C6)
>    - a. [[Exposure Base|Exposures]] (e.g., retention, closing, [[Mix of Business|mix of business]]).
>    - b. [[Pricing Action|Pricing actions]] (e.g., product and [[Underwriting Initiative|underwriting initiatives]]).
>    - c. [[Claims Experience|Claims experience]]; and
>    - d. Compliance.
> 3. Understand the rationale and practical considerations that apply in measuring price elasticity and [[Cross-Selling Opportunity|cross-selling opportunities]]. (B2)
> 4. Understand the rationale and practical considerations that apply in [[Exposure Base|exposure]] management. (B2)
