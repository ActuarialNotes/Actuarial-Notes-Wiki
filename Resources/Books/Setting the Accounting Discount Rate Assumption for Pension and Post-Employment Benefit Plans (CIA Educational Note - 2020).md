The second revision, dated December 2020 (document 220174), of a Canadian Institute of Actuaries educational note on selecting the discount rate for a Canadian defined benefit pension or post-employment plan under accounting standards. It highlights considerations for an actuary advising a plan sponsor and describes a revised approach for extrapolating the corporate Aa yield curve beyond 10 years that the committee believed robust in a variety of economic environments.

## Memorandum
- The 2011 educational note extrapolated the corporate Aa curve using Aa-rated Canadian provincial bonds plus a spread adjustment for the additional risk of Aa corporate bonds; the number of Aa-rated corporate bonds with maturities over 10 years then fell significantly, which led to the June 2018 revision.
- Volatility in March 2020 led the committee to conduct another review of the extrapolation approach in 2020; the note describes the revised approach, to be used by Fiera Capital Corporation to publish a monthly corporate Aa spot curve beginning in December 2020.

## 1 Introduction
- Pension plan sponsors are responsible for selecting the assumptions used to value plan liabilities, and the [[Discount Rate|discount rate]] is one of the most material.
- Many accounting standards provide that the discount rate can be determined with reference to high-quality corporate bond yields, including the CPA Canada Handbook, U.S. accounting standards (codification 715.30.35-43 and 44) and the International Accounting Standards.

## 9 Illustration of Developing the Yield Curve in Accordance with Approach B
- Select suitable Aa-rated corporate, Aa-rated provincial and A-rated corporate bonds.
- Fit curves to the Aa-provincial bonds and A-rated corporate bonds across all terms to maturity.
- Calculate the spread between corporate Aa and corporate A, and between corporate Aa and provincial Aa.
- For every Aa-rated provincial bond with a maturity greater than 10.5 years, calculate a provincial spread adjustment.
- Fit a curve to the Aa-rated corporate bonds across all terms and the provincial bonds of maturities greater than 10.5 years with the provincial yields adjusted upward by the provincial spread adjustment.
