A worker is injured in 2025 by a press that its manufacturer sold in 2012. The state has a two-year statute of limitations for personal injury, running from the injury, and a ten-year statute of repose for products, running from the sale. The worker sues the manufacturer three months after the injury. What is the result?

- A) The suit is timely under both statutes, because it was filed promptly after the injury
- B) The suit is barred by the statute of limitations
- C) The suit is timely, because the repose period runs only from when the defect was discovered
- D) The suit is barred by the statute of repose, although it is timely under the statute of limitations

## Explanation

The two statutes run from different events. The limitations period runs from the injury, so a suit three months later is well within two years. The repose period runs from the manufacturer's act, the 2012 sale, and expired in 2022, before the worker was even hurt. A repose statute can bar a claim before the injury occurs, and its period is not extended by tolling, so the suit is **barred by repose**.

- **A** applies only the statute of limitations. Both time limits must be met.
- **B** gets the limitations analysis wrong: three months is inside two years.
- **C** gives a statute of repose a discovery rule. Repose is measured from the defendant's act, regardless of injury or discovery.
