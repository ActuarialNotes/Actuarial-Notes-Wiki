Three hierarchal Bayesian regression models, Model I, Model II, and Model III, are fit to the same dataset. The dataset contains information that relates whether or not an insurance policy sale was completed and includes four potential covariates:

1. region, i, of the sales office where $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 7\}$
2. The type of product, j, sold where $j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 6\}$
3. The experience level of the insurance sales representative
4. Whether the sale attempt was made as part of a bundle of other products or made in isolation

All three models' fits include experience and bundle as covariates. Model II also includes region and Model III also includes both region and product.

Model III is specified as follows:

$$
\begin{align*}
y_i &\sim \text{Bernoulli}(p_i) \\
\text{logit}(p_i) &= b + b_{region[i]} + b_{product[j]} + (b_1 + b_2 \ast x_{bundle}) \ast x_{experience} \\
b_{region} &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ \sigma_{region}) \\
b_{product} &\sim \text{Normal}(0,\ \sigma_{product}) \\
b &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 10) \\
b_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 10) \\
b_2 &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 10) \\
\sigma_{region} &\sim \text{HalfCauchy}(0, 1) \\
\sigma_{product} &\sim \text{HalfCauchy}(0, 1)
\end{align*}
$$

You are given the following comparison between Model I and Model II.

| Model | WAIC | pWAIC | dWAIC | Weight | SE | dSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model I | 682.4 | 2.9 | 151.2 | 0.0 | 9.45 | 18.59 |
| Model II | 531.2 | 8.1 | 0.0 | 1.0 | 19.43 | NA |

You are also provided the posterior means and 90% Bayesian credible intervals for the parameters in Model III in the graphic below.

![Interval plot of posterior means with 90% credible intervals for Model III. The seven b_region parameters have wide intervals spread from about -2.5 to +6; the six b_product parameters all sit close to zero with very narrow intervals; b, b1 and b2 have moderate intervals; sigma_region is around 2 with a wide interval and sigma_product is small with a narrow interval](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q20-model3-intervals.png)

Determine the most likely value of pWAIC for Model III.

- A) 5.0
- B) 8.0
- C) 11.0
- D) 14.0
- E) 17.0

## Explanation

pWAIC is the **effective** number of parameters — the penalty term in WAIC — and in a hierarchical model it is smaller than the nominal count because shrinkage costs less than a free parameter.

Track how it grows across the three models:

- **Model I** carries only $b$, $b_1$ and $b_2$: three nominal parameters, and $\text{pWAIC} = 2.9$ — essentially one apiece, as expected when the priors are flat.
- **Model II** adds the seven region effects plus $\sigma_{region}$, so eight more nominal parameters. But pWAIC rises only from 2.9 to 8.1, an increase of 5.2: the hierarchical prior shrinks the region effects toward zero, so they cost less than a parameter each.
- **Model III** adds the six product effects plus $\sigma_{product}$, another seven nominal parameters. The interval plot shows how much these are shrunk: all six $b_{product}$ posteriors sit near zero with very narrow intervals, and $\sigma_{product}$ is small — far tighter than the sprawling region effects. Heavier shrinkage means a *smaller* increase in pWAIC than region produced.

So the increase over Model II's 8.1 should be positive but noticeably less than the 5.2 the regions cost:

$$
8.1 + \sim 3 \approx 11
$$

Options A and B are ruled out because pWAIC cannot fall when parameters are added, and D and E would require the product effects to cost as much as or more than the region effects, which the narrow product intervals contradict.

The answer is **C**.
