The following data shows the experience of a merit rating plan for private passenger vehicles. The merit rating plan uses multiple rating variables, including territory.

| Number of Accident-Free Years | Earned Car Years (000s) | Earned Premium (\$000s) | Number of Incurred Claims |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 or More | 250 | 500,000 | 15,000 |
| 3 and 4 | 100 | 90,000 | 13,500 |
| 1 and 2 | 80 | 60,000 | 8,000 |
| 0 | 70 | 50,000 | 10,500 |
| Total | 500 | 700,000 | 47,000 |

| Territory | Frequency | Average Premium |
|---|---|---|
| A | 0.05 | 1,500 |
| B | 0.10 | 2,000 |
| C | 0.15 | 1,250 |

## Part a (0.75 points)

Recommend and justify an exposure base for this merit rating plan.

### Explanation
Use earned car years. A premium base is appropriate only when high frequency territories are also high premium territories, and when the territorial differentials are proper. Here, territory C has the highest frequency but the lowest average premium, so earned premium would not correct the maldistribution — use earned car years instead.

Alternatively, earned premium can be defended: territory tends to be correlated with other risk characteristics, and earned premium corrects for that correlation only if high frequency territories are also high average premium territories. That does not seem to be the case here (territory C has the highest frequency but the lowest average premium), but premium could reflect the other rating variables' impact, so use earned premium as the exposure base.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to discuss that the maldistribution of rating variables can be corrected by earned premium, but only under two circumstances:

- High frequency territories are also high premium territories
- The territory differentials are properly priced

Candidates needed to use the data provided to build a case for the exposure base that they selected (premium or car years). This could be in the form of:

- Arguing that because the high frequency territories are not also high premium territories the first condition is not met and thus car years must be used
- Arguing that while the frequencies do not appear to be in-line with premiums by territory, that premium may still be a better choice as it addresses some maldistribution and should be still used as the exposure base

Common mistakes included:

- Selecting an exposure base without including the reasoning behind the selection

## Part b (0.75 points)

Calculate the relative credibility of an exposure that has been three or more years accident-free using the exposure base from part (a) above.

### Explanation
Using car years as the exposure base, compare the class accident-free for 3 or more years with the class accident-free for 1 or more years:

$$\text{3+ frequency} = \frac{13{,}500 + 15{,}000}{100 + 250} = 81.43 \qquad \text{Total frequency} = \frac{47{,}000}{500} = 94$$

$$\text{Relative frequency}_{3+} = \frac{81.43}{94} = 0.866 \qquad Z_{3+} = 1 - 0.866 = 0.134$$

$$\text{1+ frequency} = \frac{13{,}500 + 15{,}000 + 8{,}000}{250 + 100 + 80} = 84.88 \qquad \text{Relative frequency}_{1+} = \frac{84.88}{94} = 0.903 \qquad Z_{1+} = 1 - 0.903 = 0.097$$

$$\text{Relative credibility} = \frac{0.134}{0.097} = 1.38$$

Alternatively, using earned premium as the exposure base:

$$\text{3+ frequency} = \frac{13{,}500 + 15{,}000}{500{,}000 + 90{,}000} = 0.048 \qquad \text{Total frequency} = \frac{47{,}000}{700{,}000} = 0.067$$

$$\text{Relative frequency}_{3+} = \frac{0.048}{0.067} = 0.72 \qquad Z_{3+} = 1 - 0.72 = 0.28$$

$$\text{1+ frequency} = \frac{13{,}500 + 15{,}000 + 8{,}000}{500{,}000 + 90{,}000 + 60{,}000} = 0.056 \qquad \text{Relative frequency}_{1+} = \frac{0.056}{0.067} = 0.84 \qquad Z_{1+} = 1 - 0.84 = 0.16$$

$$\text{Relative credibility} = \frac{0.28}{0.16} = 1.75$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the relative credibility for the class of operators who have been accident-free for 3 or more years relative to those who have been accident-free for 1 or more years.  Full credit was given for using 0+ years accident-free instead, and full credit was given for using either car years or premium as the exposure base.
Common mistakes included:

- Failing to calculate a ratio of credibilities (many candidates simply calculated the credibility for 3 or more accident-free years)
