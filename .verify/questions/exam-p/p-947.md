## [F-001] Explanation computes E[5-T | T>2], not the expected remaining life
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-09-27T19:59Z/bfa2
- date: 2026-09-28
- severity: major
- status: open
- locus: ## Explanation, last line
- claim: $$E[5-T \mid T > 2] = 5 - 3.5 = \mathbf{1.5}$$
- evidence: Given survival to year 2, the remaining useful life is T-2, so the quantity asked is E[T-2 | T>2] = E[T | T>2] - 2 = 3.5 - 2 = 1.5; T given T>2 is uniform on [2,5] (Grinstead & Snell section 4.2, PDF p.170-171: the conditional density of a uniform variable on a sub-interval is uniform there). 5-T is the time from failure to year 5, a different variable; E[5-T | T>2] also equals 1.5 only because U[2,5] is symmetric about 3.5. For any asymmetric lifetime the explanation method gives a wrong answer, so a student copying it learns a wrong move. The keyed value 1.5 = option C is correct.
- source_rank: 3
- proposed_action: Author to replace the last line with E[T-2 | T>2] = 3.5 - 2 = 1.5.
- applied: false

## [F-002] Stem calls T the remaining useful life, then conditions on T > 2 as survival of the first 2 years
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-09-27T19:59Z/bfa2
- date: 2026-09-28
- severity: minor
- status: open
- locus: stem
- claim: A random variable $T$ represents the remaining useful life of a machine ... given that the machine has already survived the first 2 years (i.e., $T > 2$).
- evidence: If T is the remaining useful life, the expected remaining useful life given T > 2, read literally, is E[T | T>2] = (2+5)/2 = 3.5, which is not among the options (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5). The keyed 1.5 requires T to be the total lifetime from new and the quantity to be E[T-2 | T>2]. Only one option is reachable, so the answer survives, but the definition of T fights the question.
- source_rank: 5
- proposed_action: Define T as the machine's total useful life (time to failure from new) and ask for E[T-2 | T>2].
- applied: false
