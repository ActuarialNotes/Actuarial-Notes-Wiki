An insurer covers a portfolio of policies, each with a deductible of 1,000. Loss amounts are independent and uniformly distributed on the interval $(0, 5{,}000)$. A loss of 1,000 or less produces no payment.

The insurer's claims department expects to make 160 claim payments next year, that is, 160 losses that exceed the deductible.

Calculate the insurer's expected total claim payments next year.

- A) 204,800
- B) 240,000
- C) 256,000
- D) 320,000
- E) 400,000

## Explanation

The 160 is a count of **payments**, not of losses, so it pairs with the expected payment *per payment*: the expected payment given that the loss exceeds the deductible.

Given $X > 1{,}000$, the loss is uniform on $(1{,}000, 5{,}000)$, so the excess $X - 1{,}000$ is uniform on $(0, 4{,}000)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X - 1{,}000 \mid X > 1{,}000] &= \frac{4{,}000}{2} \\
                                &= 2{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\text{total}] &= 160 \times 2{,}000 \\
                &= \mathbf{320{,}000}
\end{align*}
$$

The same total comes from the loss view. With $P(X > 1{,}000) = 0.8$, the 160 payments correspond to 160 / 0.8 = 200 losses, and the expected payment per loss (zeros included) is $E[Y^L] = 0.8 \times 2{,}000 = 1{,}600$. Then 200 × 1,600 = 320,000. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** (204,800) applies the probability of a payment twice: 160 × 0.8 × 1,600. The 160 has already been thinned to the losses that pay.
- **B** (240,000) subtracts the deductible from the *unconditional* mean loss, 160 × (2,500 - 1,000). For losses that exceed the deductible, the mean loss is 3,000, not 2,500.
- **C** (256,000) multiplies the count of payments by the expected payment per *loss*, 160 × 1,600. That mean includes the zero payments on the 40 losses below the deductible, which the 160 already excludes.
- **E** (400,000) uses the mean loss, 160 × 2,500 (or grosses up to 200 losses and pairs them with the per-payment mean, 200 × 2,000). Either way, a count and a mean from different bases are mixed.
