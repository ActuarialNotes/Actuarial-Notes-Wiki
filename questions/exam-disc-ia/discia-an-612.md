A store is insured under the BPP with the causes of loss special form. During a rainstorm with no damaging wind, water enters through roof flashing that has deteriorated over many years and ruins \$12,000 of ceiling tiles and stock. Why is the loss **not** covered?

- A) Wear and tear is excluded, and interior rain damage is limited without prior roof damage
- B) Rain is a named peril only under the broad form, and the store chose the special form
- C) The water exclusion removes all damage by water, whatever its source
- D) The loss is less than the coinsurance requirement, so the coinsurance condition removes it

## Explanation

Under the special form, wear and tear and deterioration are excluded causes. Separately, the form limits loss to a building's interior or contents caused by rain unless the building first sustains damage by a covered cause of loss to its roof or walls through which the rain enters. Worn flashing is neither. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Rain is not a named peril under any form; the special form is open perils and covers more than the broad form, not less.
- **C** — The water exclusion is for flood, surface water, sewer backup and underground water, not all water; a sudden discharge from a cracked system is covered.
- **D** — Coinsurance reduces a payment when the limit is too low relative to value; it does not remove a loss for being small.
