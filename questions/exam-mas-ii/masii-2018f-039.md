An actuary creates three tree-based models using bagging, boosting, and random forests. The error on a test data set, as a function of the number of trees in each model, is plotted on the graph below.

![Test error (0.25 to 0.55) against number of trees (0 to 750) for three lines, all starting near 0.5. Line I (dotted) drops fastest and flattens at about 0.40 by roughly 100 trees. Line II (dashed) drops almost as quickly and flattens at about 0.36 by roughly 150 trees. Line III (solid) declines slowly and steadily, crossing below line I near 200 trees and below line II near 280 trees, and levels off at about 0.28 from roughly 600 trees](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2018f-q39-test-error-by-trees.png)

Determine the type of model most likely to have created each of the lines on the graph.

- A) I: Boosting, II: Bagging, III: Random forest
- B) I: Bagging, II: Boosting, III: Random forest
- C) I: Bagging, II: Random forest, III: Boosting
- D) I: Random forest, II: Bagging, III: Boosting
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D).

## Explanation

The lines have two telling features: how fast each one levels off, and where it levels off.

**Bagging and random forests** average many trees grown independently on bootstrap samples. Adding more trees does not cause overfitting, so the test error falls quickly and then stays flat once there are enough trees to average out the noise. Lines I and II are these two. A random forest considers only a random subset of the predictors at each split. This decorrelates its trees, so averaging them reduces variance further than bagging does, and its error settles lower. So line I, the higher plateau, is **bagging**, and line II, the lower plateau, is the **random forest**.

**Boosting** grows trees sequentially. Each small tree is fitted to the residuals of the current model and added with a shrinkage factor, so the model learns slowly. Its error keeps declining over hundreds of trees, and here it ends below the other two. That is line III, the slow, steady decline that crosses both plateaus and settles near 0.28 after about 600 trees.

I: Bagging, II: Random forest, III: Boosting, so the answer is **C**.
