A federally-regulated Canadian P&C insurer's Dynamic Capital Adequacy Testing (DCAT) results as of December 31, 2012 for the three most adverse scenarios are shown below. Assume the Minimum Capital Test (MCT) ratio and surplus shown below reflect the most adverse results during the forecast period.

| | Base Scenario | Frequency/Severity (Loss Ratio) | Misestimation of policy liabilities (Unpaid Claims) | Premium Volume (Decrease) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MCT ratio | 229% | 157% | 137% | 200% |
| Surplus (\$000s) | \$400,000 | \$275,000 | \$240,000 | \$350,000 |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Describe what a base scenario is in the context of the DCAT.

### Explanation
The base scenario is a realistic set of assumptions used to forecast the insurer's financial position over the forecast period. Normally, the base scenario is consistent with the insurer's business plan.

Alternatively:

The base scenario is the expected scenario over the projection period, as a result, it should be similar to the business plan.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Determine whether or not the company is in "satisfactory financial condition." Briefly explain your answer.

### Explanation
The prerequisite for a satisfactory opinion is that the insurer will be able to meet its future obligations under all plausible adverse scenarios, and under the base scenario it meets the minimum regulatory (supervisory target was also accepted) capital requirement. Since surplus is greater than 0 in all scenarios, the insurer is in satisfactory financial condition.

Alternatively:

The company is in satisfactory financial condition because under all scenarios, surplus is positive (assets > liabilities). Also, under the base scenario, the MCT exceeds the supervisory target of 150%.

### Examiner Report
For part a) and b), candidates performed well and a large proportion of them got full credit.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two possible ripple effects that the actuary may consider under the Premium Volume (Decrease) scenario.

### Explanation
Any 2 of the following:

- an increase in loss ratio due to a soft market, inadequate pricing or lost business that is relatively more profitable than the retained business;
- an increase in the fixed expense ratio;
- an increase for certain types of expenses (for example: more advertising costs to counter a very aggressive competitor);
- a shift in portfolio mix since the lost business could have a much different average premium or could be primarily from a specific market segment;
- an increase in reinsurance costs as a percentage of subject premium; and
- forced sale or liquidation of assets.

Alternatively:

- Change in mix of business.
- Increase in fixed expense ratio.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two possible management actions that the actuary may recommend under the Misestimation of Policy Liabilities scenario.

### Explanation
Any 2 of the following:

- settling claims faster by minimizing litigation or fast tracking claims handling;
- reviewing reserving and claim settlement guidelines;
- implementing rate increases, where possible; and
- reviewing the target mix by line of business or jurisdiction.

Alternatively:

- Increase rates where possible.
- Review claims settling practices.

### Examiner Report
For part c) and d), many candidates failed to get full credit either by trying to guess the answer or by giving answers related to other DCAT scenarios. Most candidates managed to get at least 1 out of 2 answers correct.
