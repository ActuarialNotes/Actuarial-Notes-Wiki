A taxicab company purchases an insurance policy that covers damages from car accidents. The number of accidents on each day is Poisson distributed with mean $\lambda$, and the number of accidents on different days are mutually independent. The expected number of accidents each week is 63.

Calculate the probability that the number of accidents in one day is two or more standard deviations below the expected number of accidents in one day.

- A) $0.006$
- B) $0.021$
- C) $0.116$
- D) $0.156$
- E) $0.207$

## Explanation

Let $D$ be the number of accidents in one day. The seven days are independent with a common mean, so

$$
E(D) = \frac{63}{7} = 9
$$

For a Poisson distribution the variance equals the mean, so $\text{Var}(D) = 9$ and $\text{SD}(D) = 3$. Two standard deviations below the mean is

$$
9 - 2(3) = 3
$$

"Two or more standard deviations below" means $D \le 3$, and since $D$ is discrete the value 3 itself is included:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[D \le 3] &= \frac{e^{-9}9^0}{0!} + \frac{e^{-9}9^1}{1!} + \frac{e^{-9}9^2}{2!} + \frac{e^{-9}9^3}{3!} \\
           &= 0.000123 + 0.001111 + 0.004998 + 0.014994 \\
           &= \mathbf{0.0212}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.006, is $P[D \le 2] = 0.00623$ — the same sum with the $D = 3$ term dropped. A count exactly two standard deviations below the mean *is* two standard deviations below it, so the boundary belongs in the sum, and here that single term is more than twice the size of everything beneath it.
