This year, losses on a block of policies are uniformly distributed on the interval $(0, 10{,}000)$. For each loss, the insurer pays the amount of the loss in excess of a deductible of 1,000, up to a maximum payment of 6,000.

Next year, every loss will be 20% larger than this year, so losses will be uniformly distributed on $(0, 12{,}000)$. The deductible and the maximum payment will not change.

Calculate the percentage increase in the insurer's expected payment per loss from this year to next year.

- A) 9.0%
- B) 11.1%
- C) 20.0%
- D) 24.5%
- E) 42.9%

## Explanation

The payment on a loss $X$ is $Y = \min\big((X - 1{,}000)^+,\ 6{,}000\big)$: zero below 1,000, $X - 1{,}000$ between 1,000 and 7,000, and the maximum 6,000 above 7,000.

**This year**, $X \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 10{,}000)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y] &= \int_{1{,}000}^{7{,}000} \frac{x - 1{,}000}{10{,}000}\,dx + 6{,}000\,P(X > 7{,}000) \\
     &= \frac{6{,}000^2/2}{10{,}000} + 6{,}000(0.3) \\
     &= 1{,}800 + 1{,}800 \\
     &= 3{,}600
\end{align*}
$$

**Next year**, $X \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 12{,}000)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y] &= \int_{1{,}000}^{7{,}000} \frac{x - 1{,}000}{12{,}000}\,dx + 6{,}000\,P(X > 7{,}000) \\
     &= \frac{6{,}000^2/2}{12{,}000} + 6{,}000\left(\tfrac{5}{12}\right) \\
     &= 1{,}500 + 2{,}500 \\
     &= 4{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\frac{4{,}000}{3{,}600} - 1 = \mathbf{11.1\%}
$$

The fixed deductible would make the insurer's payment grow *faster* than the loss, but the fixed maximum payment works the other way: every loss already above 7,000 gains nothing for the insurer, and the policyholder absorbs that growth. Here the limit dominates, so the insurer's expected payment grows by less than the 20% loss inflation. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** (9.0%) applies the maximum to the *loss* before the deductible, $\min(X, 6{,}000) - 1{,}000$, giving 3,250 rising to 3,541.67. The 6,000 caps the payment, so payments run up to a loss of 7,000.
- **C** (20.0%) assumes the insurer's payment grows at the same rate as the loss. That is only true if the deductible and the maximum also rise by 20%.
- **D** (24.5%) ignores the maximum payment: $E[(X - 1{,}000)^+]$ rises from 4,050 to 5,041.67. That is the leveraged effect of a fixed deductible alone.
- **E** (42.9%) is the growth in the policyholder's expected retained amount, $E[X] - E[Y]$, from 1,400 to 2,000, not in the insurer's payment.
