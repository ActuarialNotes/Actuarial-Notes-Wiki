A start-up that rents out a new kind of personal flying drone seeks liability insurance. Admitted insurers decline it because the exposure has no loss history. Through which part of the property-casualty marketplace is the coverage most likely to be placed?

- A) The surplus lines market, through a licensed surplus lines broker
- B) The residual market, through the state's assigned risk plan
- C) The admitted market, through a direct writer's employee
- D) A state guaranty fund, which insures risks the market declines

## Explanation

Nonadmitted insurers write risks that are not available in the admitted market, and new products with no loss history are a common reason a risk ends up there. The coverage is placed by a **licensed surplus lines broker**. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — The residual market provides basic coverage that buyers are guaranteed access to, such as compulsory auto insurance, through pools or assignments of admitted insurers. A novel commercial liability exposure is not what it is for.
- **C** — The admitted insurers have already declined the risk; a direct writer is one of them.
- **D** — A guaranty fund pays claims of insolvent licensed insurers; it does not sell coverage.
