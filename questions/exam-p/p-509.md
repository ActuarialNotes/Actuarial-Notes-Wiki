A credit card protection insurance company's monthly profits are independent and normally distributed with constant mean and variance. The probability that the company earns an overall positive profit in a given year is 0.60.

Calculate the probability that the insurance company earns a positive profit in a given month.

- A) $0.51$
- B) $0.53$
- C) $0.60$
- D) $0.81$
- E) $0.96$

## Explanation

Let $\mu$ and $\sigma$ be the monthly mean and standard deviation. Over twelve independent months the mean scales by 12, but the standard deviation scales only by $\sqrt{12}$:

$$
E[\text{annual}] = 12\mu, \qquad \text{SD}[\text{annual}] = \sqrt{12}\,\sigma
$$

Neither $\mu$ nor $\sigma$ is known, but the given annual probability fixes their ratio:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.60 &= P[\text{annual} > 0] = P\left[Z > \frac{-12\mu}{\sqrt{12}\,\sigma}\right] \\
     &= P\left[Z > -\sqrt{12}\,\frac{\mu}{\sigma}\right]
\end{align*}
$$

so $\sqrt{12}(\mu/\sigma) = 0.253347$, giving $\mu/\sigma = 0.073135$.

Now apply the same relation at the monthly scale:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{monthly} > 0] &= P\left[Z > -\frac{\mu}{\sigma}\right] \\
                      &= P[Z > -0.073135] \\
                      &= \mathbf{0.529}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 0.60, assumes the monthly and annual probabilities are equal. The monthly figure is barely above one half because aggregating twelve months multiplies the signal-to-noise ratio $\mu/\sigma$ by $\sqrt{12} = 3.46$ — a slim monthly edge compounds into a much clearer annual one.
