You are given the following information:

| Model | WAIC | Effective Number of Parameters ($p_{WAIC}$) |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | 681.8 | 2.1 |
| M2 | 682.4 | 3.0 |
| M3 | 688.1 | 1.1 |

Calculate the Akaike weight for Model M2.

- A) Less than 0.25
- B) Greater than 0.25 but less than or equal to 0.35
- C) Greater than 0.35 but less than or equal to 0.45
- D) Greater than 0.45 but less than or equal to 0.55
- E) Greater than 0.55

## Explanation

An Akaike weight turns each model's WAIC into a relative probability that it makes the best out-of-sample predictions among the set. Measure each WAIC from the best (lowest) one, then normalise $e^{-\frac{1}{2}\,\text{dWAIC}}$:

$$
w_i = \frac{\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{dWAIC}_i\right)}{\sum_j \exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{dWAIC}_j\right)}, \qquad \text{dWAIC}_i = \text{WAIC}_i - \min_j \text{WAIC}_j
$$

M1 has the lowest WAIC, so the differences are 0, $682.4 - 681.8 = 0.6$ and $688.1 - 681.8 = 6.3$.

$$
\begin{align*}
\sum_j \exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{dWAIC}_j\right) &= e^{0} + e^{-0.3} + e^{-3.15} \\
&= 1 + 0.7408 + 0.0429 \\
&= 1.7837 \\
w_{M2} &= \frac{0.7408}{1.7837} \\
&= 0.4153
\end{align*}
$$

The $p_{WAIC}$ column is not needed: WAIC already includes its penalty of $2p_{WAIC}$.

This falls in $(0.35, 0.45]$, so the answer is **C**.
