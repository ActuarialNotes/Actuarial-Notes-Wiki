- claim: Losses $\sim\text{Uniform}[0,b]$, deductible $b/2$. Calculate the ratio of $\text{Var}(\text{claim payment})$ to $\text{Var}(\text{loss})$.
- evidence: SOA Exam P Sample Questions (Aug 2026 rev.) Q230, questions PDF p.98: "Under a liability insurance policy, losses are uniformly distributed on [0, b], where b is a positive constant. There is a deductible of b/2. Calculate the ratio of the variance of the claim payment (greater than or equal to zero) from a given loss to the variance of the loss." The vault drops "(greater than or equal to zero) from a given loss", the condition that makes the payment the per-loss variable (zero below the deductible). Per loss: E[C] = b/8, E[C^2] = b^2/24, Var = 5b^2/192, ratio to b^2/12 = 5:16 = key D (SOA solution p.69 computes the same 5b^2/192). The per-payment reading the abridged stem allows, X - b/2 given X > b/2 ~ U[0, b/2], has variance b^2/48 and ratio 1:4 = distractor C.
- source_rank: 1
- proposed_action: Restore the SOA wording, in particular "(greater than or equal to zero) from a given loss" and "where b is a positive constant".
