A retailer offers two payment plans:

- i) A 2% discount if paid within 10 calendar days after purchase
- ii) Pay the full amount on the 30th day after purchase

Assume a 365-day year.

The implied annual effective yield the buyer is charged for delaying payment from day 1 to day 30 is $i$.

Calculate $i$.

- A) 24.3%
- B) 26.8%
- C) 27.9%
- D) 36.5%
- E) 44.6%

## Explanation

The discount is available until day 10, so a buyer who takes it pays 0.98 per unit of price on day 10. A buyer who delays pays the full amount of 1 on day 30, which is $30 - 10 = 20$ days later. The question says the delay runs "from day 1", but the SOA's solution, and the keyed answer, measure it from day 10, the last day the discount is available; a 29-day delay gives 28.95%, which is not among the options. The implied annual effective rate $i$ is the rate at which 0.98 grows to 1 over 20 days of a 365-day year:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.98(1+i)^{20/365} &= 1 \\
(1+i)^{20/365} &= \frac{1}{0.98} \\
i &= \left(\frac{1}{0.98}\right)^{365/20} - 1 \\
  &= 1.445853 - 1 \\
  &= 0.445853 \\
  &\approx 44.6\%
\end{align*}
$$
