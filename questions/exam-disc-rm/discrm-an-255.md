An insurer buys a \$50 million industry loss warranty in reinsurance form. It pays the full limit if U.S. hurricane industry losses reach \$30 billion and the insurer's own loss exceeds \$1 million. A hurricane causes \$26 billion of industry losses and \$400 million of losses to the insurer. What does the warranty pay, and why?

- A) \$50 million, because the insurer's own loss is far above \$1 million
- B) \$400 million, because the warranty indemnifies the insurer's actual loss
- C) \$4 million, the share of the limit proportional to the industry loss
- D) Nothing, because the industry trigger was not reached

## Explanation

Both triggers must be met. The industry loss of \$26 billion is below the \$30 billion trigger, so the warranty pays nothing, however large the insurer's own loss. That gap between the index and the insurer's own experience is basis risk, the price of the cheap, standardized cover an ILW provides. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Meets only the own-loss trigger. In reinsurance form, the low own-loss trigger supplies the indemnity, but the industry trigger still has to be met.
- **B** — An ILW pays a fixed limit, not the insurer's actual loss.
- **C** — There is no proportional payment; the warranty pays the full limit or nothing.
