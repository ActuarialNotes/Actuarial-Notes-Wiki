Given the following information:

| Bond | Par Value | Time To Maturity (years) | Annual Coupon Rate | Current Bond Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$100 | 1 | 3% | \$98.10 |
| 2 | \$100 | 2 | 5% | \$99.10 |
| 3 | \$100 | 3 | 5% | X |
| 4 | \$100 | 4 | 3% | \$86.65 |

- The 1-year forward rate for year 4 is 10%.
- The liquidity premium in year 3 is 1%.
- Coupons are paid annually.
- Assume annual compounding.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate X.

### Explanation
Bootstrap the spot rates from bonds 1, 2 and 4:

$$98.10 = \frac{103}{1 + S_1} \rightarrow S_1 = 5\%$$

$$99.10 = \frac{5}{1.05} + \frac{105}{(1 + S_2)^2} \Rightarrow S_2 = 5.5\%$$

Bond 4 uses the given 1-year forward rate for year 4 (10%) to discount its final payment from year 3 to year 4:

$$86.65 = \frac{3}{1.05} + \frac{3}{1.055^2} + \frac{3}{(1 + S_3)^3} + \frac{103}{(1 + S_3)^3(1.10)}$$

Let $x = (1 + S_3)^{-3}$:

$$81.0975 = 3x + \frac{103}{1.10}x$$

$$x = 0.8392 = (1 + S_3)^{-3} \rightarrow S_3 = 6.02\%$$

$$\text{Bond 3 price } (X) = \frac{5}{1.05} + \frac{5}{1.055^2} + \frac{105}{1.0602^3} = 97.36$$

Alternatively, with one-year forward rates $R_1, R_2, R_3$ (as printed: the final-payment numerators appear as 1.05 and 1.03, for the 105 and 103 payments, and the sum 86.825 as 86,825):

$$\text{Bond 1: } \frac{103}{98.10} = 1.05 \Rightarrow R_1 = 0.05$$

$$\text{Bond 2: } \frac{5}{1.05} + \frac{1.05}{(1 + R_2)(1.05)} = 99.10 \Rightarrow R_2 = 0.06$$

$$\text{Bond 4: } 86.65 = \frac{3}{1.05} + \frac{3}{(1.05)(1.06)} + \frac{3}{(1.05)(1.06)(1 + R_3)} + \frac{1.03}{(1.05)(1.06)(1 + R_3)(1.1)}$$

$$81.097 = \frac{2.6954}{1 + R_3} + \frac{84.1297}{1 + R_3} = \frac{86{,}825}{1 + R_3} \Rightarrow R_3 = 0.07$$

$$X = \frac{5}{1.05} + \frac{5}{(1.05)(1.06)} + \frac{1.05}{(1.05)(1.06)(1.07)} = 97.42$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to be able to bootstrap spot and forward rates from the given bonds.
Generally candidates received full credit by calculating the 4 year spot rate and then either the 3
year spot or forward rate.  Common errors included:

- Candidates mistakenly added or removed the liquidity premium in their bootstrapping calculations, but this should not be included because prices are available at time 0
- Candidates confused yield to maturity with the spot rate

## Part b (0.75 points)

Calculate the price of bond 3 at time 2 under the expectations hypothesis theory. Assume the coupon has already been paid.

### Explanation
Under the expectations hypothesis, the forward rate is the actual expected interest rate for that time period. Only the final coupon and principal (105) are left to be paid, in 1 year, so discount at the year-3 forward rate of 0.07 from part a:

$$\frac{105}{1.07} = 98.13$$

Alternatively, from the spot rates: the 1-year forward rate for year 3 is

$$\frac{1.0602^3}{1.055^2} - 1 = 7.07\%$$

and under the expectations hypothesis the forward rate equals the expected future spot rate, so the 1-year spot rate at time 2 is 7.07% and the price is $\frac{1.05}{1.0707} = 98.07$ (numerator as printed, for the 105 payment).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate or describe knowledge of the expectations hypothesis.
The majority of candidates performed well, using or calculating the forward rate from time 2 to time 3.  Common errors included:

- Incorrectly removed the liquidity premium from the forward rate
- Calculated the forward rate for the wrong time period
- Discounted to the wrong time
- Used the spot rate instead of the forward rate to discount

## Part c (0.75 points)

Calculate the price of bond 3 at time 2 under the liquidity preference theory. Assume the coupon has already been paid.

### Explanation
Under liquidity preference, the premium for year 3 is 1%. This premium is no longer applicable at time 2, as the bond's remaining maturity is now 1 year. Thus we use $0.07 - 0.01 = 0.06$:

$$\frac{105}{1.06} = 99.06$$

Alternatively, from the 7.07% forward rate of part b: under liquidity preference theory, forward rates include a liquidity premium over the expected spot rate, so the 1-year spot rate at time 2 is 7.07% - 1% = 6.07%, and the price is $\frac{1.05}{1.0607} = 98.99$ (numerator as printed, for the 105 payment).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate or describe knowledge of the liquidity preference theory.  Candidates should have recognized that the expected future short rate was equal to the forward rate minus the liquidity premium, since the liquidity premium will disappear at time 2, when the liquidity risk no longer exists according to the theory. Candidates did not perform as well on this part of the question.  Common errors included:

- Incorrectly added the liquidity premium to the forward rate
- Removed the liquidity premium from the spot rate
