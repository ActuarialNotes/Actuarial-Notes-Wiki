The random variable $Y$ has density function

$$
f(y) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{y}{6} - \dfrac{y^2}{36}, & \text{for } 0 < y < 6 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate $P[1 < Y < 3 \mid 2 < Y < 4]$.

- A) $0.111$
- B) $0.241$
- C) $0.481$
- D) $0.500$
- E) $0.885$

## Explanation

The two intervals **overlap** rather than nest, so the intersection is neither one of them:

$$
\{1 < Y < 3\} \cap \{2 < Y < 4\} = \{2 < Y < 3\}
$$

Getting that intersection right is the whole difficulty of the question.

Integrate the density to get the distribution function:

$$
F(y) = \int_0^y\left(\frac{t}{6} - \frac{t^2}{36}\right)dt = \frac{y^2}{12} - \frac{y^3}{108}
$$

A check: $F(6) = 3 - 2 = 1$, so the density is valid. Evaluate at the three points needed:

$$
\begin{align*}
F(2) &= \frac{4}{12} - \frac{8}{108} = \frac{7}{27} \\
F(3) &= \frac{9}{12} - \frac{27}{108} = \frac{1}{2} \\
F(4) &= \frac{16}{12} - \frac{64}{108} = \frac{20}{27}
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
P[1 < Y < 3 \mid 2 < Y < 4] &= \frac{F(3) - F(2)}{F(4) - F(2)} \\
                            &= \frac{\tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{7}{27}}{\tfrac{20}{27} - \tfrac{7}{27}} \\
                            &= \frac{13/54}{13/27} \\
                            &= \mathbf{0.500}
\end{align*}
$$

The 13s cancel exactly. Treating the intersection as $\{1 < Y < 3\}$ would give $F(3)-F(1)$ in the numerator and a different answer; the conditioning event's lower endpoint of 2 overrides the 1.
