You observe a linear relationship between the amount of snowfall in a territory and the number of collision claims, $Y$, filed by insured drivers in that territory. You describe that relationship as follows:

$$
\begin{align*}
Y &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu, \sigma) \\
\mu &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 \ast snowfall
\end{align*}
$$

You decide to model this relationship using linear regression with Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) and compare it to the same model fit using Bayesian Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), with appropriate priors.

You are given the following statements regarding your models:

I. The MCMC model is specified with one likelihood function and three prior distributions.
II. Parameter estimates from an OLS model and MCMC model will yield similar estimates when using flat priors.
III. The OLS regression provides information on the uncertainty of the estimate of the sigma parameter ($\sigma$).

Determine which of the above statements are true about your models.

- A) None of I, II, or II is true
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I is true.** The likelihood is the single line $Y \sim \text{Normal}(\mu, \sigma)$. Three quantities remain unknown and each needs a prior: $\beta_0$, $\beta_1$ and $\sigma$. Note that $\mu$ needs no prior — it is a deterministic function of $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$, not a parameter in its own right.

**II is true.** With flat (non-informative) priors the posterior is proportional to the likelihood, so the posterior mode coincides with the maximum likelihood estimate — which, for a normal linear model, is the OLS estimate. The two approaches then agree closely.

**III is false.** OLS produces a point estimate $\hat{\sigma}^2 = \text{RSS}/(n-p)$ but no standard error or interval for it; the usual OLS output quantifies uncertainty in the *coefficients* only. This is one of the practical advantages of the Bayesian fit — MCMC returns a full posterior for $\sigma$ alongside those for $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$.

Statements I and II only, so the answer is **B**.
