## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate ultimate claim ratios for each accident year using the reported Bornheutter-Ferguson technique with a 70% expected claims ratio.

### Explanation
**Development factors** (volume-weighted, all years; the individual factors are very stable):

$$\text{12–24: } \frac{34{,}530}{27{,}600} = 1.2511 \qquad \text{24–36: } \frac{25{,}170}{22{,}830} = 1.1025 \qquad \text{36–48: } \frac{15{,}400}{14{,}670} = 1.0498 \qquad \text{48–ult: } 1.000$$

Cumulative: 48 months 1.0000; 36 months 1.0498; 24 months 1.1574; 12 months 1.4480.

**Bornhuetter-Ferguson.** Ultimate = reported + expected claims × % unreported, with expected claims = 70% × earned premium (there have been no rate changes):

| AY | Reported | Expected claims | CDF | % unreported | Ultimate | Ultimate claim ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 7,000 | 7,000 | 1.0000 | 0.0% | 7,000 | 70.0% |
| 2014 | 8,400 | 8,400 | 1.0000 | 0.0% | 8,400 | 70.0% |
| 2015 | 10,500 | 10,500 | 1.0498 | 4.7% | 10,998 | 73.3% |
| 2016 | 11,700 | 12,600 | 1.1574 | 13.6% | 13,413 | 74.5% |
| 2017 | 11,800 | 15,400 | 1.4480 | 30.9% | 16,564 | 75.3% |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to perform a Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique and know the conditions necessary for the technique to provide a suitable estimate of ultimate claims.

Candidates were expected to perform a Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique.

Common errors included:

- Calculating ultimate claims but not the ultimate claim ratio.
- Miscalculating the ultimate claim ratio as ultimate claims/reported claims.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Evaluate the appropriateness of using the reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique as applied in part a. above.

### Explanation
The technique is not appropriate as applied. The ultimate claim ratios rise steadily from 70% in 2013–2014 to over 75% in 2017, and that rise comes from the reported claims alone — the 70% expected claims ratio is the same in every year. The experience shows the claims ratio is deteriorating, so a flat 70% a priori understates the unreported claims of the recent, immature years, where the Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique leans most heavily on it. The expected claims ratio should be selected to reflect the deterioration (for example from the recent years' developed experience, or a Cape Cod ratio) before the technique is used.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to interpret the results from part a. and note that there was an increasing claim ratio that was higher than the 70% used in the Borhuetter-Ferguson technique, making the technique inappropriate.

Common errors included:

- Stating that there was a change in case reserve adequacy, so the Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique was inappropriate and a Berquist-Sherman technique was necessary. There was no mention of claim counts in the problem, and the reported LDFs were stable, so there was no basis to definitively make this statement.
- Stating that earned premium was growing, so the Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique was inappropriate without mentioning the deteriorating claims ratio.
