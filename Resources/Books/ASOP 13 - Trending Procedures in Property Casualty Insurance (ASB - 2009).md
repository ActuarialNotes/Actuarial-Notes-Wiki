The Actuarial Standards Board's guidance for actuaries using trending procedures to estimate future values for property/casualty coverages. This revised edition (Doc. No. 133), adopted in June 2009 and updated for deviation language effective May 1, 2011, replaced the 1990 standard Trending Procedures in Property/Casualty Insurance Ratemaking and extends its guidance beyond ratemaking to reserving, valuations, underwriting and marketing. It is effective for actuarial services performed on or after November 1, 2009.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 5 (CAS)|Exam 5]] — objectives A4 and A8; the standard as revised in 2009 and updated for deviation language in 2011.

## Transmittal Memorandum

## 1 Purpose, Scope, Cross References, and Effective Date
- 1.1 Purpose
- 1.2 Scope
    - Applies to trending procedures for all property/casualty coverages — work for insurance or reinsurance companies and other risk financing systems such as self insurance.
    - A trending procedure does not encompass the process commonly referred to as "[[Loss Development|development]]", which estimates changes over time within a given exposure period.
- 1.3 Cross References
- 1.4 Effective Date

## 2 Definitions
- 2.1 Coverage
- 2.2 Experience Period
    - The period of time to which historical data used for actuarial analysis pertain.
- 2.3 Forecast Period
    - The future time period to which the historical data are projected.
- 2.4 Social Influences
    - The impact on insurance costs of societal changes such as changes in claim consciousness, court practices and legal precedents, and in other noneconomic factors.
- 2.5 Trending Period
    - The time over which [[Trend|trend]] is applied in projecting from the experience period to the forecast period.
- 2.6 Trending Procedure
    - A process by which the actuary evaluates how changes over time affect items such as claim costs, [[Frequency|claim frequencies]], expenses, exposures, premiums, retention rates, marketing/solicitation response rates and economic indices, by analysing changes between exposure periods.

## 3 Analysis of Issues and Recommended Practices
- 3.1 Purpose or Use of Trending Procedures
    - Trending is used in ratemaking, reserving, valuations, underwriting and marketing; the trend estimate may be presented as a point estimate, a range, a point estimate with a margin for adverse deviation, or a probability distribution.
- 3.2 Historical Insurance and Non-Insurance Data
    - In selecting data, consider its [[Credibility|credibility]], the period available, its relationship to the items trended, and known biases or distortions such as [[Catastrophe Loss|catastrophic influences]], seasonality, coverage changes, nonrecurring events, claim practices, and distributional changes in [[Deductible|deductibles]], types of risks and policy limits.
- 3.3 Economic and Social Influences
- 3.4 Selection of Trending Procedures
    - Relevant information includes procedures established by precedent or common usage, procedures used in previous analyses, procedures that predict insurance trends from insurance, econometric and other non-insurance data, and the context in which the trend estimate is used.
- 3.5 Criteria for Determining Trending Period
    - Consider the lengths of the experience and forecast periods, and changes in the mix of data between them.
- 3.6 Evaluation of Trending Procedures
- 3.7 Reliance on Data or Other Information Supplied by Others
- 3.8 Documentation

## 4 Communications and Disclosures
- 4.1 Actuarial Communication
    - Disclose the intended purpose or use of the trending procedure and significant adjustments to data or assumptions that may materially affect the result, with the ASOP No. 41 deviation disclosures.
- 4.2 Additional Disclosures
    - The basis of a range of trend estimates, and changes from a prior analysis for the same purpose that might materially affect the results.

## Appendix 1 Background and Current Practices
- Background
- Current Practices
    - Actuaries generally rely on data from the book of business analysed, other insurance data, and non-insurance data, in that order of preference.

## Appendix 2 Comments on the Exposure Draft and Responses
- The reviewers carved "development" out of the scope while keeping the other uses of trend in reserving, such as the [[Cape Cod Method|Cape Cod]], [[Bornhuetter-Ferguson Method|Bornhuetter Ferguson]] and [[Frequency-Severity Method|frequency/severity methods]].
