Homeowner losses due to tornado damage are exponentially distributed with standard deviation $\sigma$. A homeowners policy covers tornado losses in full, subject to a deductible. The probability that a random loss exceeds the deductible by at least $\sigma$ is 0.20.

Calculate the probability that a random loss exceeds the deductible by at least $0.5\sigma$.

- A) $0.33$
- B) $0.40$
- C) $0.46$
- D) $0.54$
- E) $0.60$

## Explanation

For an exponential distribution the mean equals the standard deviation, so the mean is $\sigma$ and the survival function is

$$
P[L > x] = e^{-x/\sigma}
$$

Write $d$ for the deductible. The given condition is

$$
\begin{align*}
P[L > d + \sigma] &= e^{-(d+\sigma)/\sigma} \\
                  &= e^{-d/\sigma}e^{-1} \\
                  &= 0.20
\end{align*}
$$

The quantity asked for splits the same way, but with $e^{-0.5}$ in place of $e^{-1}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[L > d + 0.5\sigma] &= e^{-(d + 0.5\sigma)/\sigma} \\
                     &= e^{-d/\sigma}e^{-0.5}
\end{align*}
$$

The unknown factor $e^{-d/\sigma}$ is common to both, so divide it out by multiplying the given 0.20 by $e^{-0.5}/e^{-1} = e^{0.5}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[L > d + 0.5\sigma] &= (0.20)e^{0.5} \\
                     &= \mathbf{0.3297}
\end{align*}
$$

The deductible never needs a value — the exponential's memorylessness means only the *extra* distance beyond $d$ matters, and halving that distance multiplies the survival probability by $e^{0.5} = 1.65$. Choice (B), 0.40, is $2(0.20)$, which would follow if survival probability were inversely proportional to distance rather than exponential in it.
