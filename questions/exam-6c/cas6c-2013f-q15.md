## Part a (1 point)

Briefly describe the four criteria that must be met for a terrorism event to be certified under current Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) rules.

### Explanation
- Insured property and casualty losses must exceed \$100 Million
- Event must be dangerous to human life, property or infrastructure
- Damage must occur inside the United States, or if outside the United States at the premises of a United States mission or to certain US flagged vessels and air carries
- Event must be committed by an individual or individuals as part of an effort coerce the civilian population of the United States or influence the policy or conduct of the United States government (Note do not give points if candidate states foreign individual without also stating that domestic is included)

Alternatively:

- Damages from the event must exceed \$100 million
- Must present a danger to human life, property or infrastructure

- Must occur in the USA, or if outside of the USA at a US mission or on a US flagged vessel or air carrier
- Performed via an effort to coerce the US civilian population or influence the conduct and policies of the US government (can be either domestic or foreign)

## Part b (0.5 points)

Identify two ways the private market could replace TRIA.

### Explanation
- Capital market financing (Catastrophe bonds)
- Risk pools
- Reinsurance

Alternatively:

- Through catastrophe bond market
- Use of private reinsurance market

## Part c (0.5 points)

Describe two arguments to support the position that TRIA is not insurance.

### Explanation
- Insurers do not pay premiums prior to incurring losses
- Government may recoup some of the losses that it pays, but otherwise, insurers are simply reimbursed for a portion of their costs
- Offered to any insurer universally rather than through a risk selective process

Alternatively:

- Insurers do not pay premiums until a loss occurs
- Coverage is provided to everybody and is heavily subsidized by general tax revenue
