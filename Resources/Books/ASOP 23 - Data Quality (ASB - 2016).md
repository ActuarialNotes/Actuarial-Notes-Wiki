The standard governing how an actuary selects, reviews, uses and relies on **data**, and what must be disclosed about its [[Data Quality|quality]]. It is a Domain A reading for [[Exam PCPA (CAS)|PCPA]]. The revised edition was adopted in December 2016 and applies to work whose data were provided on or after April 30, 2017.

## Section 1. Purpose, Scope, Cross References, and Effective Date

- 1.1 Purpose
- 1.2 Scope — applies when selecting data, reviewing it, using it or relying on data supplied by others, and to an actuary preparing data that other actuaries will use. It does not require an audit
- 1.3 Cross References
- 1.4 Effective

## Section 2. Definitions

- 2.1 Appropriate Data — suitable for the intended purpose and relevant to the system or process being analyzed
- 2.2 Audit
- 2.3 Data — numerical, census or classification information, but not general or qualitative information; assumptions are not data
- 2.4 Data Element
- 2.5 Review — an examination of the obvious characteristics of data, less detailed than an audit
- 2.6 Sufficient

## Section 3. Analysis of Issues and Recommended Practices

- 3.1 Overview
- 3.2 Selection of Data — the data elements wanted and their alternatives; whether the data are appropriate, current, internally consistent, reasonable against external information, and [[Data Quality|sufficient]]; known limitations; sampling methods
- 3.3 Review of Data — learn each data element's definition, and identify questionable values or inconsistent relationships (see [[Outlier]], [[Missing Data]]); compare with the data used in a prior analysis
- 3.4 Use of Data — acceptable as is, in need of enhancement, usable with judgmental adjustments, or too inadequate to use
- 3.5 Reliance on Data Supplied by Others
- 3.6 Reliance on Other Information Relevant to the Use of Data
- 3.7 Confidentiality
- 3.8 Limitation of the Actuary's Responsibility — no duty to detect falsified data, to compile extra data just to search for errors, or to audit

## Section 4. Communications and Disclosures

- 4.1 Communication and Disclosure — the source of the data, limitations, whether a review was performed, unresolved questionable values, steps taken to improve the data, judgmental adjustments, and reliance on others
