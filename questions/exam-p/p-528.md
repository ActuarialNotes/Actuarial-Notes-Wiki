An actuary makes the following observations about an insurer's fire and flood insurance policies:

i) The probability that a client purchases neither policy exceeds the probability that a client purchases both policies by 0.20.

ii) The probability that a client purchases both policies is positive.

iii) The probability that a client purchases a fire policy given that the client purchases a flood policy is twice the probability that a client purchases a flood policy given that the client purchases a fire policy.

Calculate the probability that a client purchases a flood policy.

- A) $0.27$
- B) $0.33$
- C) $0.40$
- D) $0.53$
- E) $0.80$

## Explanation

Let $A$ be the fire policy and $B$ the flood policy.

**Statement (iii) relates the two marginals.** Both conditional probabilities share the same numerator $P[A \cap B]$:

$$
P[A \mid B] = \frac{P[A \cap B]}{P[B]}, \qquad P[B \mid A] = \frac{P[A \cap B]}{P[A]}
$$

Setting the first equal to twice the second and cancelling $P[A \cap B]$ — legitimate because statement (ii) guarantees it is nonzero — gives

$$
\frac{1}{P[B]} = \frac{2}{P[A]} \implies P[B] = \tfrac{1}{2}P[A]
$$

**Statement (i) plus the addition rule finishes it.** Writing $x$ for the probability of neither policy, statement (i) says $P[A \cap B] = x - 0.20$, and $x = 1 - P[A \cup B]$. Substituting into $P[A \cup B] = P[A] + P[B] - P[A \cap B]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 - x &= P[A] + P[B] - (x - 0.20) \\
0.80 &= P[A] + P[B]
\end{align*}
$$

The unknown $x$ cancels. Combining with $P[B] = \tfrac{1}{2}P[A]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\tfrac{3}{2}P[A] &= 0.80 \\
P[A] &= \tfrac{8}{15} \\
P[B] &= \tfrac{4}{15} = \mathbf{0.2666}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 0.53, is $P[A]$ — the fire policy rather than the flood policy. Neither $P[A \cap B]$ nor $x$ is ever determined individually; only their difference is fixed, which is enough because both drop out.
