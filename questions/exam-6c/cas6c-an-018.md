When IFRS 17 took effect in Canada for fiscal years beginning on or after January 1, 2023, an insurer held three blocks of in-force contracts:

- **Block 1:** multi-year commercial contracts with complete historical pricing and cash flow records.
- **Block 2:** long-duration contracts acquired many years ago, for which the original assumptions were never kept. After every reasonable effort, the insurer cannot reconstruct them and has no reasonable and supportable information with which to approximate them.
- **Block 3:** contracts for which full reconstruction is impracticable, but reasonable and supportable information exists for an approximation.

Which assessment of the transition approaches is correct?

- A) The insurer may choose the fair value approach for all three blocks, since it is the least costly to apply
- B) Block 2 must use the modified retrospective approach, because it is the closest approximation to full retrospective application
- C) Block 1 requires full retrospective application, and Block 2 can use the fair value approach
- D) All three blocks may keep their IFRS 4 measurement, because IFRS 17 grandfathers contracts already in force
- E) Block 3 must use the fair value approach, because full retrospective application is impracticable for it

## Explanation

The IFRS Foundation's *Effects Analysis on IFRS 17* (p. 70, "Transition reliefs") states that a company must account for its insurance contracts "as if IFRS 17 had always been applied unless this is impracticable (for example, if, after making every reasonable effort, the company is unable to gather historical data...)." Only when it is impracticable may the company use either:

- (a) a **modified retrospective approach**, "which can be used only if reasonable and supportable information is available"; or
- (b) a **fair value transition approach**.

Block 1 has the data, so full retrospective application is required. For Block 2, full retrospective application is impracticable and the modified retrospective approach is unavailable for lack of reasonable and supportable information, which leaves the fair value approach. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — cost is not the test. The simpler approaches open up only where full retrospective application is impracticable, which is not the case for Block 1.
- **B** — the modified retrospective approach needs reasonable and supportable information, which Block 2 lacks.
- **D** — IFRS 17 replaced IFRS 4 for all contracts in force. The reliefs offer simpler ways to measure them, not an exemption.
- **E** — for Block 3 the insurer may choose *either* the modified retrospective or the fair value approach. Neither is mandated.
