A Congressional Research Service report (updated January 9, 2023) on the role of private insurers in U.S. flood insurance and the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP). It describes the current role of private insurers in the NFIP, discusses barriers to private sector involvement, considers the potential effects of increased private participation on the NFIP and consumers, and outlines the provisions on private flood insurance in House and Senate NFIP reauthorization bills.

## Summary
- The NFIP is the main source of primary flood insurance coverage in the United States, collecting over $4.6 billion in premiums, fees and surcharges for over five million policies; one of the primary reasons for creating the NFIP in 1968 was the withdrawal of private insurers from providing flood coverage.
- Private insurers have expressed increased interest in providing flood coverage, which may offer more flexible policies, integrated coverage with homeowners insurance or lower-cost coverage for some consumers, but private coverage would not be guaranteed to all floodplain residents and consumer protections could vary by state.
- The NFIP is operating under a short-term reauthorization until September 30, 2023.
