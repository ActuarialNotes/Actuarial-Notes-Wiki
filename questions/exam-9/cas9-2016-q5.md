Given the following information:

- Risk free rate is 5%.
- Expected market return is 15%.
- The market price of risk is 40%.
- The covariance of stock A with the market is 20%.
- The current price of stock A is \$90.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the expected return for stock A using the Capital Asset Pricing Model.

### Explanation
Two readings of the market price of risk were accepted (see the examiner's report).

**1) Market price of risk as excess return over market variance**

$$\text{Market price of risk} = 0.4 = \frac{E(R_M)}{\sigma_M^2}$$

$$\sigma_M^2 = \frac{E(R_M)}{0.4} = \frac{0.15 - 0.05}{0.4} = 0.25$$

$$\beta_A = \frac{Cov(R_A, R_M)}{\sigma_M^2} = \frac{0.2}{0.25} = 0.8$$

$$E(r_A) = r_f + \beta_A[E(r_M) - r_f] = 0.05 + 0.8[0.15 - 0.05] = 13\%$$

**2) Market price of risk as excess return over market standard deviation**

$$\text{Market price of risk} = 0.4 = \frac{E(R_M)}{\sigma_M}$$

$$\sigma_M = \frac{E(R_M)}{0.4} = \frac{0.15 - 0.05}{0.4} = 0.25$$

$$\beta_A = \frac{Cov(R_A, R_M)}{\sigma_M^2} = \frac{0.2}{0.0625} = 3.2$$

$$E(r_A) = r_f + \beta_A[E(r_M) - r_f] = 0.05 + 3.2[0.15 - 0.05] = 37\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to show that they knew the formulae for the market price of risk, βA, and the expected return of an asset.  In general, candidates performed very well on this part.
Because of the confusion in the literature, between the syllabus readings and other sources on the same topic, as to whether the market price of risk was divisible by the market standard deviation or market variance, both were accepted.  As a result, the expected return on the stock price was very different.  It should be noted that the syllabus reading is clear on which is correct, and is more up-to-date than other sources on this topic.  Moreover, candidates are encouraged, as always, to use the readings cited in the syllabus as their primary source of study.  Therefore, candidates should not assume that both options will continue to be accepted in future sittings.
The most common error on this part of the question was using the market price of risk as the market standard deviation.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Explain how the current price of stock A would be expected to change if investors forecast the price of stock A to be \$99 in one year.

### Explanation
The investors' forecast implies an expected return of

$$\frac{99}{90} - 1 = 10\% < 0.13$$

so the price of A will drop. If CAPM is accurate, the current price should be $X(1.13) = 99 \Rightarrow X = 87.612$.

Alternatively: since $90(1 + 0.13) = 101.7 > 99$, the forecast projects lesser returns, so the price should be depressed (assume no dividend payments). Under the 37% reading of part a the conclusion is the same: investors expect to earn 37%, which is greater than 10%, so the price will fall until investors can buy the stock at a price they expect to earn 37%.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to explain that investors were expecting a lower return on the stock than CAPM, thus leading to a drop in the price due to its inferior return relative to its risk.
If the candidate came up with an expected return of less than 10% in part a, then full credit was given for a response of the price increasing due to increased demand.
The most common mistake was to assume the price would change from 90 to 99 as a result of investors' expectations.

## Part c (0.5 points)

The current price of stock A remains at \$90 despite the forecasted price of \$99 in one year. Briefly describe an arbitrage opportunity that exists.

### Explanation
Construct a portfolio with a 0.8 portion in the market index portfolio and a 0.2 portion in the risk-free asset — a tracking portfolio with the same beta as stock A. Short 1 portion of stock A and buy 1 portion of this portfolio:

$$E[R] = 0.13 - 0.1 = 0.03, \qquad R = 0$$

Alternatively, per share: short sell stock A for 90. Invest 0.8(90) = 72 in the market portfolio and the remaining 0.2 (18) in the risk-free asset. Expected return = 72 x 1-15 (as printed, for 1.15) + 18(1.05) = 101.7, and 101.7 - 99 = 2.7. This creates a risk-free return of 2.7 per share without upfront investment.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were able to recognize that there was an arbitrage opportunity in shorting the stock.
However, most candidates had trouble determining how to take advantage of the arbitrage opportunity.  As with part b, if the candidate came up with an expected return of less than 10% in
part a, full credit was given for a response of needing to short a tracking portfolio with the same
beta as the stock and buy stock A.

## Part d (1 point)

Describe two limitations to being able to take advantage of an arbitrage opportunity.

### Explanation
Any two of the following, explained:

- Model risk – maybe the model is not right: the current price of the stock is indeed valid, and the arbitrageur's model has some issues which led it to falsely indicate mispricing.
- Fundamental risk – the market may persist with overpriced securities, and the overpricing can get worse; the market doesn't go as expected and the arbitrage doesn't pay off.
- Transaction costs – the actual cost of implementation may make the arbitrage a wash, or even cost more money than earned.

### Examiner Report
Most candidates were able to at least list two limitations to arbitrage based on BKM chapter 12
and explain them.  The biggest errors here were that candidates could either list the limitation but not necessarily explain them or vice versa.  As an example, someone may have said that forecasting price isn't correct but not explicitly state model risk as a limitation.
