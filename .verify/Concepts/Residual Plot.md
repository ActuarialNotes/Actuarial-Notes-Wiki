## [F-001] Outliers in a residual plot equated with influential observations
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-10-04T20:47Z/8861
- date: 2026-10-04
- severity: major
- status: open
- locus: 'How to read residual plots' table, last row
- claim: 'Outliers | Influential observations; investigate further'
- evidence: ISLR 2nd ed. §3.3.3 pp.97-99 (PDF 107-109) keeps the two apart: an outlier is a point whose y_i is far from its prediction, and 'It is typical for an outlier that does not have an unusual predictor value to have little effect on the least squares fit' (observation 20, Fig 3.12, barely moves the line); high leverage is an unusual x_i; observation 41 is 'an outlier as well as a high leverage observation. This is a particularly dangerous combination!' (Fig 3.13). Penn State STAT 501 Lesson 11 §11.5: Cook's D depends on both the residual and the leverage. Goldburd et al. §6.4 p.74: influential records 'tend to be highly weighted outliers'. A point that stands out in a residual plot is an outlier; whether it is influential depends on its leverage, which a residual plot does not show.
