In each of the following situations a claim is made under an insurance policy. Determine the situation in which the policyholder had an insurable interest at the time the law requires one.

- A) A woman buys a policy on her husband's life while they are married; they later divorce, she keeps paying the premiums, and he dies
- B) A homeowner sells her house but keeps her homeowners policy in force; the house burns down two weeks after the sale
- C) A company insures a warehouse it has agreed to buy next month; the warehouse burns down before the purchase closes
- D) An investor with no financial or family tie to a stranger buys a policy on the stranger's life, naming himself beneficiary
- E) A man insures his neighbour's house, which he expects to inherit, against fire; the house burns down

## Explanation

When insurable interest must exist depends on the kind of cover:

- **Life insurance** requires an insurable interest at **inception** only. Once a valid policy is issued it stays valid if the relationship later ends.
- **Property insurance** requires an insurable interest at the **time of loss**, because it indemnifies a loss the insured actually suffers.

In **A**, the wife had an insurable interest in her husband's life when she bought the policy, so the later divorce does not void it. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** tests property interest at inception. She owned the house when she bought the policy, but on the date of the fire she owned nothing and lost nothing.
- **C** treats an intended purchase as an interest. Until the sale closes the company does not own the warehouse and suffers no loss from the fire.
- **D** has no interest at inception: the policy is a wager on the stranger's life, which is what the insurable interest requirement exists to prevent.
- **E** treats an expectation of a future benefit as an interest. The man owns no part of the house and suffers no financial loss of his own when it burns.
