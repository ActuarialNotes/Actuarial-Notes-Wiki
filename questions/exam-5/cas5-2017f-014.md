Given the following information about a homeowners insurance loss:

- Face amount of policy = \$300,000.
- Value of property = \$500,000.
- Coinsurance penalty = \$22,000.
- Indemnity payment = \$84,000.
- There is no deductible.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the required coinsurance percentage.

### Answer
75.7%

### Explanation
The question runs the coinsurance mechanics **backwards**: the penalty and the payment are given, and the coinsurance requirement that produced them has to be recovered.

**Step 1 — the amount of loss.** With no deductible, the loss is what the insurer paid plus what the insured absorbed through the penalty:

$$\text{Loss} = 84{,}000 + 22{,}000 = \$106{,}000$$

**Step 2 — the apportionment ratio.** This is the fraction of the loss the insurer actually paid:

$$a = \frac{\text{Indemnity}}{\text{Loss}} = \frac{84{,}000}{106{,}000} = 0.7925$$

**Step 3 — invert the coinsurance formula.** The apportionment ratio is the ratio of insurance carried to insurance required:

$$a = \frac{\text{Face amount}}{\text{Coinsurance \%} \times \text{Property value}}$$

$$0.7925 = \frac{300{,}000}{c \times 500{,}000}$$

$$c \times 500{,}000 = \frac{300{,}000}{0.7925} = \$378{,}571$$

$$c = \frac{378{,}571}{500{,}000} = 0.757 \Rightarrow \mathbf{75.7\%}$$

**Check:** a 75.7% requirement on a \$500,000 property calls for \$378,571 of insurance; the insured carries \$300,000, so $a = 300{,}000/378{,}571 = 0.7925$ and the indemnity is $0.7925 \times 106{,}000 = \$84{,}000$, with the remaining \$22,000 falling on the insured as the penalty.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the coinsurance formula and demonstrate knowledge of methods to resolve insurance to value coverage issues.

Candidates were expected to demonstrate how to calculate the required coinsurance percentage given other information about the policy and loss.

Common errors included:

- Using the Indemnity payment as the amount of loss.
- Providing only the apportionment ratio rather than the required coinsurance percentage.
- Failing to show all calculations for the amount of loss, apportionment ratio, coinsurance requirement and/or required coinsurance percentage.

## Part b (1 points)

Identify two homeowners insurance to value initiatives insurers could implement and briefly describe how each initiative encourages insurance to full value.

### Explanation
Any two initiatives, each with the mechanism by which it moves insureds toward full value:

**1. Inflation guard endorsement.** The amount of insurance is increased automatically each year — typically by a construction-cost index or a set percentage — without the insured having to request a change. This encourages insurance to value by keeping the face amount rising in step with replacement cost, so a policy that was written at full value does not silently drift into underinsurance as building costs rise and the insured takes no action.

**2. Guaranteed replacement cost offered only to insureds who carry insurance to value.** The insurer undertakes to rebuild the home even if the cost exceeds the face amount, but conditions that promise on the insured carrying, say, 100% of estimated replacement value. This encourages insurance to full value by offering a benefit the insured genuinely wants — protection against a total loss exceeding the limit — in exchange for buying the full amount.

Other acceptable initiatives:

- **Property valuation software** run against the in-force book to identify underinsured homes, so the insurer can contact those policyholders with a revised replacement-cost estimate.
- **Home inspections or appraisals** at new business and periodically thereafter, establishing an objective replacement cost that the amount of insurance is then set against.
- **Education and marketing** explaining that a coinsurance penalty will reduce the payment on a partial loss, and that underinsurance leaves the insured exposed on a large or total loss.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of insurance to value coverage issues by providing two different initiatives that an insurer could reasonably implement that would encourage insurance to full value.  They were also expected to be able to explain how each initiative would encourage insurance to full value.

Common errors included:

- Describing an initiative without explaining how it encourages insurance to full value.
- Providing an underwriting initiative that depends on foreknowledge of home replacement value, which is generally unknown without a loss or some sort of initiative to first determine the home replacement value.
- Providing a rating initiative that not only depends on foreknowledge of home value, but would also depart from the actuarial standard that a rate be based on an estimate of future costs, not as an inducement for a particular insured behavior.
- Describing the impact of limits on total losses, which is a basic policy feature that is designed to limit loss exposure for the insurer rather than encourage insurance to full value.
- Describing the impact of underinsurance on the insurer and/or insured rather than providing an initiative to help prevent it.
