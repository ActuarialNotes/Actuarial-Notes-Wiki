An adjuster is applying the framework for coverage analysis to a commercial property claim. The loss fits the insuring agreement, the conditions were met, and an exclusion in the causes-of-loss form appears to apply, but the exclusion contains an exception for an ensuing loss by fire, which is what damaged the property. What should the adjuster conclude?

- A) The loss is excluded, because an exclusion was found after the insuring agreement
- B) The loss is excluded unless an endorsement adds the coverage back
- C) The claim must be denied in writing and then reconsidered on appeal
- D) The exception restores coverage, so the analysis moves on to the amount payable

## Explanation

Exclusions remove coverage the insuring agreement would otherwise give, but an exception to an exclusion restores it, which is why the analysis reads each exclusion to its end. With the exception met, the loss is covered, and the remaining step is the amount: valuation, limits, deductible and any coinsurance. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Stops reading at the exclusion and misses its exception.
- **B** — An endorsement isn't needed when the exclusion's own exception restores coverage.
- **C** — There is no basis for a denial once coverage is restored.
