An insurance company expanded its product offering into a new state last year.

## Part a (1 point)

Briefly discuss an advantage and disadvantage of using the following data to determine loss trend for this state:

- i. Competitor filings
- ii. Internal company data

### Explanation
**i. Competitor filings**

- *Advantage:* the company has only one year of its own data in the new state, while a competitor that has written there for years has a larger, more credible and more mature body of data from which to measure the state's loss trend.
- *Disadvantage:* the competitor's data reflects its own mix of business, underwriting, policy forms and claims handling, which may differ from the company's, so its trend may not apply to the company's book; a filing may also be dated or not detailed enough to adjust for those differences.

**ii. Internal company data**

- *Advantage:* it reflects the company's own book of business, underwriting and claims practices, so it measures the trend the company will actually experience.
- *Disadvantage:* one year of experience in a new state is too little, too immature and too volatile to measure a trend credibly — a trend needs several periods of data.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to discuss the pros/cons of using competitor data and internal data for an insurer who had entered a new state in the prior year. They were also expected to demonstrate their knowledge of loss trend and loss development concepts and explain why they do not overlap.

Candidates were expected to demonstrate their understanding of different data sources and their relative merits in a certain situation. They needed to understand concepts such as credibility, differences in mix of business, differences in loss behavior between states, or availability of data, and discuss the advantages/disadvantages these presented in each case.

Common errors for part i. included:

- Saying competitor data could be biased (or unbiased) without stating why.
- Saying this would allow the insurer to be competitive. Competitiveness of rates should not be a consideration when selecting loss trends.
Common errors for part ii. included:

- Saying internal data could be biased (or unbiased) without stating why.
- Saying internal data would not be independent (as a disadvantage) without stating why this is a disadvantage.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Explain the loss development and trending overlap fallacy.

### Explanation
The overlap fallacy is the argument that applying both loss development and loss trend double-counts, because the development of an accident year's losses over time already includes the effect of inflation.

It is false because the two adjustments do different things. **Development** brings each accident year's losses from their current immature value to their ultimate value — still at that accident year's cost level. **Trend** moves those ultimate losses from the historical accident year's cost level to the cost level of the future policy period. Development never takes losses to a later cost level and trend never takes them to ultimate, so applying both does not overlap.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to describe the overlap fallacy and state why it is false. In doing this, they needed to demonstrate their understanding of the loss development and loss trend concepts, and why they do not overlap.

Common errors included:

- No explicit statement that trending/development do not overlap.
- Inaccurate or missing descriptions of trending/development.
- Stating that the fallacy is true, or that the effects do have overlap.
