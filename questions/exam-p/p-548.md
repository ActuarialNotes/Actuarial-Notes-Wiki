Urn A contains five red chips and four white chips. Urn B contains four red chips and five white chips. Two chips are transferred from urn A to urn B. Then a single chip is drawn from urn B.

Calculate the probability that the chip drawn from urn B will be white.

- A) $0.455$
- B) $0.535$
- C) $0.545$
- D) $0.556$
- E) $0.571$

## Explanation

Condition on how many of the two transferred chips are white. The transfer is a draw of size 2 without replacement from urn A's 4 white and 5 red, so the count is hypergeometric:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[0 \text{ white}] &= \frac{\binom{5}{2}}{\binom{9}{2}} = \frac{10}{36} \\
P[1 \text{ white}] &= \frac{\binom{4}{1}\binom{5}{1}}{\binom{9}{2}} = \frac{20}{36} \\
P[2 \text{ white}] &= \frac{\binom{4}{2}}{\binom{9}{2}} = \frac{6}{36}
\end{align*}
$$

After the transfer urn B holds 11 chips, of which $5 + w$ are white when $w$ white chips arrived. Applying the law of total probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{white}] &= \frac{10}{36}\cdot\frac{5}{11} + \frac{20}{36}\cdot\frac{6}{11} + \frac{6}{36}\cdot\frac{7}{11} \\
                &= \frac{50 + 120 + 42}{396} \\
                &= \frac{212}{396} = \frac{53}{99} \\
                &= \mathbf{0.5354}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 0.545, is $6/11$ — the answer from assuming exactly one white chip transfers, which is the most likely single case but not the average. Choice (D), 0.556, is $5/9$, urn B's white proportion after ignoring the transfer's composition entirely.
