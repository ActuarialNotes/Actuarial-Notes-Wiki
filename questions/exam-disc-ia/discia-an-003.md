An insurer discloses that its loss reserve is an actuarial estimate, explains the methods and the main sources of uncertainty, and applied those methods without error. Two years later the reserve proves to have been 8% too low. Which statement is consistent with FASB Concepts Statement No. 8?

- A) The reserve lacked faithful representation, because it was not accurate in all respects
- B) The reserve lacked relevance, because an estimate that turns out wrong cannot affect decisions
- C) The reserve could still be a faithful representation, since free from error does not mean exact
- D) The reserve lacked verifiability, because observers could not have agreed on its exact amount

## Explanation

Free from error means there are no errors or omissions in describing the item and that the process was selected and applied without error. It does not mean perfectly accurate in all respects: an estimate can be faithfully represented if it is clearly described as an estimate, the nature and limits of the process are explained, and no errors were made in applying it. All three hold here. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — this reads "free from error" as "exact", which the framework explicitly rejects.
- **B** — relevance is the capacity to make a difference to decisions. A reserve estimate has predictive and confirmatory value whether or not it later proves exact.
- **D** — verifiability requires that knowledgeable, independent observers could reach consensus, not complete agreement, that the depiction is faithful; even a range can be verifiable.
