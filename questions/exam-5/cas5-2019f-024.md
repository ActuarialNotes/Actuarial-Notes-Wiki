Given the following information, as of December 31, 2018:

| Calendar Year | Paid Claims | Incurred Claims | Paid ULAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 18,700 | 35,500 | 1,870 |
| 2016 | 19,200 | 36,500 | 1,890 |
| 2017 | 18,900 | 36,400 | 1,910 |
| 2018 | 19,800 | 37,400 | 1,990 |

| Report Year | Earned Premium | Paid Claims | Reported Claims | Percent Unreported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 77,600 | 22,400 | 29,500 | 10.7% |
| 2016 | 78,000 | 14,300 | 26,200 | 23.1% |
| 2017 | 77,800 | 5,500 | 20,700 | 55.9% |
| 2018 | 77,900 | 2,800 | 19,000 | 76.3% |

- The expected claims ratio for this book of business is 45%.
- All policies are claims-made.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate unpaid ULAE at December 31, 2018 using the Kittel refinement.

### Answer
Approximately $3,745

### Explanation
**Step 1 — the Kittel ULAE ratio.** The Kittel refinement addresses the flaw in the classical paid-to-paid method for a growing book: some ULAE is incurred when a claim is *reported* and some when it is *paid*, so the denominator should reflect both. The base is the **average of paid and incurred claims**:

$$w = \frac{\text{Paid ULAE}}{0.5(\text{Paid Claims}) + 0.5(\text{Incurred Claims})}$$

| CY | Base | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| 2015 | $0.5(18{,}700) + 0.5(35{,}500) = 27{,}100$ | $1{,}870/27{,}100 = 0.0690$ |
| 2016 | 27,850 | $1{,}890/27{,}850 = 0.0679$ |
| 2017 | 27,650 | $1{,}910/27{,}650 = 0.0691$ |
| 2018 | 28,600 | $1{,}990/28{,}600 = 0.0696$ |

The four ratios show no trend, so the variation is treated as random and the straight average is selected: $w = 0.069$.

**Step 2 — ultimate claims by reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson.**

$$\text{Ultimate} = \text{Reported} + EP \times ECR \times \%\,\text{unreported}$$

| RY | Calculation | Ultimate |
|---|---|---|
| 2015 | $29{,}500 + 77{,}600(0.45)(0.107)$ | 33,236 |
| 2016 | $26{,}200 + 78{,}000(0.45)(0.231)$ | 34,308 |
| 2017 | $20{,}700 + 77{,}800(0.45)(0.559)$ | 40,271 |
| 2018 | $19{,}000 + 77{,}900(0.45)(0.763)$ | 45,747 |
| **Total** | | **153,562** |

**Step 3 — split the unpaid claims into their components.**

$$\text{Total paid} = 22{,}400 + 14{,}300 + 5{,}500 + 2{,}800 = 45{,}000$$

$$\text{Total reported} = 29{,}500 + 26{,}200 + 20{,}700 + 19{,}000 = 95{,}400$$

$$\text{Case} = 95{,}400 - 45{,}000 = 50{,}400$$

$$\text{IBNR} = 153{,}562 - 95{,}400 = 58{,}162$$

All policies are **claims-made**, so coverage is triggered by the report date and there can be no claims incurred but not yet *reported* attaching to these report years. **Pure IBNR (IBNYR) is therefore zero, and the entire 58,162 is IBNER** — development on claims already reported.

**Step 4 — apply the Kittel unpaid ULAE formula.** Pure IBNR carries full weight because none of its ULAE has been spent — the claim has not even been set up — while case and IBNER carry half weight because the reporting half of the ULAE has already been incurred on those claims:

$$\text{Unpaid ULAE} = w \times \left[\text{Pure IBNR} + 0.5(\text{Case} + \text{IBNER})\right]$$

$$= 0.069 \times \left[0 + 0.5(50{,}400 + 58{,}162)\right] = 0.069 \times 54{,}281 = \$3{,}745$$

(Selecting $w = 7\%$ instead gives \$3,800; both were accepted.)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to estimate the ULAE ratio under the Kittel refinement, use the reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson method to estimate total IBNR, and calculate unpaid ULAE under the Kittel assumptions.

Common mistakes included using the wrong denominator for the ULAE ratio; calculating IBNR, case and unpaid ULAE for only one year rather than all four; including the wrong components — ultimate or reported claims — in the Kittel formula; applying the wrong weights to the components; **treating the IBNR for claims-made policies as pure IBNR**; omitting the IBNR component entirely; and stating the Kittel formula without carrying out the calculation.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Fully describe how the calculation in part a. above would change if the policies for this book of business were occurrence instead of claims-made.

### Explanation
Under **occurrence** policies, coverage is triggered by the date of loss rather than the date of report, so claims that have occurred but have not yet been reported *are* covered. The IBNR would therefore no longer be entirely IBNER: it would split into **pure IBNR (IBNYR)** and **IBNER**, and the calculation would change in two ways.

**1. An additional estimate is required.** The 58,162 of total IBNR would have to be separated into its pure IBNR and IBNER components — a split that part a did not need, because claims-made coverage forced pure IBNR to zero.

**2. Pure IBNR enters the formula at full weight.** The Kittel formula is

$$\text{Unpaid ULAE} = w \times \left[\text{Pure IBNR} + 0.5(\text{Case} + \text{IBNER})\right]$$

so the pure IBNR component receives **100%** weight while case and IBNER continue to receive **50%**. The reasoning is that a claim not yet reported has had *none* of its ULAE spent — the full cost of setting up, handling and settling it lies ahead — whereas a reported claim has already consumed the reporting-and-setup half of its ULAE, leaving only the settlement half.

The practical consequence is that the unpaid ULAE estimate would be **higher** than the \$3,745 calculated in part a, since a portion of the 58,162 would move from half weight to full weight.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how the components of IBNR differ between claims-made and occurrence policies, and how the Kittel refinement treats pure IBNR (IBNYR) and IBNER differently. Common mistakes included failing to identify that occurrence policies would include pure IBNR, giving the wrong weights to pure IBNR and IBNER under the Kittel refinement, and answering from the perspective of claims-made rather than occurrence policies.
