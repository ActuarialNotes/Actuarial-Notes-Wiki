A workers compensation policy written January 1 for \$24,000 is billed in twelve monthly installments of \$2,000 at the end of each month. The insured pays nothing. At June 30, the six billed installments are unpaid, and the six not yet due are matched by \$12,000 of unearned premium. Under SSAP No. 6, how much of the premium receivable is nonadmitted at June 30?

- A) \$2,000
- B) \$6,000
- C) \$12,000
- D) \$24,000

## Explanation

Under SSAP No. 6, to the extent there is no related unearned premium, uncollected premium over 90 days due is nonadmitted, and if an installment is over 90 days due, that amount plus all future installments recorded on the policy are nonadmitted. The January installment is more than 90 days past due, so all \$12,000 of billed installments are nonadmitted. The \$12,000 booked but not yet due is offset by the matching unearned premium, so nothing more is nonadmitted now; SSAP No. 6's own illustration treats a monthly-billed policy this way. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — \$2,000 nonadmits only the January installment and ignores the rule for later installments on the same policy.
- **B** — \$6,000 nonadmits only the installments individually more than 90 days past due.
- **D** — \$24,000 also nonadmits the installments not yet due, which are matched by unearned premium.
