An insurer sells coverage with an attachment point of 5,000 and a layer limit of 5,000. The following table represents the expected cumulative severity distribution and limited expected severity for the current year at various limits:

| $l$ | $F(l)$ | $E[X;l]$ |
|---|---|---|
| 4,545 | 0.842 | 1,807 |
| 5,000 | 0.859 | 1,875 |
| 5,500 | 0.875 | 1,941 |
| 9,091 | 0.938 | 2,256 |
| 10,000 | 0.947 | 2,308 |
| 11,000 | 0.954 | 2,357 |

The actuary expects a 10% severity increase for all claim sizes next year.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the percent change in frequency of claims in the layer.

### Explanation
*Sample 1*

Current Layer Frequency = F(10,000)-F(5,000) = 0.947 – 0.958 (as printed; the table's F(5,000) is 0.859) = 0.088

Future Layer Frequency = F(10,000/1.1) – F(5,000/1.1) = 0.938 – 0.842 = 0.096

% Change Frequency = 9.09%

*Sample 2*

$$t_n = \frac{S\left(\frac{a}{t}\right)}{S(a)} = \frac{S(4545)}{S(5000)} = \frac{1 - .842}{1 - .859} = 1.12 \quad => 12.1\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to apply frequency and severity distributions to determine expected losses by layer.

Credit was given for 2 interpretations of "claims in the layer":

1. All the losses above 5,000
2. Only the losses that fell in the layer 5,000 to 10,000

Candidates were expected to calculate the effect of inflation on the frequency.

Most candidates were able to calculate this correctly and received full marks. A common mistake was multiplying by the trend factor (1.1).

## Part b (1 point)

Calculate the percent change in pure premiums in the layer.

### Explanation
*Sample 1*

$$\text{Current Layer Severity} = \frac{E[X;10000]-E[X;5000]}{F(10000)-F(5000)} = \frac{2308-1875}{0.088} = 4920$$

$$\text{Future Layer Severity} = \frac{1.1(E[X;9091] - E[X;4545])}{F(9091) - F(4545)} = \frac{1.1(2256 - 1807)}{0.096} = 1.1 \times 4677 = 5145$$

$$\%\ \text{Change severity} = \frac{5145}{4920} - 1 = 4.57\%$$

$$\%\ \text{Change PP} = (1 + 0.099)(1 + 0.0457) - 1 = 14.06\%$$

(0.099 as printed; the frequency change from part a is 9.09%)

*Sample 2*

$$t_s = \frac{t(E[X;10000/1.1] - E[X;5000/1.1])}{E[X;10000] - E[X;5000]} - 1 = \frac{1.1(2256 - 1807)}{2308 - 1875} - 1 = 14.06\%$$

*Sample 3*

$$\text{Current Layer Severity} = \frac{E[X;10000]-E[X;5000]}{1-F(5000)} = \frac{2308-1875}{1-.859} = 3070.9$$

$$\text{Future Layer Severity} = \frac{1.1(E[X;9091] - E[X;4545])}{1 - F(4545)} = \frac{1.1(2256 - 1807)}{1 - .842} = 3125.9$$

$$\%\ \text{Change severity} = \frac{3125.9}{3070.9} - 1 = 1.8\%$$

$$\%\ \text{Change PP} = (1.121)(1.018) - 1 = 14.1\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the effect of inflation on the severity and pure premium.

Common mistakes included:

- Multiplying the pure premium trend by the frequency trend
- Forgetting to multiply the severity trend by the frequency trend
- Forgetting to multiply the future layer severity by the trend factor (1.1)
