An article in BMJ Open (volume 11, issue 2, e041432, February 2021) that investigates whether cancer decedents who received palliative care early, more than six months before death, and those who did not had a different risk of using hospital care and supportive home care in the last month of life. It uses a population-based cohort of cancer decedents in Ontario, Canada from 2004 to 2014 and propensity score matching.

## Abstract
- Objectives: to investigate whether cancer decedents who received palliative care early (more than 6 months before death) and not-early had different risk of using hospital care and supportive home care in the last month of life.
- Design: a population-based cohort of cancer decedents between 2004 and 2014 in Ontario, using linked administrative data, with decedents propensity-score matched on receiving early or not-early palliative care using billing claims.
- Two groups of matched pairs were created, with and without Resident Assessment Instrument (RAI) home care assessments, to control for confounders such as health instability and pain.
- Results: in the No-RAI group, 36 238 pairs were identified; the early group had a lower absolute risk difference of dying in hospital (-10.0%) and receiving hospital care (-10.4%) and a higher absolute risk difference of receiving supportive home care (23.3%); the Yes-RAI group of 3586 pairs was similar in magnitude and direction.
