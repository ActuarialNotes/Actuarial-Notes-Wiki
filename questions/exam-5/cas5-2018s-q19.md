Briefly evaluate the appropriateness of the paid development technique and the reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique in the following scenarios:

## Part a (0.5 points)

The company pays a large shock loss early in the life of the accident year.

### Explanation
- **Paid development:** not appropriate. The shock loss sits in the latest paid diagonal and is multiplied by the full (large) paid development factor for an immature year, as though it were a normal part of a much larger ultimate — the estimate is overstated.
- **Reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson:** more appropriate. The shock loss enters the estimate once, in reported claims; the unreported portion comes from the expected claims, which are not leveraged by the loss, so the estimate is not distorted.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to briefly assess the appropriateness of two common techniques (paid development and reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson) for estimating ultimate claims under several scenarios. This required candidates to have knowledge of the mechanics of these two techniques, as well as how they react to changing conditions in the data being analyzed.

Candidates were expected to assess the impact of a large unexpected loss being paid early in the life of the accident year.

Common errors included:

- Stating that the Reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson (BF) technique overstated ultimates as a result of the shock loss, due to the concept of BF as a credibility-weighted average of development and expected claims techniques.
- Discussing the paid BF technique rather than the paid development technique.

## Part b (0.5 points)

The company has difficulty compiling an accurate history of rate changes.

### Explanation
- **Paid development:** appropriate. It uses only the claim triangle, so the rate change history does not enter it.
- **Reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson:** less appropriate. Its expected claims are usually an expected claims ratio applied to on-level earned premium, and without an accurate rate change history the premium cannot be put on level, so the expected claims — and the estimate — may be wrong. It remains usable only if the expected claims can be set another way (for example an expected pure premium times exposures).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to assess the impact of an unreliable history of rate changes on the two techniques.

Common errors included:

- Simply stating the reported B-F technique does not involve on-level earned premium without supplying an alternate source of expected claims other than the historic data.
- Assuming rate changes had an effect on paid claims development patterns.
- Discussing the paid BF technique rather than the paid development technique.

## Part c (0.5 points)

The company begins to settle claims earlier.

### Explanation
- **Paid development:** not appropriate. Faster settlement puts more paid claims on the latest diagonal than the historical pattern assumes, but the historical paid development factors are still applied to it, so the estimate is overstated.
- **Reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson:** appropriate. A change in settlement speed moves claims from case reserves into paid but leaves reported claims (paid + case) unchanged, so the reported development pattern — and the estimate — is not distorted (assuming case reserves are adequate).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to assess the impact of a speedup in claim settlement on the two techniques.

Common errors included:

- Asserting that the speedup in claim settlement would understate ultimate claims due to lowering development factors.
- Asserting that the speedup in payment would have a distorting effect on reported development factors.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Severity trend is higher than expected.

### Explanation
- **Paid development:** appropriate. It projects from the actual claims, which already reflect the higher severity, so the estimate responds to it.
- **Reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson:** less appropriate. Its expected claims rest on an expected claims ratio built with a lower severity trend, so the unreported portion — the largest part for immature years — is understated, and the estimate responds only slowly to the higher trend.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to assess the impact of an unexpectedly high severity trend on the two techniques.

Common errors included:

- Reversing the assessment of appropriateness, claiming that the development technique would overstate and the Bornhuetter-Ferguson would temper the overstatement.
- Asserting that the paid triangle needed to be adjusted via Berquist-Sherman techniques.
