Given the following information as of December 31, 2014:

Paid Claims ($) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 200 | 560 | 570 |
| 2012 | 150 | 250 | 400 |
| 2013 | 150 | 350 | |
| 2014 | 50 | | |

Received Salvage and Subrogation ($) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 20 | 90 | 100 |
| 2012 | 16 | 40 | 70 |
| 2013 | 15 | 56 | |
| 2014 | 5 | | |

- Ultimate claims for accident year 2014 = $150.
- There is no development beyond 36 months.
- A simple all-year average is used for all development factors.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Estimate the ultimate salvage and subrogation for accident year 2014 using the development technique.

### Explanation
Develop received salvage and subrogation on its own triangle:

| Accident Year | 12-24 | 24-36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 4.500 | 1.111 |
| 2012 | 2.500 | 1.750 |
| 2013 | 3.733 | |
| **Selected** | **3.578** | **1.431** |
| **Age-to-ultimate** | **5.118** | **1.431** |

$$
\text{AY 2014 ultimate S\&S} = 5 \times 3.578 \times 1.431 = \$25.59
$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the ultimate salvage and subrogation using the development technique given paid claims and received salvage and subrogation triangles.
A common mistake was not calculating the development triangle.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Estimate the ultimate salvage and subrogation for accident year 2014 using a ratio approach.

### Explanation
Take the ratio of received salvage and subrogation to paid claims first, then develop the ratio — far more stable than developing the small S&S amounts directly.

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 0.100 | 0.161 | 0.175 |
| 2012 | 0.107 | 0.160 | 0.175 |
| 2013 | 0.100 | 0.160 | |
| 2014 | 0.100 | | |

| Accident Year | 12-24 | 24-36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 1.607 | 1.092 |
| 2012 | 1.500 | 1.094 |
| 2013 | 1.600 | |
| **Selected** | **1.569** | **1.093** |
| **Age-to-ultimate** | **1.714** | **1.093** |

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Ultimate ratio} &= 0.100 \times 1.714 \\
                      &= 0.1714 \\
\text{AY 2014 ultimate S\&S} &= 150 \times 0.1714 \\
                              &= \$25.72
\end{align*}
$$

An additive development of the same ratios (selected 0.058 and 0.015 age-to-age, 0.073 to ultimate) gives $0.100 + 0.073 = 0.173$ and an ultimate of $150 \times 0.173 = \$25.93$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the ultimate salvage and subrogation using the ratio approach. Candidates were expected to calculate the Salvage+Subrogation-to-paid claims triangle, calculate the development triangle of the ratios, and apply the selected CDF to the undeveloped ratio to calculate ultimate salvage and subrogation.
Common mistakes included:

- Selecting directly an ultimate ratio instead of calculating development factors and applying the CDF.
- Selecting development factors based on a single pair of ratios instead of using the whole triangle.
- Incorrectly calculating the CDF or the ultimate Salvage+Subrogation ratio or dollar amount.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Given the following additional information for accident year 2015 as of December 31, 2015:

- Ultimate claims = $175
- Salvage and subrogation received = $12

Recommend and briefly justify an ultimate salvage and subrogation estimate for accident year 2015.

### Explanation
Use the ratio approach: apply the selected ratio to accident year 2015's ultimate claims.

$$
\text{AY 2015 ultimate S\&S} = 175 \times 0.100 \times 1.714 = \$30
$$

The direct development technique in part a. is built on very small S&S amounts, so its factors are volatile and highly leveraged — a small change at 12 months moves the ultimate a long way. The ratio approach rides on paid claims, which are far more stable, and the ratios themselves are consistent across accident years.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to recognize that the development factors in part a) were highly leveraged and would result in a more volatile answer whereas the ratio approach provided stability. Candidates were expected to recommend the ratio approach and point out the stability of the ratio method over the highly leverage development method.
Common mistakes included:

- Recommending an ultimate using the development technique
- Using undeveloped Salvage+Subrogation to ultimate claims ratio
- Not adequately justifying the recommendation of the ratio approach
