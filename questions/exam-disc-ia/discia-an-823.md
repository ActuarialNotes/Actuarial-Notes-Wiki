A diner's customer burns her hand on a coffee pot. The owner immediately applies first aid and, wanting to keep her goodwill, promises to pay her \$5,000 for her trouble. The owner notifies the insurer the next day. Two months later the customer sues the diner and, in the same suit, names the diner's insurer as a defendant. Which statement is correct under the CGL conditions (CG 00 01 04 13)?

- A) The first aid voids coverage, because no insured may incur any expense without the insurer's consent
- B) The insurer must pay the \$5,000 promised, because the owner gave prompt notice
- C) The customer may join the insurer in her suit against the diner, because the insurer will pay any damages
- D) The \$5,000 promise is at the owner's own cost, and the customer may not join the insurer in the suit

## Explanation

Two conditions apply. Duties in the event of occurrence: no insured will, except at its own cost, voluntarily make a payment, assume an obligation or incur an expense, **other than for first aid**, without the insurer's consent — so the first aid is fine but the \$5,000 promise is the owner's own cost. Legal action against us: no one may join the insurer as a party in a suit asking for damages from an insured; a claimant can sue the insurer only on an agreed settlement or a final judgment. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — first aid is the stated exception to the voluntary payment rule.
- **B** — prompt notice doesn't turn a voluntary promise into a covered obligation.
- **C** — the legal action condition forbids joining the insurer in the suit against the insured.
