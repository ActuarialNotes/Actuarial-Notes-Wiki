For a general liability policy, loss amounts, $Y$, follow the Weibull distribution with probability density function:

$$
f(y) = e^{-\left(\frac{y}{\theta}\right)^2}\left(\frac{2}{y}\right)\left(\frac{y}{\theta}\right)^2, \qquad y > 0
$$

For reinsurance purposes we are interested in the distribution of the largest loss amount in a random sample of size 10, denoted by $Y_{(10)}$.

The $90^{\text{th}}$ percentile of $Y_{(10)}$ is equal to $k\theta$ for some constant $k$.

Calculate $k$.

- A) Less than 1.2
- B) At least 1.2, but less than 1.6
- C) At least 1.6, but less than 2.0
- D) At least 2.0, but less than 2.4
- E) At least 2.4

## Explanation

The largest of 10 independent losses is at most $y$ only if all ten are, so its CDF is the tenth power of the Weibull CDF.

The given density is a Weibull with shape 2, whose CDF is

$$
F(y) = 1 - e^{-(y/\theta)^2}
$$

(differentiating gives $e^{-(y/\theta)^2} \cdot 2y/\theta^2$, which is the density shown). Then

$$
F_{Y_{(10)}}(y) = \left[1 - e^{-(y/\theta)^2}\right]^{10}
$$

Set this equal to 0.9 at $y = k\theta$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\left[1 - e^{-k^2}\right]^{10} &= 0.9 \\
1 - e^{-k^2} &= 0.9^{0.1} \\
&= 0.989519 \\
e^{-k^2} &= 0.010481 \\
k^2 &= 4.5582 \\
k &= 2.135
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[2.0, 2.4)$, so the answer is **D**.
