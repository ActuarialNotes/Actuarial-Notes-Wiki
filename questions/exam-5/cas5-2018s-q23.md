Given the following information as of December 31, 2017:

| Calendar Year | Paid ULAE (\$) | Paid Claims (\$) | Incurred Claims (\$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 25,000 | 62,500 | 250,000 |
| 2015 | 50,000 | 250,000 | 500,000 |
| 2016 | 75,000 | 500,000 | 750,000 |
| 2017 | 90,000 | 600,000 | 900,000 |

**Accident Year 2017**

|  |  |
|---|---|
| \$150,000 | Case outstanding |
| \$100,000 | Total IBNR |
| 60% | Percent of total IBNR attributed to future case development on known claims |

## Part a (1.25 points)

Estimate unpaid ULAE for accident year 2017 using the classical technique where 100% of the paid to paid ratio is applied only to the claims incurred but not yet reported (IBNYR). Briefly justify the selected ULAE ratio.

### Explanation
**Paid ULAE to paid claims, by calendar year:** 2014 25,000/62,500 = 0.40; 2015 0.20; 2016 0.15; 2017 0.15.

Select **0.15**: the ratio has fallen and stabilized at 0.15 in the two latest years, and 2014's 0.40 is an outlier (paid claims were very small that year, as they are early in a book's life), so including it would overstate the ratio.

**Reserves for AY 2017:** IBNER (future development on known claims) $= 60\% \times 100{,}000 = 60{,}000$; IBNYR $= 40{,}000$; case outstanding $= 150{,}000$.

The classical technique assumes half of a claim's ULAE is spent when it is opened and half when it is closed, so 50% of the ratio applies to case and IBNER (already reported) and 100% to IBNYR:

$$\text{Unpaid ULAE} = 0.15 \times \big[0.5 \times (150{,}000 + 60{,}000) + 1.0 \times 40{,}000\big] = 0.15 \times 145{,}000 = 21{,}750$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to estimate unpaid ULAE using the classical technique and Kittel refinement and provide a scenario that distorts the classical technique along with the details of how the Kittel refinement technique addresses the distortion.

Candidates were expected to calculate calendar year paid ULAE to paid claims ratios, calculate accident year IBNER and IBNYR, and estimate unpaid ULAE using the classical technique, including the selection and justification of a reasonable ULAE ratio.

Common errors included:

- Calculating ratios that use a base other than paid claims.
- Selecting a ratio that included calendar year 2014, an outlier within the four years, which fails to properly adjust for the change going forward.
- Failing to justify the selected paid ULAE to paid claims ratio.
- Failing to or incorrectly calculating IBNER and IBNYR.
- Applying an assumption other than the classical 50/50 assumption.
- Omitting case reserves, IBNER, or IBNYR from the unpaid calculation.
- Failing to use the accident year 2017 reserve amounts given.
- Failing to apply half of the ratio to the case reserves and IBNER.

## Part b (1 point)

Estimate unpaid ULAE for accident year 2017 using the Kittel refinement where 100% of the paid to paid ratio is applied only to the IBNYR.

### Explanation
Kittel's refinement divides paid ULAE by the average of paid and incurred claims, since ULAE is spent both when claims are reported and when they are paid:

| CY | Paid ULAE ÷ 1/2(paid + incurred) |
|---|---|
| 2014 | 25,000 / 156,250 = 0.160 |
| 2015 | 50,000 / 375,000 = 0.133 |
| 2016 | 75,000 / 625,000 = 0.120 |
| 2017 | 90,000 / 750,000 = 0.120 |

Selecting 0.12 (the two stable latest years) with the same 50/50 assumption:

$$\text{Unpaid ULAE} = 0.12 \times \big[0.5 \times (150{,}000 + 60{,}000) + 40{,}000\big] = 0.12 \times 145{,}000 = 17{,}400$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to apply the Kittel refinement by calculating ratios of calendar year paid ULAE to average paid and incurred claims and applying this ratio appropriately to case reserves, IBNER, and IBNYR.

Common errors included:

- Calculating ratios that use a base of incurred claims only.
- Calculating ratios that use a base of paid claims only.
- Applying an assumption other than the classical 50/50 assumption.
- Omitting case reserves, IBNER, or IBNYR from the unpaid calculation.
- Failing to use the AY 2017 reserve amounts given.
- Failing to apply half of the ratio to the case reserves and IBNER.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Identify a scenario that distorts the classical technique and briefly explain how it is addressed by the Kittel refinement.

### Explanation
A **growing book of business** (or any period in which claim volume is changing) distorts the classical technique. In a growing book, the ULAE spent opening the many new claims is paid now, while the claim payments on those claims come later, so paid ULAE runs ahead of paid claims and the paid-to-paid ratio is overstated. Kittel's refinement uses the average of paid and incurred claims as the base; incurred claims grow with the newly reported claims, so the base keeps pace with the ULAE spent on reporting them, and the ratio is no longer distorted by the growth.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify one scenario that distorts the classical technique and explain how the Kittel refinement technique addresses the distortion.

Common Classical distortion scenario errors included:

- Failing to identify a scenario in which the ULAE/claims relationship could clearly change over time.

Common Kittel adjustment errors included:

- Stating that the Kittel technique uses incurred claims only.
- Simply stating that the Kittel technique adjusts for the distortion without providing an explanation.
