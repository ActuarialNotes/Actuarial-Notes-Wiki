In a certain region, the number of earthquakes in the next decade is modeled by a Poisson distribution. The probability of exactly two earthquakes is 0.43 times the probability of exactly one earthquake.

Calculate the probability of at least three earthquakes in the next decade in this region.

- A) $0.010$
- B) $0.012$
- C) $0.045$
- D) $0.056$
- E) $0.080$

## Explanation

The stated relationship is between consecutive Poisson probabilities, whose ratio has a simple form:

$$
\frac{P[X=2]}{P[X=1]} = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^2/2!}{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^1/1!} = \frac{\lambda}{2}
$$

Setting that equal to 0.43:

$$
\lambda = 0.86
$$

Now take the complement of the three smallest counts:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \geq 3] &= 1 - e^{-0.86}\left[1 + 0.86 + \frac{(0.86)^2}{2}\right] \\
            &= 1 - (0.42316)(2.2298) \\
            &= \mathbf{0.056}
\end{align*}
$$

Working with the ratio avoids ever writing out the exponentials — the $e^{-\lambda}$ factors cancel, leaving a one-step solve for $\lambda$. Choice (E), 0.080, is roughly what $\lambda = 1$ would give; the answer is sensitive to $\lambda$ here because the tail beyond 3 is small and falls steeply.
