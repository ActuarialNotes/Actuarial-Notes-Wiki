An insurance policy covers losses incurred by a policyholder, subject to a deductible of 20. Losses incurred follow a distribution with probability density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
kx^{0.25}, & \text{for } 0 < x < 100 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $k$ is a constant.

Calculate the 90th percentile of losses that exceed the deductible.

- A) $89$
- B) $90$
- C) $91$
- D) $92$
- E) $93$

## Explanation

First normalize the density:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= \int_0^{100} kx^{0.25}\,dx \\
  &= \frac{k}{1.25}x^{1.25}\bigg|_0^{100} \\
  &= \frac{k}{1.25}100^{1.25}
\end{align*}
$$

so $k = 1.25/100^{1.25}$ and the distribution function is

$$
F(x) = \frac{x^{1.25}}{100^{1.25}}
$$

The phrase "losses that exceed the deductible" makes this a **conditional** percentile: the population of interest is $\{X > 20\}$, not all losses. So find $p$ with

$$
\begin{align*}
0.9 &= P[X \leq p \mid X > 20] \\
    &= \frac{F(p) - F(20)}{1 - F(20)} \\
    &= \frac{p^{1.25} - 20^{1.25}}{100^{1.25} - 20^{1.25}}
\end{align*}
$$

The $100^{1.25}$ denominators cancel throughout. Solving for $p$:

$$
\begin{align*}
p^{1.25} &= 0.9\left(100^{1.25} - 20^{1.25}\right) + 20^{1.25} \\
         &= 0.9(316.23 - 42.29) + 42.29 \\
         &= 288.83 \\
p        &= 288.83^{1/1.25} \\
         &= \mathbf{93.01}
\end{align*}
$$

Ignoring the conditioning and solving $F(p) = 0.9$ directly gives $p = 100(0.9)^{0.8} = 91.9$, close to choice (D) — the answer choices are deliberately clustered so that skipping the conditioning still lands on a plausible-looking number. Conditioning on exceeding 20 removes the smallest losses, which pushes every percentile up.
