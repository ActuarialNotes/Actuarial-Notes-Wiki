Given the following information below regarding a series of bonds:

| Bond | Maturity (Years) | Face Value | Annual Coupon |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1.0 | \$1000 | 0% |
| B | 2.0 | \$1000 | 0% |
| C | 3.0 | \$1000 | 3% |
| D | 3.0 | \$1000 | 0% |

- The price of bond A is \$951.23.
- The two-year spot rate is 8%, continuously compounded.
- The price of bond C is \$901.78.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Plot the pure yield curve.

### Explanation
Sample 1:

Bond A: price = Face value $(1+r_1)^{-1}$; $951.23 = 1000e^{-r_1}$; $r_1 = 5\%$

Assuming all rates should be continuously compounded as that is how 2 yr spot rate is provided

$r_2 = 8\%$

Bond C: $901.78 = 30e^{-r_1} + 30e^{-r_2 \times 2} + 1030e^{-r_3 \times 3}$ (coupon $.03 \times 1000$); $3r_3 = .1948$; $r_3 = 6.49\%$

(Graph: yield against time — $(1,\ .05)$, $(2,\ .08)$, $(3,\ .0649)$, joined by straight lines.)

Sample 2:

Assume all interest rates are annual unless specified otherwise

$$951.23 = \frac{1000}{1 + y_1} \qquad y_1 = .0513$$

$$e^{-.08(2)} = \frac{1}{(1 + y_2)^2} \qquad y_2 = .0833$$

$$901.78 = \frac{30}{1.0513} + \frac{30}{1.0833^2} + \frac{1030}{(1 + y_3)^3} \qquad y_3 = .0671$$

(Graph: E(r) against time — $(1,\ .0513)$, $(2,\ .0833)$, $(3,\ .0671)$, joined by straight lines.)

### Examiner Report
General Commentary

Most candidates scored well. Average scores were around 1.6 out of 2.0 points. 37% of the candidates got a perfect score, while another 20% scored 1.75 out of 2.0 points.

Candidate should be able to calculate the rates at 3 different maturities, and present it clearly with a graph, in order to obtain full credit.

Common errors were due to miscalculation. Other candidates confused forward rates with zero rates.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Explain the shape of the pure yield curve plotted in part a. above using a term-structure theory of interest rates.

### Explanation
The yield curve above reflects the expectations theory of term-structure. Rates are expected to rise for 2 yrs, but then decrease after that.

Alternatively:

Segmentation theory – the yields offered at each point in time (short, medium, long term) are driven by the supply and demand for bonds at that time.

There must be a low demand for bonds at t=2, which is why it is offering a higher rate than at times 1 and 3.

### Examiner Report
Candidates should be able to explain the different term structure theories to obtain full credit.

Most  candidates  got  this  part  correctly.  One  of  the  common  mistakes  here  is  the  demand  supply relationship and how it affects the yields. A number of candidates incorrectly thought that a higher demand would correspond to a higher yield.
