The annual amount a travel insurance company pays for claims has standard deviation 3. The 52 weekly amounts the company pays for claims are independent and have the same standard deviation.

Calculate the standard deviation of each of these weekly amounts.

- A) $\dfrac{3}{52^2}$
- B) $\dfrac{3}{52}$
- C) $\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{52}}$
- D) $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{52}}$
- E) $3\sqrt{52}$

## Explanation

The annual total is the sum of 52 independent weekly amounts, so **variances** add. Writing $\sigma$ for the common weekly standard deviation:

$$
\begin{align*}
3^2 &= 52\sigma^2 \\
\sigma^2 &= \frac{9}{52} \\
\sigma &= \mathbf{\frac{3}{\sqrt{52}}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), $3/52$, divides the annual standard deviation by 52 rather than by $\sqrt{52}$ — that would be right for the *variance*, not for the standard deviation. Choice (C) takes the square root of $3/52$ instead of dividing 3 by $\sqrt{52}$; the two differ because the 3 must be squared before the division.

Numerically the answer is $0.416$, so the weekly figure is about one seventh of the annual one — not one fifty-second. Aggregation multiplies a standard deviation by $\sqrt{n}$, which is why an annual total is far less than 52 times as volatile as a single week.
