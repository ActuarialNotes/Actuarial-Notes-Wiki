You are given the following statements to evaluate regarding the benefits of using a multilevel approach in your Bayesian model:

I. They provide improved estimates when different clusters have a different number of observations in the training dataset.
II. Estimating from the posterior distribution is simpler.
III. The models produced are always easier to understand.

Determine which of the above statements are benefits of a Bayesian multilevel modelling approach.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I is a benefit.** Unbalanced clusters are exactly where multilevel modelling earns its keep. Partial pooling shrinks each cluster's estimate toward the overall mean by an amount that depends on how much data that cluster has — a cluster with two observations is pulled hard toward the grand mean, one with two hundred is left close to its own average. This is the credibility idea, and it automatically avoids the overfitting that no-pooling produces on sparse clusters.

**II is not a benefit.** A multilevel model adds hyperparameters and a hierarchical dependence structure, so the posterior is *harder* to work with, not simpler. It generally requires MCMC where a simpler model might have a closed form, and it is more prone to sampling difficulties.

**III is not a benefit.** "Always" makes this indefensible. Extra levels, varying effects and hyperpriors add interpretive burden; a multilevel model is often harder to explain to a non-technical audience than a single-level one.

Only statement I, so the answer is **A**.
