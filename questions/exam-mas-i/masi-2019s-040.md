You are fitting a model to predict the height of an adult male using shoe size as a predictor. You have a sample size of 200.

Determine which one of the following model forms will both have a discontinuity in the fitted curve and most likely overfit the data.

- A) Piecewise cubic with two knots
- B) Piecewise linear with two knots
- C) Cubic spline with one knot
- D) Natural cubic spline with two knots
- E) Smoothing spline with 10 degrees of freedom

## Explanation

Two properties to check: whether the fitted curve can jump at a knot, and how flexible (how many degrees of freedom) the fit is.

**Discontinuity.** A spline is a piecewise polynomial *constrained* to join up at the knots — a cubic spline is continuous with continuous first and second derivatives, a natural cubic spline adds linearity beyond the boundary knots, and a smoothing spline is a natural cubic spline with a knot at every $x_i$. So C, D and E are all continuous. A plain piecewise polynomial fits a separate polynomial in each region with no constraints at all, so its fitted curve jumps at the knots: only **A** and **B** have a discontinuity.

**Overfitting.** Two knots make three regions, each with its own polynomial:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Piecewise cubic:} \quad 3 \times 4 &= 12 \text{ parameters} \\
\text{Piecewise linear:} \quad 3 \times 2 &= 6 \text{ parameters}
\end{align*}
$$

The piecewise cubic is twice as flexible, so it is the one more likely to chase noise in a relationship like height against shoe size.

The discontinuous form most likely to overfit is the piecewise cubic with two knots, so the answer is **A**.
