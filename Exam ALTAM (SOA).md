# Exam ALTAM
The ALTAM Exam is a three-hour exam consisting of six questions, worth a total of 60 points.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The exam will be taken at Prometric testing centers. The questions will be displayed on the computer. Candidates will also be provided with an Excel Workbook. The Excel Workbook will contain one question. It will also contain tables and formulas that may be used for any of the six questions.
>
> Five of the questions are to be answered in pen in exam answer booklets provided by Prometric. For these questions candidates may use the Excel Tables, and may use Excel for calculations, but only the answers provided in the exam answer booklet will be graded.
>
> One of the questions will be answered in the Excel Workbook. For this question, only the information provided in the Excel Workbook will be graded. Candidates may use the scratch paper booklet for rough notes, but nothing written on the scratch paper will be graded.
>
> At the end of the exam candidates will upload their Excel Workbook for grading the Excel question and will submit their exam answer booklets to Prometric to forward to the SOA for grading the questions answered in the answer booklets.
>
> Knowledge of the FM, P, and FAM exams, and the Mathematical Statistics VEE material is assumed for the ALTAM Exam.
>
> Exam Registration Candidates may register online or with an application.
>
> Introductory Study Note The Introductory Study Note has a complete listing of all study notes as well as errata and other important information.
>
> Past Exams Past LTAM Exams from October 2018 through 2022 and past ALTAM Exams from Spring 2023 through present are available on the SOA website.
>
> Updates Candidates should be sure to check the Updates page on the exam home page periodically for additional corrections or notices.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Survival Models for Contingent Cash Flows {10–20%}
> The Candidate will understand key concepts concerning [[Multiple|multiple]] state [[Mortality-morbidity|mortality/morbidity]] and [[Individual-joint Life Mortality|individual/joint life mortality]] models for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Understand and explain [[Feature|features]] of typical [[Mortality|mortality curves]] and [[Mortality|heterogeneities]] in [[Mortality|mortality]].
> 2. Apply [[Multiple State Model|Markov multiple]] state models to [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-contingent life]] and [[Health Insurance|long-term health insurance benefits]], and [[Continuing Care Retirement Community|Continuing Care Retirement Communities]] ([[CCRC|CCRCs]]).
> 3. Understand and critique the assumptions underlying [[Multiple State Model|Markov multiple]] state models for [[Long-Term Insurance Coverage|long-term insurance benefits]].
> 4. Derive and apply [[Kolmogorov's Forward Equation|Kolmogorov’s forward equations]] for [[Multiple State Model|continuous time Markov multiple]] state models.
> 5. Calculate [[Multiple State Model|state-dependent probabilities]] for [[Multiple State Model|continuous time Markov]] models.
> 6. Apply the [[Chapman-Kolmogorov Equations|Chapman-Kolmogorov equations]] to calculate [[Transition Probability Matrix|discrete time transition probabilities]] in the [[Markov]] model.
> 7. Construct and [[Multiple Decrement Model|deconstruct multiple decrement tables]] using the associated [[Multiple Decrement Model|single decrement]] models and appropriate [[Fractional Age Assumption|fractional age]] assumptions.
> 8. Calculate [[Maximum Likelihood Estimate|maximum likelihood estimates]] of [[Transition Intensity|transition intensities]] and [[Probability|probabilities]] for [[Multiple|multiple]] state and [[Multiple Decrement Model|multiple decrement]] models, assuming [[Transition Intensity|piecewise constant transition intensities]].
> 9. Calculate [[Approximate Confidence Interval|approximate confidence intervals]] for the [[Estimator|estimators]] in Topic 1(h), using [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation|asymptotic properties]] of the [[Maximum Likelihood Estimator|maximum likelihood estimators]].

> [!example]- Premium and Policy Valuation for Long-Term State-Dependent Coverages {12–20%}
> The Candidate will be able to perform calculations on the present value [[Random Variable|random variables]] associated with [[Benefit|benefits]] and [[Expenses|expenses]] for [[Future Lifetime Random Variable|single life]], [[Joint Life|joint life]], [[CCRC|CCRCs]], or other [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Define and interpret [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent insurance]] and [[Annuity|annuity]] present value [[Random Variable|random variables]] and identify and calculate their expected [[Value|values]].
> 2. Derive and apply [[Two-Term|two-term]] and [[Woolhouse's Formula|three-term Woolhouse approximations]] for calculating expected present [[Value|values]] of [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent cash flows]].
> 3. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent life insurance]], [[Health Insurance|long-term health insurance]], and [[CCRC|CCRCs]] using the [[Equivalence Principle|equivalence principle]].
> 4. Calculate [[Policy Value|policy values]] for [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent life insurance]], [[Health Insurance|long-term health insurance]], and [[CCRC|CCRCs]].
> 5. Identify and apply [[Thiele's Differential Equation|Thiele’s differential equation]] in a [[Future Lifetime Random Variable|single life]] or [[Multiple|multiple]] state setting.

> [!example]- Joint Life Insurance and Annuities {8–16%}
> The Candidate will understand key concepts concerning [[Estimation|estimation]] and construction of [[Multiple|multiple]] state and [[Joint Life|joint life]] models for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Understand how [[Joint Life|joint-life mortality]] can be [[Modelled|modelled]] using (i) a [[Joint Life|time-to-status-failure random variable]], and ([[Ii|ii]]) a [[Multiple|multiple]] state model.
> 2. Understand the implications of [[Independence|independence]] or [[Dependence|dependence]] of [[Future Lifetime Random Variable|future lifetimes]] in both [[Version|versions]] of the [[Joint Life|joint life]] model from 3(a). Identify sources of [[Dependence|dependence]] and understand how they are accommodated in the models.
> 3. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuities|annuities]] on [[Joint Life|joint lives]] using the [[Equivalence Principle|equivalence principle]].
> 4. Calculate [[Policy Value|policy values]] for [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuities|annuities]] on [[Joint Life|joint lives]].

> [!example]- Profit Analysis {10–20%}
> The Candidate will be able to analyze [[Profit Vector|emerging surplus]], and to set [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] and [[Reserve|reserves]], using [[Profit Testing|profit testing]].
>
> 1. Calculate and interpret expected [[Profit|profit]] and [[Actual Profit|actual profit]],
> 2. Analyze and interpret [[Gain|gains]] by [[Source|source]].
> 3. Calculate and interpret [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], [[Net|net]] present value, [[Internal Rate of Return|internal rate]] of [[Return|return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback period]] for [[Life Insurance|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]], and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
> 4. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[Life Insurance|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] based on a specified [[Profit|profit]] objective.
> 5. Calculate [[Reserve|reserves]] for [[Life Insurance|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] using [[Profit Testing|profit testing]].

> [!example]- Pension Plans and Retirement Benefits {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand how [[Pension Plan|retirement benefits]] are [[Accrued|accrued]], valued, and [[Funded|funded]].
>
> 1. Calculate [[Replacement|replacement]] ratios for Defined [[Contribution]] ([[Defined Contribution Plan|DC]]), and Defined [[Benefit]] ([[Defined Benefit Plan|DB]]) [[Plan|plans]], including [[Defined Benefit Plan|final average salary]] ([[FAS]]), [[Defined Benefit Plan|career average earnings]] ([[CAE]]), and [[Defined Benefit Plan|career average revalued earnings]] ([[CARE]]) [[Plan|plans]].
> 2. Calculate the [[Defined Contribution Plan|required contribution rate]] to [[Meet|meet]] a [[Replacement Ratio|target replacement]] ratio for a [[Defined Contribution Plan|DC plan participant]], using a [[Deterministic|deterministic]] approach.
> 3. Identify, interpret, and apply [[Service Table|service table]] and [[Salary Scale|salary scale]] functions for [[Pension Plan|pension plan valuation]].
> 4. For a [[Defined Benefit Plan|DB plan]], calculate and interpret [[Accrued Benefit|accrued benefits]], including [[Benefit|benefits]] on [[Accrued Benefit|early exit]] from the [[Plan|plan]].
> 5. For a [[Defined Benefit Plan|DB plan]], calculate and interpret the actuarial [[Actuarial Accrued Liability|accrued liability]] and the [[Normal Cost|normal cost]] for [[Benefits Payable|benefits payable]] on [[Service Table|age retirement]] or [[Accrued Benefit|early exit]] using the [[Projected Unit Credit Method|projected unit credit]] ([[PUC]]) and [[Traditional Unit Credit Method|traditional unit credit]] ([[TUC]]) [[Valuation|valuation]] methods.

> [!example]- Universal Life Insurance {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand the [[Policy|policy]] design and [[Benefits Payable|benefits payable]] under Type A and Type B [[Universal Life Insurance|Universal Life contracts]] and be able to assess and quantify [[Universal Life Insurance|account values]], [[Insurance Premium|premiums]], and [[Reserve|reserves]].
>
> 1. Understand the [[Cash Flow|cashflows]] and calculate [[Universal Life Insurance|account values]] and [[Benefit|benefits]] under Type A and Type B [[Universal Life Insurance|Universal Life policies]].
> 2. Calculate [[Reserve|reserves]] for [[No-Lapse Guarantee|no-lapse guarantees]].
> 3. Use [[Profit Testing|deterministic profit testing]] to calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] or [[Reserve|reserves]], and assess [[Profit Vector|emerging surplus]] for [[Universal Life Insurance|Universal Life insurance]], including [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], [[Net|net]] present value, [[Internal Rate of Return|internal rate]] of [[Return|return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback periods]], under [[Best|best]] estimate or [[Stress|stress]] test assumptions.

> [!example]- Embedded Options in Life Insurance and Annuity Products {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand various types of [[Equity-Linked Insurance|equity-linked life insurance guarantees]], [[Option|options]] that are [[Embedded|embedded]] in [[Life Insurance|life insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] and be able to price, [[Reserve|reserve]], and [[Hedging|hedge]] the [[Risk|risk]] inherent in these [[Option|options]].
>
> 1. Define and calculate [[Payoff|payoffs]] under each of the following [[Equity-Linked Insurance|options embedded]] in [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]]:
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit|Guaranteed minimum death benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Maturity Benefit|Guaranteed minimum maturity benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Income Benefit|Guaranteed minimum income benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Withdrawal Benefit|Guaranteed minimum withdrawal benefit]]
> 2. Value the following [[Equity-Linked Insurance|options embedded]] in [[Insurance|insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]], using the [[Black-Scholes Model|Black-Scholes]] model:
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit|Guaranteed minimum death benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Accumulation-maturity Benefit|Guaranteed minimum accumulation/maturity benefit]]
> 3. Construct a [[Replicating Portfolio|replicating portfolio]] for the [[Option|options]] in 7(b) using [[Delta Hedging|delta-hedging]]
> 4. Understand and evaluate the [[Cost|costs]] associated with [[Delta Hedging|discrete-time rebalancing]].
> 5. Use [[Profit Testing|deterministic profit testing]] to assess [[Profit Vector|emerging surplus]] for [[Equity-Linked Insurance|equity-linked life insurance cash flows]], including [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], [[Net|net]] present value, [[Internal Rate of Return|internal rate]] of [[Return|return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback periods]], under [[Best|best]] estimate or [[Stress|stress]] test assumptions.
