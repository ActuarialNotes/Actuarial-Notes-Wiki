Let A, B and C be three events such that the following statements are true:

i) $P[A \cup B \cup C] = 1$
ii) $P[B] = 0.80$
iii) $P[A \mid B] = 0.15r$
iv) $P[A \cup C] = 0.17r$
v) B and C are mutually exclusive.

Calculate $r$.

- A) $0$
- B) $20/17$
- C) $100/29$
- D) $4$
- E) The correct answer is not given by (A), (B), (C) or (D).

## Explanation

Start from inclusion-exclusion on three events:

$$
P[A \cup B \cup C] = P[A] + P[B] + P[C] - P[A \cap B] - P[A \cap C] - P[B \cap C] + P[A \cap B \cap C]
$$

Statement (v) kills two terms at once: $B$ and $C$ disjoint means $P[B \cap C] = 0$, and the triple intersection sits inside $B \cap C$, so $P[A \cap B \cap C] = 0$ as well.

What remains regroups neatly, because $P[A] + P[C] - P[A \cap C]$ is exactly $P[A \cup C]$:

$$
P[A \cup B \cup C] = P[B] - P[A \cap B] + P[A \cup C]
$$

Now use (iii) via the multiplication rule, $P[A \cap B] = P[B]\,P[A \mid B] = (0.80)(0.15r) = 0.12r$, and substitute (i), (ii) and (iv):

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= 0.80 - 0.12r + 0.17r \\
1 &= 0.80 + 0.05r \\
r &= \mathbf{4.00}
\end{align*}
$$

The regrouping is what makes this tractable: $P[A]$ and $P[C]$ are never determined individually, only through the union $P[A \cup C]$ that statement (iv) hands you. Checking the answer, $P[A \mid B] = 0.60$ and $P[A \cup C] = 0.68$ — both valid probabilities, which confirms $r = 4$ rather than (E).
