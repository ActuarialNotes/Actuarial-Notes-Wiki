The joint distribution of television viewers' habits in the two entertainment categories of Sports and Movies and the one non-entertainment category of News is:

Regularly watches Sports:

| | Regularly watches News | Does not regularly watch News |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regularly watches Movies | 0.35 | 0.10 |
| Does not regularly watch Movies | 0.08 | 0.06 |

Does not regularly watch Sports:

| | Regularly watches News | Does not regularly watch News |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regularly watches Movies | 0.22 | 0.06 |
| Does not regularly watch Movies | 0.05 | 0.08 |

Calculate the variance of the number of entertainment categories regularly watched by those television viewers who regularly watch News.

- A) $0.242$
- B) $0.388$
- C) $0.400$
- D) $0.478$
- E) $0.571$

## Explanation

Restrict to the News column of both tables, then count how many of the two *entertainment* categories (Sports, Movies) each cell represents.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{2 categories (Sports and Movies)} &: 0.35 \\
\text{1 category} &: 0.08 \text{ (Sports only)} + 0.22 \text{ (Movies only)} = 0.30 \\
\text{0 categories} &: 0.05
\end{align*}
$$

These total $P[\text{News}] = 0.70$. Dividing by that gives the conditional distribution of the count $N$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[N=2 \mid \text{News}] &= \frac{0.35}{0.70} = 0.5 \\
P[N=1 \mid \text{News}] &= \frac{0.30}{0.70} = 0.42857 \\
P[N=0 \mid \text{News}] &= \frac{0.05}{0.70} = 0.07143
\end{align*}
$$

Now the moments:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[N] &= 0(0.07143) + 1(0.42857) + 2(0.5) = 1.42857 \\
E[N^2] &= 0(0.07143) + 1(0.42857) + 4(0.5) = 2.42857
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(N) &= 2.42857 - (1.42857)^2 \\
              &= \mathbf{0.3878}
\end{align*}
$$

News is a *non*-entertainment category, so it never contributes to the count — it only defines the conditioning event. Treating it as a third category would change every probability above; that reading is what the two-table presentation is designed to test.
