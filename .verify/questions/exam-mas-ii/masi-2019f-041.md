- claim: topic: Autocorrelation Function; wiki_link: Autocorrelation Function, Time Series, Covariance, Correlation Coefficient — on a question that asks for the sample lag-1 cross-correlation between two series.
- evidence: The paper (CAS MAS-I Fall 2019 Q41, PDF p.45, sha256:32e26dbc1bd463e053ea0f9116473551be6d1b2f35b6d3670a716ecb86a141bd) asks for the 'sample lag 1 cross-correlation' from a ccvf table, and the vault explanation correctly computes ccvf(1)/sqrt(acvf_x(0)·acvf_y(0)) = 3.422/4.4032 = 0.777 (C, = CAS key). Cross-correlation is a function of its own in the syllabus reading: Cowpertwait & Metcalfe introduce 'the cross-correlation function' in §3.2.2 (Springer Ch 3 preview, p.46, sha256:4e82ea222f4086d8fe28f8c2db0fff93f4c41b910d754e0d335f196b5d77fdf2 — https://page-one.springer.com/pdf/preview/10.1007/978-0-387-88698-5_3), and Penn State STAT 510 §8.2 defines the sample CCF as the sample correlations between x_{t+h} and y_t (https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat510/Lesson08). A case-insensitive grep for 'cross' in Concepts/Autocorrelation Function.md, Time Series.md, Covariance.md, Correlation Coefficient.md and Correlogram.md finds no mention of cross-correlation, and no Concepts/ page covers it, so a student who misses this question is routed to pages that do not teach the measure it tests.
