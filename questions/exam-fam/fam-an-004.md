An employer's pension plan pays each retiring member an annual life pension of 1.5% of final average salary for each year of service. Over the past ten years the plan's investments have earned much less than the valuation assumed, and its retirees are living longer than the valuation assumed.

Which of the following best describes who bears the cost of these two experiences?

- A) Members bear both, through pensions reduced in line with the fund's shortfall
- B) Members bear the investment shortfall; the employer bears the longer lifetimes
- C) Neither, since the employer's contribution is fixed as a percentage of payroll
- D) The employer bears both, through higher contributions; pensions are unchanged
- E) Members bear the longer lifetimes, through lower pensions for each year lived

## Explanation

The plan defines the **benefit** by a formula (accrual rate × service × final average salary), so it is a defined benefit plan. The member's pension depends only on the formula, not on how the fund performs or how long retirees live. Whatever the formula's benefits cost, the plan sponsor must fund: poor investment returns and longer lifetimes both increase the sponsor's required contributions. The sponsor bears **investment risk and longevity risk**, and the members' pensions are unchanged. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** describes a defined contribution plan, where the benefit is whatever the member's account can buy, so a shortfall in the fund lowers the benefit.
- **B** splits the risks, as if the pension were linked to the fund's returns but guaranteed for life. Under a formula benefit, the investment risk is the sponsor's too.
- **C** confuses a fixed *benefit* with a fixed *cost*. A fixed contribution rate is the defining feature of a defined contribution plan; in a defined benefit plan the contribution is whatever funding the promise requires.
- **E** has the life annuity backwards. A member who lives longer receives the formula pension for more years; the extra cost falls on the plan, not on the member's annual amount.
