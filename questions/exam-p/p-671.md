Two individuals were born today. The age at death of the first is normally distributed with mean 70 and standard deviation 14. The age at death of the second is normally distributed with mean 80 and standard deviation 20. The ages at death are independent.

Calculate the probability that the average age at death of the two individuals exceeds 80.

- A) $0.119$
- B) $0.238$
- C) $0.341$
- D) $0.381$
- E) $0.659$

## Explanation

Work with the sum first. The mean of the sum is $70 + 80 = 150$, and since the two ages are independent the variances add:

$$
\text{SD(sum)} = \left[14^2 + 20^2\right]^{0.5} = \sqrt{596} = 24.4131
$$

Halving turns the sum into the average — both the mean and the standard deviation scale by $1/2$:

$$
\text{mean} = \frac{150}{2} = 75, \qquad \text{SD} = \frac{24.4131}{2} = 12.2066
$$

The average of independent normals is normal, so

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\bar{X} > 80] &= P\!\left[Z > \frac{80 - 75}{12.2066}\right] \\
                &= P[Z > 0.4096] \\
                &= \mathbf{0.341}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 0.659, is the complement $P[\bar{X} < 80]$. Note the standard deviation of the average is *not* the average of the standard deviations, $(14+20)/2 = 17$: independence makes the average less variable than either individual age.
