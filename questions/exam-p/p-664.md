The amount of losses for barn fires is exponentially distributed with mean 20,000.

Determine the probability that at least nine of a random sample of ten barn fires will have losses in excess of 20,000.

- A) $\dfrac{9e - 10}{e^{10}}$
- B) $\dfrac{10e - 9}{e^{10}}$
- C) $\dfrac{(9 + e)\left(1 - \frac{1}{e}\right)^9}{e^{10}}$
- D) $10\left(\dfrac{e-1}{e^{10}}\right)$
- E) $9\left(\dfrac{e-1}{e}\right)^{10}$

## Explanation

First find the per-fire probability. With mean 20,000 the survival function gives

$$
P[X > 20{,}000] = e^{-\frac{1}{20{,}000}(20{,}000)} = e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e}
$$

Any exponential exceeds its own mean with probability $e^{-1}$, whatever that mean is.

Each of the ten fires independently exceeds 20,000 with this probability, so the count is binomial with $n = 10$ and $p = 1/e$. "At least nine" is two terms:

$$
\binom{10}{9}\left(\frac{1}{e}\right)^9\left(1 - \frac{1}{e}\right) + \binom{10}{10}\left(\frac{1}{e}\right)^{10}
$$

Putting both over $e^{10}$:

$$
= \frac{10(e-1) + 1}{e^{10}} = \mathbf{\frac{10e - 9}{e^{10}}}
$$

which is 0.000825.

Choice (D), $10\left(\frac{e-1}{e^{10}}\right)$, is the "exactly nine" term alone, with the all-ten case left out. "At least nine" has to include ten, and here that omitted term is more than half the total.
