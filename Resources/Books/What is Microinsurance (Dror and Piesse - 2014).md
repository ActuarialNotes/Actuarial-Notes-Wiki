Chapter 2 of *A Practical Guide to Impact Assessments in Microinsurance*, defining microinsurance by its two aspects — what constitutes insurance, and what is micro in microinsurance — through the target group, the product and the process. It describes the business models that deliver microinsurance, why it is an important risk management tool for low-income people in developing countries, and the challenges it faces in regulation, technology and risk management.

## 2.1 Introduction
- Latest estimates put microinsurance clients in the developing world at some 500 million; about 70% of microinsurance schemes are operated in Asia, historically concentrated in India and West Africa.
- Products cover health, life/funeral, disability, agriculture (crop-based weather index and livestock), property, credit life and disaster risks.

## 2.3 Business models for microinsurance
- At least four operating business models: the partner-agent model, the provider-driven model, the charitable insurance model and the mutual/cooperative insurance model; in reality schemes are often combinations of these and can change over time.
