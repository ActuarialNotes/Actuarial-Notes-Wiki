A company must pay liabilities of 1000 at the end of year 1 and $X$ at the end of year 2. The only investments available are:

- i) One-year zero-coupon bonds with an annual effective yield of 5%
- ii) Two-year bonds with a par value of 1000 and 10% annual coupons, with an annual effective yield of 6%

The company constructed a portfolio that creates an exact cash flow matching strategy for these liabilities. The total purchase price of this portfolio is 1783.76.

Calculate the amount invested in the one-year zero-coupon bonds.

- A) 784
- B) 831
- C) 871
- D) 915
- E) 935

## Explanation

Let $A$ be the redemption value of the zero-coupon bonds purchased and $B$ the number of two-year bonds purchased. Each two-year bond pays a coupon of 100 at time 1 and $1000 + 100 = 1100$ at time 2, and costs

$$P_B = 100(1.06)^{-1} + 1100(1.06)^{-2} = 94.3396 + 978.9961 = 1073.3357$$

The total purchase price is

$$1783.76 = A/1.05 + 1073.3357B = 0.95238A + 1073.3357B$$

To match the time-1 liability exactly, $A + 100B = 1000$, so $B = 10 - 0.01A$. Substituting:

$$
\begin{align*}
1783.76 &= 0.95238A + 10{,}733.357 - 10.733357A \\
9.780977A &= 8{,}949.597 \\
A &= 915
\end{align*}
$$

The amount invested in the one-year zero-coupon bonds is

$$915/1.05 = 871.43 \approx 871$$

(Then $B = 10 - 9.15 = 0.85$ and $X = 1100(0.85) = 935$, option E, which is the time-2 liability, not the amount asked for.)
