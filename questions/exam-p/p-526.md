A company insures homes in 50 different territories. The company models annual tornado losses within each territory by an exponential distribution with mean 100,000. Because of the local nature of most tornadoes, losses in one territory are independent of losses in other territories.

Calculate the approximate probability that total annual tornado losses over all 50 territories exceed 5.5 million.

- A) $0.14$
- B) $0.18$
- C) $0.24$
- D) $0.33$
- E) $0.46$

## Explanation

An exponential distribution has standard deviation equal to its mean, so each territory has mean 100,000 and standard deviation 100,000.

By the Central Limit Theorem, the total over 50 independent territories is approximately normal. Means add; **variances** add, so the standard deviation grows by $\sqrt{50}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[S]         &= 50(100{,}000) = 5{,}000{,}000 \\
\text{SD}(S) &= \sqrt{50}(100{,}000) = 707{,}107
\end{align*}
$$

Standardize the 5.5 million threshold:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S > 5{,}500{,}000] &= P\left[Z > \frac{5{,}500{,}000 - 5{,}000{,}000}{707{,}107}\right] \\
                     &= P[Z > 0.7071] \\
                     &= \mathbf{0.2398}
\end{align*}
$$

The individual losses are strongly right-skewed, but averaging 50 of them makes the normal approximation reasonable. Choice (E), 0.46, is roughly what the *exponential* tail $e^{-5.5/5}$ would suggest for a single territory scaled up — pooling reduces relative volatility, so a 10% overrun on the total is much less likely than a 10% overrun on any one territory.
