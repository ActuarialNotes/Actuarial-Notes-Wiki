| # of Points | Frequency | Severity | Pure Premium |

- All policies are semi-annual.
- Rates are to be in effect for 2 years.
- There is no development after 36 months.
- An underwriting change went into effect on July 1, 2017, materially changing the composition of the book of business.
- A planned rate change will go into effect on January 1, 2019.

## Part a (2 points)

Calculate the projected 2015 accident year loss and ALAE to be used in the rate change analysis. Justify any trend selections.

### Answer

Approximately \$17,735 (thousands)

### Explanation

**Development.** Accident year 2015 is at 36 months as of December 31, 2017 and there is no development after 36 months, so the \$15,000 is already at ultimate.

**Trend period.** Rates take effect January 1, 2019 and remain in effect for two years, so the last policy is written December 31, 2020 and the average written date is January 1, 2020. Policies are semi-annual, so the average loss date is one quarter later:

$$\text{Average loss date} = 1/1/2020 + 3 \text{ months} = 4/1/2020$$

The average accident date of AY 2015 is July 1, 2015, giving a total trend period of **4.75 years**.

**Trend selections.** The underwriting change on July 1, 2017 materially changed the book, so a **two-step trend** is required — the historical data before the change no longer describes the business being priced.

- *Frequency:* the long-term (16-point) trend of **+7%** describes the pre-change experience, and applies from 7/1/2015 to 7/1/2017 (**2.0 years**). After the underwriting change, the short-term 4-point trend of **−7%** is the only selection that reflects the new composition, and applies from 7/1/2017 to 4/1/2020 (**2.75 years**).
- *Severity:* the severity trend is stable at 5% across essentially every point selection, so a single **+5%** trend applies over the full **4.75 years**.

$$15{,}000 \times (1.07)^{2.0} \times (0.93)^{2.75} \times (1.05)^{4.75} = 15{,}000 \times 1.1449 \times 0.8191 \times 1.2608 = 17{,}735$$

*Alternatively*, a two-step **pure premium** trend is equally acceptable — the all-point (16-point) pure premium trend of 12% for the first step and the 4-point trend of −3% for the second:

$$15{,}000 \times (1.12)^{2.0} \times (0.97)^{2.75} = 17{,}304$$

A frequency selection of 6% (12-point) for the first step, paired with 5% severity, gives:

$$15{,}000 \times (1.06 \times 1.05)^{2.0} \times (0.93 \times 1.05)^{2.75} = 17{,}405$$

Any of these was accepted provided the selections were justified and the trend periods were correct.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to perform a two-step trend, choosing appropriate trends (either frequency and severity separately, or pure premium) and calculating appropriate trend periods, in order to project losses.

Common mistakes included performing a one-step trend, which fails to reflect the underwriting change; choosing trends not supported by the data provided; failing to justify the trend selections; using an incorrect projection date; and calculating the trend periods incorrectly.
