The number of hurricanes in a year is modeled by a Poisson distribution with mean 1. The number of hurricanes in different years are mutually independent.

Calculate the probability that there are exactly ten hurricanes in a decade, given that there were exactly eight hurricanes in the first seven years of the decade.

- A) $0.053$
- B) $0.125$
- C) $0.130$
- D) $0.191$
- E) $0.224$

## Explanation

Years are independent, so the first seven years and the last three are separate Poisson counts. Given that eight hurricanes have already occurred, reaching exactly ten over the decade requires exactly two in the remaining three years — and the count in those three years is unaffected by what happened earlier.

The number of hurricanes in 3 years is Poisson with mean $3(1) = 3$. Let $E$ be the event of exactly two hurricanes in the last three years:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[E] &= \frac{e^{-3}\,3^2}{2!} \\
     &= \frac{9e^{-3}}{2} \\
     &= \mathbf{0.2240}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 0.125, is the *unconditional* probability of exactly ten hurricanes in a decade, $e^{-10}10^{10}/10! = 0.1251$ — the answer if the information about the first seven years is discarded. Discarding it costs a lot here: eight hurricanes in seven years is a fast pace, and conditioning on it makes ten by year ten distinctly more likely.
