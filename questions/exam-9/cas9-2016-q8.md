Given the following quarterly historical information for a firm:

![Line chart titled Earnings (Million), quarterly from 2014-2 to 2016-2, vertical axis 0.0 to 3.0. Earnings are flat at roughly 0.4 to 0.6 from 2014-2 through 2015-4 (a small rise near 2015-1 and dip near 2015-2), then jump to 2.0 at 2016-1 and 2.5 at 2016-2.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas9-2016-q8-quarterly-earnings.png)

![Line chart titled Stock Price, quarterly from 2014-2 to 2016-2, vertical axis 0 to 70. The price is flat at roughly 12 to 14 from 2014-2 through 2015-4, then jumps to 50 at 2016-1 and about 65 at 2016-2.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas9-2016-q8-stock-price.png)

- The market forecasted earnings for 2016-3 are \$3.5 million.
- The market forecasted stock price for 2016-3 is \$90.

## Part a (1 point)

Identify and briefly describe two potential information processing errors in the scenario above.

### Explanation
Any two of the following, related to this firm's forecast:

- **Forecasting error** – too much weight is often put on recent experience when forecasting expected earnings/stock prices. Both were flat for a while and recently have had steep increases. Expectations are forecasted that these steep increases will continue instead of flattening out.
- **Overconfidence** – people sometimes place too much confidence in the precision of their estimates. The steep increase continuing is not a very conservative assumption, so predictors must be pretty confident in their skills to predict a continued drastic increase.
- **Sample size neglect and representativeness** – investors overestimate the credibility of a small sample, giving this information too much weight in their model. The investors may be identifying a growth pattern from limited data, i.e. growth from 2015-4 to 2016-2; such a short period is not adequate to credibly identify a pattern. With only 2 quarters of strong earnings growth, investors may be overreaching and overprojecting future earnings growth, possibly leading them to pay too much for the security.

### Examiner Report
- The candidates were expected to identify and describe two information processing errors
- Common errors made by candidates:
  - Listing "Conservatism"; while it is a possible information processing error as described in the exam material, it is not applicable to the specific stock forecast presented in the question. The forecast of a further jump in both earnings and price is indicative of an overreaction by analysts to the recent dramatic growth in earnings and stock price for this firm, whereas conservatism would lead investors to react more slowly to new information. A number of candidates suggested that the fact that the price chart didn't perfectly follow the earnings chart in some earlier quarters prior to 2016 was indicative of the presence of conservatism, but this was more representative of some minor random fluctuations rather than a delayed reaction to a trend.
  - The use of the statement "forecasting too far into the future" without further explanation or without further relating it to the specific stock in the questions was not a sufficient response for describing either the forecasting error or the sample size neglect error
  - Describing the sample size neglect error as "using data form a single firm" or discussing the small firm size instead of the small data sample size
  - Overconfidence error was frequently confused with investors optimism about the stock price outlook
  - Post earnings announcement drift was listed as an information processing error

## Part b (0.5 points)

An individual investor believes the forecast contains information processing errors as described in part a. above. Briefly explain how the investor expects the stock price to change and identify an investment strategy this investor should pursue.

### Explanation
The stock price may fall after the investors in the market realize the overreaction and correct the mistake. The investor who identifies this in advance can short sell this stock now and profit.

Other responses in the sample answers:

- Exclude this stock from the portfolio, or short-sell it.
- Buy puts on the stock: since the market expects the stock to go up a lot, a put at a high price (e.g. a strike equal to the forecasted price) will be relatively cheap, and when the price doesn't actually reach that level the investor can exercise the put and sell at the higher price. Short-selling or buying puts is speculative and risky; a more risk-averse investor would avoid buying the stock until the future price correction.
- The current price is \$65 and the investor believes \$90 is too high next quarter: sell a call option at the \$90 strike, and when the price fails to reach \$90 the investor profits.
- Short the security; since this is speculative, to reduce the risk the investor could also go long a portfolio with the same $\beta$.
- The investor may still expect the price to rise, just not by as much as forecast: buy the stock, but not as much as if the \$90 forecast were believed, or reduce exposure to the stock or diversify to protect from the potential error in prediction.
- Since the stock price increased tremendously due to the information processing errors, it will reverse downwards in the future: pursue a contrarian strategy and short the stock.

### Examiner Report
- The candidates were expected to describe how the stock price will be affected as a result of information processing errors in Part A being recognized and what an appropriate investment strategy would be in that case.
- Common errors made by candidates:
  - Suggestions of trying to "time" the market (for example, wait until stock reaches its peak price to sell)
  - "Contrarian strategy" or "buying recent losers" without explanation of what that strategy means in the context of the specific stock in question
  - Strategy of short selling the stock without expectations of the stock price going down
  - Candidates took the earnings/growth forecasts as fact (assumed it is correct as is), and based their discussion of the accuracy of the stock price forecast on how that relates to the earnings/growth forecast
- Candidates that provided a correct strategy that aligns with a conservatism error still received partial credit for Part B as long as their response in Part A also stated and correctly described conservatism.
