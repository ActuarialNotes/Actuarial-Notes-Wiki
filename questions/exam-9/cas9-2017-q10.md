Given the following:

| Bond | Par Value | Time To Maturity (years) | Current Bond Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$100 | 1 | \$96.15 |
| 2 | \$100 | 2 | \$91.40 |
| 3 | \$100 | 3 | \$85.40 |

- Assume annual compounding.
- All bonds are zero-coupon bonds.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the forward rate in year three.

### Explanation
$$f_3 = \frac{91.40}{85.40} - 1 = 7\%$$

Alternatively:

From the yields to maturity: $91.40 = 100/(1+y)^2 \rightarrow y = 4.6\%$ and $85.40 = 100/(1+y)^3 \rightarrow y = 5.4\%$, so $(1.054)^3 = (1.046)^2 (1 + f_3) \rightarrow f_3 = 7.0\%$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use 2 and 3 year yields to maturity to calculate a 3 year forward rate.

There were no common errors on this part.

## Part b (0.5 points)

An investor wants to borrow \$100 two years from now for one year.

Outline a strategy the investor could take to construct a synthetic forward loan and identify all of the investor's cash flows.

### Explanation
Sell 1.07 units of bond 3. Use the proceeds (\$85.4 × 1.07 = \$91.4) to buy bond 2. At time 2, receive \$100 from bond 2. At time 3, pay back \$107 for bond 3.

Alternatively:

Buy \$91.4 of a 2-year bond. Sell \$91.4 of a 3-year bond.

| Time | Cash Flow |
|---|---|
| 0 | -91.4 + 91.4 = 0 |
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | +100 (the 2-year bond matures and pays the investor) |
| 3 | -107 (pay the 3-year bond = 100 × 91.4 / 85.4) |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to recommend buying bond 2 and selling bond 3 to create a \$100 loan at time 2, showing the correct cash flows.

A common error was recommending to *sell* bond 2 and *buy* bond 3, instead of the reverse.

## Part c (0.75 points)

Using a theory of term structure, explain why an investor would want to construct a synthetic forward loan.

### Explanation
By liquidity preference theory, it is possible that long-term investors dominate the market, so there is a negative liquidity premium. Therefore the current $f_3 < E(r_3)$. The investor can borrow at a lower rate by locking in the forward rate now, compared to borrowing after 2 years.

Alternatively:

Under liquidity preference theory, investors demand a liquidity premium for investments outside their preferred horizon, and may prefer some lengths of maturity over others. If investors prefer longer-term investments, this leads to $f_3 < E(r_3)$ (a liquidity discount: to such an investor the liquidity premium is negative). In that case a borrower may want to lock in a lower future rate with a synthetic forward loan — for example, one who foresees the future short rate going up.

### Examiner Report
Part c. was more complex than the other parts of this question as it required the candidate to apply liquidity preference theory to a synthetic forward loan. Candidates performed much better on parts a. and b. than on part c.

Candidates were expected to identify the proper theory of term structure and apply it to create a synthetic forward loan.

Common errors include:

- Selecting Expectation Hypothesis instead of Liquidity Preference Theory
- Failing to recognize that long-term investors would dominate the market according to the given information due to the upward-sloping yield curve
