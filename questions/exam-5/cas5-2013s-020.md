Given the following information for a line of business:

- Assume no reported claims development past 36 months.
- Annual claim severity trend = +5%.
- Paid claim development method ultimate loss estimate for accident year 2012 = \$10,275,000.
- Reported claim development method ultimate loss estimate for accident year 2012 = \$9,650,000.

**Cumulative Paid Claims (\$000s)**

| Accident Year | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | \$2,100 | \$6,410 | \$8,300 |
| 2011 | \$2,210 | \$7,000 |  |
| 2012 | \$2,550 |  |  |

**Cumulative Closed Claim Count**

| Accident Year | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 35 | 75 | 99 |
| 2011 | 35 | 80 |  |
| 2012 | 40 |  |  |

**Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000s)**

| Accident Year | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | \$5,300 | \$7,810 | \$8,500 |
| 2011 | \$5,500 | \$8,130 |  |
| 2012 | \$6,000 |  |  |

**Cumulative Reported Claim Count**

| Accident Year | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 80 | 98 | 100 |
| 2011 | 79 | 97 |  |
| 2012 | 82 |  |  |

**Outstanding Claims (\$000s)**

| Accident Year | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | \$3,200 | \$1,400 | \$200 |
| 2011 | \$3,290 | \$1,130 |  |
| 2012 | \$3,450 |  |  |

**Outstanding Claim Count**

| Accident Year | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 45 | 23 | 1 |
| 2011 | 44 | 17 |  |
| 2012 | 42 |  |  |

Fully discuss the considerations in deciding between using the paid or the reported claim development method to estimate ultimate claims for this line of business, and recommend an ultimate loss estimate for accident year 2012.

### Answer

**Select the Reported Development Method.** Recommended ultimate for AY 2012 = **\$9,650,000** (the reported development method ultimate).

### Explanation

The choice turns on whether settlement rates or case adequacy have changed, so test both and compare the severities with the known +5% severity trend. All amounts are in \$000s.

**Diagnostic 1: Average paid claim** = cumulative paid claims / cumulative closed counts

| AY | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 2,100/35 = 60.00 | 6,410/75 = 85.47 | 8,300/99 = 83.84 |
| 2011 | 2,210/35 = 63.14 | 7,000/80 = 87.50 |  |
| 2012 | 2,550/40 = 63.75 |  |  |

Down the columns: at 12 months +5.2% (2010 to 2011) and +1.0% (2011 to 2012); at 24 months +2.4%. Apart from the first step at 12 months, average paid claims are rising at less than the 5% severity trend.

**Diagnostic 2: Closed-to-reported claim count ratio** = cumulative closed counts / cumulative reported counts

| AY | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 35/80 = 0.4375 | 75/98 = 0.7653 | 99/100 = 0.9900 |
| 2011 | 35/79 = 0.4430 | 80/97 = 0.8247 |  |
| 2012 | 40/82 = 0.4878 |  |  |

The ratio rises down each column (0.4375, 0.4430, 0.4878 at 12 months; 0.7653, 0.8247 at 24 months): claims are being settled faster.

**Diagnostic 3: Average case outstanding** = outstanding claims / outstanding counts

| AY | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 3,200/45 = 71.11 | 1,400/23 = 60.87 | 200/1 = 200.00 |
| 2011 | 3,290/44 = 74.77 | 1,130/17 = 66.47 |  |
| 2012 | 3,450/42 = 82.14 |  |  |

At 12 months average case outstanding rises +5.1% and +9.9%; at 24 months +9.2%. Read alone this looks like a change in case adequacy, but with settlement speeding up the claims still open are a different mix, so case alone is inconclusive.

**Diagnostic 4: Average reported claim** = cumulative reported claims / cumulative reported counts

| AY | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 5,300/80 = 66.25 | 7,810/98 = 79.69 | 8,500/100 = 85.00 |
| 2011 | 5,500/79 = 69.62 | 8,130/97 = 83.81 |  |
| 2012 | 6,000/82 = 73.17 |  |  |

At 12 months +5.1% and +5.1%; at 24 months +5.2%. Average reported claims are increasing steadily at the 5% severity trend.

**Conclusion and recommendation**

1. Settlement has sped up (closed-to-reported ratios rising) and average paid claims are trending below 5%: the insurer is closing more small claims quickly. The paid development method applies historical CDFs to a paid amount that is further along than history, so its ultimate (\$10,275,000) is overstated.
2. Average reported claims move at the 5% severity trend, so reported claims are consistent across accident years and the reported development method is not distorted by the change in settlement.

**Recommendation: use the reported claim development method ultimate of \$9,650,000 for AY 2012.**

### Examiner Report

Candidates were supposed to evaluate Average Paid (and/or Outstanding) and Average Reported trends and compare them to the known severity of 5%. They should have noticed the increase in paid settlement and that reported trends matched the 5% severity. From there they were to conclude to use the reported method and not the paid. This conclusion should have been reached by evaluating changes (or lack of change) in both case adequacy and settlement rates.

Many candidates calculated Average Paid and Average Case severities, but did not calculate the Average Reported severities. Most candidates did calculate trend from year to year. Many of those lost credit by not making any statement on the stability or instability of the resulting trends. Also, comparisons of the observed paid severity to the outstanding severity, or the observed severities along the diagonal rather than down the columns of the triangle did not receive full credit.

Many candidates that only looked at average paid and case decided the change in trend of the case outstanding disproved using the reported method. But case alone is inconclusive in determining reported stability. Many of those candidates did not test for settlement rate changes, likely with the thought that they had identified the relevant piece of information to make their choice. Some candidates further went on to test the settlement rate but did not see how an apparent case adequacy change is influenced by a real settlement rate change.

Those that did calculate Average Reported often noticed that the year to year trend was stable and some of those mentioned that the trend was consistent with the 5% severity.

A large number of candidates went off onto a Berquist-Sherman technique or an "adjusted" reported methodology which was incorrect as the reported method without adjustment is the preferred method.

Full credit for the selection of the reported method was given if the correct choice was made or even if the words "select the reported method" and no numerical choice was made. If the candidate mistook the reported ultimate for incurred and then applied an LDF, or created their own LDF instead of using the ultimate given, full credit was still awarded. If they adjusted the reported triangle using a BS or other methodology and then developed to ultimate, no credit was given for selecting the reported method.

The question asked the candidates to choose between the paid and reported methods. Some candidates choose an average of them and got a number "Between." Since the reported was accurate and the paid was not candidates did not receive full credit.
