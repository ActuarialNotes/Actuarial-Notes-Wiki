The **Voluntary Private Insurance Market** is the part of the market in which privately owned insurers choose which risks to accept and at what price, within the limits of regulation, and buyers choose whether and from whom to buy. It is defined by contrast with [[Residual Market|residual markets]], government insurers and compulsory social programs, where one side of the bargain is compelled.

The market is voluntary because of four features:

1. Freedom to underwrite (select, decline, set terms).
2. Risk-based pricing.
3. Competition.
4. Private capital at risk.

Regulation narrows each of these: [[Rate Regulation|rate approval]], [[Risk Classification Restrictions|classification restrictions]], the [[Take-All-Comers Rule]] in Ontario auto and compulsory purchase. The tighter the constraints, the more business the voluntary market pushes elsewhere.

Where the voluntary market fails, [[Government and Industry Insurance Programs|programs]] step in. It fails on correlated catastrophe losses (flood, terrorism, crop), on severe [[Adverse Selection|adverse selection]], and where prices for compulsory coverage are held below cost.

## Interactions with programs

Programs interact with the voluntary market in five ways:

| Interaction | What the program does | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Residual | Takes what the voluntary market declines, with voluntary writers sharing its results by market share | [[Facility Association]], risk sharing pools ([[Risk Sharing Pool]]) and the [[Plan de Répartition des Risques]] in Canada; [[Automobile Plans]], FAIR plans and workers' compensation pools in the U.S. |
| Private delivery | Has insurers sell and service its policies for a fee without bearing the risk | NFIP "Write Your Own" carriers, crop insurance providers, Facility Association servicing carriers |
| Backstop | Caps the tail with a government layer so private cover remains available | [[TRIA]], the [[Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund]] |
| Complement | Provides a public floor with private insurance above it | Optional and excess auto above public basic cover ([[Public Auto Insurance]]), supplementary health above medicare ([[Health Care Insurance]]) |
| Competition and crowd-out | Displaces private writers if subsidised or a monopoly, or is left adversely selected when private writers cherry-pick its over-priced risks | Private flood cover beside the NFIP |

Every voluntary writer also funds the failures of its competitors through post-assessment [[Guaranty Funds|guaranty funds]] such as [[PACICC]].

> [!example]- How do private flood insurers interact with the public program in the U.S. and in Canada? {Example}
> In the U.S., private insurers have begun writing primary residential flood cover alongside the National Flood Insurance Program. In Canada, private insurers offer overland flood endorsements but decline the highest-risk properties.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - In the U.S., where NFIP rates exceed a property's modelled risk, a private insurer can undercut them and take the policy. Where NFIP rates are below cost, private writers leave the risk alone.
> > - The NFIP keeps a worsening mix, which is adverse selection against the public program. Moving NFIP rates toward risk-based pricing reduces the gap private writers exploit, at the cost of affordability for the highest-risk owners.
> > - In Canada, the voluntary market covers most homes but not the highest-risk ones, which remain uninsured and reliant on [[Disaster Financial Assistance Arrangements|disaster assistance]].
> > - The Canadian policy question is whether a public high-risk pool should take them, a residual layer beneath a voluntary market. This is the reverse of the U.S. position.

## Residual share

The share of a market's exposures in the residual market is

> $$\text{Residual share} = \frac{\text{Residual exposures}}{\text{Voluntary exposures} + \text{Residual exposures}}$$

A growing residual share signals that approved rates or permitted classifications no longer let the voluntary market write part of the risk spectrum. Depopulation, returning those risks to voluntary writers, follows rate adequacy, not exhortation.

> [!example]- What is this jurisdiction's residual share, and what does the residual plan cost this insurer? {Example}
> A jurisdiction has $2{,}000{,}000$ insured vehicles, $90{,}000$ of them in the residual plan. The plan's annual deficit of $\$36$ million is shared by voluntary market share. An insurer with $12\%$ of the voluntary market writes $\$300$ million of voluntary premium.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - The residual share is $90{,}000 / 2{,}000{,}000 = 4.5\%$.
> > - The insurer's share of the deficit is $0.12 \times \$36\text{M} = \$4.32\text{M}$.
> > - As a share of its premium, that is $4.32 / 300 = 1.44\%$.
> > - Voluntary policyholders pay about $1.4\%$ more to subsidise drivers the voluntary market would not write at approved rates. If rate suppression doubled the plan to $180{,}000$ vehicles ($9\%$), the deficit and the load would grow with it.

> [!trap] Treating the residual market as a cost to the plan alone
> A residual plan's deficit is shared among voluntary writers by market share, so it is paid by voluntary policyholders. Rate suppression that grows the plan raises the load on everyone else.
