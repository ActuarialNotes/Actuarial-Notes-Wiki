Describe why allocation of surplus by state and line using premium-to-surplus ratios may be problematic for each of the following:

## Part a (0.5 points)

i. An individual multi-line national insurer.

### Explanation
Any one of these reasons (the report's sample answers, grouped by theme):

- **All surplus:** the insurer's entire surplus supports all of the insurer's business, regardless of state or line, so this allocation is arbitrary. All its surplus stands behind all its business – the company can't go insolvent by line or by state.
- **Correlation:** the risk of each line interacts with other lines, so allocating by P/S ratio may overstate the surplus in total. The line of business is correlated with sources of risk, so the total surplus may be less than the sum of the surplus of each line.
- **Premium:** many risks (pricing, reserving, credit, CAT risk) are not proportional to premium. Thus multi-line insurers will be exposed to many different risks, and using P/S to allocate surplus is not appropriate.

### Examiner Report
- The candidate was expected to know why allocation of Surplus to state and line using P/S ratio may be problematic for a multi-line national insurer.
- Common Errors
    - Assuming there is single P/S ratio to be applied to all state/lines.

## Part b (0.5 points)

ii. The insurance industry as a whole.

### Explanation
Any one of these reasons (the report's sample answers, grouped by theme):

- **Wide range of risks:** different insurers are exposed to different types of risks, so it's not proper to use an industry ratio to allocate. Individual insurers can have very different risk profiles – what may be appropriate for one insurer may not be for another, e.g. a monoline workers compensation carrier in CA vs a multi-line, national insurer that writes in CA.
- **Unique risks:** there is no one P:S appropriate for the industry. Each insurer writes a different mix of business and is exposed to different risks, so surplus allocated should reflect the level of risk inherent in each insurer, and premium alone does not reflect this.
- **Premium:** there are risks in the insurance industry that are not associated with premium, such as asset risk, credit risk, catastrophic risk, strategy risk or reserve risk – some of the risk is independent of premium.

### Examiner Report
- The candidate was expected to know why allocation of Surplus to state and line using P/S ratio may be problematic for the insurance industry as a whole.
- Common Errors
    - Assuming there is a single P/S ratio to be applied to all state/lines.
    - Assuming total industry surplus is available to any state/line
    - No specific mention of differences in risk among various insurers.
    - Responses focused on rate of return and regulation related issues.
