An insurance agent sells life insurance policies. The number of life insurance policies sold in a month can be modeled by a Poisson distribution with mean 4.

The agent sold at least one life insurance policy in a particular month.

Calculate the probability that the agent sold more than three life insurance policies in that month.

- A) $0.567$
- B) $0.577$
- C) $0.624$
- D) $0.776$
- E) $0.839$

## Explanation

Since $\{X > 3\}$ is contained in $\{X \geq 1\}$, the intersection is just $\{X > 3\}$ and

$$
P[X > 3 \mid X \geq 1] = \frac{P[X > 3]}{P[X \geq 1]}
$$

With $\lambda = 4$, evaluate the four smallest probabilities:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[0] &= e^{-4} = 0.018316 \\
P[1] &= 4e^{-4} = 0.073263 \\
P[2] &= 8e^{-4} = 0.146525 \\
P[3] &= \tfrac{32}{3}e^{-4} = 0.195367
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 3] &= 1 - (0.018316 + 0.073263 + 0.146525 + 0.195367) = 0.566530 \\
P[X \geq 1] &= 1 - 0.018316 = 0.981684
\end{align*}
$$

Dividing:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 3 \mid X \geq 1] &= \frac{0.566530}{0.981684} \\
                       &= \mathbf{0.5771}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.567, is the unconditional $P[X > 3]$ with the division left undone. The conditioning changes little here because $P[X = 0]$ is under 2% — with a mean of 4, almost every month already has at least one sale.
