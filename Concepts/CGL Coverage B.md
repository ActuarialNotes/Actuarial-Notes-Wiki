**CGL Coverage B** is the personal and advertising injury liability coverage of the ISO [[Commercial General Liability|Commercial General Liability Coverage Form]] (CG 00 01, 04 13 edition). It pays the damages an insured becomes legally obligated to pay because of "personal and advertising injury" and defends suits seeking those damages, provided the injury is caused by an offense arising out of the named insured's business and committed in the coverage territory during the policy period.

"Personal and advertising injury" is injury, including consequential bodily injury, arising out of one or more of seven offenses:

- (a) False arrest, detention or imprisonment.
- (b) Malicious prosecution.
- (c) Wrongful eviction from, wrongful entry into, or invasion of the right of private occupancy of a room, dwelling or premises a person occupies, committed by or for its owner, landlord or lessor.
- (d) Oral or written publication of material that slanders or libels a person or organization or disparages its goods, products or services.
- (e) Oral or written publication of material that violates a person's right of privacy.
- (f) Using another's advertising idea in the named insured's "advertisement".
- (g) Infringing another's copyright, trade dress or slogan in the named insured's advertisement.

An injury that isn't one of these offenses isn't covered by Coverage B. An "advertisement" is a notice broadcast or published to the general public or specific market segments about the named insured's goods, products or services to attract customers or supporters. Material on the Internet counts, but only the part of a website about the insured's goods or services for that purpose is an advertisement.

The insurer has the right and duty to defend a suit seeking these damages, but no duty to defend a suit to which the insurance doesn't apply. It may investigate any offense and settle at its discretion. Its right and duty to defend end once the applicable limit is used up in judgments or settlements under Coverages A or B or medical expenses under Coverage C. Other costs are covered only as [[CGL Supplementary Payments|supplementary payments]].

> [!example]- Under which coverage of the supermarket's CGL does each part of this suit fall? {Example}
> A supermarket's security guard, an employee acting within his duties, wrongly detains a customer for an hour on suspicion of shoplifting. The customer faints during the detention and hits her head. She sues the supermarket for false imprisonment and for her head injury.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - False detention is offense (a), committed during the policy period and arising out of the supermarket's business, so it falls under Coverage B.
> > - Her head injury is bodily injury, but it arose out of the personal and advertising injury, so Coverage A's exclusion o removes it from Coverage A.
> > - The definition of personal and advertising injury includes consequential bodily injury, so Coverage B covers both parts under the Personal and Advertising Injury limit.
> > - Exclusion a would apply only if the guard acted knowing the detention would violate her rights and inflict injury.

## The offense trigger

The insurance applies only to injury caused by an offense committed during the policy period. The date of the offense controls, not the date of the injury or the claim, which is the main structural difference from the occurrence trigger of [[CGL Coverage A Insuring Agreement|Coverage A]].

Coverage A's exclusion o removes bodily injury arising out of personal and advertising injury, so consequential bodily injury from, say, a false arrest is paid under Coverage B and its limit ([[CGL Coverage A Exclusions]]).

## Exclusions

Coverage B does not apply to personal and advertising injury:

| Exclusion | What it removes |
| :--- | :--- |
| a | Injury caused by or at the direction of the insured knowing the act would violate another's rights and inflict the injury |
| b | Injury from publishing material the insured knew was false |
| c | Injury from material first published before the policy period began |
| d | Injury from a criminal act committed by or at the direction of the insured |
| e | Injury for which the insured assumed liability in a contract, except liability it would have without the contract |
| f | Injury from a breach of contract, except an implied contract to use another's advertising idea in the insured's advertisement |
| g | Injury from goods, products or services failing to conform to a statement of quality or performance in the insured's advertisement |
| h | Injury from a wrong description of the price of goods, products or services in the insured's advertisement |
| i | Injury from infringing a copyright, patent, trademark, trade secret or other intellectual property right, except infringement of copyright, trade dress or slogan in the insured's advertisement |
| j | Injury committed by an insured whose business is advertising, broadcasting, publishing or telecasting, designing or determining website content for others, or providing Internet search, access, content or service, except offenses a to c |
| k | Injury from an electronic chatroom or bulletin board the insured hosts, owns or controls |
| l | Injury from unauthorized use of another's name or product in the insured's e-mail address, domain name or metatag, or similar tactics to mislead another's potential customers |
| m | Injury from the discharge or escape of pollutants at any time |
| n | Any loss, cost or expense from a request, demand, order or statutory or regulatory requirement to test for, clean up or otherwise respond to pollutants, or a governmental claim or suit for those costs |
| o | Injury from war, warlike action or insurrection |
| p | Injury from violations of the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, the CAN-SPAM Act of 2003, the Fair Credit Reporting Act or any similar law limiting the distribution of material or information |

Exclusion c looks to the first publication, so material first published before the policy period is excluded even if it is republished during it. For exclusion i, using another's advertising idea is not an intellectual property right. For exclusion j, placing frames, borders, links or advertising on the Internet is not by itself one of those businesses.

> [!example]- Which of these four suits against a retailer's television campaign are covered under Coverage B? {Example}
> A sporting goods retailer runs a television campaign during the policy period. Four suits follow:
>
> - (a) A competitor alleges the campaign uses its well-known slogan.
> - (b) Another company alleges the retailer's new product design infringes its patent.
> - (c) A customer alleges the advertised "waterproof" jacket leaks.
> - (d) A former employee alleges the retailer published her photograph in the advertisement without consent.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Suit (a) is covered. Infringing another's slogan in the insured's advertisement is offense (g), and exclusion i gives back infringement of copyright, trade dress or slogan in the insured's advertisement.
> > - Suit (b) is not covered. Patent infringement is not one of the seven offenses, and exclusion i removes it in any case.
> > - Suit (c) is not covered. Exclusion g removes injury from goods failing to conform to a statement of quality or performance in the advertisement.
> > - Suit (d) is covered. Publishing material that violates a person's right of privacy is offense (e).

## The Personal and Advertising Injury limit

The Personal and Advertising Injury limit is the most paid for all personal and advertising injury sustained by any one person or organization, subject to the general aggregate. No each occurrence limit applies, because Coverage B responds to offenses rather than occurrences. The payment for person or organization $j$ is

> $$\text{Paid}_j = \min\left(D_j,\ L_{\text{PAI}}\right)$$

- $D_j$ is the damages because of all personal and advertising injury sustained by person or organization $j$.
- $L_{\text{PAI}}$ is the Personal and Advertising Injury limit.

Coverage B payments reduce the general aggregate, as Coverage A (other than products-completed operations) and Coverage C payments do ([[CGL Limits of Insurance]]). The capped payments are added, and the total Coverage B pays is

> $$\text{Total paid} = \min\Big(\sum_j \text{Paid}_j,\ G\Big)$$

- $G$ is what remains of the general aggregate.

> [!example]- What does Coverage B pay for these two claims against a newspaper publisher? {Example}
> A newspaper publisher's CGL policy, which carries no media endorsement, has a $\$1{,}000{,}000$ Personal and Advertising Injury limit and a $\$2{,}000{,}000$ general aggregate. Its in-house security guard falsely detains a visitor, who recovers $\$1{,}300{,}000$. Separately, a landlord subsidiary named on the policy wrongfully evicts a tenant, who recovers $\$400{,}000$.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - The publisher's business is publishing, so exclusion j applies, but it does not apply to offenses a to c. False arrest and wrongful eviction are both covered.
> > - The visitor is paid $\min(1{,}300{,}000,\ 1{,}000{,}000) = \$1{,}000{,}000$.
> > - The tenant is paid $\min(400{,}000,\ 1{,}000{,}000) = \$400{,}000$.
> > - The total, $\$1{,}400{,}000$, is within the $\$2{,}000{,}000$ general aggregate, which has $\$600{,}000$ left.
> > - Coverage B pays $\$1{,}400{,}000$ and the publisher bears $\$300{,}000$. A libel claim against the newspaper's content would be excluded by exclusion j.

> [!trap] Treating an intentional offense as excluded
> Several offenses are intentional acts, such as an eviction or a libel, and they are covered. Coverage B excludes only acting with knowledge that the act would violate another's rights and inflict injury (exclusion a), or publishing material the insured knew was false (exclusion b).
