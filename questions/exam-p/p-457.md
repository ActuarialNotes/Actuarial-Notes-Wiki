Let $X$ denote the number of illnesses a person experiences during a one-year period. The probability function of $X$ is:

| $x$ | Probability |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.28 |
| 1 | 0.12 |
| 2 | 0.42 |
| 3 | 0.18 |

If $X = 0$, then the person makes no doctor visits during the one-year period. If $X = k$, for $k = 1, 2, 3$, then the number of doctor visits is Poisson distributed with mean $k$.

Calculate the probability that the person makes at least one doctor visit during the one-year period.

- A) $0.18$
- B) $0.39$
- C) $0.61$
- D) $0.72$
- E) $0.89$

## Explanation

Work with the complement: the probability of **no** doctor visits, conditioning on the number of illnesses.

When $X = 0$ there are certainly no visits, contributing the full 0.28. When $X = k \geq 1$, visits are Poisson with mean $k$, so the probability of zero visits is $e^{-k}$.

By the law of total probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{no visits}] &= 0.28 + 0.12e^{-1} + 0.42e^{-2} + 0.18e^{-3} \\
                    &= 0.28 + 0.04415 + 0.05684 + 0.00896 \\
                    &= 0.39
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{at least one visit}] &= 1 - 0.39 \\
                             &= \mathbf{0.61}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 0.39, is the complement reported instead of the answer. Note that the 0.28 term dominates the no-visit probability: once a person has even one illness, $e^{-1} = 0.37$ means they will probably visit a doctor, so most of the "no visits" mass (0.28 of 0.39, about 72%) comes from having no illness at all.
