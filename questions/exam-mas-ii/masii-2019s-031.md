An actuary is reviewing a portfolio of auto claims, and is interested in analyzing the claim severity distribution using a Bayesian model. The portfolio's observed auto claim severity sample follows a normal distribution with mean 1,000 and standard deviation 200.

The graph below represents the samples from the posterior distribution for the mean (mu) and standard deviation (sigma).

![Scatter plot of posterior draws with mu on the horizontal axis from about 900 to 1000 and sigma on the vertical axis from about 160 to 240. The cloud is centred near mu = 955 and sigma = 195, spanning roughly 920 to 995 in mu and 165 to 225 in sigma](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019s-q31-posterior-scatter.png)

Determine which of the following most likely represents the prior distributions used by the actuary.

- A) $\mu \sim \text{Normal}(600, 100^2)$; $\sigma \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 200)$
- B) $\mu \sim \text{Normal}(600, 100^2)$; $\sigma \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 400)$
- C) $\mu \sim \text{Normal}(1000, 100^2)$; $\sigma \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 400)$
- D) $\mu \sim \text{Normal}(1400, 100^2)$; $\sigma \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 400)$
- E) $\mu \sim \text{Normal}(2000, 100^2)$; $\sigma \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 400)$

## Explanation

Read the two axes of the posterior cloud separately.

**The $\mu$ axis.** The posterior for $\mu$ sits around 955, clearly *below* the sample mean of 1,000. A posterior mean is a credibility-weighted blend of the data and the prior, so the prior mean must lie below 1,000 and pull the estimate down. That rules out (C) — a prior centred at the data would leave the posterior at 1,000 — and rules out (D) and (E), whose priors sit above 1,000 and would pull the posterior *up*. The prior mean is 600.

**The $\sigma$ axis.** The posterior draws for $\sigma$ run up past 220. A $\text{Uniform}(0, 200)$ prior assigns zero density above 200, so no posterior draw could ever exceed 200. Since draws above 200 are plentiful, the upper bound must be 400, which eliminates (A).

Prior mean 600 with $\sigma \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 400)$, so the answer is **B**.
