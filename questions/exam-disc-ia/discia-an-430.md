An HO-3 (HO 00 03 05 11) insured rents a lake cabin for a week of vacation, which makes it an insured location. While there, he causes \$40,000 of damage to the cabin when he leaves a pan unattended and starts a kitchen fire, and separately spills bleach on the owner's \$2,000 rug. How does Section II respond?

- A) Coverage E pays the fire damage; the rug is paid up to \$1,000 under an additional coverage
- B) Coverage E pays both, because the cabin is an insured location
- C) Coverage E pays the rug but not the fire damage, because fire is a Section I peril
- D) Neither is covered, because all damage to property rented to an insured is excluded from Coverage E

## Explanation

Coverage E excludes property damage to property rented to, occupied or used by, or in the care of an insured, **except damage caused by fire, smoke or explosion**. The fire damage to the rented cabin is covered under Coverage E. The bleach damage to the rug is excluded from Coverage E, but the damage to property of others additional coverage pays its replacement cost up to \$1,000 per occurrence. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — being an insured location doesn't lift the exclusion for property in the insured's care.
- **C** reverses the exception: fire is the cause that restores coverage for rented property.
- **D** applies the exclusion without its fire, smoke or explosion exception and without the additional coverage.
