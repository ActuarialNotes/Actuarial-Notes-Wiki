A household insures one car with a base premium of \$900 and a vehicle relativity of 1.10. The parent drives it nearly every day (driver relativity 0.95), and the 16-year-old drives it occasionally (driver relativity 2.00). The insurer rates each car on the highest-risk driver in the household. What is the car's premium?

- A) \$940.50
- B) \$990.00
- C) \$1,881.00
- D) \$1,980.00

## Explanation

Under a highest-risk principal operator rule the car is rated on the riskiest driver who may use it, here the teenager, however little she drives it.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Premium} &= 900 \times 1.10 \times 2.00 \\
&= 1{,}980
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Uses the parent's relativity, which is the most-use rule, not this insurer's rule.
- **B** — Leaves out the driver relativity altogether.
- **C** — Multiplies both drivers' relativities; only the principal operator's applies.
