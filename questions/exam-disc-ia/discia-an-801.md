A U.S. manufacturer of space heaters sells them only through U.S. retailers. A buyer takes one to Spain, where it catches fire and injures her. She sues the manufacturer in a Spanish court and wins a judgment there, without the insurer agreeing to any settlement. Is the injury within the coverage territory of the manufacturer's ISO CGL (CG 00 01 04 13)?

- A) No, because liability was not decided in a suit on the merits in the United States, Puerto Rico or Canada
- B) No, because injury anywhere outside the United States, Puerto Rico and Canada is never within the coverage territory
- C) Yes, because the heater that injured her was made and sold by the named insured within the United States
- D) Yes, because the buyer's home is in the United States and she was away from it for only a short time

## Explanation

Spain is outside the core territory (the United States with its territories and possessions, Puerto Rico and Canada). The worldwide part of the definition can reach injury that arises from goods the named insured made or sold in the core territory, but only if the insured's responsibility to pay damages is determined in a suit on the merits in the core territory, or in a settlement the insurer agrees to. Liability was decided in Spain, so the injury is outside the coverage territory. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** forgets the worldwide extension altogether; products made or sold in the core territory can be covered anywhere.
- **C** stops at the first half of the extension and ignores the requirement about where liability is decided.
- **D** misapplies a different branch: the "short time away" branch covers the activities of a person whose home is in the core territory and who is away on the *insured's* business, not a customer on holiday.
