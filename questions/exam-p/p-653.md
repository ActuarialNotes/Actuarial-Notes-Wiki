Each of two fair six-sided dice has 1 on two faces and 2 on the other four faces. The two dice are rolled twice.

Calculate the probability that the sum showing on the two dice is different on the two rolls.

- A) $\dfrac{12}{27}$
- B) $\dfrac{14}{27}$
- C) $\dfrac{16}{27}$
- D) $\dfrac{18}{27}$
- E) $\dfrac{20}{27}$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the value on one face, so $P[X = 1] = 2/6 = 1/3$ and $P[X = 2] = 4/6 = 2/3$. The sum $S$ of the two dice takes three values:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S = 2] &= \left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 = \tfrac{1}{9} \\
P[S = 3] &= 2\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right) = \tfrac{4}{9} \\
P[S = 4] &= \left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \tfrac{4}{9}
\end{align*}
$$

Work with the complement. The two rolls are independent, so the probability of matching sums is the sum of squares:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{two sums are the same}] &= \left(\tfrac{1}{9}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{4}{9}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{4}{9}\right)^2 \\
                                &= \frac{1 + 16 + 16}{81} = \frac{33}{81}
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
P[\text{different}] = 1 - \frac{33}{81} = \frac{48}{81} = \mathbf{\frac{16}{27}}
$$

Choice (D), $18/27 = 2/3$, is what comes out if the three possible sums are treated as equally likely: matching would then have probability $3(1/3)^2 = 1/3$. The sums are far from uniform — 3 and 4 are four times as likely as 2 — and that concentration makes a match *more* likely, pulling the answer below $2/3$.
