Given the following information:

- The insurance company entered the market in State X at the beginning of 2008.
- All policies are annual.
- Rates will be in effect for 12 months beginning on July 1, 2014.

## Part a (6.25 points)

Calculate the indicated rate level change for State X using the loss ratio method. Use two-step trending to project premiums.

### Answer

Approximately **+21.8%** (CAS's sample answers print +21.77% and +21.5%; the result moves a little with the development factors selected).

$$\text{Indicated Change} = \frac{0.7945 + 0.07}{1 - 0.21 - 0.08} - 1 = +21.8\%$$

### Explanation

**Step 1 — On-level factors for earned premium (parallelogram method).**

Current rate level (CRL) relative to the level before July 1, 2010: $1.05 \times 1.07 = 1.1235$.

With annual policies and premium earned evenly, the +5% change on July 1, 2010 touches $\tfrac{1}{2}(0.5)^2 = \tfrac{1}{8}$ of CY 2010 earned premium, and the +7% change on April 1, 2012 touches $\tfrac{1}{2}(0.75)^2 = \tfrac{9}{32}$ of CY 2012 earned premium.

| CY | Average rate level | OLF = 1.1235 ÷ avg. level |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | $\tfrac{1}{8}(1.05) + \tfrac{7}{8}(1.00) = 1.00625$ | 1.11652 |
| 2011 | $\tfrac{1}{8}(1.00) + \tfrac{7}{8}(1.05) = 1.04375$ | 1.07641 |
| 2012 | $\tfrac{9}{32}(1.1235) + \tfrac{23}{32}(1.05) = 1.07067$ | 1.04934 |

**Step 2 — Two-step premium trend.**

*Step 1 (current trend):* bring each year's average earned premium at CRL to the latest average written premium at CRL. CY 2012 written premium is on-levelled with the written OLF $1.1235 \div [0.25(1.05) + 0.75(1.1235)] = 1.01663$:

$$\text{Latest avg. WP at CRL} = \frac{\$18{,}750{,}000 \times 1.01663}{15{,}000} = \$1{,}270.78$$

*Step 2 (projected trend):* from the average written date of CY 2012 (July 1, 2012) to the average written date of the policies the new rates will cover (written July 1, 2014 – June 30, 2015, so January 1, 2015): 2.5 years, $(1.02)^{2.5} = 1.05075$.

| CY | EP at CRL | Earned exposures | Avg. EP at CRL | Step 1 factor | Projected EP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | \$446,609 | 400 | \$1,116.52 | 1.13816 | \$534,112 |
| 2011 | \$2,368,096 | 2,000 | \$1,184.05 | 1.07325 | \$2,670,559 |
| 2012 | \$17,628,930 | 14,000 | \$1,259.21 | 1.00919 | \$18,693,910 |
| **Total** | | | | | **\$21,898,581** |

Each projected EP equals earned exposures × \$1,270.78 × 1.05075.

**Step 3 — Develop losses to ultimate.**

State X is a new, fast-growing book and its own link ratios are volatile, so the countrywide factors are selected (CAS's examiner's report accepted either, provided the selections were reasonable). Countrywide age-to-age factors and their simple averages:

**Step 4 — Trend, add ULAE.**

Losses are trended at 4% a year from each accident year's average accident date (July 1) to the average accident date of the policies the new rates will cover (July 1, 2015).

| AY | Loss + ALAE | CDF | Ultimate | Trend | ULAE | Projected loss + LAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Total** | | | | | | **\$17,399,442** |

**Step 5 — Indicated rate change.**

$$\text{Projected loss and LAE ratio} = \frac{17{,}399{,}442}{21{,}898{,}581} = 0.7945$$

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{\text{LR} + F}{1 - V - Q} - 1 = \frac{0.7945 + 0.07}{1 - 0.21 - 0.08} - 1 = \frac{0.8645}{0.71} - 1 = +21.8\%$$

### Examiner Report

Most candidates correctly calculated the current rate level factors. Candidates did struggle with the premium trend, with common mistakes of missing the trend period or applying to written premium. Most candidates did well with the loss component, receiving full credit on the loss development and loss trend portion of the question. Candidates could get full credit for using either state specific development factors or countrywide as long as the selections were reasonable.

## Part b (0.5 points)

The assumption that the premium is earned evenly should not hold for State X given that it is a new book of business. Briefly describe two alternatives to the traditional parallelogram method that would improve the accuracy of the estimated projected premiums.

### Explanation

1. **Extension of exposures method:** Re-price each individual policy at the current rate level by applying current base rates, class factors, and expense fees to each policy's exposures. This eliminates the need for any earning assumption and produces the most accurate on-level premium.

2. **Finer time periods (e.g., quarterly or monthly data):** Instead of using annual calendar-year data, break the premium data into quarterly or monthly segments. Shorter intervals better capture the shift in the average earning date as the book grows rapidly, reducing the distortion caused by the uneven earning pattern within each year.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were often able to identify alternatives like extension of exposure or using more refined time periods, but some did lose points for lack of description.

## Part c (0.75 points)

Fully discuss the impact to the rate level indication for State X by assuming the premium is earned evenly. Include the directional change to the rate level indication that would result if adjusting for the actual earning of the premium.

### Explanation

Because State X is a rapidly growing new book of business, more exposure is written toward the end of each calendar year than at the beginning. As a result, more of the earned premium actually occurs toward the end of the year — after rate changes have taken effect — than the even-earning assumption implies.

The parallelogram method, by assuming even earning, underestimates the fraction of premium that has already received the benefit of rate increases. Specifically, the on-level factors computed under the even-earning assumption are **too high** (they assume less of the premium was written at the new rate level than actually was), which **overstates** the on-level earned premium.

An overstated on-level earned premium means the projected denominator (premium) in the loss ratio is too large, which makes the **loss ratio appear too low**, resulting in an **understated indicated rate increase** (or an indication that rates need to increase by less than they actually do).

**Directional impact:** If the actual (front-loaded toward year-end) earning pattern were used instead of the even-earning assumption, the on-level factors would be **lower**, the projected premium would be **lower**, the loss ratio would be **higher**, and the indicated rate change would be **larger (more positive)**.

### Examiner Report

Most candidates received partial credit. Candidates were often able to identify the impact on the indication given a premium change, but lacked the discussion leading up to the reason behind the premium change. Credit was also given for a complete discussion of the impact of changing trend periods due to different average earned and average accident dates.
