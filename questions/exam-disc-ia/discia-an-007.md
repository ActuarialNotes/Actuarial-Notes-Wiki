No SSAP addresses a particular transaction. The NAIC *Annual Statement Instructions* call for one treatment, an interpretation (INT) adopted by the NAIC's Statutory Accounting Principles Working Group calls for a different one, and FASB Concepts Statement No. 8 suggests a third. The insurer's domiciliary state has no prescribed practice on the point and has granted no permitted practice. Under the statutory hierarchy, which treatment should the insurer follow?

- A) The Annual Statement Instructions, because they govern the statutory blank
- B) The INT, because Level 2 guidance governs over Level 3 when they conflict
- C) The Concepts Statement, because conceptual guidance outranks specific rules
- D) Any of the three, because none of them is a Level 1 source

## Explanation

With the SSAPs (Level 1) silent, the preparer applies a relevant principle from Level 2 or Level 3. INTs adopted by the working group are Level 2, and the *Annual Statement Instructions* are Level 3; where they conflict, the preparer follows the higher level. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — the instructions are Level 3, so they give way to a conflicting Level 2 interpretation.
- **C** — FASB Concepts Statements are Level 5, nonauthoritative GAAP literature, considered only when no established statutory principle applies.
- **D** — the hierarchy exists precisely to rank sources below Level 1; the choice is not free.
