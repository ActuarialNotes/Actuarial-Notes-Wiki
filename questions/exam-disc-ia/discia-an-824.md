A contractor's CGL shows an advance premium of \$18,000 based on estimated payroll. At the end of the audit period the insurer computes the earned premium from the actual payroll as \$15,500, and the contractor has paid the full \$18,000. Under the premium audit condition, what happens?

- A) Nothing, because the advance premium is the premium for the policy
- B) The insurer returns \$2,500 to every named insured in equal shares
- C) The insurer returns \$2,500 to the first named insured
- D) The insurer applies the \$2,500 as a credit against the next policy's premium, because return premiums are not allowed on audit

## Explanation

The premium audit condition says premium shown as advance premium is a deposit only. At the close of each audit period the insurer computes the earned premium and notifies the first named insured; if the advance and audit premiums paid exceed the earned premium, the insurer returns the excess **to the first named insured**. Here \$18,000 less \$15,500 leaves \$2,500. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats the advance premium as final; the form calls it a deposit.
- **B** — rights and duties under the audit condition belong to the first named insured, not every named insured.
- **D** — the form provides for return of the excess.
