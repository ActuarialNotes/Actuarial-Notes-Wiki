The insurance-law text on the [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] syllabus for the common-law provinces, read for objective A2. The syllabus assigns **pp. A1-1, A2-1 to A2-9, B1-1 to B1-2, B2-1 to B2-3, and B4-1 to B4-3**, and states that **candidates are responsible for all cases cited in the text**.

## Why a life text sits on a P&C syllabus

The assigned extracts cover the **general law of the insurance contract** — the doctrines that are common to all lines and were worked out in the life cases, not doctrines peculiar to life insurance:

- **Formation of the contract** — offer, acceptance, and when coverage attaches.
- **Insurable interest** — the requirement that distinguishes an insurance contract from a wager, and who may take out a policy on whom.
- **[[Duty of Good Faith|Utmost good faith]] and disclosure** — the applicant's duty to disclose material facts, misrepresentation, and the insurer's remedy for a breach.
- **Statutory conditions and the effect of provincial Insurance Acts** on the terms of the contract — the parties cannot contract out of them.
- **Agency** — when an agent's knowledge or conduct binds the insurer, the point *Fletcher v. MPIC* turns on (see [[Baer and Rendall]]).
- **The cases cited throughout**, which the syllabus makes examinable in their own right.

## Why it is on the syllabus

Domain A asks candidates to interpret judicial decisions and say what they mean for insurers. That requires the doctrinal frame the decisions are argued within — what makes a contract voidable, what a material misrepresentation is, when the insurer is fixed with its agent's knowledge. [[Landmark Legal]] supplies the decisions; this text supplies the frame.
