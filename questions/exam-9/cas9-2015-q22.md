The chief actuary of a small monoline P&C carrier writing coverage in one state files rates that are 10% higher than they were in the prior year. To support the filing, the chief actuary claims that investors are demanding higher rates of return than what the company has historically achieved. Additionally, the chief actuary notes that competitors have been achieving higher rates of return on equity.

## Part a (1 point)

Evaluate whether the chief actuary's justification for the rate increase is reasonable from the regulator's perspective.

### Explanation
Firms with higher equity would have lower ROE even if they are as profitable. It could mean that insurer is not using capital efficiently.

Investors demanding higher ROE should not be passed on to policyholder as long as surplus protection is sufficient (no need to attract new capital).

Chief actuary justification may not be reasonable.

Alternatively:

No, policyholders should not be subject to the general investment risk of the insurer. They are not owners of capital (shareholders are) so they should not benefit or be penalized from return on surplus. Should not compare one company's return on equity to another. Each company has a different risk profile therefore needs different amounts of surplus. Therefore, rate increase is NOT reasonable.

### Examiner Report
The candidates were expected to explain that the regulator is unconcerned with investor demands, that regulatory focus is on rate equity not rate of return equity, and that ROE may be distorted by surplus allocation methods.
For full credit, both assertions made by the Actuary had to be addressed. Most candidates responded to the ROE assertion, but failed to address the justification related to investor demands. A common mistake was providing general knowledge that did not address the Actuary's statements from a regulatory perspective.

## Part b (1 point)

Identify and briefly describe two additional items the regulator may wish to consider when evaluating whether the proposed rates of **any** insurer are acceptable.

### Explanation
- **Return on sales** – the return divided by the premium. This has intuitive appeal as it is similar to the concept of a markup and may not be distorted by the varying leverage ratios of insurance companies.
- **Industry conditions** – certain signals in the market may support the idea that rates are inadequate such as an increasing size of the residual market, reduced product diversity and innovation, and dividends being paid out of the industry faster than capital is being paid in.

Alternatively:

- **Residual mkt** → growth in the residual market usually means companies are not generating enough returns & so they are tightening underwriting standards / shrinking their books. This may signal to the regulators that rates need to be increased.
- **Investor activity** → is the insurance industry attracting investors? If so, the returns being earned must be adequate. If not, if investors are leaving the industry this may again signal to the regulators that rates need to be raised.

### Examiner Report
Many candidates received full credit on part b. Candidates mentioning a regulatory focus on return-on-sales and using market characteristics to determine overall rate adequacy received full credit. Responses such as the rate must not be excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory received partial credit.

Common mistakes included:

- answering with general information that was not directly related to both (1) the regulator's perspective and (2) ratemaking
- stating items that were appropriate to consider from an industry-wide perspective and applying them to the individual company
