An edited volume on predictive modeling, the use of data to forecast future events, for actuaries and other financial analysts. The publisher describes it as written for analysts developing their expertise in statistics who want concrete examples of predictive modeling, and for seasoned analysts wanting an overview of advanced statistical topics relevant to actuarial practice. It is the first volume of a two-volume set, edited by Edward W. Frees, Richard A. Derrig and Glenn Meyers with each chapter by its own authors, and the project was funded by the Casualty Actuarial Society and the Canadian Institute of Actuaries.

### 6 Frequency and Severity Models
- By Edward W. Frees
- Many insurance datasets record [[Frequency|frequency]], how often claims arise, as well as [[Severity|severity]], the claim size; the chapter introduces tools for handling their joint distribution.
- Frequency-severity modeling matters in insurance because of features of contracts, policyholder behaviour, the databases insurers maintain, and regulatory requirements.
- Model selection depends on the form of the data (a claim amount, with zero meaning no claim in the period, or individual claim amounts) and on the purpose of the inference; the chapter highlights the [[Tweedie Distribution|Tweedie]] generalized linear model as a desirable option.
- A case study of Massachusetts automobile claims compares models by out-of-sample validation.

- 6.2 Sampling and the [[Generalized Linear Model]]
- 6.3 [[Frequency-Severity Models]]
- 6.4 Application: Massachusetts Automobile Claims
