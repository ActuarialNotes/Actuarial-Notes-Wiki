A city hires a contractor to repave a street and requires the contractor to buy an Owners and Contractors Protective (OCP) liability policy (ISO CG 00 09). A pedestrian is injured by the contractor's equipment and sues both the city and the contractor. Which statement about the OCP policy is correct?

- A) It covers both the city and the contractor, because the contractor bought it and paid its premium
- B) It covers only the contractor, because the city's liability is left to its own general liability program
- C) It covers the city's liability from the contractor's work and the city's supervision, not the contractor's
- D) It covers the city only after the contractor's own CGL limits have been used up by the claim

## Explanation

An OCP policy is a stand-alone policy covering the named insured's liability for bodily injury and property damage caused, in whole or in part, by an independent contractor's work for it, including the named insured's own acts or omissions in its general supervision of the work. The contractor (the "designated contractor") buys it on behalf of the party it works for but is not itself an insured, so its own liability stays on its own CGL. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats the buyer as an insured; the designated contractor is not an insured under the OCP policy.
- **B** reverses who is protected: the city is the named insured, and the contractor is not an insured at all.
- **D** treats the OCP policy as excess over the contractor's CGL; it is a separate policy for the owner.
