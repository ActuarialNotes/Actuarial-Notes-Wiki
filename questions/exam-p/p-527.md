Let $X$ and $Y$ be random variables for which

i) $\text{Var}(X) = \text{Var}(Y)$
ii) $\text{Var}(X + Y) = 10$
iii) $\text{Var}(X - 2Y) = 16$.

Calculate $\text{Cov}(X,Y)$.

- A) $-1.0$
- B) $-0.1$
- C) $0.1$
- D) $1.0$
- E) $2.0$

## Explanation

Expand each variance using $\text{Var}(aX + bY) = a^2\text{Var}(X) + b^2\text{Var}(Y) + 2ab\,\text{Cov}(X,Y)$, and write $V$ for the common variance:

$$
\begin{align*}
10 &= \text{Var}(X+Y) = V + V + 2\text{Cov}(X,Y) = 2V + 2\text{Cov}(X,Y) \\
16 &= \text{Var}(X-2Y) = V + 4V - 4\text{Cov}(X,Y) = 5V - 4\text{Cov}(X,Y)
\end{align*}
$$

The first equation gives $V = 5 - \text{Cov}(X,Y)$. Substituting into the second:

$$
\begin{align*}
16 &= 5\bigl[5 - \text{Cov}(X,Y)\bigr] - 4\text{Cov}(X,Y) \\
16 &= 25 - 9\text{Cov}(X,Y) \\
\text{Cov}(X,Y) &= \mathbf{1.0}
\end{align*}
$$

so $V = 4$. Checking back: $2(4) + 2(1) = 10$ and $5(4) - 4(1) = 16$, both as given.

The signs are where this goes wrong. In $\text{Var}(X - 2Y)$ the coefficient $-2$ squares to $+4$ on the variance term but leaves $2ab = -4$ on the covariance — one sign survives and one does not. Choice (A) is the answer with that cross-term sign flipped.
