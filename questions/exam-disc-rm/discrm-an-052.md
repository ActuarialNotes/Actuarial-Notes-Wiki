A large, financially strong retailer owns a headquarters building in an earthquake zone. A major earthquake is unlikely in any year but would cause a loss the retailer could not absorb in full. The insurance market is hard, and earthquake premiums have risen sharply. Which treatment is most appropriate?

- A) Avoid the exposure by abandoning the headquarters and leasing space elsewhere
- B) Retain the whole exposure, since a rare loss belongs in the retention cell of the matrix
- C) Insure with a higher deductible, keeping a layer it can absorb and transferring the rest
- D) Rely on loss prevention alone, since it lowers how often earthquakes occur

## Explanation

A rare, severe exposure points to **transfer by insurance** in the risk management matrix. The matrix is only a starting point: the retailer's size and financial strength, and the high price of insurance in a hard market, argue for retaining more of the risk. Combining the two, it retains a layer it can afford through a higher deductible and transfers the part that would threaten its survival. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Avoidance fits exposures that are both frequent and severe; abandoning a building over a rare event gives up far more than it saves.
- **B** — The retention cell is for losses of low frequency *and* low severity. Retaining a loss the firm cannot absorb fails the survival objective.
- **D** — Nothing the retailer does changes how often earthquakes happen. Loss reduction, such as seismic bracing, can lower the severity, but it does not replace financing.
