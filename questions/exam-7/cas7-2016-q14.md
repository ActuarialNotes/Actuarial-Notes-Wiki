## Part a (2.25 points)

Calculate the actuary's sampled incremental losses for accident years 2012-2014 between 12 and 24 months for the sample under consideration.

### Explanation
The residuals need to be adjusted for heteroscedasticity. Group them by AY: 2010–2012 have similar standard deviations, and so do 2013–2015.

$$\sigma_{2010-2012} = 4.741, \qquad \sigma_{2013-2015} = 1.537, \qquad \max \sigma = 4.741$$

Hetero-adjustment factors:

$$h_{2013-2015} = \frac{4.741}{1.537} = 3.085, \qquad h_{2010-2012} = \frac{4.741}{4.742} = 1.000$$

A sampled residual is multiplied by the factor of the group it is drawn from and divided by the factor of the group it is placed in. The fitted incremental losses from 12 to 24 months are

$$m_{3,2} = 7{,}612 - 3{,}500 = 4{,}112, \qquad m_{4,2} = 8{,}749 - 4{,}210 = 4{,}539, \qquad m_{5,2} = 10{,}654 - 5{,}400 = 5{,}254$$

and the sampled incremental loss is

$$q^*(w,d) = r^*\sqrt{m_{w,d}} + m_{w,d}$$

| AY | Unadjusted Sample Residual | AY Sampled From | Hetero-Adjustment | Adjusted $r^*$ | $q^*(w,d)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | -1.67 | 2014 | 3.085 | 5.152 | 4,442.37 |
| 2013 | -4.70 | 2010 | 1 / 3.085 | -1.524 | 4,436.32 |
| 2014 | -1.94 | 2013 | 1.000 | -1.94 | 5,113.38 |

(The AY 2012 residual is printed as -1.67 in the examiner's report; the residual at (5, 1) in the exhibit is 1.67, which is what the adjusted 5.152 uses.)

$$q^*(3,2) = 5.151\sqrt{4{,}112} + 4{,}112 = 4{,}442.37$$

$$q^*(4,2) = -1.524\sqrt{4{,}539} + 4{,}539 = 4{,}436.32$$

$$q^*(5,2) = -1.94\sqrt{5{,}254} + 5{,}254 = 5{,}113.38$$

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know how to select hetero-groups based on the standard deviations provided. Solutions with different hetero-groups were given credit as long as the candidate provided justification.
- Candidates needed to recognize that sampled residuals should be adjusted for heteroscedasticity.
- Candidates had difficulty earning full credit for this part.
- Common errors include the following:

o No justification provided for selected hetero-groups.
o Hetero-adjustment factors were calculated using the wrong standard deviations o Entirely skipped hetero-adjustments.
o Applied hetero-adjustment factors to the wrong sampled residuals.
o Candidates failed to divide by the hetero-adjustment factors to bring the adjusted residuals back to the original distribution.
o Some candidates did not use incremental fitted losses and applied the process to cumulative losses.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Explain why heteroscedastic residuals might cause issues when using a bootstrapping technique to estimate variance of unpaid claim estimates.

### Explanation
The bootstrapping process assumes we can sample residuals from anywhere in the triangle. If the variance of the residuals differs, the assumption that the residuals are independent and identically distributed is not valid. Adjusting for heteroscedasticity keeps each iteration from overstating or understating the sampled incremental losses (depending on how the variance in that cell relates to other cells), so the bootstrap variance of the loss estimate is not artificially distorted.

Alternatively:

The bootstrapping technique requires the residuals to be IID so that residuals can be sampled from any place in the triangle. Heteroscedastic residuals violate this requirement.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know that residuals are independent and identically distributed in order to sample residuals from the whole triangle.
- Generally, candidates did not earn full credit for this part.
- Common errors include the following:

o Noting that bootstrapping requires residuals that are independent and identically distributed, but did not fully explain the issue when the assumption is violated.
o Other candidates used terms such as "the variance", "variance of incremental losses", or "losses are iid" without mentioning that it's the residuals that have to be IID.
o Many candidates mentioned that it would "increase the variance of the unpaid claim estimate" which is not always the case.
o Full or partial credit was given to candidates who explained the variance of the unpaid claims would increase or decrease due to heteroscedastic residuals.
