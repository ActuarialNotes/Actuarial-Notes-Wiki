A homeowner invites a friend to dinner. A stair tread has worked loose, but the homeowner has not used those stairs in months and does not know about it. The friend falls and is hurt. Under the traditional entrant categories, is the homeowner likely to be liable?

- A) Yes, because a dinner guest is an invitee, owed inspection for hidden hazards
- B) Yes, because an occupier is strictly liable for dangerous conditions on its land
- C) No, because a social guest is a licensee, owed a warning only of known dangers
- D) No, because a guest who goes beyond the dining room becomes a trespasser

## Explanation

A social guest is a **licensee**, even when invited to dinner, because the visit serves no business purpose of the host. The occupier must warn a licensee of dangerous conditions it knows about and the licensee is unlikely to discover, but has no duty to inspect for unknown hazards. The homeowner didn't know of the loose tread, so the duty was not breached.

- **A** misclassifies the guest. Invitees, such as store customers, are owed reasonable inspection, and an invitation alone does not make a guest an invitee.
- **B** applies strict liability, which is reserved for activities such as blasting, not the ordinary condition of a home.
- **D** confuses this with an invitee who strays beyond the area of the invitation. Using the stairs of the home she was invited into does not make the friend a trespasser.
