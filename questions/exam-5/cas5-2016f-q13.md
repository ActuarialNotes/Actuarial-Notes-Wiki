An insurance company is considering updating its territorial relativities given the following information:

| Territory | Number of Exposures | Trended and Ultimate Incurred Losses & ALAE | Current Territorial Relativity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30,000 | $3,000,000 | 1.100 |
| 2 | 50,000 | $4,000,000 | 1.000 |
| 3 | 25,000 | $1,500,000 | 0.850 |

- The base territory remains the same.
- Exposures are homogeneous within each territory.
- The full credibility standard = 45,000 exposures.
- Partial credibility is determined by the square root rule.
- Complement of credibility is equal to normalized current territorial relativities.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate the credibility weighted territorial relativities using the pure premium approach.

### Explanation
Pure premium per territory is trended ultimate losses & ALAE over exposures; credibility is the square root rule against the 45,000-exposure standard; the complement is the current relativity normalised by the exposure-weighted average.

| Territory | Pure Premium | Credibility | Ind. PP Rel. | Norm. Curr. Rel. | Cred. Wtd. Rel. | Cred. Wtd. Rel. @ Base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 81.6% | 1.235 | 1.108 | 1.212 | 1.226 |
| 2 | 80 | 100.0% | 0.988 | 1.007 | 0.988 | 1.000 |
| 3 | 60 | 74.5% | 0.741 | 0.856 | 0.770 | 0.780 |
| **Total** | **81** | | | **0.993** | | **1.012** |

Territory 1 in full:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Pure premium} &= \frac{3{,}000{,}000}{30{,}000} \\
                    &= 100 \\
Z &= \sqrt{\frac{30{,}000}{45{,}000}} \\
  &= 0.816 \\
\text{Ind. PP relativity} &= \frac{100}{80.95} \\
                          &= 1.235 \\
\text{Norm. current relativity} &= \frac{1.100}{0.993} \\
                                 &= 1.108 \\
\text{Cred. wtd. relativity} &= 0.816(1.235) + (1 - 0.816)(1.108) \\
                              &= 1.212 \\
\text{Rebased to territory 2} &= \frac{1.212}{0.988} \\
                               &= 1.226
\end{align*}
$$

All totals are exposure weighted, and the overall average pure premium is 80.95.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to calculate territorial relativities using the pure premium approach, including calculating partial credibility, the credibility-weighted indicated relativities, and normalizing the current and indicated relativities to the correct base.

Common mistakes included:

- Miscalculating the normalized current territorial relativities
- Credibility weighting the indicated pure premium relativities balanced to the base territory with the normalized current territorial relativities balanced to the average rating factor

## Part b (0.75 points)

Determine the percent change by territory, assuming the indicated relativities are to be adopted and no overall premium change is desired.

### Explanation
The indicated change per territory is the new relativity over the current one; the off-balance divides that by the exposure-weighted total so no overall premium change results.

| Territory | Ind. Terr. Rel. Change | Offset = 1/1.019 | % Change with Off-Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 11.5% | 0.987 | 9.4% |
| 2 | 0.0% | 0.987 | -1.9% |
| 3 | -8.3% | 0.987 | 0.0% |

Territory 1:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Ind. terr. rel. change} &= \frac{1.226}{1.100} - 1 \\
                               &= +11.5\% \\
\text{With off-balance} &= \frac{1 + 0.115}{1 + 0.019} - 1 \\
                        &= +9.4\%
\end{align*}
$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to be able to calculate the indicated factor change by territory, use the results to determine the base rate offset needed to achieve a revenue-neutral rate change, and calculate the final percentage change by territory.
Common mistakes included:

- Not calculating the offset, simply dividing the indicated territorial relativities from part a by the current territorial relativities
- Calculating the wrong offset by using either the current territorial relativities or the indicated territorial relativities, but not both, in the calculation
- Not converting the territorial impacts to a final percentage change

## Part c (0.75 points)

Briefly discuss three reasons why proposed rate changes might deviate from indicated rate changes.

### Explanation
Any three of:

- Regulation may restrict large rate increases or decreases.
- Large premium swings are avoided to keep customers from leaving.
- Competitive concerns — an increase could cost market share.
- Lifetime profitability: losses run higher on new business than on renewals, so a long-term pricing approach may be preferred to the one-year indication.
- On a volatile line, where the data's volatility and credibility produce very large indications, judgment may be used to propose a more reasonable change.
- The insurer may address the imbalance through underwriting guidelines instead of rates.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know why, generally, proposed changes might deviate from indicated changes.
A common mistake was explaining why actual performance could be different than indicated performance.
