Given the following information:

| | Calendar Year 2014 |
|---|---|
| Written premium | $560.00 |
| Earned premium | $616.00 |
| Commissions | $67.20 |
| Taxes, licenses and fees | $19.60 |
| General expenses | $73.92 |
| LAE ratio (to loss) | 8.2% |
| Combined ratio | 100% |

Calculate the 2014 operating expense ratio.

## Explanation

First the underwriting expense ratios. Commissions and taxes are incurred when a policy is written, so they are taken against written premium; general expenses are taken against earned premium.

$$\begin{align*}
\text{Commissions} &= \frac{\$67.20}{\$560.00} \\
                   &= 0.120 \\[4pt]
\text{Taxes, licenses and fees} &= \frac{\$19.60}{\$560.00} \\
                                &= 0.035 \\[4pt]
\text{General expenses} &= \frac{\$73.92}{\$616.00} \\
                        &= 0.120
\end{align*}$$

Next the LAE ratio to earned premium. The combined ratio is $100\%$, and it is the loss-and-LAE ratio plus the underwriting expense ratio (assuming, as for a combined ratio, that the underwriting expenses are incurred at policy inception and so are taken against written premium):

$$100\% = (\text{loss ratio})(1 + 0.082) + \frac{\$67.20 + \$19.60 + \$73.92}{\$560.00}$$

$$\text{loss ratio} = \frac{0.713}{1.082} = 0.659$$

$$\frac{\text{LAE}}{\text{earned premium}} = 0.659 \times 0.082 = 0.054$$

The operating expense ratio is the LAE ratio plus the underwriting expense ratios:

$$\text{Operating expense ratio} = 0.054 + 0.120 + 0.035 + 0.120 = 0.329$$
