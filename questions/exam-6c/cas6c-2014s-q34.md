According to the Canadian Institute of Actuaries publication "Report: Materiality":

## Part a (0.25 points)

Identify the main factor underlying the selection of a materiality level in an actuary's work.

### Explanation
Is the user perspective of the work

### Examiner Report
- Candidates are expected to know the main factor for materiality level selection
- Candidates generally did well on this part

## Part b (0.25 points)

Briefly describe when an omission, understatement or overstatement is considered material.

### Explanation
Is considered material if the actuary thinks it will materially impact the decision making of the user or the reasonable expectation of the user.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates are expected to know when a materiality change will need to be reported.
- Candidates generally scored well on this part

## Part c (0.5 points)

Describe the difference between the materiality level for Dynamic Capital Adequacy Testing work and the materiality level for valuation work.

### Explanation
The level for DCAT is less rigorous that the one for the valuation work.

- Materiality for valuation is to see the material impact on net income, earning
- Materiality level for DCAT is for the surplus used in the scenario testing, but no need to be as rigourous.

Additional accepted answer: For DCAT, the materiality level is related to adverse scenarios; for valuation, the materiality level is related to financial statement, policy liability or profit level.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates are expected to know the differences between DCAT and valuation in terms of materiality level.
- Most candidates failed to describe what determines the materiality levels for DCAT and valuation work, and they simply stated that DCAT materiality level is less rigorous than valuation.
