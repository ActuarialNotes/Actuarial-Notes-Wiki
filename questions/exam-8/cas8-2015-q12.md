An actuary is evaluating the effectiveness of an experience rating plan and has calculated the following values:

| Risk size | Standard Loss Ratio: Risks with credit mod | Standard Loss Ratio: Risks with debit mod | Sample Variance in Loss Ratios: Unmodified | Sample Variance in Loss Ratios: Modified |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 1.05 | 1.08 | 0.07 | 0.008 |
| Medium | 0.98 | 0.96 | 0.05 | 0.004 |
| Large | 0.99 | 1.00 | 0.04 | 0.004 |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Evaluate whether this plan satisfies the necessary condition for proper credibility.

### Explanation
Necessary condition: credit or debit mods should have equal standard loss ratios in the prospective period.

The debit/credit mod standard LRs are fairly close, thus I'd say the condition is met.

Alternatively:

The necessary condition for credibility says that debit and credit risks should have the same permissible loss ratio.

The ratios are not the same, so the plan does not satisfy this condition.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to understand the necessary condition and how to apply it to the given data.
- Candidates scored very well on part a, with the majority receiving full credit.
- Common mistakes included confusing the sufficient condition with the necessary condition, and comparing standard loss ratios between size groups rather than credit/debit mod groups.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Determine which risk size has the most accurate experience rating based on the efficiency test.

### Explanation
Efficiency test statistic = variance(modified LR) / variance(unmodified LR):

| Risk Size | Test Statistic |
|---|---|
| Small | .008/.07 = 0.114 |
| Medium | .004/.05 = 0.08 |
| Large | .004/.04 = 0.1 |

Medium risk has the lowest statistic, so it has the most accurate experience rating based on the efficiency test.

Alternatively, with the ratio inverted — variance(manual LR) / variance(standard LR), where higher is better — Small .07/.008 = 8.75, Medium 12.5 and Large 10: medium risks have the most accurate experience rating.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know how to conduct the efficiency test and interpret the results.
- Candidates scored very well on part b, with the majority receiving full credit.
- A common mistake was making calculation errors that lead to an incorrect conclusion.
- Candidates who calculated the efficiency test statistic correctly but drew the incorrect conclusion received partial credit.
- Candidate that made small math errors in the calculation but drew the right conclusion received full credit as they were able to demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the concept.

## Part c (0.5 points)

It has been determined that premiums are inadequate for small risks. Discuss whether premium inadequacy is better corrected by changing the manual rates or the experience rating plan.

### Explanation
It's better to correct with the manual rates, because an overall inadequacy is best handled in the base rates. The experience mod plan is intended to adjust for individual cost differences.

Alternatively: changing manual rates will be better to correct for inadequacy because if the experience rating plan adjusts for the inadequacy (off-balance increases) then the problem will persist. The off-balance will increase and mask some of the actual rate level needed.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to understand the purpose of experience rating as a tool to correct for individual risk differences rather than overall premium adequacy.
- Part c was more challenging for candidates, though the majority received at least partial credit.

- A common mistake was identifying the experience rating plan as the better way to correct premium inadequacy.
