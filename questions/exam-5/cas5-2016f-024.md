Given the following information as of December 31, 2014:

Paid Claims (\$) as of (months)

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 200 | 560 | 570 |
| 2012 | 150 | 250 | 400 |
| 2013 | 150 | 350 | |
| 2014 | 50 | | |

Received Salvage and Subrogation (\$) as of (months)

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 20 | 90 | 100 |
| 2012 | 16 | 40 | 70 |
| 2013 | 15 | 56 | |
| 2014 | 5 | | |

- Ultimate claims for accident year 2014 = \$150.
- There is no development beyond 36 months.
- A simple all-year average is used for all development factors.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Estimate the ultimate salvage and subrogation for accident year 2014 using the development technique.

### Answer
\$25.59

### Explanation
The development technique treats received salvage and subrogation exactly like a claim triangle: build age-to-age factors on the S&S dollars themselves and develop the latest diagonal to ultimate.

**Age-to-age factors:**

| Accident Year | 12-24 | 24-36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | $90/20 = 4.500$ | $100/90 = 1.111$ |
| 2012 | $40/16 = 2.500$ | $70/40 = 1.750$ |
| 2013 | $56/15 = 3.733$ | |
| Simple average | 3.578 | 1.431 |

**Cumulative factors.** There is no development beyond 36 months, so the tail is 1.000:

$$\text{CDF}_{24} = 1.431,\qquad \text{CDF}_{12} = 3.578 \times 1.431 = 5.118$$

**Ultimate for accident year 2014:**

$$\text{Ultimate S\&S} = 5 \times 5.118 = \mathbf{\$25.59}$$

Note how leveraged this is: the entire estimate rests on \$5 of receipts at 12 months multiplied by a factor above 5, and the individual 12-24 link ratios range from 2.500 to 4.500.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to build a salvage and subrogation development triangle from the received S&S data, select all-year average age-to-age factors, cumulate them to a 12-to-ultimate factor, and apply it to the accident year 2014 diagonal.

The common error was skipping the development triangle entirely rather than deriving the factors from the data given.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Estimate the ultimate salvage and subrogation for accident year 2014 using a ratio approach.

### Answer
\$25.72

### Explanation
The ratio approach develops the **ratio of received salvage and subrogation to paid claims** rather than the S&S dollars, then applies the developed ratio to ultimate claims. Because both numerator and denominator grow with the size of the book, the ratio is far more stable than the raw S&S triangle.

**Step 1 — S&S-to-paid-claims ratio triangle.**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | $20/200 = 0.100$ | $90/560 = 0.161$ | $100/570 = 0.175$ |
| 2012 | $16/150 = 0.107$ | $40/250 = 0.160$ | $70/400 = 0.175$ |
| 2013 | $15/150 = 0.100$ | $56/350 = 0.160$ | |
| 2014 | $5/50 = 0.100$ | | |

The 12-month ratio has been essentially 0.100 in every year, and the 36-month ratio 0.175 in both mature years.

**Step 2 — develop the ratios.**

| Accident Year | 12-24 | 24-36 |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 1.607 | 1.092 |
| 2012 | 1.500 | 1.094 |
| 2013 | 1.600 | |
| Simple average | 1.569 | 1.093 |

$$\text{CDF}_{12} = 1.569 \times 1.093 = 1.714$$

**Step 3 — ultimate ratio and ultimate S&S.**

$$\text{Ultimate ratio}_{2014} = 0.100 \times 1.714 = 0.1714$$

$$\text{Ultimate S\&S} = 150 \times 0.1714 = \mathbf{\$25.72}$$

*Alternatively*, the ratios can be developed **additively** instead of multiplicatively — the increment added to the ratio between maturities. The 12-24 increments are 0.061, 0.053 and 0.060 (average 0.058) and the 24-36 increments are both 0.015, so the 12-to-ultimate addition is 0.073 and the ultimate ratio is $0.100 + 0.073 = 0.173$, giving $150 \times 0.173 = \$25.93$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to divide received salvage and subrogation by paid claims to form a ratio triangle, develop that triangle to obtain a 12-to-ultimate factor, apply it to the accident year 2014 ratio, and multiply the resulting ultimate ratio by ultimate claims of \$150.

Common errors included selecting an ultimate ratio judgmentally off the mature years instead of developing the 12-month ratio; picking development factors off a single pair of ratios rather than the whole triangle; and arithmetic slips in cumulating the factors or converting the ultimate ratio into dollars.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Given the following additional information for accident year 2015 as of December 31, 2015:

- Ultimate claims = \$175
- Salvage and subrogation received = \$12

Recommend and briefly justify an ultimate salvage and subrogation estimate for accident year 2015.

### Answer
Approximately \$30, using the ratio approach.

### Explanation
Compare what the two techniques would produce for accident year 2015 at 12 months.

**Development technique:** $12 \times 5.118 = \$61.4$. That is 35% of ultimate claims of \$175, roughly double the 17.5% ratio every mature year has settled at. The estimate is driven by a single small, volatile 12-month figure multiplied by a factor above 5, so any noise in the diagonal is magnified fivefold.

**Ratio approach:** the S&S-to-claims ratio has been stable across the whole triangle — 0.100 at 12 months in all four prior years, 0.160 at 24 months, 0.175 at 36 months. Developing the selected 12-month ratio to ultimate:

$$\text{Ultimate S\&S} = 175 \times (0.100 \times 1.714) = 175 \times 0.1714 = \$30.0$$

Selecting the mature 36-month ratio of 0.175 directly gives essentially the same figure, $175 \times 0.175 = \$30.6$.

**Recommendation:** use the ratio approach, roughly \$30. Accident year 2015 is immature, the direct development factors are highly leveraged and volatile, and the ratio approach anchors the estimate to a relationship that has been consistent year over year.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to recognize that the age-to-age factors in part a are highly leveraged on an immature accident year and would give a volatile, overstated answer, and to recommend the ratio approach on the grounds of its stability, supporting the recommendation with a numerical estimate.

Common errors included recommending the development-technique ultimate; applying an undeveloped salvage-and-subrogation-to-claims ratio to ultimate claims; and giving a recommendation without an adequate justification.
