In a large state, drivers are divided into three distinct age groups. The following table provides the probabilities that a one-car accident, involving a driver in a given age group, occurs in an urban, suburban or rural location in that state.

| Driver's age | Urban | Suburban | Rural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 25 | 7/10 | 1/5 | 1/10 |
| 25-65 | 1/4 | 3/5 | 3/20 |
| Over 65 | 1/20 | 7/20 | 3/5 |

Fifty-five percent of one-car accidents involve a driver who is under 25, and fifteen percent involve a driver who is over 65.

A one-car accident occurs in a suburban location.

Calculate the probability that the driver is under 25.

- A) $0.11$
- B) $0.17$
- C) $0.32$
- D) $0.55$
- E) $0.76$

## Explanation

The three age groups partition the accidents, and the middle group's share follows by subtraction:

$$
P[\text{25-65}] = 1 - 0.55 - 0.15 = 0.30
$$

The table rows are **conditional** on age group, so the total probability of a suburban accident weights each row by its age-group share:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{suburban}] &= (0.55)\left(\tfrac{1}{5}\right) + (0.30)\left(\tfrac{3}{5}\right) + (0.15)\left(\tfrac{7}{20}\right) \\
                   &= 0.11 + 0.18 + 0.0525 \\
                   &= 0.3425
\end{align*}
$$

Bayes' theorem then picks out the under-25 share of that total:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{under 25} \mid \text{suburban}] &= \frac{(0.55)\left(\tfrac{1}{5}\right)}{0.3425} \\
                                        &= \frac{0.11}{0.3425} \\
                                        &= \mathbf{0.3212}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 0.55, is the prior — the answer if location told you nothing. It should fall, because under-25 drivers have the *lowest* suburban rate of the three groups (1/5 against 3/5 and 7/20). Choice (A), 0.11, is the joint probability without the division.
