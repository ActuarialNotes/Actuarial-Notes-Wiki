An insurance agent meets twelve potential customers independently, each of whom is equally likely to purchase an insurance product. Six are interested only in auto insurance, four are interested only in homeowners insurance, and two are interested only in life insurance.

The agent makes six sales.

Calculate the probability that two are for auto insurance, two are for homeowners insurance, and two are for life insurance.

- A) 0.001
- B) 0.024
- C) 0.069
- D) 0.097
- E) 0.500

## Explanation

Since each customer is equally likely to purchase an insurance product and the agent makes exactly six sales, this is a hypergeometric probability problem (selecting 6 out of 12 customers, where the customers belong to three different groups).

The total number of ways to choose 6 customers from 12 is $\binom{12}{6}$.
The number of ways to choose 2 auto (out of 6), 2 homeowners (out of 4), and 2 life (out of 2) is $\binom{6}{2} \binom{4}{2} \binom{2}{2}$.

The probability is:
$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \frac{\binom{6}{2}\binom{4}{2}\binom{2}{2}}{\binom{12}{6}} \\
  &= \frac{15 \times 6 \times 1}{924} \\
  &= \frac{90}{924} \\
  &\approx 0.097
\end{align*}
$$
