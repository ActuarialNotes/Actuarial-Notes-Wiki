The guide to Canada's P&C **[[Guaranty Funds|guaranty fund]]** — the compensation plan that pays policyholders when a property and casualty insurer fails. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objectives B1–B3.

> [!warning]- Coverage limits are updated on PACICC's site
> The CAS content outline directs candidates to [pacicc.ca/industry-information/coverage](http://www.pacicc.ca/industry-information/coverage/) for the **current** coverage and limit, not the figures printed in the 2010 guide. As currently published, the maximum recovery is **\$435,000** for automobile, **\$545,000** for personal property and home warranty, and **\$400,000** for commercial and other covered policies; the unearned-premium refund is **70% of the unexpired premium, to a maximum of \$2,500** paid in. Learn the mechanism from the guide and the numbers from the site.

## Origin

The Plan came out of five years of negotiation with the provincial and federal Superintendents of Insurance. [[PACICC]] was incorporated as a **federal non-profit corporation in 1988**, and the Plan has operated country-wide since **1989**. Before PACICC could enter into an agreement with a province or territory, that jurisdiction's Superintendent had to agree with PACICC on **prudential criteria** — the solvency standards imposed on every P&C insurer in the jurisdiction. A jurisdiction must consult PACICC before amending them. That is the structural point: a guaranty fund that pays for failures acquires a say in the standards that prevent them.

## 1. The Plan — what is covered

Most claims under most P&C policies. Excluded because of their distinctive characteristics: **aircraft, credit, crop, directors' and officers', employer's liability, certain errors and omissions (medical malpractice is *not* excluded), fidelity, financial guarantee, marine, mortgage, surety and title**. Life insurers have their own plan and are excluded from this one. Automobile insurance in **Manitoba and Saskatchewan** is excluded, as are bodily-injury claims from Quebec auto accidents compensated by the SAAQ — but [[Public Auto Insurance|government-owned insurers]] otherwise participate. Accident and sickness is protected where the insurer also writes general insurance.

Unearned premium was originally not covered at all; for insolvencies after 1996 the Plan responds to unearned-premium claims, subject to the percentage and cap above.

## 2–3. Membership and operation

Membership is a condition of licensing in participating jurisdictions. PACICC is run by a **board elected by its members**, working closely with [[OSFI]] and the provincial [[Superintendent of Insurance|Superintendents]], who may designate non-voting representatives to board discussions and to any insolvency advisory committee.

**PACICC's obligations arise only on a formal winding-up order under the federal *Winding-Up and Restructuring Act*.** Until that order is made, PACICC has no financial responsibility for the insurer.

## 4–7. Procedure, payment, and recovery

- Immediately after the winding-up order, PACICC representatives establish a working relationship with the court-appointed liquidator (informal consultation typically begins earlier).
- **The limit applies per named insured, per occurrence**, aggregating all claims under all policies issued by the insolvent insurer. The insured's entitlement is computed first under the policy terms ([[Deductible|deductibles]], co-insurance), then capped at the PACICC limit. On a \$300,000 claim with a \$250 deductible the insured looks to PACICC for the limit; on a \$175,000 claim with a \$500 deductible, for \$174,500. **The Plan provides basic compensation, not full protection.**
- Because claims are aggregated per occurrence, PACICC may have to set **priorities** for how a payment is applied among competing claimants.
- Before payment the policyholder must **certify that any claim against a solvent insurer covering the same loss has been exhausted**, and must **assign to PACICC all rights against the insolvent insurer** under the policy. The assignment is normally handled in the wind-up order: on a \$300,000 claim where PACICC pays the limit, the insured shares in the liquidator's distribution only to the extent the distribution exceeds what PACICC paid.
- PACICC takes **first priority** against amounts the insured recovers from third parties for the same loss.

## 8. Assessment process

PACICC recovers what it advanced by **assessing participating insurers**, separately for each participating jurisdiction in which the insolvent insurer wrote business. Assessments are limited to the shortfall between what PACICC advanced and what it recovered from the estate and from third parties. Each insurer's share is its **direct written premium for protected policies in that jurisdiction, over the industry total for that jurisdiction** — taken from the returns insurers file with the Superintendents.

**The maximum annual levy on an insurer is 1.5% of its direct written premium in the jurisdiction.** In practice the board makes a general assessment early in an insolvency reflecting anticipated maximum exposure and then draws on it; alternatively it may borrow from the **Compensation Fund** and delay assessing until the exposure can be estimated, the borrowing to be repaid with interest. The board also levies **administrative assessments** for PACICC's running costs.

## The exam angles

- **[[Moral Hazard|Moral hazard]]** — a guaranty fund reduces the policyholder's incentive to shop on financial strength, which is the argument for the prudential criteria and the per-claim cap.
- **Post-assessment funding** — PACICC assesses *after* a failure rather than pre-funding, so the cost of a large failure falls on surviving insurers when they may themselves be under stress. The 1.5% annual cap limits the pace at which it can be recovered.
- **Coverage design** — the excluded classes are the commercial and specialty lines whose buyers are presumed able to assess an insurer's strength.
