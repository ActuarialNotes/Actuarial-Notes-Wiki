A general contractor applying for CGL coverage subcontracts about 60% of its work, including all of its electrical and roofing work. Which request best addresses the exposure the subcontracting creates?

- A) Its payroll records, so the subcontracted work can be rated on payroll
- B) Certificates of insurance from its subcontractors and copies of the subcontracts
- C) A list of the products it sells, so the products hazard can be rated
- D) An inspection of its office, to judge the condition of its premises

## Explanation

A general contractor is liable for injuries caused by its subcontractors' work. The underwriter needs to know whether each subcontractor carries its own liability insurance with adequate limits, and whether the subcontracts transfer liability back to the subcontractor and add the general contractor as an additional insured. Certificates of insurance and the contracts are the evidence. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Subcontracted work is rated on its total cost, and payroll records don't show whether the subcontractors are insured.
- **C** — A contractor's products hazard is its completed work; a product list doesn't address the subcontractors.
- **D** — A contractor's exposure is mostly at job sites, not at its own office.
