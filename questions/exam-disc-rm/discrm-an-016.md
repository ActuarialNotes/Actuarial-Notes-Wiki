With illustrative figures, an insurer sets next year's rates for a line expecting losses of \$120 million and expenses of \$60 million on premium of \$200 million. During the year, storms push incurred losses to \$150 million; expenses come in as planned, and investment income on the line's funds is \$16 million. Which statement is correct?

- A) The combined ratio is 90%, so the line made an underwriting profit.
- B) The combined ratio is 105%, an underwriting loss, but the line is profitable after investment income.
- C) The combined ratio is 105%, so the line lost money overall.
- D) The combined ratio is 113%, so the line lost money even after investment income.

## Explanation

The price was set before the cost was known, and the cost turned out higher:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{CR} &= \frac{150}{200} + \frac{60}{200} \\
&= 105\% \\
\text{Operating ratio} &= 105\% - \frac{16}{200} \\
&= 97\%
\end{align*}
$$

Above 100%, the combined ratio is an underwriting loss of \$10 million, but the \$16 million of investment income leaves a \$6 million profit. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — 90% is the planned combined ratio, $(120 + 60)/200$. The premium was fixed in advance, but the losses were not.
- **C** — The combined ratio leaves out investment income; an insurer can lose money on underwriting and still be profitable.
- **D** — 113% adds the investment income ratio to the combined ratio instead of subtracting it.
