An insurer's losses are modeled by a random variable $X$, with density function, $f$, where $f(x)$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, for $x > 100$, and 0, otherwise.

Calculate the median of $X$.

- A) $100$
- B) $120$
- C) $150$
- D) $200$
- E) $300$

## Explanation

Write $f(x) = cx^{-2}$ and find $c$ from the requirement that the density integrates to 1:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= c\int_{100}^\infty x^{-2}\,dx \\
  &= -cx^{-1}\Big|_{100}^\infty \\
  &= \frac{c}{100} \\
c &= 100
\end{align*}
$$

Now set the cumulative probability to one half at the median $M$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.5 &= 100\int_{100}^{M} x^{-2}\,dx \\
    &= -100x^{-1}\Big|_{100}^{M} \\
    &= 1 - \frac{100}{M}
\end{align*}
$$

so $100/M = 0.5$ and

$$
M = \mathbf{200}
$$

The survival function here is $P[X > x] = 100/x$, which halves exactly when $x$ doubles — so the median being twice the lower bound is a structural feature of this density, not a coincidence of the numbers. Choice (C), 150, is the midpoint of 100 and 200, which would be the median only for a uniform distribution; this density is heavily concentrated near 100 and has a long right tail.
