A manufacturer makes hydraulic valves that a customer installs in its packaging machines. Consider three losses under the manufacturer's CGL: (1) a batch of valves turns out to be defective, so the customer's machines run at half speed until the valves are swapped, and the customer claims its lost output; (2) the manufacturer withdraws the whole batch from the market and spends \$200,000 replacing valves; (3) after a machine has been in normal use for a month, one valve suddenly bursts, shutting the customer's line for two days, and the customer claims its lost output. Which loss does Coverage A cover?

- A) Loss 1 only
- B) Loss 2 only
- C) Losses 1 and 3
- D) Loss 3 only

## Explanation

- **Loss 1** is loss of use of "impaired property": the machines can be restored by replacing the defective valves. Exclusion m removes property damage to impaired property or property not physically injured arising out of a defect in the insured's product.
- **Loss 2** is a recall cost, excluded by exclusion n (loss of use, withdrawal, recall, replacement of the insured's product because of a known or suspected defect).
- **Loss 3** falls in the exception to exclusion m: loss of use of other property arising out of *sudden and accidental physical injury* to the insured's product after it has been put to its intended use.

Only loss 3 is covered, so the answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** and **C** miss the impaired property exclusion for loss 1.
- **B** treats the insurer as paying recall costs, which exclusion n removes.
