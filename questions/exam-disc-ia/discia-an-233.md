Match each suit to the basis of liability it rests on: (1) a driver who ran a red light while texting; (2) a homeowner who punched a delivery driver during an argument; (3) a fireworks company whose display exploded in spite of every precaution.

- A) (1) intentional tort, (2) negligence, (3) strict liability
- B) (1) negligence, (2) intentional tort, (3) strict liability
- C) (1) negligence, (2) strict liability, (3) intentional tort
- D) (1) strict liability, (2) intentional tort, (3) negligence

## Explanation

Tort law has three bases of liability. Running a red light while texting is a failure to use reasonable care, so it is **negligence**. A deliberate punch is a battery, an **intentional tort**. A fireworks display is an abnormally dangerous activity, so the company is liable without fault under **strict liability**, whatever precautions it took.

- **A** treats the careless driver as intending harm and the punch as careless.
- **C** puts the punch under strict liability and the precautions case under intent.
- **D** puts the driver under strict liability, which does not apply to ordinary driving, and the fireworks company under negligence, which it did not commit.
