The owner of a whole life policy has paid level annual premiums for six years and now stops paying. Under a state law based on the NAIC Standard Nonforfeiture Law for Life Insurance, what must the policy provide?

- A) Nothing, because the cash value is forfeited when a scheduled premium is missed
- B) A refund of every premium paid, less any policy loan outstanding
- C) A paid-up nonforfeiture benefit, or a cash surrender value on surrender
- D) Continued coverage at the full face amount, with the charges taken from the cash value

## Explanation

Nonforfeiture laws exist so that a lapse does not forfeit the value built up by level premiums. On default, the policy must grant a paid-up nonforfeiture benefit stated in the policy, and because premiums have been paid for at least three full years, the owner may instead surrender the policy for a cash surrender value. A specified paid-up benefit takes effect automatically unless the owner elects another option within 60 days.

- **A** is the outcome nonforfeiture laws were written to prevent.
- **B** confuses the cash surrender value with a premium refund. The law requires a cash surrender value computed by its own formula, not a return of the premiums paid.
- **D** describes a universal life policy, whose flexible premiums and account value keep it in force while the account covers the charges. Whole life premiums follow a set schedule.
