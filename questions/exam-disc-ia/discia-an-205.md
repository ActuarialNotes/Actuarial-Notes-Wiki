A 58-year-old has \$150,000 to set aside for retirement in ten years. She will not accept any loss of principal from a market decline, but she wants her money to earn more than a fixed rate when the stock market rises, and she accepts that her share of a rise may be capped. Which annuity best fits her?

- A) A fixed indexed deferred annuity
- B) A variable deferred annuity invested in stock subaccounts
- C) A single premium immediate annuity, life only
- D) A fixed deferred annuity with a three-year initial rate

## Explanation

A fixed indexed annuity credits interest based on part of an index's rise, limited by participation rates, caps or spreads, and the credit is never less than zero. Her account does not fall when the index falls, so long as she does not withdraw, which meets both of her conditions.

- **B** gives full market exposure but no guarantee: if the subaccounts fall, she can end with less than she paid in.
- **C** starts income now and gives up the money as a lump sum, while she wants growth for ten years first.
- **D** protects principal, but its interest is set by the insurer and does not rise with the market.
