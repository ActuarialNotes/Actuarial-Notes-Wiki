An article in the November 2024 issue of The Financial Reporter, the newsletter of the Society of Actuaries' Financial Reporting Section. It explains that even when a block of business is fully ceded, earnings discrepancies may still appear between the direct and ceded businesses under both long-duration targeted improvements (LDTI) and IFRS 17, resulting in non-zero net earnings. It focuses on the ceding commission structure and on a reinsurance treaty implemented after the direct business was issued.

## Ceding Commission Structure
- LDTI
    - LDTI requires that no immediate earnings or losses be recognized at the start of a reinsurance treaty; a ceding commission excess or shortfall relative to the expenses paid by the direct writer is recorded and amortized through the "cost of reinsurance".
- IFRS 17
    - Paragraph 38 of IFRS 17 stipulates that no gains should be recognized at the start date of a reporting unit; differences between direct expenses and ceding commissions make the contractual service margin (CSM) differ between direct and ceded, and so the amortized amounts differ.

## Block Treaty
- A block treaty is a reinsurance agreement that covers in-force business.
- LDTI
    - The day 1 premium may differ from the actual ceded benefit, creating a balance that must be deferred and amortized as a cost of reinsurance; the net premium ratio and the locked-in discount rate also differ between the direct and ceded liability for future policyholder benefits.
- IFRS 17
    - The reinsurance premium paid, the ceding commission less the assets transferred by the direct writer, is a key driver of the initial ceded CSM, which can be materially different from the direct CSM.
