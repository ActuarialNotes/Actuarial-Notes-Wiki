A building is covered by two commercial property policies written on the same plan, terms, conditions and provisions: Policy A with a \$300,000 limit and Policy B with a \$200,000 limit. A covered fire causes a \$150,000 loss. Under the other insurance condition of the commercial property conditions, how much does Policy A pay?

- A) \$60,000
- B) \$75,000
- C) \$90,000
- D) \$150,000

## Explanation

When other insurance covers on the same plan, terms, conditions and provisions, each insurer pays the proportion its limit bears to the limits of all insurance covering on the same basis:

$$
\text{Share}_A = 150{,}000 \times \frac{300{,}000}{500{,}000} = 90{,}000
$$

The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — \$60,000 is Policy B's share.
- **B** — Splits the loss equally rather than in proportion to the limits.
- **D** — Treats Policy A as primary. The excess rule applies only to other insurance that is *not* on the same basis.
