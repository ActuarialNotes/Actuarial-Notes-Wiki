An employer's U.S. workers compensation policy was written on estimated payroll of \$1,500,000, all in a clerical classification with a rate of \$0.30 per \$100 of payroll. The final audit finds actual payroll of \$1,600,000, of which \$400,000 was paid to employees who load trucks in the warehouse, a classification rated at \$4.20 per \$100. What is the audit adjustment?

- A) Additional premium of \$300
- B) Additional premium of \$15,900
- C) Additional premium of \$16,800
- D) Additional premium of \$20,400

## Explanation

The audit checks the classification of each employee's duties as well as the total payroll:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Deposit} &= \frac{1{,}500{,}000}{100} \times 0.30 \\
&= 4{,}500 \\
\text{Audited} &= \frac{1{,}200{,}000}{100} \times 0.30 + \frac{400{,}000}{100} \times 4.20 \\
&= 3{,}600 + 16{,}800 \\
&= 20{,}400 \\
\text{Adjustment} &= 20{,}400 - 4{,}500 \\
&= 15{,}900
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**. Payroll is the premium base, and the misclassification, not the growth in payroll, is most of the difference.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Corrects the payroll but leaves everyone in the clerical class: $16{,}000 \times 0.30 - 4{,}500$.
- **C** — Is the warehouse premium alone, ignoring both the clerical premium and the deposit.
- **D** — Is the audited premium, not the adjustment to the deposit already paid.
