Insurance Bureau of Canada's national news release of October 22, 2026 reporting that severe weather caused more than $3.7 billion in insured losses in the summer of 2026, according to estimates from Catastrophe Indices and Quantification Inc. (CatIQ). That makes the summer of 2026 the third-costliest summer in Canadian history for losses related to severe weather. The amount of insured damage is an estimate provided by CatIQ under licence to IBC; see [[Catastrophe Loss]].

## Summer's insured losses underscore the growing financial impact of severe weather across Canada
- Severe weather caused more than $3.7 billion in insured losses this summer, per CatIQ's estimates.
- Four of the five costliest summers in Canadian history have occurred within the last six years.
- Noteworthy events: June storms in Manitoba, Saskatchewan and the city of Montreal; July and August flooding in Ontario's Niagara Region; late-June and early-July storms across Ontario and Quebec; intense wildfires across British Columbia; and multiple flooding, wind and hail events across the Prairies.

## Rising costs of extreme weather in Canada
- Over the last decade, insured losses from extreme weather in Canada have averaged more than $3.7 billion annually, up from $1.4 billion in the previous decade and $883 million in the decade before that.
- Annual losses have increased by roughly 300% since the late 1990s and early 2000s, even after accounting for inflation.
- In 2024, Canada experienced its costliest year on record, with insured losses from extreme weather exceeding $9 billion.

## Protecting Canadians from severe weather risks
- IBC calls on all orders of government to build new homes away from high-risk flood and wildfire zones, provide incentives for homeowners to invest in mitigation, require community-level mitigation when building in high-risk areas is unavoidable, invest in resilient infrastructure, strengthen building codes and construction standards, and provide clear, accessible risk information to homeowners, renters and businesses.
