A state has adopted the NAIC Nonadmitted Insurance Model Act, and its minimum capital and surplus for a licensed property and casualty insurer is \$3,000,000. A surplus lines broker wants to place a risk with a nonadmitted insurer domiciled in another state, authorized there for the line, which holds capital and surplus of \$9,000,000. Is the insurer eligible?

- A) Yes, because \$9,000,000 exceeds the state's \$3,000,000 minimum
- B) No, and it can never be, because it holds less than \$15,000,000
- C) Not as of right, but the commissioner may find it acceptable because it holds more than \$4,500,000
- D) Yes, because a nonadmitted insurer is not subject to capital tests in the insured's state

## Explanation

The model requires a U.S.-domiciled surplus lines insurer to hold capital and surplus of at least the greater of the state's minimum and \$15,000,000:

$$
\max(3{,}000{,}000,\ 15{,}000{,}000) = 15{,}000{,}000
$$

At \$9,000,000 the insurer falls short. The commissioner may still make an affirmative finding of acceptability, based on factors such as management, parent capital, results and reputation, but never for an insurer with less than \$4,500,000. This one is above that floor.

- **A** applies the licensed-insurer minimum alone and forgets the \$15,000,000 alternative.
- **B** forgets the commissioner's power to accept a smaller insurer.
- **D** is wrong: eligibility is a capital test the surplus lines broker must check before placing the business.
