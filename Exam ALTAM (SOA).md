# Exam ALTAM
The ALTAM Exam is a three-hour exam consisting of six questions, worth a total of 60 points.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The exam will be taken at Prometric testing centers. The questions will be displayed on the computer. Candidates will also be provided with an Excel Workbook. The Excel Workbook will contain one question. It will also contain tables and formulas that may be used for any of the six questions.
>
> Five of the questions are to be answered in pen in exam answer booklets provided by Prometric. For these questions candidates may use the Excel Tables, and may use Excel for calculations, but only the answers provided in the exam answer booklet will be graded.
>
> One of the questions will be answered in the Excel Workbook. For this question, only the information provided in the Excel Workbook will be graded. Candidates may use the scratch paper booklet for rough notes, but nothing written on the scratch paper will be graded.
>
> At the end of the exam candidates will upload their Excel Workbook for grading the Excel question and will submit their exam answer booklets to Prometric to forward to the SOA for grading the questions answered in the answer booklets.
>
> Knowledge of the FM, P, and FAM exams, and the Mathematical Statistics VEE material is assumed for the ALTAM Exam.
>
> Exam Registration Candidates may register online or with an application.
>
> Introductory Study Note The Introductory Study Note has a complete listing of all study notes as well as errata and other important information.
>
> Past Exams Past LTAM Exams from October 2018 through 2022 and past ALTAM Exams from Spring 2023 through present are available on the SOA website.
>
> Updates Candidates should be sure to check the Updates page on the exam home page periodically for additional corrections or notices.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Survival Models for Contingent Cash Flows {10–20%}
> The Candidate will understand key concepts concerning [[Multiple State Model|multiple state mortality/morbidity]] and [[Joint Life|individual/joint life mortality]] models for insurance and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Understand and explain features of typical [[Mortality|mortality curves]] and [[Mortality|heterogeneities]] in [[Mortality|mortality]].
> 2. Apply [[Multiple State Model|Markov multiple state models]] to [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-contingent life]] and [[Health Insurance|long-term health insurance benefits]], and [[Continuing Care Retirement Community|Continuing Care Retirement Communities]] (CCRCs).
> 3. Understand and critique the assumptions underlying [[Multiple State Model|Markov multiple state models]] for [[Long-Term Insurance Coverage|long-term insurance benefits]].
> 4. Derive and apply [[Kolmogorov's Forward Equation|Kolmogorov’s forward equations]] for [[Multiple State Model|continuous time Markov multiple state models]].
> 5. Calculate [[Multiple State Model|state-dependent probabilities]] for [[Multiple State Model|continuous time Markov]] models.
> 6. Apply the [[Chapman-Kolmogorov Equations|Chapman-Kolmogorov equations]] to calculate [[Transition Probability Matrix|discrete time transition probabilities]] in the Markov model.
> 7. Construct and [[Multiple Decrement Model|deconstruct multiple decrement tables]] using the associated [[Multiple Decrement Model|single decrement]] models and appropriate [[Fractional Age Assumption|fractional age assumptions]].
> 8. Calculate [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation|maximum likelihood estimates]] of [[Transition Intensity|transition intensities]] and [[Probability|probabilities]] for [[Multiple State Model|multiple state]] and [[Multiple Decrement Model|multiple decrement models]], assuming [[Transition Intensity|piecewise constant transition intensities]].
> 9. Calculate [[Approximate Confidence Interval|approximate confidence intervals]] for the [[Estimator|estimators]] in Topic 1(h), using [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation|asymptotic properties]] of the [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation|maximum likelihood estimators]].

> [!example]- Premium and Policy Valuation for Long-Term State-Dependent Coverages {12–20%}
> The Candidate will be able to perform calculations on the present value [[Random Variable|random variables]] associated with benefits and [[Expenses|expenses]] for [[Future Lifetime Random Variable|single life]], [[Joint Life|joint life]], CCRCs, or other [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Define and interpret [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent insurance]] and annuity present value [[Random Variable|random variables]] and identify and calculate their [[Expected Value|expected values]].
> 2. Derive and apply two-term and [[Woolhouse's Formula|three-term Woolhouse approximations]] for calculating expected [[Present Value|present values]] of [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent cash flows]].
> 3. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent life insurance]], [[Health Insurance|long-term health insurance]], and CCRCs using the [[Equivalence Principle|equivalence principle]].
> 4. Calculate [[Policy Value|policy values]] for [[State-Dependent Insurance|state-dependent life insurance]], [[Health Insurance|long-term health insurance]], and CCRCs.
> 5. Identify and apply [[Thiele's Differential Equation|Thiele’s differential equation]] in a [[Future Lifetime Random Variable|single life]] or [[Multiple State Model|multiple state]] setting.

> [!example]- Joint Life Insurance and Annuities {8–16%}
> The Candidate will understand key concepts concerning estimation and construction of [[Multiple State Model|multiple state]] and [[Joint Life|joint life]] models for insurance and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
>
> 1. Understand how [[Joint Life|joint-life mortality]] can be modelled using (i) a [[Joint Life|time-to-status-failure random variable]], and (ii) a [[Multiple State Model|multiple state model]].
> 2. Understand the implications of independence or dependence of [[Future Lifetime Random Variable|future lifetimes]] in both versions of the [[Joint Life|joint life]] model from 3(a). Identify sources of dependence and understand how they are accommodated in the models.
> 3. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for insurance and [[Annuities|annuities]] on [[Joint Life|joint lives]] using the [[Equivalence Principle|equivalence principle]].
> 4. Calculate [[Policy Value|policy values]] for insurance and [[Annuities|annuities]] on [[Joint Life|joint lives]].

> [!example]- Profit Analysis {10–20%}
> The Candidate will be able to analyze [[Profit Vector|emerging surplus]], and to set [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] and [[Reserve|reserves]], using [[Profit Testing|profit testing]].
>
> 1. Calculate and interpret expected profit and [[Analysis of Surplus|actual profit]],
> 2. Analyze and interpret gains by source.
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Analysis of Surplus]]
> 3. Calculate and interpret [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], net present value, [[Internal Rate of Return|internal rate of return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback period]] for [[Life Insurance|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]], and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]].
> 4. Calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] for [[Life Insurance|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] based on a specified profit objective.
> 5. Calculate [[Reserve|reserves]] for [[Life Insurance|long-term life]] and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] using [[Profit Testing|profit testing]].

> [!example]- Pension Plans and Retirement Benefits {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand how [[Pension Plan|retirement benefits]] are accrued, valued, and funded.
>
> 1. Calculate [[Replacement Ratio|replacement ratios]] for [[Defined Contribution Plan|Defined Contribution]] ([[Defined Contribution Plan|DC]]), and [[Defined Benefit Plan|Defined Benefit]] ([[Defined Benefit Plan|DB]]) plans, including [[Defined Benefit Plan|final average salary]] (FAS), [[Defined Benefit Plan|career average earnings]] (CAE), and [[Defined Benefit Plan|career average revalued earnings]] (CARE) plans.
> 2. Calculate the [[Defined Contribution Plan|required contribution rate]] to meet a [[Replacement Ratio|target replacement]] ratio for a [[Defined Contribution Plan|DC plan participant]], using a deterministic approach.
> 3. Identify, interpret, and apply [[Service Table|service table]] and [[Salary Scale|salary scale]] functions for [[Pension Plan|pension plan valuation]].
> 4. For a [[Defined Benefit Plan|DB plan]], calculate and interpret [[Accrued Benefit|accrued benefits]], including benefits on [[Accrued Benefit|early exit]] from the plan.
> 5. For a [[Defined Benefit Plan|DB plan]], calculate and interpret the [[Actuarial Accrued Liability|actuarial accrued liability]] and the [[Normal Cost|normal cost]] for benefits payable on [[Service Table|age retirement]] or [[Accrued Benefit|early exit]] using the [[Projected Unit Credit Method|projected unit credit]] (PUC) and [[Traditional Unit Credit Method|traditional unit credit]] (TUC) [[Valuation|valuation]] methods.

> [!example]- Universal Life Insurance {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand the policy design and benefits payable under Type A and Type B [[Universal Life Insurance|Universal Life contracts]] and be able to assess and quantify [[Universal Life Insurance|account values]], [[Insurance Premium|premiums]], and [[Reserve|reserves]].
>
> 1. Understand the [[Cash Flow|cashflows]] and calculate [[Universal Life Insurance|account values]] and benefits under Type A and Type B [[Universal Life Insurance|Universal Life policies]].
> 2. Calculate [[Reserve|reserves]] for [[No-Lapse Guarantee|no-lapse guarantees]].
> 3. Use [[Profit Testing|deterministic profit testing]] to calculate [[Insurance Premium|premiums]] or [[Reserve|reserves]], and assess [[Profit Vector|emerging surplus]] for [[Universal Life Insurance|Universal Life insurance]], including [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], net present value, [[Internal Rate of Return|internal rate of return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback periods]], under best estimate or stress test assumptions.

> [!example]- Embedded Options in Life Insurance and Annuity Products {10–18%}
> The Candidate will understand various types of [[Equity-Linked Insurance|equity-linked life insurance guarantees]], options that are embedded in [[Life Insurance|life insurance]] and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] and be able to price, [[Reserve|reserve]], and [[Hedging|hedge]] the [[Risk|risk]] inherent in these options.
>
> 1. Define and calculate [[Payoff|payoffs]] under each of the following [[Equity-Linked Insurance|options embedded]] in insurance and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]]:
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit|Guaranteed minimum death benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Maturity Benefit|Guaranteed minimum maturity benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Income Benefit|Guaranteed minimum income benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Withdrawal Benefit|Guaranteed minimum withdrawal benefit]]
> 2. Value the following [[Equity-Linked Insurance|options embedded]] in insurance and [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]], using the [[Black-Scholes Model|Black-Scholes model]]:
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit|Guaranteed minimum death benefit]]
>    - [[Guaranteed Minimum Maturity Benefit|Guaranteed minimum accumulation/maturity benefit]]
> 3. Construct a [[Replicating Portfolio|replicating portfolio]] for the options in 7(b) using [[Delta Hedging|delta-hedging]]
> 4. Understand and evaluate the costs associated with [[Delta Hedging|discrete-time rebalancing]].
> 5. Use [[Profit Testing|deterministic profit testing]] to assess [[Profit Vector|emerging surplus]] for [[Equity-Linked Insurance|equity-linked life insurance cash flows]], including [[Profit Signature|profit signature]], [[Profit Vector|profit vector]], net present value, [[Internal Rate of Return|internal rate of return]], [[Profit Margin|profit margin]], and [[Discounted Payback Period|discounted payback periods]], under best estimate or stress test assumptions.
