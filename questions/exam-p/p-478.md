A company provides health insurance to employees located at four different plants. Health insurance costs at each plant are independent of those costs at any other plant. Plant managers have calculated the following statistics:

| Plant | Average Cost | Standard Deviation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 5 | 3 |
| 4 | 7 | 4 |

Calculate the standard deviation of the total company health insurance costs.

- A) $2.5$
- B) $5.5$
- C) $7.5$
- D) $10.0$
- E) $12.5$

## Explanation

Standard deviations do not add. **Variances** do, when the components are independent, so square each standard deviation first:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var(total)} &= 1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2 + 4^2 \\
                  &= 1 + 4 + 9 + 16 \\
                  &= 30
\end{align*}
$$

Then take the square root:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{SD(total)} &= \sqrt{30} \\
                 &= \mathbf{5.4772}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 10.0, is $1 + 2 + 3 + 4$ — adding the standard deviations directly, which would be correct only if the four plants' costs were perfectly correlated. Independence makes the total's volatility substantially smaller than that sum, which is the diversification effect. The average costs are not needed at all.
