In a given year, a manufacturer's profit from selling warranties is normally distributed with mean 20 and variance 16.

Calculate the interval, centered at the mean, containing 25% of the probability associated with a single yearly profit.

- A) $(12.2, 27.8)$
- B) $(14.9, 25.1)$
- C) $(16.0, 24.0)$
- D) $(17.3, 22.7)$
- E) $(18.7, 21.3)$

## Explanation

The standard deviation is $\sqrt{16} = 4$.

An interval centered at the mean holding 25% of the probability leaves 75% outside, split evenly into two tails of 37.5% each. So the upper endpoint is the 87.5th percentile — not the 75th:

$$
0.25 + 0.375 = 0.625 \quad\text{below the lower endpoint's complement}
$$

More directly, the interval is $\mu \pm z\sigma$ where $\Phi(z) - \Phi(-z) = 0.25$, giving $\Phi(z) = 0.625$ and

$$
z = 0.3186
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{interval} &= 20 \pm 0.3186(4) \\
                &= 20 \pm 1.27 \\
                &= \mathbf{(18.7,\ 21.3)}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), $(12.2, 27.8)$, uses $z = 1.96$ — the value for a *95%* interval. The intuition to hold onto is that capturing only a quarter of the probability requires a very narrow band: this one spans less than two-thirds of a standard deviation in total.
