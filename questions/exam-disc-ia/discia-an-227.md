A neighbor takes a homeowner's riding mower from her garage without permission and sells it at a flea market. Which tort has the neighbor committed, and what is the usual measure of recovery?

- A) Trespass to land, with nominal damages because the garage was not harmed
- B) Trespass to chattels, with damages for the homeowner's loss of use of the mower
- C) Slander, with general damages for harm to the homeowner's reputation
- D) Conversion, with damages equal to the mower's full value

## Explanation

**Conversion** is an interference with personal property so serious, such as destroying or selling it, that the defendant must pay its full value. Selling the mower is the classic example, so the homeowner recovers the mower's value.

- **A** fits the entry into the garage, but it misses the tort against the mower and its measure.
- **B** describes a lesser interference, such as borrowing the mower for an afternoon and returning it. Selling it goes well beyond that.
- **C** requires a false spoken statement harming her reputation, which there was not.
