An asset manager wants to construct a Collateralized Debt Obligation Squared (CDO²) composed of the junior tranches of two Collateralized Debt Obligations (CDOs).

Given the following:

- The CDOs are composed of two 2-year securities, which pay \$500 at maturity and \$0 at default
- One CDO is composed of securities with 10% default probability and the other CDO is composed of securities with 30% default probability
- All securities are uncorrelated
- Each CDO and CDO² has a junior tranche with attachment points 0% - 50% of the notional principal and a senior tranche with attachment points 50% - 100% of the notional principal
- The discount rate and risk-free rate are both 5%

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the price of the senior tranche of the CDO².

### Explanation
A CDO's junior tranche (0%–50%) defaults if either of its two securities defaults:

$$\Pr(\text{Default } \text{CDO}_{\text{Junior}1}) = 1 - 0.9^2 = 0.19$$

$$\Pr(\text{Default } \text{CDO}_{\text{Junior}2}) = 1 - 0.7^2 = 0.51$$

The CDO² is built from those two junior tranches, and its senior tranche (50%–100%) defaults only if both of them default:

$$\Pr(\text{Default } \text{CDO}^2_{\text{Senior}}) = 0.19 \times 0.51 = 0.0969$$

Its expected payment of 500 at maturity is discounted for the two years at 5%:

$$\text{Price } \text{CDO}^2_{\text{Senior}} = \frac{500 \times (1 - 0.0969)}{1.05^2} = 409.57$$

The senior tranche of the CDO² is priced at \$409.57.

Alternatively:

The junior tranches' default probabilities can be built up from the default outcomes, $2 \times 0.1 \times 0.9 + 0.1^2 = 0.19$ and $2 \times 0.3 \times 0.7 + 0.3^2 = 0.51$, with the rest unchanged. A third sample answer took the senior tranche's payment at maturity as 1,000: $\dfrac{1000 \times (1 - 0.0969)}{1.05^2} = 819.14$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to compute the probability of default for various tranches of CDO and CDO², and to price the CDO².

Common mistakes included:

- Not understanding that each CDO is composed of the same 2 underlying securities (CDO₁ is composed of two 10% default securities and CDO₂ is composed of two 30% default securities)
- Failure to discount the price of the senior tranche of the CDO² to t = 0
- Discounting only one year instead of two as stated in question
- Calculating incorrect probabilities for the underlying CDOs

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the default premium of the senior tranche of the CDO².

### Explanation
The CDO² senior tranche's yield over the two years:

$$\text{CDO}^2 \text{ yield} = \sqrt{\frac{500}{409.57}} - 1 = 10.49\%$$

With the risk-free rate at 5%:

$$\text{Default premium} = 10.49\% - 5\% = 5.49\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know that default premium is the difference between the CDO² return and the risk-free rate.

Partial credit was awarded if the default premium was computed as the difference between \$500 at time 0 (using risk-free rate) and the price of the CDO²

Common mistakes included:

- Not considering the risk-free rate
- Errors in computing the CDO² yield to maturity
- Not discounting to t=0 (2 years)
- Assuming the price computed in part a was the answer for part b.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Upon further review, the asset manager determines that the correct default probabilities should be 10 percentage points higher for each security. Discuss whether the CDO junior tranche or CDO² senior tranche is more sensitive to this change.

### Explanation
Compute the new probabilities of default with the updated default probabilities:

$$\Pr(\text{Default } \text{CDO}_{\text{Junior}1}) = 1 - 0.8^2 = 0.36$$

$$\Pr(\text{Default } \text{CDO}_{\text{Junior}2}) = 1 - 0.6^2 = 0.64$$

$$\Pr(\text{Default } \text{CDO}^2_{\text{Senior}}) = 0.36 \times 0.64 = 0.2304$$

Then the change from part a:

$$\Delta(\text{Default } \text{CDO}_{\text{Junior}1}) = \frac{0.36}{0.19} - 1 = 89\%$$

$$\Delta(\text{Default } \text{CDO}_{\text{Junior}2}) = \frac{0.64}{0.51} - 1 = 25\%$$

$$\Delta(\text{Default } \text{CDO}^2_{\text{Senior}}) = \frac{0.2304}{0.0969} - 1 = 138\%$$

The CDO² senior tranche's probability of default has increased the most, so it is more sensitive to a change in the default probabilities of the underlying securities.

Alternatively:

The CDO² senior tranche is more sensitive to variation in default probabilities of the underlying securities since it compounds the effect from the junior tranches of the CDOs from which it is built.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the relative sensitivity of both CDO and CDO² to variation in default probabilities of underlying securities

Common mistakes included:

- Not computing the sensitivity relative to the current probabilities of default of the tranches
- Stating that since junior tranches absorb losses first, that they were more sensitive
