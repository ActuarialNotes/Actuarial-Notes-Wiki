A Benefits Canada feature of June 2021 by Kelsey Rolfe on what employees working from anywhere, within Canada or abroad, mean for plan sponsors’ benefits and total rewards offerings. It draws on consultants and employers, and on a December 2020 Aon survey of nearly 1,000 North American employers about relocation policies.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam GH-201C (SOA)|Exam GH-201C]] — GH201-713-25: How Will the Potential Work-from-Anywhere Boom Post-Pandemic Impact Benefit Plans?; outcomes 3a.

## Plan proactively
- Employers should determine their remote-working policies, including for employees working in another province or country, and how they will address benefits coverage and duty of care and communicate the policies clearly
- The article reports an uptick in employers seeking benefits solutions for workers in other countries, often reacting to an employee who has decided to move

## By the numbers
- December 2020 Aon survey of nearly 1,000 North American employers on global relocation policies: 66% would not allow remote employees to choose a work location outside their current country, 8% would allow it on a temporary basis, 2% on a permanent basis, 11% were unsure and 9% were actively considering it

## Inter-provincial mobility
- Out-of-province staff raise payroll and taxation questions, including whether the province of employment or of residence governs and which payroll deductions apply
- Some benefits plans require employees to be part of their province’s health-care plan, and there is a three-month window after a move before the new province’s system can be accessed
- Employers with staff moving into or out of Quebec face additional complications because the province requires employers’ prescription [[Pharmacy Benefit|drug plans]] to at least match the Régie de l’assurance maladie du Québec plan

## The benefits of the global talent pool
- Companies with permanent work-from-anywhere programs say it lets them find the best person for the job rather than the best person in their area

## Fleeing the coop
- Employees who leave the country can lose provincial health-care coverage and, with it, parts of their benefits coverage; most out-of-country policies have a duration of, for example, 30, 60 or 90 days
- Long- and short-term [[Disability Income Insurance|disability insurance]] also have residency requirements, so employees must return to Canada if they become disabled
- Who is responsible for coverage when an employee moves abroad is not settled, and the cost of covering an employee outside the country can be a major issue for employers

## Going abroad
- Benefits plans for global employees must meet the host country’s employment laws and tax rules and may require establishing a local corporation
- Benefits that are competitive in Canada may look different in another country, where, for example, health and dental are covered by a national health service
- Employers need to understand the networks their benefits provider has in the countries where employees are based

## Taking a gap year?
- Experts are divided as to whether working from anywhere is here to stay on a large scale
