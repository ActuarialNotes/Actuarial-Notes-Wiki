The trace plot for a Bayesian MCMC chain is provided below.

![Trace plot of 1,000 MCMC iterations, with sampled values on a vertical axis from about 0.65 to 1.12. The chain does not fluctuate around a stable level: it drifts slowly, starting near 0.93, rising to a spike of about 1.12 near iteration 190, falling to around 0.7 between iterations 330 and 460, wandering between about 0.7 and 0.94 through iteration 800 (low of about 0.65 near 790), then climbing steadily to about 1.04 at iteration 1,000. Neighbouring draws sit close together, so the path moves in long, slow excursions rather than rapid zig-zags](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2018f-q20-trace-plot.png)

Determine which of the following best describes any problem with the chain and the action that should be taken.

- A) There is a low level of auto-correlation; the mean of the likelihood distribution should be changed.
- B) There is a low level of auto-correlation; many more iterations are needed to make inferences from the samples of the posterior.
- C) There is a high level of auto-correlation; the variance of the prior distribution should be reduced.
- D) There is a high level of auto-correlation; many more iterations are needed to make inferences from the samples of the posterior.
- E) There are no issues with this chain.

## Explanation

A healthy trace plot looks like a "fuzzy caterpillar": the chain zig-zags rapidly around a stable level, so any window of iterations looks like any other. This chain instead drifts in long, slow excursions — up to about 1.1 near iteration 200, down around 0.7 for more than a hundred iterations, then climbing steadily to over 1.0 at the end. Each draw sits close to the one before it, which is the signature of **high autocorrelation**. The 1,000 draws carry far less information than 1,000 independent samples (a small effective sample size), and the chain has not yet explored the posterior thoroughly enough to be trusted.

- **(A) and (B)** misread the plot: slow wandering means high, not low, autocorrelation.
- **(C)** changes the model rather than fixing the sampler. The problem is how the chain moves, not the prior being targeted.
- **(E)** ignores the obvious drift.

The remedy offered is to run many more iterations, so that enough effectively independent draws accumulate to make inferences from. The answer is **D**.
