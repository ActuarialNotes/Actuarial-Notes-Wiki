A claimant injured by the insured has \$40,000 of medical bills, all paid by her own health insurer, and \$15,000 of lost wages; a jury values her pain and suffering at \$60,000. The state follows the traditional collateral source rule, and no exception applies. What are her special damages, and are they reduced for the health insurer's payments?

- A) \$55,000, reduced to \$15,000 for the health insurance
- B) \$55,000, not reduced for the health insurance
- C) \$115,000, not reduced for the health insurance
- D) \$60,000, reduced to \$20,000 for the health insurance

## Explanation

Special damages are those that can actually be ascertained, here the \$40,000 of medical bills and the \$15,000 of lost wages, a total of \$55,000. Pain and suffering is a general, noneconomic damage. Under the collateral source rule, damages are not reduced by compensation the plaintiff received from other sources such as health insurance. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — deducts the health insurance payments, which the collateral source rule forbids where no exception applies.
- **C** — \$115,000 is total compensatory damages, adding the general damages for pain and suffering.
- **D** — treats pain and suffering as the special damages and also deducts the collateral source.
