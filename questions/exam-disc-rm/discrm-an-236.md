A retailer's general liability policy is rated at \$2.50 per \$1,000 of gross sales. The deposit premium was based on estimated sales of \$3,200,000, and the final audit finds actual sales of \$2,800,000. What is the result of the audit?

- A) An additional premium of \$1,000
- B) A return premium of \$1,000
- C) A return premium of \$7,000
- D) No change, because the deposit premium is final

## Explanation

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Deposit} &= 2.50 \times \frac{3{,}200{,}000}{1{,}000} \\
&= 8{,}000 \\
\text{Audited} &= 2.50 \times \frac{2{,}800{,}000}{1{,}000} \\
&= 7{,}000 \\
\text{Adjustment} &= 7{,}000 - 8{,}000 \\
&= -1{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

Sales were lower than estimated, so the insurer returns \$1,000. The answer is **B**. An audit fixes the premium to the actual exposure in either direction.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Gets the direction wrong; lower sales mean less premium.
- **C** — Is the audited premium, not the adjustment.
- **D** — The deposit is an estimate, and the audit determines the final premium.
