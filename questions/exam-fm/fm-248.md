Kate buys a five-year 1000 face amount bond today with a 100 discount. The annual nominal coupon rate is 5% convertible semiannually.

One year later, Wallace buys a four-year bond. It has the same face amount and coupon values as Kate's and is priced to yield an annual nominal interest rate of 10% convertible semiannually. The discount on Wallace's bond is $D$.

The book value of Kate's bond at the time Wallace buys his bond is $B$.

Calculate $B - D$.

- A) 724
- B) 738
- C) 756
- D) 838
- E) 917

## Explanation

Kate's bond pays coupons of $1000(0.05/2) = 25$ every half-year for 10 half-years and costs $1000 - 100 = 900$. Its yield rate per half-year, $j = i^{(2)}/2$, solves

$$
900 = 25a_{\overline{10}|j} + 1000v_j^{10} \quad\Rightarrow\quad j = 0.0371551
$$

Wallace's bond has the same coupons of 25, 8 half-years to run, and is priced at 5% per half-year:

$$
\begin{align*}
1000 - D &= 25a_{\overline{8}|0.05} + 1000(1.05)^{-8} \\
  &= 25(6.46321) + 676.84 \\
  &= 838.42 \\
D &= 161.58
\end{align*}
$$

The book value of Kate's bond at time 1 (after 2 of its 10 coupons) is the price of the remaining 8 coupons and the redemption at her yield rate:

$$
\begin{align*}
B &= 25a_{\overline{8}|0.0371551} + 1000(1.0371551)^{-8} \\
  &= 25(6.81255) + 1000(0.746879) \\
  &= 917.19
\end{align*}
$$

The difference is $B - D = 917.19 - 161.58 = 755.61 \approx 756$, answer C.
