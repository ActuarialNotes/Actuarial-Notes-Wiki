A property insurer writes two accounts with the following modeled events:

| Event | Probability | Loss for Account X | Loss for Account Y |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5% | 75,000 | 35,000 |
| 2 | 1.0% | 50,000 | 25,000 |
| 3 | 2.0% | 40,000 | 10,000 |
| 4 | 5.0% | 25,000 | 5,000 |

- Target return on equity = 15%.
- Probability of ruin = 2.5%.
- Account X is written first.

## Part a (2 points)

Under the build up scenario, use the marginal surplus method to calculate the risk load for account X and account Y.

### Explanation
A 2.5% probability of ruin means $N(-z) = 0.025$ (printed "Prob(rain) = O.25"), so $z = 1.96$.

The standard deviations, treating each modeled event as occurring or not:

$$\sigma_x^2 = (0.005)(0.995)(75{,}000)^2 + (0.01)(0.99)(50{,}000)^2 + (0.02)(0.98)(40{,}000)^2 + (0.5)(0.95)(25{,}000)^2 \text{ (as printed)}$$

$$\sigma_x = 10{,}666.86$$

$$\sigma_y^2 = (0.005)(0.995)(35{,}000)^2 + (0.01)(0.99)(25{,}000)^2 + (0.02)(0.98)(10{,}000)^2 + (0.5)(0.95)(50{,}000)^2 \text{ (as printed)}$$

$$\sigma_y = 3{,}928.02$$

$$\sigma_{x+y}^2 = (0.005)(0.995)(11{,}000)^2 \text{ (as printed)} + (0.01)(0.99)(75{,}000)^2 + (0.02)(0.98)(50{,}000)^2 + (0.5)(0.95)(30{,}000)^2 \text{ (as printed)}$$

$$\sigma_{x+y} = 14{,}409.55$$

The risk load multiplier, with the 15% target return $y$:

$$\lambda = \frac{yz}{1 + y} = \frac{(0.15)(1.96)}{1.15} = 0.25565$$

Build-up scenario — Account X is written first, so it is charged for its own standard deviation and Account Y for the increase it causes:

$$r_x = \lambda\sigma_x = (0.25565)(10{,}666.86) = 2{,}726.98$$

$$r_y = \lambda(\sigma_{x+y} - \sigma_x) = (0.25565)(14{,}409.55 - 10{,}666.86) = 956.82$$

### Examiner Report
Most people responded correctly. Common mistakes included:

- Calculating SD as SQRT ( E[X^2] – E[X]^2)
- Not translating p-ruin=2.5% to z=1.96, and calculating risk load Y on a stand-alone basis, or not at all (sometimes included as answer to part b).

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the marginal surplus required to support Account Y.

### Explanation
The surplus is $V = zS - R$. For Account X alone:

$$V = 1.96 \times 10{,}667 - 2{,}727 = 18{,}180$$

With Account Y added:

$$V' = zS' - R' = 1.96 \times 14{,}410 - (2{,}727 + 957) = 24{,}560$$

The marginal surplus for Y is $V' - V = 24{,}560 - 18{,}180 = 6{,}380$.

Alternatively, any of the following (the report accepted four methods):

- Directly: marginal surplus $= z(S' - S) - R = 1.96(14{,}410 - 10{,}667) - 957 = 6{,}379.28$.
- Solving with the risk load as the return on the marginal surplus: $(V' - V) = z(S' - S) - 0.15(V' - V) = 1.96(14{,}409.55 - 10{,}666.86) - 0.15(V' - V)$, so $V' - V = 6{,}378.85$.
- From the risk load: $V_y = \dfrac{956.91}{0.15} = 6{,}379.32$.

### Examiner Report
Most people did not respond correctly. Common mistakes included answering with marginal *risk load* of Y, rather than the *marginal surplus* requirement for Y. Four methods accepted for calculation of surplus, see model responses.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe why the marginal surplus method fails to be renewal additive.

### Explanation
The marginal surplus method uses the change in standard deviation, which is the square root of variance. The square root is sub-additive: $\sqrt{x + y} < \sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}$. At renewal,

$$r_x = \lambda(\sigma_{x+y} - \sigma_y), \qquad r_y = \lambda(\sigma_{x+y} - \sigma_x)$$

and because of sub-additivity the renewal risk loads for the pieces of the portfolio add up to less than the risk load for the aggregate portfolio — all accounts are undercharged at renewal.

### Examiner Report
Most people responded correctly that the standard deviation or square root is sub-additive for partial credit. Most people did not provide sufficient explanation of what renewal additivity was. Common mistakes for the latter included the candidate not specifying the component risk loads (X and Y) were calculated under a renewal scenario when compared to total portfolio risk load (rather than build-up or stand-alone scenario).
