Determine which of the following statements about the property-casualty insurance marketplace are true.

- I. A property-casualty insurer must set its price before it knows what the losses on the policy will cost.
- II. Because most states regulate property-casualty rates, the underwriting cycle does not affect property-casualty prices.
- III. In a hard market, after losses exceed expectations, insurers' capacity falls and prices rise.

- A) I only
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) I, II and III

## Explanation

- **I. True:** Ratemaking is prospective, because the rate must be developed before the risk is transferred; the true cost emerges only as claims are reported and settled.
- **II. False:** Rate regulation does not stop the cycle. Property-casualty markets still swing between soft markets, with competitive prices and loose underwriting, and hard markets, with rising prices.
- **III. True:** Hard markets follow losses above expectations, when insurers draw on capital, capacity falls and prices rise.

The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Leaves out III, which describes a hard market correctly.
- **B** — Includes II, which is false.
- **D** — Includes II as well.
