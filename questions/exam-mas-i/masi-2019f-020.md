An insurance company has classified claims into 5 categories based on their severity.

The null hypothesis $H_0$ assumes the numbers of claims for Categories 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 appear in the following ratios:

$$
12 : 8 : 6 : 4 : 1
$$

In 2017, the insurance company recorded the numbers of claims as follows:

| Category | # of Claims |
|:--------:|:-----------:|
| 1 | 1172 |
| 2 | 829 |
| 3 | 605 |
| 4 | 347 |
| 5 | 102 |

Calculate the Chi-square goodness-of-fit statistic.

- A) Less than 5.5
- B) At least 5.5, but less than 6.5
- C) At least 6.5, but less than 7.5
- D) At least 7.5, but less than 8.5
- E) At least 8.5

## Explanation

Under $H_0$ each category's expected count is its share of the ratio, $12 + 8 + 6 + 4 + 1 = 31$ parts, applied to the total of $1172 + 829 + 605 + 347 + 102 = 3{,}055$ claims. The statistic is $\sum (O - E)^2 / E$:

| Category | Observed $O$ | Expected $E$ | $(O - E)^2/E$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 1172 | $3055 \cdot 12/31 = 1182.58$ | 0.0947 |
| 2 | 829 | $3055 \cdot 8/31 = 788.39$ | 2.0921 |
| 3 | 605 | $3055 \cdot 6/31 = 591.29$ | 0.3179 |
| 4 | 347 | $3055 \cdot 4/31 = 394.19$ | 5.6501 |
| 5 | 102 | $3055 \cdot 1/31 = 98.55$ | 0.1209 |

$$
\begin{align*}
Q &= 0.0947 + 2.0921 + 0.3179 + 5.6501 + 0.1209 \\
&= 8.276
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[7.5, 8.5)$, so the answer is **D**.
