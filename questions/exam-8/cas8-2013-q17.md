In "Workers Compensation Excess Ratios: An Alternative Method of Estimation," Mahler discusses a method of estimating excess ratios using empirical data up to a certain truncation point and a fitted curve to handle larger loss sizes.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Briefly discuss how to choose a truncation point for this method.

### Explanation
The actuary should maximize the reliance on the reported data while leaving enough data above the truncation point to permit reasonable curve fitting. It is also desirable that the truncation point be a nice round number prior to the data thinning out.

### Examiner Report
Mahler listed several components of an ideal truncation point, two of which were deemed more meaningful than the others (maximum reliance on reported; enough data above truncation point to allow for reasonable fit) with most candidates receiving full credit for a response that was nearly verbatim from the paper on these two points.

## Part b (1 point)

Part of Mahler's method to handle larger loss sizes involves fitting a mixed Exponential-Pareto curve for estimating excess ratios. Evaluate the following alternative distributions in estimating excess ratios:

- Pareto curve only
- Mixed exponential – lognormal

### Explanation
- **Pareto curve only:** a Pareto-only curve has a higher mean residual life than the mixed distribution — it has a heavier tail. This would result in higher excess ratios for limits above the truncation point.
- **Mixed exponential – lognormal:** the lognormal curve is less heavy-tailed than the Pareto (but heavier than the exponential). The excess ratios at very high limits would be modeled more on the lognormal curve, and since this distribution is less heavy-tailed than the Pareto, the excess ratios would be lower.

Alternatively: the smaller losses wouldn't fit well with the heavier-tailed Pareto, so the excess ratios for lower limits would likely be overstated; the lognormal doesn't have as heavy a tail as the Pareto, so the larger limits' excess ratios would likely be understated.

### Examiner Report
Full credit responses discussed both the tail of the distributions and the impact on the excess ratios. The Pareto curve only was more straightforward as both it and the Exponential were discussed in the paper, while the Lognormal was never mentioned in the paper. The majority of candidates commented on the tails of the distributions, but most did not tie that back to the excess ratios.
