Justify whether each of the following propositions is accurate.

## Part a (0.25 points)

MfADs should be the same for premium and claim liabilities.

### Explanation
Not necessarily. Risks inherent in premium and claim liabilities may be different, thus may be subject to different MfADs.

Alternatively:

No, since premium and claim liabilities have different payment patterns and duration.

Alternatively:

They can be different, if they expect changes to the future that do not affect the past, for example.

Alternatively:

No. Since premium liabilities have even more uncertainties than claim liabilities, MfADs should be higher for premium liabilities.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide justification on the accuracy of different statements related to MfADs, DPAE and the MCT calculation.

Candidates were expected to briefly explain whether MfADs should be the same for premium and claim liabilities. They were expected to demonstrate some understanding of the considerations involved in selecting the MfADs.
Common mistakes included:

- Answering without providing context.
- Mentioning the statement is false without any justification, for example, stating "MfADs should be different between premium and claim liabilities."

## Part b (0.25 points)

The MCT calculation considers diversification benefits between and within all risk components.

### Explanation
No. MCT diversification benefit considers correlation between asset risk and insurance risk, where asset risk = credit risk + market risk.

Alternatively:

No. Only between asset risk and insurance risk.

Alternatively:

No. Operational risk has not been considered in the diversification calculations.

Alternatively:

No. Diversification between market risk and credit risk is not considered.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate an understanding of the diversification considerations in the MCT calculation.
Common errors included:

- Answering that the statement provided in the question is true
- Answering the statement provided in the question is false without any justification
- Not providing a precise justification regarding the accuracy of the statement such as stating "No. Only between risks."

## Part c (0.25 points)

If there is a premium deficiency, the booked Deferred Policy Acquisition Expenses (DPAE) must be 0.

### Explanation
Yes. Premium deficiency occurs when equity in the net UPR is negative.

Alternatively:

Yes. If premium is not sufficient to meet future expected liabilities, then no DPAE can be deferred.

Alternatively:

True. Max DPAE = max(0, calculated DPAE)

Alternatively:

Yes.  DPAE is an asset that can only be booked when there is an expected future profit (i.e. no premium deficiency)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate understanding of the relationship between the booked DPAE and the premium deficiency.
Common errors included:

- Mentioning the statement is false
- Mentioning the statement is true without any justification
- Confusing maximum allowable DPAE and booked DPAE

## Part d (0.25 points)

Flood insurance is not taken into account in the MCT calculation.

### Explanation
Yes and no. Flood insurance would be included in the claim and premium liabilities, but there is not a consideration for flood risk the way there is for earthquake.

Alternatively:

True. Floods are not insured in Canada and therefore not incorporated into MCT as flood insurance.

Alternatively:

False. If flood insurance were sold it would be reflected in insurance risk.

Alternatively:

False. Flood premium increase overall premium, which increase margin for premium liabilities and operational risk.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate an understanding of the flood insurance business in Canada and know how flood risk is covered in the MCT calculation.
Common mistakes included:

- Mentioning the statement is true without any justification.
- Mentioning the statement is false without any justification.
- Saying the statement is accurate because MCT catastrophe risk only covers earthquake and nuclear risks. Candidates also needed to mention that while flood insurance is not taken into account directly in catastrophe risk, as long as it is covered by the insurer, it is included in the MCT within the claim and premium liability risks.
- Stating incorrectly that flood insurance risk is covered in the MCT catastrophe risk category.  Only earthquake and nuclear risks specifically trigger additional catastrophe capital in the MCT.
