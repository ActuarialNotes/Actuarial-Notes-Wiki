A named insured with Part C (ISO PP 00 01 09 18) swerves to avoid a car that crosses into her lane, hits a guardrail and is injured. The other car never touches her and speeds away, and its driver is never identified. She reports it to the police at once. Why does Part C not pay her bodily injury claim?

- A) Part C pays only after Part B medical payments are used up
- B) Uninsured motorists coverage excludes accidents in which the insured's car hits a fixed object
- C) The insured must first obtain a judgment against the unidentified driver
- D) An unidentified vehicle counts only if it hits the insured, her car or a vehicle she occupies

## Explanation

Under the ISO form, a hit-and-run vehicle is an "uninsured motor vehicle" only if its owner or operator can't be identified **and it hits** the insured or a family member, a vehicle they are occupying, or the covered auto. A "phantom" vehicle that causes the accident without contact does not qualify, so there is no uninsured motor vehicle. The answer is **D**. (Some state forms, such as Virginia's, do count a hit-and-run vehicle that causes an accident without hitting anything.)

**Why not the others**

- **A** — the parts are not stacked in sequence; the policy only bars duplicate payments for the same element of loss.
- **B** — there is no such exclusion; hitting the guardrail is a collision for Part D but irrelevant to Part C.
- **C** — no judgment is required; a judgment obtained without the insurer's consent is in fact not binding on it.
