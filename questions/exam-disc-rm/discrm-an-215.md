An underwriter evaluating a commercial property account reviews the provisions that affect the amount the insurer pays on a loss. Which of these is **not** one of those provisions?

- A) Whether the limit is specific to each building or blanket over several
- B) Whether property is valued at replacement cost or actual cash value
- C) The deductible and the coinsurance percentage
- D) The two-year limit on suits against the insurer

## Explanation

The provisions that set what a loss costs the insurer are the limits (specific or blanket), the valuation basis, the deductible and the coinsurance percentage with its options. The two-year limit on suits is a commercial property condition about when the insured may sue; it doesn't change the amount of a covered loss. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — A blanket limit can make more insurance available for one building's loss than a specific limit would.
- **B** — Replacement cost pays more than actual cash value on the same loss.
- **C** — The deductible and coinsurance directly reduce what is paid.
