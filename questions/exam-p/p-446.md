Once each morning and once each afternoon, the driver of a delivery truck is assigned to a route with a length that depends upon the items being delivered. The morning route is 5, 10, or 40 miles. The afternoon route is 0, 5, or 30 miles. The routes are assigned with the following probabilities:

| Morning route (miles) | Afternoon 0 | Afternoon 5 | Afternoon 30 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 0 | $2x$ | $3x$ |
| 10 | 0 | $2x$ | 0 |
| 40 | $y$ | 0 | 0 |

The expected length of the assigned afternoon route is 11 miles.

Calculate the variance of the length of the afternoon route.

- A) $159.0$
- B) $168.5$
- C) $181.5$
- D) $259.0$
- E) $269.0$

## Explanation

Sum each column to get the marginal distribution of the afternoon length $A$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[A = 0]  &= y \\
P[A = 5]  &= 2x + 2x = 4x \\
P[A = 30] &= 3x
\end{align*}
$$

The given expected value supplies one equation:

$$
\begin{align*}
11 &= 0(y) + 5(4x) + 30(3x) \\
   &= 110x \\
x  &= 0.10
\end{align*}
$$

The probabilities must total 1, which gives the other:

$$
\begin{align*}
y + 4(0.10) + 3(0.10) &= 1 \\
y &= 0.30
\end{align*}
$$

So $A$ takes the values 0, 5, 30 with probabilities 0.3, 0.4, 0.3. Computing the variance directly from the definition:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(A) &= (0-11)^2(0.3) + (5-11)^2(0.4) + (30-11)^2(0.3) \\
              &= 36.3 + 14.4 + 108.3 \\
              &= \mathbf{159.0}
\end{align*}
$$

The morning route is a distraction — only the column sums matter. The variance is dominated by the 30-mile route: although it carries just 30% of the probability, its squared deviation of 361 contributes over two-thirds of the total.
