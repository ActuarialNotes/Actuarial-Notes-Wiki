A February 2016 report, sponsored by the SOA's Pension Section, that documents the stochastic models used to investigate how different target benefit plan design features and funding strategies affect performance over the short and long term. In a Canadian context target benefit plans are collective pension schemes whose target benefit is defined in advance but not guaranteed. The report also gives background and context for the project's Issue Briefs.

## 7 Key Findings
- Choice of asset mix
    - The choice of investment mix has a greater impact on the size of adjustments than on their frequency.
- Choice of discount rate
    - Valuing all benefits at the risk-free rate (assumption set A) is compared with the best-estimate rate (set B), and with using one rate to value converted benefits at inception and the other for the affordability test (sets C and D).
- Triggers and actions
    - When the plan has only a single trigger for action, benefits are volatile, and a “no-action range” can reduce the frequency of benefit changes.
- Adjusting the target accrual rate
    - Designs 8 and 9 explore adjusting the target accrual rate at the same time as accrued benefits, and before them.
