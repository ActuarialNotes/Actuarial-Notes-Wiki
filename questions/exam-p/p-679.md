Claims paid against a particular insurance policy are equal to the amount of the loss and follow a normal distribution with mean 525 and standard deviation 100.

A deductible, $d$, is written into the policy and applies separately to each loss. As a result, only 14% of the losses result in a claim payment exceeding 500.

Calculate $d$.

- A) $36$
- B) $39$
- C) $61$
- D) $83$
- E) $133$

## Explanation

With a deductible the claim payment on a loss $X$ is $X - d$, so a payment exceeds 500 exactly when the loss exceeds $500 + d$:

$$
0.14 = P(X > 500 + d) = P\!\left(Z > \frac{500 + d - 525}{100}\right)
$$

A right-tail area of 0.14 corresponds to $z = 1.0803$, so

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{500 + d - 525}{100} &= 1.0803 \\
500 + d &= 525 + 108.03 = 633.03 \\
d &= \mathbf{133.03}
\end{align*}
$$

The step to watch is translating the payment threshold into a loss threshold. Without a deductible, $P(X > 500) = P(Z > -0.25) = 0.599$ — far more than 14% — so the deductible has to push the effective loss cutoff well past 500, and $d$ ends up larger than the gap between 500 and the mean rather than smaller.
