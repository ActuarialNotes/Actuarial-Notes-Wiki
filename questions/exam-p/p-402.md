The number of phone calls received per minute at a call center is modeled by a Poisson distribution. The second moment of the number of calls received per minute is 0.2756. The numbers of calls received during non-overlapping one-minute time intervals are mutually independent random variables.

Calculate the probability that more than two calls are received in a 15-minute interval.

- A) $0.655$
- B) $0.781$
- C) $0.805$
- D) $0.850$
- E) $0.918$

## Explanation

For a Poisson distribution the mean and variance are both $\lambda$, so the second moment is

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= \text{Var}(X) + \bigl(E[X]\bigr)^2 \\
       &= \lambda + \lambda^2
\end{align*}
$$

Setting that equal to 0.2756 gives a quadratic in $\lambda$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\lambda^2 + \lambda - 0.2756 &= 0 \\
\lambda &= \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4(0.2756)}}{2} \\
        &= 0.2250
\end{align*}
$$

(The negative root is discarded — a Poisson mean must be positive.)

Independent Poisson counts over non-overlapping intervals add, so the count $Y$ over 15 minutes is Poisson with

$$
15\lambda = 3.37474
$$

Take the complement of the three smallest counts:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[Y > 2] &= 1 - \bigl(P[0] + P[1] + P[2]\bigr) \\
         &= 1 - e^{-3.37474}\left[1 + 3.37474 + \frac{3.37474^2}{2}\right] \\
         &= \mathbf{0.65536}
\end{align*}
$$

The two traps are both in the setup: the given 0.2756 is the *second moment*, not the variance or the mean, and the rate has to be scaled to 15 minutes before any probability is computed. Treating 0.2756 as $\lambda$ directly and scaling gives $15\lambda = 4.134$ and a probability of 0.781 — exactly choice (B).
