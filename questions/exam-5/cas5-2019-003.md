## Part a (0.5 points)

Briefly describe one advantage and one disadvantage of calendar year data aggregation.

### Explanation
**Advantage:** Calendar year data is finalized as soon as the period ends — both the premium/exposure and the loss data require no further development — so it is the fastest and least costly basis to compile and is available immediately for analysis.

**Disadvantage:** Calendar year aggregation mixes premium/exposure and losses from multiple accident or policy periods within the same calendar period, so it provides a poor match between the losses analyzed and the premium/exposure that generated them. This makes calendar year data more susceptible to distortion from rate level changes, mix shifts, or changes in claims handling that occurred partway through the period.

Alternatively:

Advantages:

- Fixed at year end

- Easy to obtain

- Readily available at the end of the CY

- It's immediately available after the year is ended

Disadvantages:

- Poor matching of premium and losses

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the Calendar Year, Accident (Calendar/Accident) Year, Policy Year, and Report Year data aggregation methods.  They were expected to know the advantages and disadvantages of these methods, when it may be appropriate to use each method in ratemaking, and the mechanics/definitions of these methods.

Candidates were expected to know an advantage and a disadvantage of using the Calendar Year data aggregation method.

Common mistakes included:

- Only discussing the loss data or the premium/exposure data, not both.
- Citing no loss development as a disadvantage
- Stating that an advantage of calendar year aggregation is its suitability for financial reporting.  The advantage of using calendar year data is its availability.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Justify an appropriate data aggregation approach for a risk classification plan for a long-tailed line of business.

### Explanation
Accident year (or policy year) aggregation is appropriate. A risk classification analysis compares loss experience across classes, so it is important that the losses being compared are matched to the exposures/premium that produced them. Accident year data groups losses by the period in which they occurred and ties them to the exposures earned during that same period, giving a far better match between losses and premium than calendar year data — even though, for a long-tailed line, the accident year losses will need to be developed to an ultimate value. Policy year aggregation gives an even cleaner match (all losses tied to a single cohort of policies), at the cost of an even longer development tail.

Alternatively:

- Policy Year aggregation would match up the losses and premium perfectly

- Calendar/accident year aggregation would provide a better match between premium/exposure and losses

- Accident year data aggregation, it develops more quickly than policy year aggregation

- If the long-tailed line sells claims-made policies (as is common) then I'd recommend using Report Year data aggregation because it will provide the best match of premium to losses.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know that the Accident Year and/or Policy Year data aggregation methods are appropriate for a risk classification plan for a long-tailed line of business.

Candidates were also expected to know why their selected data aggregation method may be appropriate in this situation.

Common mistakes included:

- Not selecting an appropriate data aggregation method, such as Calendar Year or Report year without the assumption that coverage was provided through a claims-made policy
- When using losses as justification, failing to discuss the appropriateness of the match between losses and premium/exposure.  Examples of this include:
  - Stating that the selected method can be used to calculate IBNR
  - Stating that the selected method can be used to develop losses
  - Explaining the development patterns of a long-tailed line
- Stating that accident year provides the 'best' or is a 'perfect' match between premium and losses

## Part c (0.5 points)

Justify using report year data aggregation for a claims-made line of business.

### Explanation
Report year aggregation is appropriate for a claims-made line. Under claims-made coverage, the policy that responds to a claim is determined by the date the claim is *reported*, not the date it occurred — so grouping losses by report year matches them to the premium/exposure of the policy that actually provides coverage, giving the best available match between losses and premium. In addition, because claims-made policies have essentially no pure IBNR once a policy period ends (only IBNER on claims already reported), report year data develops quickly toward its ultimate value, making it a practical and reliable basis for this line.

Alternatively:

- At the end of a claims made policy there is no pure IBNR so at the end of the policy period almost all losses are known (except for IBNER). This aligns with report year data aggregation, so it is a good fit. This will also tie premium/exposures to their losses.

- There is no pure IBNR in either report year aggregation or a claims-made policy.

Reporting is also the trigger for a CM policy so grouping claims by RY makes sense.

- The coverage trigger in the case of a claims-made policy is the report of the claim. That makes the match between premium and losses to be ideal using a report year data aggregation.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand why Report Year data aggregation is appropriate for a claims-made line of business.

Common mistakes included:

- Providing no justification, only stating that Report Year is appropriate
- Providing a response that was too brief, such as:
  - Claims-made policies cover reported claims
  - There is no pure IBNR
  - Report year matches premium to losses
- Declaring claims-made policies and/or report year data aggregation do not have IBNR without clarifying that claims-made policies only have IBNER.
- Justifying the use of a claims made policy rather than discussing appropriateness of report year aggregation
