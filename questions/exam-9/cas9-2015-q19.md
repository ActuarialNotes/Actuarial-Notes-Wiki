The actuarial department of a property and casualty insurer is reviewing two potential growth opportunities, referred to as Option A and Option B. Given the following information about these options:

- Policies are in-force for one year.
- Premium is collected at the beginning of the policy period.
- Variable expenses equal to 25% of the premium are paid at policy inception.
- Both options are expected to achieve an 85% loss ratio.
- Equal loss amounts are expected to be paid out at the end of each year over a two-year period for both options.
- The company allocates capital in a way that results in a reserve-to-surplus ratio of 2:1 for Option A and 3:1 for Option B.
- Initial required surplus is set to the loss portion of the unearned premium reserve.
- Supporting capital can be invested at a 10% rate of return.
- The cost of capital is 8% per year.
- Investment income is earned at the end of a given policy year.
- There are no taxes.

Calculate the internal rate of return (IRR) and net present value (NPV) for each growth opportunity.

### Explanation
Five model solutions are provided as examples, but graders gave full credit to other solutions, depending on assumptions made by the candidate regarding balance sheet items and investment income.

**Sample 1.** Premium $P$ is collected at time 0 and expenses of $0.25P$ paid then; losses of $0.425P$ are paid at times 1 and 2. Required surplus is the reserve divided by 2 for Option A and by 3 for Option B.

| | t = 0 | t = 1 | t = 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium | P | 0 | 0 |
| Expense | .25P | 0 | 0 |
| Loss | 0 | .425P | .425P |
| Reserve (RSV) | .85P | .425P | 0 |
| A surplus (RSV / 2) | .425P | .2125P | 0 |
| B surplus (RSV / 3) | .283P | .14167P | 0 |

Option A (asset post = RSV + surplus):

| | t = 0 | t = 1 | t = 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset pre | 1.275P | 1.4025 (as printed) | .70125P |
| Contribution | .525P | – | – |
| Asset post | 1.275P | .6375P | 0 |
| Distribution | – | .34P | .27625P |

Option B:

| | t = 0 | t = 1 | t = 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset pre | 1.133P | 1.2463P | .623337 (as printed) |
| Contribution | .383P = 1.133P - (P - .25P) | – | – |
| Asset post | 1.133P | .56667P | 0 |
| Distribution | – | .25463P | .198337P = .62337P - 0 - .423P (as printed) |

IRR — divide by $P$ and it cancels:

$$A: \; -0.525P + \frac{0.34P}{1 + R_A} + \frac{0.27625P}{(1 + R_A)^2} = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad IRR_A = 11.819\%$$

$$B: \; -0.383P + \frac{0.25463P}{1 + R_B} + \frac{0.198337P}{(1 + R_B)^2} = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad IRR_B = 12.51\%$$

NPV at the 8% cost of capital:

$$NPV_A = -0.525P + \frac{0.34P}{1.08} + \frac{0.27625P}{1.08^2} = 0.0267P$$

$$NPV_B = -0.383P + \frac{0.25463P}{1.08} + \frac{0.198337P}{1.08^2} = 0.0228P$$

Alternatively:

The other four model solutions set up the balance sheet differently, and each reached its own figures:

- **Sample 2** — initial reserves include the full UEPR, not just the loss portion; investment income is earned on all assets, not just surplus; NPV at the 8% cost of capital. Required surplus is $0.85P$ at time 0 (required assets $1.85P$). Option A's equity flows are $-1.1P$, $+0.9725P$, $+0.27625P$, so $IRR = 11.03\%$ and $NPV = P(-1.1 + 0.9725/1.08 + 0.27625/1.08^2) = 0.0373P$. Option B's are $-1.1P$, $+1.043P$, $+0.1987P$, so $IRR = 11.08\%$ and $NPV = 0.0361P$.
- **Sample 3** — required surplus is the initial loss portion of the UEP reserve, $0.85P$, so investors contribute $(P - 0.25P - 0.85P) + 0.85P = 0.95P$; investment income is $0.17P$ and $0.06375P$. Option A distributes $0.8075P$ and $0.27625P$: $-0.95P + \frac{0.8075P}{1 + IRR} + \frac{0.27625P}{(1 + IRR)^2} = 0$ gives $IRR = 11.16\%$, and $NPV = 0.0345P$. For Option B only the required surplus at time 1 changes ($0.1417P = 0.425P/3$): $IRR = 11.23\%$ on flows of $-0.95P$, $0.8783P$, $0.19837P$, and $NPV_B = 0.0333P$.
- **Sample 4** — required equity at time 0 is $0.85P/2$ for A and $0.283P$ for B. Option A's equity flows are $-0.675P$, $+0.505P$, $+0.27625P$, giving $IRR_A = 11.5\%$ and $NPV_A = 0.0296P$; Option B's are $-0.533P$, $+0.4193P$, $+0.1987P$, giving $IRR_B = 12\%$ and $NPV_B = 0.0255P$.
- **Sample 5** — reserve and required surplus are each $P$ at time 0 and investment income at time 1 is $0.2P$. Option A's equity flows are $-1.25P$, $+1.1375P$, $+0.27625P$, giving $IRR = 10.924\%$ and $NPV = 0.04P$; Option B's are $-1.25P$, $+1.208P$, $+0.1987P$, giving $IRR = 10.965\%$ and $NPV = 0.0389P$.

### Examiner Report
In order to obtain full credit candidates were expected to calculate the correct expenses, reserves, loss payments, and investment income in order to come up with the correct equity flows, from which the candidate would then calculate the correct IRR and NPV. This had to be done for BOTH option A and option B for full credit.

A very large proportion of the candidates got full credit or close to full credit. Common mistakes included:

- calculating the NPV of the premium, expense and loss payment cash flows only, i.e.
NPV = P – 0.25P – 0.425P/1.08 – 0.425P/1.08^2 = -0.0079P.
- calculating the correct NPV, but inverting the sign, i.e. providing the correct answer but adding a negative sign in front when there should have been none.
- a few candidates set up the UPR at t = 0, but did NOT set up any subsequent loss reserve, hence giving a required surplus of 0 at t = 1.
