You are fitting a Linear Mixed Model on a clustered dataset and considering the following two models:

**Model I**

$$
Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_{ij}^{(1)} + \beta_2 X_{ij}^{(2)} + \beta_3 X_{ij}^{(3)} + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}
$$

$u_j$ follows a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance $\sigma_c^2$; $\varepsilon_{ij}$ follows a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$; all $\varepsilon_{ij}$ are independent.

**Model II**

$$
Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_{ij}^{(1)} + \beta_2 X_{ij}^{(2)} + \beta_3 X_{ij}^{(3)} + \varepsilon_{ij}
$$

$\varepsilon_{ij}$ follows a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$; all $\varepsilon_{ij}$ are independent.

The fit statistics are as follows:

| | Model I | Model II |
|---|---|---|
| -2 log-likelihood | 401.1 | 489.9 |

You are using the test statistic to choose between Model I and Model II. Determine which of the following statements are true:

- A) Based on referring the test statistic to a chi-squared distribution with 1 degree of freedom, we select Model I at a confidence level of 0.005
- B) Based on referring the test statistic to an average of chi-squared distributions with 0 and 1 degrees of freedom, we select Model I at a confidence level of 0.005
- C) Based on referring the test statistic to a chi-squared distribution with 1 degree of freedom, we select Model II at a confidence level of 0.005
- D) Based on referring the test statistic to an average of chi-squared distributions with 0 and 1 degrees of freedom, we select Model II at a confidence level of 0.005
- E) The correct answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

Model II is Model I with the random effect removed, so the null hypothesis is $H_0: \sigma_c^2 = 0$ against $H_1: \sigma_c^2 > 0$. The likelihood ratio statistic is the drop in $-2\log L$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{LRT} &= 489.9 - 401.1 \\
           &= 88.8
\end{align*}
$$

**The reference distribution.** A variance cannot be negative, so $\sigma_c^2 = 0$ sits on the boundary of the parameter space and the usual $\chi^2_1$ result does not apply. The correct null distribution is the 50:50 mixture

$$
0.5\,\chi^2_0 + 0.5\,\chi^2_1,
$$

which rules out options (A) and (C) regardless of the conclusion.

**The decision.** The mixture gives $p = 0.5\,P(\chi^2_1 > 88.8)$, which is far below 0.005, so $H_0$ is rejected: the cluster-level random effect is needed and Model I is selected.

Referring the statistic to the average of $\chi^2_0$ and $\chi^2_1$ and selecting Model I, the answer is **B**.
