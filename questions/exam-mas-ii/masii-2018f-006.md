You are given the following information:

- A block of insurance policies had 1,384 claims this period.
- The claims had a mean loss of 55 and variance of loss of 6,010.
- The mean frequency of these claims is 0.085 per policy.
- The block has 21,000 policies.
- Full credibility is based on a coverage probability of 98% for a range of within 5% deviation from the true mean.

You calculate the partial-credibility factor for severity, $Z_X$, and the partial-credibility factor for pure premium, $Z_P$, using the limited-fluctuation credibility method.

Calculate the absolute difference between $Z_X$ and $Z_P$.

- A) Less than 0.05
- B) At least 0.05, but less than 0.15
- C) At least 0.15, but less than 0.25
- D) At least 0.25, but less than 0.35
- E) At least 0.35

## Explanation

Both factors use the square-root rule $Z = \sqrt{n/n_F}$ against a full-credibility standard built from the same $(p, k) = (98\%, 5\%)$ pair. Frequency is taken as Poisson, since no frequency variance is given. The base standard is

$$
\begin{align*}
\lambda_0 &= \left(\frac{z_{0.99}}{0.05}\right)^2 \\
&= \left(\frac{2.326}{0.05}\right)^2 \\
&= 2{,}164.8
\end{align*}
$$

and the severity's squared coefficient of variation is

$$
\begin{align*}
CV_X^2 &= \frac{6{,}010}{55^2} \\
&= 1.9868
\end{align*}
$$

**Severity.** The standard is $\lambda_0 CV_X^2$ claims, measured against the 1,384 claims actually observed:

$$
\begin{align*}
n_X &= 2{,}164.8(1.9868) \\
&= 4{,}300.9 \\
Z_X &= \sqrt{\frac{1{,}384}{4{,}300.9}} \\
&= 0.5673
\end{align*}
$$

**Pure premium.** With Poisson frequency the standard is $\lambda_0(1 + CV_X^2)$ *expected* claims, and the block's expected claim count is its 21,000 policies at 0.085 claims each:

$$
\begin{align*}
n_P &= 2{,}164.8(2.9868) \\
&= 6{,}465.6 \\
E[N] &= 21{,}000(0.085) \\
&= 1{,}785 \\
Z_P &= \sqrt{\frac{1{,}785}{6{,}465.6}} \\
&= 0.5254
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
|Z_X - Z_P| &= 0.5673 - 0.5254 \\
&= 0.0418
\end{align*}
$$

This is less than 0.05, so the answer is **A**.
