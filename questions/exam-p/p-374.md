A random variable $X$ has density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{5}{72}\left[3(x-2)^2 - (x-2)^4 + 4\right], & \text{for } 0 < x < 3 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the mode of the distribution.

- A) $0.000$
- B) $0.775$
- C) $2.000$
- D) $3.000$
- E) The correct answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D).

## Explanation

The mode of a continuous distribution is the $x$ that maximizes the density, so differentiate and set to zero:

$$
\begin{align*}
f'(x) &= \frac{5}{72}\left[6(x-2) - 4(x-2)^3\right] \\
      &= 0
\end{align*}
$$

Factor out $(x-2)$:

$$
(x-2)\left[6 - 4(x-2)^2\right] = 0
$$

so either $x = 2$, or

$$
\begin{align*}
(x-2)^2 &= \frac{6}{4} \\
x       &= 2 \pm 1.2247
\end{align*}
$$

That gives $x = 0.7753$ and $x = 3.2247$; the latter is outside the domain $(0,3)$ and is discarded.

A stationary point is not automatically the maximum, so compare the density at every surviving critical point **and** at the endpoints of the domain:

$$
\begin{align*}
f(0)      &= 0 \\
f(0.7753) &= 0.4340 \\
f(2)      &= 0.2778 \\
f(3)      &= 0.4167
\end{align*}
$$

The largest value is at $x = \mathbf{0.775}$.

The endpoint check is what this question is really testing. $f(3) = 0.4167$ is close behind, and $x=2$ is a *local minimum* despite being a stationary point — choosing it gives choice (C). Since the domain is open at 3, the endpoint could not have been the mode anyway, but its value shows how near the contest was.
