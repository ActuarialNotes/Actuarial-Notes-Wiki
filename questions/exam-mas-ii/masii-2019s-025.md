You fit the following model:

$$
\begin{align*}
y_i &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu, 4) \\
\mu &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 100)
\end{align*}
$$

You propose new values for $\mu^{(k)}$ by generating a random value from the following proposal distribution:

$$
\mu^* \sim \text{Normal}\left(\mu^{(k-1)}, \sigma^2\right)
$$

where $\mu^{(j)}$ is the $j^{th}$ value of $\mu$ in your chain. The trace plot of $\mu^{(k)}$ from the above proposal distribution is labeled "First Pass" (after sufficient warm-up). You then re-run the sampling process after changing the proposal distribution to:

$$
\mu^{(k)} \sim \text{Normal}\left(\mu^{(k-1)}, 100\sigma^2\right)
$$

![Six MCMC trace plots of mu against iteration k from 0 to about 9000, all on the same 2.4 to 3.2 vertical scale. First Pass is a dense band of rapid noise centred near 2.95. Plot I is a smooth slowly-wandering line. Plot II is a dense band similar to First Pass. Plot III is a dense band centred slightly lower near 2.7. Plot IV is a flat step function that holds a value for long stretches before jumping. Plot V is a dense band with visibly larger vertical excursions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019s-q25-trace-plots.png)

Determine which of the plots above is most likely the resulting trace plot with the new proposal distribution.

- A) Plot I
- B) Plot II
- C) Plot III
- D) Plot IV
- E) Plot V

## Explanation

The proposal standard deviation is multiplied by 10 ($100\sigma^2$ is $(10\sigma)^2$), so the sampler now proposes moves roughly ten times further from the current value.

In a Metropolis-Hastings sampler, a proposal that overshoots the bulk of the posterior lands in the low-density tails most of the time, and those proposals are **rejected**. When a proposal is rejected the chain repeats its current value, so the trace flattens out into long horizontal runs punctuated by occasional accepted jumps — a step-function appearance with a low acceptance rate and high autocorrelation.

Reading the plots against that signature:

- **Plot I** wanders smoothly and slowly — the signature of a proposal that is far *too small*, so nearly every move is accepted but each move is tiny.
- **Plots II, III and V** are dense, well-mixed bands like the First Pass; none shows the stalling that a ten-fold larger step size produces.
- **Plot IV** is the step function: long flat stretches where proposals were rejected, with occasional jumps.

Plot IV is the trace from the wider proposal, so the answer is **D**.
