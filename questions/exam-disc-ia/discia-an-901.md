A Texas driver is convicted a second time of driving without liability insurance. The state now requires him to have an insurer file an SR-22 certificate and to keep the coverage for two years, and the insurer must tell the state if the coverage lapses. What kind of law imposes this requirement?

- A) A financial responsibility law, requiring proof of ability to pay damages after a triggering event
- B) A no-fault law, requiring personal injury protection from his own insurer
- C) An unsatisfied judgment fund law, requiring him to pay into a state fund
- D) A no pay, no play law, barring him from suing for noneconomic damages

## Explanation

A financial responsibility law requires vehicle owners to provide evidence of their ability to pay damages arising from the use of a vehicle. In Texas, a crash-related suspension, a second conviction for driving uninsured or a civil judgment from a crash triggers the SR-22 filing, which certifies that the driver carries the minimum liability coverage. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — No-fault laws govern who pays injury benefits and when a driver may sue; they don't require a certificate after a conviction.
- **C** — An unsatisfied judgment fund pays victims when an at-fault driver can't; it doesn't certify a driver's coverage.
- **D** — A no pay, no play law limits an uninsured driver's own lawsuits; it doesn't require proof of insurance.
