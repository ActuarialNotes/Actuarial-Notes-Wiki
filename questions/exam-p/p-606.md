Along a highway, automobiles are randomly selected for tire inspections. Let $X$ denote the number of bad front tires and $Y$ the number of bad rear tires on a randomly selected automobile. The joint distribution of $X$ and $Y$ is given by the probability function

$$
p(x, y) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{(6 - x)(3 - y)}{58\left(1 + |x - y|\right)}, & \text{for } x = 0, 1, 2 \text{ and } y = 0, 1, 2 \\[6pt]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the variance of the number of bad front tires among those automobiles with one bad rear tire.

- A) $7/16$
- B) $49/100$
- C) $2/3$
- D) $7/10$
- E) $1$

## Explanation

Tabulating $p(x, y)$ over the nine cells (all with denominator 116) gives

| | $y = 0$ | $y = 1$ | $y = 2$ | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $x = 0$ | 36/116 | 12/116 | 4/116 | 52/116 |
| $x = 1$ | 15/116 | 20/116 | 5/116 | 40/116 |
| $x = 2$ | 8/116 | 8/116 | 8/116 | 24/116 |
| total | 59/116 | 40/116 | 17/116 | 1 |

The column $y = 1$ has marginal $40/116$, so the conditional distribution is

$$
p(x \mid y = 1) = \begin{cases}
12/40, & x = 0 \\
20/40, & x = 1 \\
8/40, & x = 2
\end{cases}
$$

Then

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X \mid Y = 1] &= 0\left(\tfrac{12}{40}\right) + 1\left(\tfrac{20}{40}\right) + 2\left(\tfrac{8}{40}\right) = \frac{36}{40} \\
E[X^2 \mid Y = 1] &= 0\left(\tfrac{12}{40}\right) + 1\left(\tfrac{20}{40}\right) + 4\left(\tfrac{8}{40}\right) = \frac{52}{40}
\end{align*}
$$

and the variance is

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X \mid Y = 1) &= \frac{52}{40} - \left(\frac{36}{40}\right)^2 \\
                         &= 1.3 - 0.81 \\
                         &= \mathbf{49/100}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), $7/10$, is the conditional *standard deviation* — $\sqrt{49/100} = 0.7$. The pair 0.49 and 0.7 is a standard trap on any variance question; here the giveaway is that 49/100 is a perfect square over a perfect square.
