The random variable $X$ is uniformly distributed on an interval and has median 6 and 90th percentile 13.20.

Calculate the second moment of $X$.

- A) $6$
- B) $27$
- C) $33$
- D) $36$
- E) $63$

## Explanation

Let the interval be $[a, b]$. A uniform's median is its midpoint:

$$
6 = \frac{a+b}{2} \implies a + b = 12
$$

Percentiles of a uniform are linear interpolations between the endpoints, so the 90th percentile is $0.1a + 0.9b$:

$$
13.20 = 0.1a + 0.9b \implies a + 9b = 132
$$

Subtracting the first equation from the second gives $8b = 120$, so $b = 15$ and $a = -3$.

The second moment follows from the density $1/18$ on $[-3, 15]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E(X^2) &= \int_{-3}^{15} x^2\left(\frac{1}{18}\right) dx = \left.\frac{x^3}{54}\right|_{-3}^{15} \\
       &= \frac{3375 + 27}{54} = \mathbf{63}
\end{align*}
$$

Equivalently, $E(X^2) = \text{Var}(X) + \left[E(X)\right]^2 = \frac{18^2}{12} + 6^2 = 27 + 36 = 63$.

That decomposition names the two wrong answers: choice (D), 36, is $\left[E(X)\right]^2$ with the variance dropped, and choice (B), 27, is the variance with the squared mean dropped. Note also that the interval extends below zero — nothing in the problem forces $X$ to be positive.
