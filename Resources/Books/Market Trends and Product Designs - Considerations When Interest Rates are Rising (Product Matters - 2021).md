An article in the November 2021 issue of the SOA's Product Matters! newsletter on current market trends for long-duration products, including life insurance, annuities and long-term care (LTC) insurance, following an extended period of low interest rates. It also draws lessons carriers can learn from historical periods of rising interest rates. The authors state that it does not estimate the duration or magnitude of the current rise in rates.

## Introduction
- Interest rates have trended up after the November 2018 to August 2020 downturn; as of June 2021 the 10-year treasury rate was about 110 bps above August 2020 but still well below 2 percent.
- The focus is on current market trends for long-duration products and on lessons from historical periods of rising interest rates.

## Where Are Carriers Today with Historically Low Interest Rates?
- Profitability for most long-duration products depends at least in part on the investment income a company can earn to support its guarantees and pricing targets.
- Life Insurance
    - Premium rates have increased for most products, especially term insurance.
    - Product innovation includes indexed universal life (IUL), registered indexed universal life and the resurgence of variable universal life.
    - Companies are actively managing non-guaranteed elements, including raising cost of insurance charges.
- Annuities
    - New products focus less on long-tailed secondary guarantees and more on accumulation.
    - Registered index linked annuity (RILA) markets have expanded, and fixed and fixed indexed annuity writers have increased their use of reinsurance.
- Long-Term Care Insurance
    - Carriers continue to exit the standalone LTC market.
    - New standalone LTC products have simpler designs, less rich benefits or cost-sharing features, and carriers have expanded LTC combination products.

## What Can Carriers Learn from Prior Periods of Rising Interest Rates?
- The 10-year treasury rate rose in three periods over the past 20 years (2003 to 2006, 2012 to 2013 and 2016 to 2018); in each it increased only around 1 percent to 1.5 percent over about two years, and each period was followed by a sharp decrease.
- Lesson 1: Focus On Product Designs That Perform Well In Rising Interest Rate Environments
    - Table 1 shows annual sales growth rates for key life and annuity products from 2016 to 2019; whole life products were generally more stable and IUL sales showed a moderate increase, while more interest rate sensitive products such as ULSG tended to have lower sales.
    - Indexed products and variable annuities also performed well in past rising interest rate environments.
    - Table 2 lists flexible product designs used to promote sales and mitigate interest risk, with the pro to insurers and the pro to policyholders of each.
- Lesson 2: Be Mindful of New Money Rate Implications on Portfolio Returns
    - Even if interest rates keep rising slowly, insurers' portfolio rates will likely continue to decrease, stressing profit margins further.
    - Recommendations include being prudent in providing higher credited rates and longer guarantees, considering reducing non-guaranteed benefits and costs, and closely monitoring product features where profitability is interest sensitive, such as return of premium riders.
    - Asset earned rates typically lag interest rate fluctuations, depending on the duration of the assets.
- Lesson 3: Pay Close Attention to Long-Term Impact and Tail-Risk Scenarios
    - In the early 1980s, universal life writers shortened portfolio duration to take advantage of higher short-term rates; when short-term rates dropped, they could not support their crediting rates, and three large life insurers ultimately went insolvent with mass UL policy surrenders.

## Conclusion
- The current rising interest trend is unlikely to substantially increase insurers' portfolio rates because of its moderate magnitude.
- Focusing on innovative and flexible product features will continue to be essential, and insurers should consider profitability across a wide breadth of interest rate scenarios.
