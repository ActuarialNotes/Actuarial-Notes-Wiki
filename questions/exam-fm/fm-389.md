An asset provides equal annual payments, with the first payment occurring one year from today. Based on an annual effective interest rate of $j$, the present value of the payments provided by the asset is 10 and the modified duration is 4 years.

If all payments were deferred six years so that payments began at the end of the seventh year, the modified duration at interest rate $j$ would be 9 years.

If the original set of cash flows is altered by adding a single payment of 3 today, the Macaulay duration of the altered cash flows is $X$ years, at interest rate $j$.

Calculate $X$.

- A) 3.08
- B) 3.42
- C) 3.69
- D) 3.75
- E) 4.04

## Explanation

Since the Macaulay duration of a set of cash flows is a weighted average of the payment times, deferring every payment by six years must add exactly 6 to the Macaulay duration. The Macaulay duration is $(1+j)$ times the modified duration, so

$$
\begin{align*}
(1+j)(9-4) &= 6 \\
1+j &= 1.2 \\
j &= 0.2
\end{align*}
$$

Let $P(i)$ denote the present value of the original set of cash flows at rate $i$, so $P(j) = 10$. The modified duration of 4 gives $P'(j)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
4 &= \frac{-P'(j)}{P(j)} \\
  &= \frac{-P'(j)}{10} \\
P'(j) &= -40
\end{align*}
$$

Adding a payment of 3 today adds 3 to the present value but nothing to its derivative, because a payment at time 0 does not depend on the interest rate. The modified duration of the altered payments is

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{MOD} &= -\frac{0 + P'(j)}{3 + P(j)} \\
        &= \frac{40}{13} \\
        &= 3.077
\end{align*}
$$

The Macaulay duration is

$$
\begin{align*}
X &= (1+j)D_{MOD} \\
  &= 1.2(3.077) \\
  &= 3.69
\end{align*}
$$
