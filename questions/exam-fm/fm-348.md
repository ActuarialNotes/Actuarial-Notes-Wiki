You are given the following information regarding two annuities with annual payments:

  i) Annuity X is a 20-payment annuity-immediate which provides an initial payment of 2500 and each subsequent payment is 5% larger than the preceding payment.
  ii) Annuity Y is a 30-payment annuity-due which provides an initial payment of $k$ and each subsequent payment is 4% larger than the preceding payment.

Using an annual effective interest rate of 4%, Annuity X and Annuity Y have the same present value.

Calculate $k$.

- A) 1,758
- B) 1,828
- C) 1,901
- D) 2,078
- E) 2,262

## Explanation

**Annuity X.** The present value of an $n$-payment annuity-immediate whose first payment is $P$ and whose payments grow by a factor $1+g$ each year, at interest rate $i \ne g$, is

$$PV = P\cdot\frac{1-\left(\frac{1+g}{1+i}\right)^{n}}{i-g}$$

With $P = 2500$, $g = 0.05$, $i = 0.04$ and $n = 20$:

$$
\begin{align*}
PV_X &= 2500\cdot\frac{1-\left(\frac{1.05}{1.04}\right)^{20}}{0.04-0.05} \\
     &= 2500\cdot\frac{1-1.21093044}{-0.01} \\
     &= 2500(21.093044) \\
     &= 52{,}732.61
\end{align*}
$$

**Annuity Y.** The growth rate equals the interest rate. The payment at time $t$ ($t = 0, 1, \dots, 29$, since Y is an annuity-due) is $k(1.04)^t$, and its present value is $k(1.04)^t\,v^t = k$. So each of the 30 payments is worth $k$ today:

$$PV_Y = 30k$$

**Equate the present values:**

$$30k = 52{,}732.61 \implies k = 1{,}757.75 \approx 1{,}758$$
