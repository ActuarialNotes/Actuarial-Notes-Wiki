An actuary is reviewing results of a hurricane model on a potential new reinsurance program. The actuary simulates five years of losses with the following results:

**Combined Ratios:**

| Simulation | Direct | Net of Current Program | Net of Proposed Program |
|---|---|---|---|
| #1 | 130% | 105% | 115% |
| #2 | 180% | 140% | 130% |
| #3 | 80% | 90% | 100% |
| #4 | 90% | 95% | 105% |
| #5 | 200% | 160% | 150% |

## Part a (1.25 points)

Construct a coverage option cumulative distribution function chart using the simulations above.

### Explanation
Sort each program's five simulated combined ratios from lowest to highest and plot them against the cumulative probability (0.2, 0.4, ..., 1.0):

| CDF | Direct | Net of Current | Net of Proposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 80% | 90% | 100% |
| 0.4 | 90% | 95% | 105% |
| 0.6 | 130% | 105% | 115% |
| 0.8 | 180% | 140% | 130% |
| 1.0 | 200% | 160% | 150% |

![Sample answer chart: combined ratio (vertical axis, 0% to 200%) against CDF (horizontal axis, 0.2 to 1). Direct rises from 80% at 0.2 to 200% at 1; Current rises from 90% to about 160%; Proposed starts highest at 100% but ends lowest at about 150%, crossing below Current in the upper tail.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas7-2019-q19-sample-cdf-chart.png)

The examiner's report also accepted the chart with the axes swapped (CDF on the vertical axis against combined ratio), drawn either as lines or as a step function.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide a plot of Combined Ratios vs CDF with the three sets of simulations plotted.
Common mistakes included:

- Providing alternative charts such as a Program vs Combined Ratio plot
- Plotting simulations on an axis
- Not sorting the observations
- Only providing discrete points for CDF

## Part b (0.5 points)

Select a reinsurance program and briefly explain your selection based on the simulations above if an actuary is primarily concerned about losses in the tail.

### Explanation
Proposed program has lower Combined Ratio in the tail, use proposed.

Alternatively:

I choose proposed because it outperforms current & bare at around α = .7 and above, even though it performs worse at lower α's.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify the proposed reinsurance program that would satisfy the actuary's concern about tail risk.

Common mistakes included:

- Not selecting the program based on the concern over tail risk

## Part c (0.75 points)

Calculate the 80th percentile results using direct data for the following:

- i. V@R
- ii. TV@R
- iii. XTV@R

### Explanation
- V@R = 80th percentile = 180%
- TV@R = (180% + 200%)/2 = 190%
- XTV@R = TV@R - mean:

$$190\% - \frac{80 + 90 + 130 + 180 + 200}{5}\% = 54\%$$

Alternatively, taking TV@R as the average of the outcomes strictly above the 80th percentile:

- VaR $= E(X \mid X = 80\%) = 180\%$
- TVaR $= E(X \mid X > 80\%) = 200\%$
- XTVaR = TVaR - mean $= 200\% - 136\% = 64\%$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide a numerical solution for each of the three statistics using the direct data.
There were several acceptable answers for TV@R and they were all given full credit.

Common mistakes included:

- V@R set at the 3rd observation (60th percent) rather than the 4th observation
- Incorrect formula used for XTV@R
- Calculating statistics for the current or proposed program instead of the direct book

## Part d (0.25 points)

Define WTV@R.

### Explanation
Sample Responses

- WTVaR is TVaR calculated with probability distribution weighted to emphasize large loss.
- WTVaR is similar to TVaR, however the probabilities assigned to the simulations are assigned through a Wang Transformation instead of being uniform like TVaR.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to describe that WTVaR is the same calculation as TVaR with transformed probabilities to put additional weight on the tail values.
Common mistakes included:

- Responding with only the acronym fully spelled out.

## Part e (0.5 points)

Briefly describe a weakness of TV@R and briefly explain how WTV@R corrects the weakness.

### Explanation
Sample Responses

- TVaR treats all losses equally. This doesn't always reflect true risk appetite. WTVaR corrects this by placing more weight on extreme values.
- TVaR treats losses linearly in the tail (i.e., a loss 2x as large is 2x as bad), which does not accurately reflect market attitudes. WTVaR is not linear and places higher weights on more extreme losses, which is more appropriate.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to describe that TVaR does not properly reflect the risk averse attitude towards risk and that WTVaR corrects for this by applying additional weight on larger losses to reflect their increasing impact to the company.
Common mistakes included:

- Not stating the risk averseness or market attitude of increasingly larger losses.
