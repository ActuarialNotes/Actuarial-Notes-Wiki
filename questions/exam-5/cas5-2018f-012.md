Given the following for an insurance company:

$$\text{Premium} = (\text{Base Rate}) \times (\text{Rating Factor 1}) \times (\text{Rating Factor 2})$$

| Variable 1 Segment | Rating Factor 1 |
|---|---|
| A | 0.90 |
| B | 2.00 |
| C | 1.00 |

| Variable 2 Segment | Rating Factor 2 |
|---|---|
| X | 0.75 |
| Y | 0.95 |
| Z | 1.00 |

**Earned Exposures**

| Variable 2 | Variable 1: A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 800 | 1,500 | 600 |
| Y | 300 | 750 | 500 |
| Z | 500 | 500 | 1,500 |

**Reported Loss & ALAE (\$)**

| Variable 2 | Variable 1: A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 320,000 | 2,100,000 | 400,000 |
| Y | 170,000 | 1,535,000 | 500,000 |
| Z | 305,000 | 1,100,000 | 1,600,000 |

- The base rate is \$1,000.
- The base classification for Variable 1 is C.
- The proposed overall rate level change is 0%.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate the indicated relativities for Variable 1 using the adjusted pure premium method.

### Answer

A: 0.557; B: 1.965; C: 1.000

### Explanation

The adjusted pure premium method removes the distortion caused by the uneven distribution of the *other* rating variable, by weighting each cell's exposures with the Variable 2 rating factor before computing pure premiums.

**Adjusted exposures** (exposure × Rating Factor 2):

$$A: 800(0.75) + 300(0.95) + 500(1.00) = 600 + 285 + 500 = 1{,}385$$

$$B: 1{,}500(0.75) + 750(0.95) + 500(1.00) = 1{,}125 + 712.5 + 500 = 2{,}337.5$$

$$C: 600(0.75) + 500(0.95) + 1{,}500(1.00) = 450 + 475 + 1{,}500 = 2{,}425$$

**Losses** by Variable 1 segment:

$$A: 320{,}000 + 170{,}000 + 305{,}000 = 795{,}000$$

$$B: 2{,}100{,}000 + 1{,}535{,}000 + 1{,}100{,}000 = 4{,}735{,}000$$

$$C: 400{,}000 + 500{,}000 + 1{,}600{,}000 = 2{,}500{,}000$$

**Adjusted pure premium and relativity to base class C:**

| Segment | Adjusted Exposures | Loss & ALAE | Adjusted Pure Premium | Indicated Relativity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1,385 | 795,000 | 574.01 | $574.01 / 1{,}030.93 = 0.557$ |
| B | 2,337.5 | 4,735,000 | 2,025.67 | $2{,}025.67 / 1{,}030.93 = 1.965$ |
| C | 2,425 | 2,500,000 | 1,030.93 | 1.000 |

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to understand the adjusted pure premium methodology and how to calculate class relativities from it.

Common mistakes included using unadjusted exposures, and failing to rebase the indicated relativities to the base class.

## Part b (0.5 point)

Calculate the proposed base rate assuming the company selects half of the indicated relativity change for each segment of Variable 1.

### Answer

\$1,035

### Explanation

**Selected relativities** — move half way from current to indicated:

| Segment | Current | Indicated | Selected |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.90 | 0.56 | $0.90 + 0.5(0.56 - 0.90) = 0.73$ |
| B | 2.00 | 1.96 | $2.00 + 0.5(1.96 - 2.00) = 1.98$ |
| C | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

Changing the relativities alone changes the average rate level, so an off-balance correction is needed. Weight the current and selected relativities by **adjusted exposures**:

$$\overline{R}_{\text{current}} = \frac{1{,}385(0.90) + 2{,}337.5(2.00) + 2{,}425(1.00)}{6{,}147.5} = \frac{8{,}346.5}{6{,}147.5} = 1.358$$

$$\overline{R}_{\text{selected}} = \frac{1{,}385(0.73) + 2{,}337.5(1.98) + 2{,}425(1.00)}{6{,}147.5} = \frac{8{,}064.3}{6{,}147.5} = 1.312$$

$$\text{Off-balance factor} = \frac{1.358}{1.312} = 1.035$$

The proposed overall rate level change is 0%, so the base rate absorbs the off-balance entirely:

$$\text{Proposed base rate} = 1{,}000 \times 1.035 = \$1{,}035$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to understand how to calculate a proposed base rate given a premium change goal and a constraint on the change in class relativities.

Common mistakes included using raw exposures rather than adjusted exposures as the weights for the total relativity change, and off-balancing on the indicated relativities rather than the selected ones.

## Part c (0.5 point)

Briefly describe two social criteria for evaluating the appropriateness of rating variables.

### Explanation

- **Affordability.** The resulting premium should remain affordable, particularly where the coverage is compulsory — a variable that prices some insureds out of a mandatory market raises a social objection even if it is actuarially sound.
- **Controllability.** The insured should be able to influence the characteristic and thereby their rate — a controllable variable (e.g. installing an alarm) is more socially acceptable than one the insured cannot change, and it encourages loss control.

Also acceptable: **privacy** (the variable should not require intrusive information about the insured) and **causality** (there should be an intuitive, logical relationship between the variable and the loss, not merely a statistical correlation).

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to recognize the social criteria applied to rating variables.

Common mistakes included giving legal criteria or operational criteria instead of social ones.
