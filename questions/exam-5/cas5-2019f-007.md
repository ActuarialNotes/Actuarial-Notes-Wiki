Given the following data as of December 31, 2018:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Reported Loss + ALAE (\$000s) as of 12 months | as of 24 months | as of 36 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 3,440 | 4,107 | 4,522 |
| 2017 | 3,427 | 4,109 | |
| 2018 | 3,545 | | |

| Calendar Year | Earned Premium (\$000s) | Fixed Expenses (\$000s) |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 10,500 | 1,155 |
| 2017 | 12,000 | 3,600 |
| 2018 | 12,500 | 1,500 |

| Rate Change History — Effective Date | Change |
|---|---|
| July 1, 2017 | 5% |
| July 1, 2018 | 2% |

| Value | Item |
|---|---|
| 4% | Annual loss and ALAE trend |
| 3% | Annual premium trend |
| 60% | Expected Loss and ALAE Ratio |
| 30% | Variable Expense Ratio |
| 5% | Profit and Contingencies Provision |
| 7% | ULAE Provision (as % of Loss and ALAE) |
| 1.031 | 36-to-ultimate tail factor |

- In 2017 the company implemented a new policy issuance system.
- Rates are in effect for one year.
- All policies are annual.
- Exposures are written evenly throughout each calendar year.

Calculate the indicated rate change for policies effective January 1, 2020 using the reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique for the last three accident years.

### Answer
-12.0%

### Explanation
**Step 1 — development factors.** Age-to-age factors from the reported triangle, averaged across the available years:

| Age | Factors | Selected |
|---|---|---|
| 12–24 | $4{,}107/3{,}440 = 1.1939$, $4{,}109/3{,}427 = 1.1990$ | $1.1965$ |
| 24–36 | $4{,}522/4{,}107 = 1.1010$ | $1.1010$ |
| 36–ult | given | $1.031$ |

Cumulating **by multiplying** (not adding):

$$CDF_{36} = 1.031, \qquad CDF_{24} = 1.1010 \times 1.031 = 1.135, \qquad CDF_{12} = 1.1965 \times 1.135 = 1.358$$

**Step 2 — Bornhuetter-Ferguson ultimates.** The expected losses are the *earned premium* times the 60% expected loss ratio — **not** on-level earned premium, since the expected loss ratio is stated on the premium as written. The BF ultimate adds the unreported portion to the actual reported losses:

$$U = \text{Reported} + EP \times ELR \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{CDF}\right)$$

| AY | Reported | $EP \times 0.60$ | $1 - 1/CDF$ | BF Ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 4,522 | 6,300 | $1 - 1/1.031 = 0.0301$ | $4{,}711.4$ |
| 2017 | 4,109 | 7,200 | $1 - 1/1.135 = 0.1190$ | $4{,}966.4$ |
| 2018 | 3,545 | 7,500 | $1 - 1/1.358 = 0.2637$ | $5{,}522.9$ |

**Step 3 — trend the losses and load ULAE.** Rates go into effect 1/1/2020 for one year, on annual policies, so the exposure period runs 1/1/2020 – 12/31/2021 and its **average accident date is 1/1/2021**. Trending from each accident year's average accident date (July 1):

$$2016:\ 4.5 \text{ yrs}, \qquad 2017:\ 3.5 \text{ yrs}, \qquad 2018:\ 2.5 \text{ yrs}$$

Note the trend is applied to the *BF ultimate*, not to the reported losses inside the BF formula. Loading ULAE at 7%:

| AY | BF Ultimate | $\times 1.04^{n}$ | $\times 1.07$ | Ultimate Trended Loss + LAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 4,711.4 | $1.04^{4.5}$ | 1.07 | 6,014.3 |
| 2017 | 4,966.4 | $1.04^{3.5}$ | 1.07 | 6,096.0 |
| 2018 | 5,522.9 | $1.04^{2.5}$ | 1.07 | 6,518.3 |

**Step 4 — on-level factors (parallelogram method).** With annual policies written evenly, a rate change effective July 1 affects a triangle of $\tfrac{1}{2} \times (0.5)^2 = 12.5\%$ of that calendar year's earned premium. Current rate level is $1.05 \times 1.02 = 1.071$.

| CY | Rate level composition | Average rate level | OLF |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | $100\%$ at $1.000$ | $1.00000$ | $1.071 / 1.00000 = 1.071$ |
| 2017 | $87.5\%$ at $1.000$, $12.5\%$ at $1.050$ | $1.00625$ | $1.071 / 1.00625 = 1.064$ |
| 2018 | $12.5\%$ at $1.000$, $75\%$ at $1.050$, $12.5\%$ at $1.071$ | $1.04638$ | $1.071 / 1.04638 = 1.024$ |

**Step 5 — trended on-level earned premium and loss ratios.** Premium trends at 3% over the same periods:

| AY | EP | OLF | $1.03^{n}$ | Trended OLEP | Loss Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 10,500 | 1.071 | $1.03^{4.5}$ | 12,845.4 | $46.82\%$ |
| 2017 | 12,000 | 1.064 | $1.03^{3.5}$ | 14,164.3 | $43.04\%$ |
| 2018 | 12,500 | 1.024 | $1.03^{2.5}$ | 13,775.4 | $47.32\%$ |

$$\text{Average loss and LAE ratio} = \frac{46.82\% + 43.04\% + 47.32\%}{3} = 45.73\%$$

**Step 6 — fixed expense ratio.** Fixed expenses are taken as a ratio to **earned premium** (not on-level premium):

$$2016: \frac{1{,}155}{10{,}500} = 11.0\%, \qquad 2017: \frac{3{,}600}{12{,}000} = 30.0\%, \qquad 2018: \frac{1{,}500}{12{,}500} = 12.0\%$$

The 2017 ratio is a clear outlier, explained by the one-time cost of implementing the new policy issuance system that year. Treating it as non-recurring and averaging the two clean years:

$$\text{Selected fixed expense ratio} = \frac{11.0\% + 12.0\%}{2} = 11.5\%$$

**Step 7 — the indication.** Under the loss ratio method with a fixed expense provision:

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{\text{Loss \& LAE ratio} + \text{Fixed expense ratio}}{1 - \text{Variable expense} - \text{Profit}} - 1$$

$$= \frac{0.4573 + 0.1150}{1 - 0.30 - 0.05} - 1 = \frac{0.5723}{0.65} - 1 = -0.120$$

$$\boxed{-12.0\%}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to complete an overall rate level indication using the loss ratio method: calculating development factors, calculating on-level factors from the rate change history, trending and developing losses, on-leveling and trending premium, applying a ULAE load, and incorporating an appropriate fixed expense load.

Common mistakes included failing to treat 2017 as an outlier fixed expense year; using on-level earned premium as the base for the expected losses in the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method, or as the base for the fixed expense load; assuming all expenses were variable; using the development method rather than the BF method to develop losses; adding rather than multiplying the age-to-age factors to build the CDFs; including fixed expense in the loss and LAE ratio; trending the reported losses inside the BF formula; and calculating a separate rate indication for each year rather than one indication from the averaged loss ratio.
