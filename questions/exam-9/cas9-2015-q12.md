Given the following information about two companies in different industries:

- ABC is in an industry that is expected to become more competitive.
- XYZ has a business plan that relies on the successful development of a new product.
- One company has Ba-rated debt, and the other B-rated debt.
- B-rated and Ba-rated debt have expected cumulative default rates described in the table below:

| Term (years) | 5 | 10 | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ba | 10% | 20% | 30% |
| B | 30% | 50% | 58% |

## Part a (1 point)

Evaluate which bond rating more accurately describes the expected change in probability of default as a function of time for Company XYZ.

### Explanation
Descriptive Answer:

ABC is already established. It is not likely to default in the near future. However, as competition grows, it will be more likely to default. This is reflected in Ba since the conditional profit default increases. (as printed)

$$Q_5 = 10\% \qquad Q_5 = \frac{20-10}{100-10} = 11.1\% \qquad Q_5 = \frac{30-10}{1-20} = 12.5\% \quad \text{(as printed)}$$

XYZ is unlikely to survive the next couple years, since the new product would fail. However, if the new product is successful and XYZ survives the next years, it is more likely to survive longer. This is reflective in the decreasing conditional default Pr of B.

$$Q_5 = 30\% \qquad Q_{20} = \frac{50-30}{1-2070} = 28.1\% \qquad Q_{P5} = \frac{58-50}{100-50} = 16\% \quad \text{(as printed)}$$

Alternatively:

Unconditional Probability calc:

Change in prob default as a function of time

| | 5-10 yrs | 10-15 yrs |
|---|---|---|
| A | 20-10=10% | 30-20=10% |
| B | 50-30=20% | 58-50=8% |

Bond rating B more accurately reflects expected change in prob of default as a function of time for XYZ.

Since this requires successful development of a new product, the earlier years of development are the most critical and should see the greatest change in default probability.

Once the company has survived that initial period and the new product is successful, it shouldn't see major changes in default prob.

This is best shown by B, since 20% > 8% (while A shows steady decline 10% = 10 yrs), which may relate better to ASBC, being in an industry & country competitive. (as printed)

Alternatively:

Hazard Rate calc:

Default intensity given survival.

| | 5 | 10 | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ba | 0.1 | 0.111 | 0.125 |
| B | 0.3 | 0.286 | 0.16 |

As seen above the default intensify $\lambda(t)$ decreases for the B rated., which matches the XYZ company. Some of it depends on successful development of a new product, making it right short term (70% on 5 yrs) but if they survived, chance of default in the next 5 yrs as shown above. And the trends go on for 10-15 yrs as well. So again, B. Also make note that ABC's rate goes up as a competitive environment. (as printed)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to recognize that a company that was reliant on a new product would have a period with a high probability of default followed by declining hazard rates if the product is successful.  By far, the most common error was not including any discussion about the declining hazard rate portion of the pattern.
Other errors were generally of the form where candidates argued that the Ba-rated bond with the constant hazard rate was more appropriate.

## Part b (1 point)

Assuming both companies are still in business in 10 years, use the table of default rates to determine which company is expected to have better credit-worthiness.

### Explanation
Calculate hazard rate for yr 15

$$\text{(ABC) Ba} \rightarrow = \frac{.3 - .2}{1 - .2} = 12.5\%$$

$$\text{(XYZ) B} \rightarrow = \frac{.58 - .5}{1 - .5} = 16\%$$

Since the Ba rated bond (ABC) has a lower hazard rate, they are more credit worthy.

### Examiner Report
Most of the errors for part b were using the change in the unconditional probability instead of using the hazard rates to make a decision on which company would have better creditworthiness.
Since the question asked under the condition that both companies survived, the answer needed to use the conditional probability or hazard rates.  Most other deductions were caused by calculation errors.
