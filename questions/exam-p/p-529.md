The number of errors on an application is modeled by a Poisson random variable with variance 0.25.

The number of errors on any application is independent of the number of errors on all other applications.

Calculate the probability that two applications contain a total of more than one error.

- A) $0.03$
- B) $0.09$
- C) $0.30$
- D) $0.61$
- E) $0.91$

## Explanation

For a Poisson distribution the variance equals the mean, so each application has $\lambda = 0.25$.

Independent Poisson counts add to a Poisson count whose mean is the sum, so the total over two applications is Poisson with

$$
\lambda_{\text{total}} = 2(0.25) = 0.50
$$

Take the complement of the two smallest counts:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 1] &= 1 - P[X=0] - P[X=1] \\
         &= 1 - e^{-0.5} - 0.5e^{-0.5} \\
         &= 1 - 1.5e^{-0.5} \\
         &= 1 - 1.5(0.60653) \\
         &= \mathbf{0.0902}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 0.61, is $e^{-0.5}$, which is $P[X = 0]$ — an intermediate value rather than the answer. Note the mean must be doubled *before* the mass function is evaluated; using $\lambda = 0.25$ and then squaring or doubling the resulting probability answers a different question.
