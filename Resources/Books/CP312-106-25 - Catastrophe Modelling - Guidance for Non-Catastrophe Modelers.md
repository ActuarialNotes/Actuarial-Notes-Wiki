A June 2013 guide from the Lloyd's Market Association (LMA) Exposure Management Working Group for colleagues who do not work on catastrophe ("cat") modelling. It provides background on what a cat model is, explains the terminology frequently used by modellers and answers many frequently asked questions. It is not intended to be a comprehensive guide to cat modelling, but a quick reference giving non-cat modellers a basic understanding of the work of cat modellers.

## Introduction
- Uses of Catastrophe Modelling
    - A cat model is a computerised system that generates a set of simulated events and estimates the magnitude, intensity and location of each event to determine the damage and the insured loss from a catastrophic event such as a hurricane or an earthquake, and the first core use the guide lists within the insurance industry is risk pricing.

## Catastrophe Modelling practice
- Different components of a Catastrophe model
    - A cat model is built up of a number of modules that must all operate in coordination, among them an exposure data module (the risks to be assessed, with at least a geo-location and an insured value) and the hazard and vulnerability modules, each of which could be considered a model in its own right.

## Catastrophe Modelling Terminology
- EP curve
- OEP and an AEP curves
- VaR and TVaR
- Event Loss Table
- Coefficient of Variation (CoV)
- Difference between Near Term, Long Term and Historical rates
- Difference between Ground Up, Gross, Net and Final Net losses
