You are provided the following data set with a single variable $X$.

| $i$ | $X$ |
|---|---|
| 1 | 9 |
| 2 | 15 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 18 |

A dendogram is built from this data set using agglomerative hierarchical clustering with complete linkage and Euclidean distance as the dissimilarity measure.

Calculate the tree height at which observation $i = 1$ fuses.

- A) Less than 6
- B) 6
- C) 7
- D) 8
- E) At least 9

## Explanation

Agglomerative clustering repeatedly fuses the two closest clusters. Under complete linkage, the distance between two clusters is the *largest* distance between a point in one and a point in the other. In one dimension the Euclidean distance is just $|x_i - x_j|$.

Sorted, the data are 2 (obs. 4), 4 (obs. 3), 9 (obs. 1), 15 (obs. 2), 18 (obs. 5).

**First fuse.** The closest pair is 2 and 4, at distance 2, so $\{3, 4\}$ fuses at height 2.

**Second fuse.** The next closest pair is 15 and 18, at distance 3, so $\{2, 5\}$ fuses at height 3. Observation 1 (at 9) is not yet in a cluster, because its nearest points are 5 and 6 away.

**Third fuse.** Three clusters remain. With complete linkage:

$$
\begin{align*}
d(\{1\}, \{3,4\}) &= \max(|9-4|,\ |9-2|) \\
&= 7 \\
d(\{1\}, \{2,5\}) &= \max(|9-15|,\ |9-18|) \\
&= 9 \\
d(\{3,4\}, \{2,5\}) &= \max(|4-15|,\ |4-18|,\ |2-15|,\ |2-18|) \\
&= 16
\end{align*}
$$

The smallest is 7, so observation 1 fuses with $\{3, 4\}$ at height 7. Single linkage would have used the *minimum*, 5, which is the trap in option A.

The answer is **C**.
