## Part a (1 point)

Discuss two reasons why the relationship between shareholders and debtholders often makes it worthwhile for a firm to minimize a specific diversifiable risk, regardless of whether the firm is an insurer.

### Explanation
- **Agency costs:** if shareholders (as agents of the debtholders) take risky gambles with the debtholders' money, debtholders may demand higher yields and stricter bond covenants, so the firm should try to minimize these agency issues.
- **Underinvestment:** if the firm becomes troubled, shareholders may underinvest in safer projects that provide a consistent, if lower, return (the "underinvestment problem"), which hurts the debtholders and the firm's viability. The shareholders' stake decreases when the firm is in distress but the bondholders' stake does not.

Alternatively:

Agency theory describes the conflict between shareholders and debtholders. (1) Outside of financial distress, the shareholders' goal (optimize return on investment) conflicts with the debtholders' (minimize the firm's default probability), so debtholders are risk averse; to appease them, a firm may transfer diversifiable risks that could lead to default. (2) In financial distress the conflict heightens: shareholders become less risk averse, taking chances to get some of their money out (debtholders have the first claim), and since they control the firm's assets this can lead to underinvestment — foregoing profitable opportunities that would benefit debtholders more. Reducing the risk of distress helps avoid these issues.

### Examiner Report
- The candidate was expected to know that in spite of the inevitability of the conflict between shareholders and debt holders (pertaining to risk tolerance levels) there would be situations where it would be advantageous for the shareholders to adopt a risk- minimizing strategy under normal circumstances and a strategy of reducing the probability of financial distress.
- The candidates needed to describe the relationships between shareholders and debt holders, describe the conflict situations, and describe the conflict resolution mechanism.
- Many candidates mentioned that the interests are aligned. The divergence was not properly discussed. Many candidates made general statements instead of focusing on the relationship between shareholders and debt holders.

## Part b (1 point)

Describe two ways the relationship between shareholders and debtholders differs for insurance firms and non-insurance firms.

### Explanation
- In an insurance company the biggest "bondholders" are the policyholders, because the unearned premium and loss and LAE reserves dominate an insurer's balance sheet.
- Policyholders cannot diversify away their risk the way a bondholder can by investing in many companies, municipalities or governments — a policyholder cannot buy multiple policies to spread the risk.

Alternatively:

- Policyholders are debtholders (they supply the insurer's reserve and unearned premium liabilities) who cannot diversify their risk — one cannot buy a personal accident policy from three insurers — so they are particularly risk averse. A policy is a promise of future payment contingent on specific conditions, so policyholders especially want the insurer to be stable.
- Because the loss and unearned premium reserves are huge compared with other forms of capital, these debtholders are especially important: losing them means losing both customers and a source of financing.

### Examiner Report
- The candidate was expected to know that the policyholders are the primary debt holders for an insurance company. This situation leads to a Customer/Business relationship that supersedes shareholder/debt holder relationship. Hence, in order to retain their customer base, insurance companies have to manage the risk preferences of their customers. This situation does not become an issue for non-insurance companies.
- Candidates needed to mention that policyholders/customers are the primary debt holders and how insurance companies have to consider their preferences.
- Most candidates identified policyholders/customers as the primary debt holders of an insurance company. Many unnecessarily brought in the issue of mutual companies; this was not part of the question.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Explain how the differences in part b. above could affect the amount of risk an insurance firm might want to retain relative to a non-insurance firm.

### Explanation
An insurer may want to retain less risk since they may lose policyholders in the event of financial distress and w/ little recourse to set them back, since lowering rates will worsen distress.

Alternatively:

As policyholders, who prefer to buy from stable insurers, play an important part in insurance company capital decision making (see part b), insurance company tend to retain less risk than non-insurers.

### Examiner Report
- The candidate was expected to know that the debt holder/customer would be risk-averse and would expect the insurer to remain solvent to pay claims. This emphasis would force insurers to reduce risk much more than non-insurance companies.
- Most candidates were able to identify customers' needs with respect to risk reduction.
However, many were unable to connect this situation with risk reduction for the firm.
