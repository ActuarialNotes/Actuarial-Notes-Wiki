It reports that Ontario is introducing a policy requiring cheaper versions of pricey biologic drugs to be offered to most patients who need them. It is the SOA's study note GH201-715-25 (formerly GHVR-715-F23).

## The policy
- The policy, approved by cabinet the previous Wednesday, will save money and better ensure the long-term sustainability of the province's drug plan, according to a government official.
- Similar mandatory switching policies have already been adopted in several other provinces, including British Columbia and Alberta.

## Biologics and biosimilars
- Biologic drugs are made from living organisms and are used to treat conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease and some forms of cancer; they are expensive and make up a significant chunk of drug spending in Canada.
- A Patented Medicine Prices Review Board report puts biologic sales at $10 billion in 2020, up from $3.3 billion in 2011, representing 34 percent of pharmaceutical sales in 2020.
- Biosimilars are nearly identical versions of biologic drugs, typically offered at a much lower cost; Biosimilars Canada says mandatory switching would save the province about $150 million a year.

## Mandatory switching
- Mandatory switching requires clinicians to prescribe biosimilar medications to most patients.
- It is controversial: the biologics industry advocates against such policies, saying patients and care providers should be able to choose the appropriate medication regardless of cost, and the Alliance for Safe Biologic Medicines says forced switching may result in unexpected or harmful outcomes.
- Ontario approved mandatory switching in 2020, but it was sidetracked by the pandemic.

## Transition
- Under the new policy a nine-month transition period starts March 31, giving patients time to discuss the changes and their options with their care providers.
- Certain patients, such as those who are pregnant and people with certain types of cancer, are exempt from mandatory switching.
- The province would reach out to various stakeholders that week to discuss the plan and its implications.
