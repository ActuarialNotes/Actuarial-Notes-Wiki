You are given a random sample from a uniform distribution $[0, \theta]$:

$$
1.3, \quad 2.7, \quad 8.2, \quad 5.3, \quad X
$$

You find that the estimate of $\theta$ using method of moments is equal to the estimate of $\theta$ using the maximum likelihood.

Calculate the largest possible value of $X$.

- A) Less than 4
- B) At least 4, but less than 6
- C) At least 6, but less than 8
- D) At least 8, but less than 10
- E) At least 10

## Explanation

Write both estimators in terms of $X$ and solve for the values of $X$ that make them equal. The maximum likelihood estimate depends on whether $X$ is the largest observation, so there are two cases.

For a uniform distribution on $[0, \theta]$, $E[X] = \theta/2$, so the method of moments estimate is twice the sample mean. The four known values sum to $1.3 + 2.7 + 8.2 + 5.3 = 17.5$:

$$
\hat{\theta}_{\text{MoM}} = 2\bar{x} = \frac{2(17.5 + X)}{5}
$$

The likelihood $\theta^{-5}$ is decreasing in $\theta$ but requires $\theta \ge$ every observation, so the maximum likelihood estimate is the sample maximum, $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} = \max(8.2, X)$.

**Case 1, $X \le 8.2$:** the MLE is 8.2.

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{2(17.5 + X)}{5} &= 8.2 \\
17.5 + X &= 20.5 \\
X &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

**Case 2, $X > 8.2$:** the MLE is $X$ itself.

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{2(17.5 + X)}{5} &= X \\
35 + 2X &= 5X \\
X &= 11.67
\end{align*}
$$

Both solutions satisfy their case's condition, and the larger is $X = 35/3 = 11.67$. This falls in $[10, \infty)$, so the answer is **E**.
