A December 2017 web-exclusive article in The Actuary magazine, by three authors at Wakely Consulting Group, on risk adjustment and market stabilization in the individual health insurance market. Starting from the American Academy of Actuaries' conditions for a stable and sustainable market, it looks at what is driving the instability of the ACA individual market and what could improve it.

## Balanced Risk Pool
- A balanced risk pool needs a reasonable distribution of enrollees; the ACA encourages younger and healthier individuals to enroll through an individual mandate, subsidies based on income and enrollment limitations.
- Individual Mandate
    - A tax penalty on individuals deemed able to afford coverage but who choose not to buy it; uncertainty over its enforcement led many issuers to increase their 2018 rates.
- Subsidies
    - Advanced premium tax credits are calculated relative to the second lowest cost silver plan in an enrollee's area and end above 400 percent of the federal poverty level.
- Temporary Risk Stabilization Programs
    - Risk mitigation programs are risk corridors, reinsurance and risk adjustment; risk adjustment is the only continuing federal program.
- Other Key Factors
    - Outreach and advertising, Medicaid expansion, enforcement of special enrollment periods, and the ability to develop adequate rates in a stable regulatory environment.

## Sufficient Health Insurer Participation and Plan Offerings
- Risk adjustment was intended to equalize the profitability of members so that issuers are not benefited or hurt by enrolling a certain type of member.
- Risk Adjustment
    - A budget neutral program that redistributes funds, within each state and market, from issuers with lower-risk, lower-cost enrollees to issuers with higher-risk, higher-cost enrollees, using a methodology produced by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.
    - For some segments of the market, such as platinum plans and broad networks, the program fell short of adequate compensation, and issuers have changed their plan offerings, reducing consumer choice.
- 2015 Results
    - Wakely Consulting Group analysed 2015 ACA-compliant data of approximately 5 million people from more than 100 issuers in more than 20 states, viewing market stability through profitability net of risk adjustment transfers.

## Conclusion
- From 2014 to 2016 issuers experienced large losses as premiums were often too low and policy provisions such as risk corridors, the individual mandate and subsidies did not produce stable markets in all states.
- Policies often have unintended consequences, and ensuring that issuers and the public understand the rules is necessary for successful implementation of any market stabilization program.
