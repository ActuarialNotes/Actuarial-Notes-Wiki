An investor wants to purchase a total of ten units of two assets, A and B, with annual payoffs per unit purchased of $X$ and $Y$, respectively. Each asset has the same purchase price per unit. The payoffs are independent random variables with equal expected values and with $\text{Var}(X) = 30$ and $\text{Var}(Y) = 20$.

Calculate the number of units of asset A the investor should purchase to minimize the variance of the total payoff.

- A) $0$
- B) $2$
- C) $4$
- D) $5$
- E) $6$

## Explanation

Let $u$ be the units of asset A, so $10 - u$ units of B are bought and the total payoff is

$$
uX + (10-u)Y
$$

The two payoffs are independent, so the variances add with squared coefficients:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}\bigl(uX + (10-u)Y\bigr) &= u^2(30) + (10-u)^2(20) \\
                                   &= 30u^2 + 20(100 - 20u + u^2) \\
                                   &= 50u^2 - 400u + 2000
\end{align*}
$$

This is an upward-opening parabola in $u$. Differentiate and set to zero:

$$
\begin{align*}
100u - 400 &= 0 \\
u &= \mathbf{4}
\end{align*}
$$

The second derivative is $100 > 0$, confirming a minimum.

Because the expected payoffs are equal, every allocation has the same expected total — only the variance distinguishes them, so the investor is free to minimize it outright. The optimum tilts *away* from the riskier asset A but does not abandon it: choice (A), 0 units of A, gives variance 2000, while $u = 4$ gives 1200. Diversifying across two independent assets beats concentrating in the safer one alone, which is the point of the question.
