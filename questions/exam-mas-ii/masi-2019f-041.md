Two time series (X and Y) are shown in the graph below:

![Line plot of two time series against Time from 1 to 24, vertical axis from 0 to 10. Series X (solid line) and Series Y (dotted line) both trend upward, from about 0.2 and 0.8 at time 1 to about 9.2 and 9.8 at time 24, zigzagging between roughly 2 and 7.5 in between. Series Y's peaks and troughs arrive about one period before Series X's: Y peaks near 5.1 at time 3 and X near 4.9 at time 4; Y near 6.5 at time 6 and X near 6.1 at time 7; Y dips to 1.7 at time 10 and X to 2.4 at time 11; Y peaks near 7.4 at time 15 and X near 7.0 at time 16](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masi-2019f-q41-series-x-y.png)

The autocorrelation variance function (acvf) and cross covariance function (ccvf) are estimated at lags 0, 1, 2 & 3 in the table below.

| Lag | acvf(x) | acvf(y) | ccvf(x,y) |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 0 | 4.165 | 4.655 | 2.626 |
| 1 | 1.594 | 1.960 | 3.422 |
| 2 | 1.123 | 0.518 | 0.950 |
| 3 | 0.478 | 1.064 | 0.389 |

Calculate the sample lag 1 cross-correlation.

- A) Less than 0.30
- B) At least 0.30, but less than 0.55
- C) At least 0.55, but less than 0.80
- D) At least 0.80, but less than 1.05
- E) At least 1.05

## Explanation

The sample cross-correlation at lag $h$ scales the cross-covariance at that lag by the two series' lag-0 autocovariances, which are their sample variances. The autocovariances at lag $h$ do not enter:

$$
\hat\rho_{xy}(h) = \frac{\hat\gamma_{xy}(h)}{\sqrt{\hat\gamma_x(0)\,\hat\gamma_y(0)}}
$$

At $h = 1$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\hat\rho_{xy}(1) &= \frac{3.422}{\sqrt{4.165 \times 4.655}} \\
&= \frac{3.422}{4.4032} \\
&= 0.777
\end{align*}
$$

The lag-1 value is larger than the lag-0 one ($2.626/4.4032 = 0.596$), which matches the plot: Series Y's peaks and troughs arrive about one period before Series X's.

This falls in $[0.55, 0.80)$, so the answer is **C**.
