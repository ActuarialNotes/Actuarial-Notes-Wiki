A state has adopted the NAIC Property and Casualty Commercial Rate and Policy Form Model Law, and the commissioner has found the commercial auto market not reasonably competitive. An insurer files a new commercial auto endorsement. The 30-day waiting period passes without the commissioner disapproving the filing, extending the period or asking for more information. What is the status of the endorsement?

- A) It is deemed to meet the law's requirements and may be used
- B) It may not be used until the commissioner approves it in writing
- C) It could have been used from the filing date, because commercial forms are file and use
- D) It must be refiled, because a filing not acted on within the period lapses

## Explanation

In a noncompetitive market the model puts commercial forms on file for a **30-day waiting period**. A filing is deemed to meet the requirements of the law unless the commissioner disapproves it within the waiting period or an extension, and neither the insurer nor the commissioner may waive that deemer. So the endorsement may now be used.

- **B** describes prior approval without a deemer provision, which this model does not use.
- **C** applies the competitive-market rule, under which forms are filed a set number of days before use. The commissioner's finding of a noncompetitive market changes that.
- **D** reverses the deemer: silence lets the form take effect.
