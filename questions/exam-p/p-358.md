The annual number of accidents for a driver is modeled by a Poisson distribution with mean 2.5.

Calculate the mode of the annual number of accidents.

- A) $1.0$
- B) $1.5$
- C) $2.0$
- D) $2.5$
- E) $3.0$

## Explanation

The mode is the value of $x$ with the largest probability. For a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda = 2.5$,

$$
P[X = x] = \frac{e^{-2.5}(2.5)^x}{x!}
$$

Evaluate the mass function over the small values of $x$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X = 0] &= 0.082 \\
P[X = 1] &= 0.205 \\
P[X = 2] &= 0.257 \\
P[X = 3] &= 0.214
\end{align*}
$$

The probabilities rise to $x = 2$ and fall thereafter — from $x = 3$ on, each successive ratio $P[X=x+1]/P[X=x] = \lambda/(x+1)$ is less than 1 — so the largest probability occurs at

$$
\text{mode} = \mathbf{2}
$$

The mode of a count variable must be a possible count, which rules out the non-integer choices (B) and (D) on sight. Choice (D) in particular is the *mean* offered as the mode; for a Poisson distribution the two coincide only when $\lambda$ happens to be an integer.
