**The Autorité des marchés financiers** (AMF) is Quebec's integrated financial regulator, supervising insurance, securities, deposit institutions and distribution in a single body. Unlike the rest of Canada, Quebec's insurance regulator does **both** prudential and market-conduct supervision for provincially chartered insurers, and Quebec's private auto market operates alongside a public bodily injury scheme.

- **Integrated model.** The AMF combines functions that elsewhere sit in separate agencies ([[OSFI]] plus a provincial superintendent plus a securities commission). Federally incorporated insurers writing in Quebec are still supervised prudentially by OSFI; the AMF licenses them to operate in the province.
- **Quebec auto is a hybrid.** Bodily injury is covered by the public, no-fault **SAAQ** scheme funded by driver and vehicle levies; **property damage** is written by private insurers under the Direct Compensation agreement. So a Quebec auto rate filing concerns property damage and optional coverages only — one reason Quebec auto premiums are the lowest in Canada and are not comparable to Ontario's.
- The **Civil Code of Québec** governs the insurance contract, not a common-law *Insurance Act*. Quebec is a civil law jurisdiction, so precedent from a [[Court Case]] in another province is persuasive at best, and doctrines such as [[Duty of Good Faith|good faith]] arise from Code articles rather than from case law.
- Quebec has its own **[[Guaranty Funds|guaranty fund]]** arrangements; [[PACICC]] covers members across Canada, and Quebec insurers participate.
- The AMF issues its own **sound commercial practices** and governance guidelines, which parallel but do not simply copy OSFI's [[Corporate Governance]] and [[ORSA]] expectations.

> [!example]- Why Quebec Auto Premiums Are Not Comparable {Example}
> A commentator notes that average Quebec auto premiums are far below Ontario's and concludes that Quebec's private insurers are more efficient. Evaluate.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The comparison is invalid because the **products are different**.
> >
> > - In Quebec, **bodily injury is not in the private premium at all** — it is covered by the SAAQ public plan and funded separately through driver's licence and registration fees. A Quebec private auto premium buys property damage and optional coverages.
> > - In Ontario, the private premium buys bodily injury tort exposure *plus* [[Statutory Accident Benefits|accident benefits]] *plus* property damage.
> >
> > Bodily injury and accident benefits are the dominant cost in Ontario, so removing them removes most of the premium. To compare honestly, the SAAQ levies must be added back to the Quebec figure, and even then the schemes differ in benefit generosity and access to [[Tort Litigation|tort]].
> >
> > The general lesson for any cross-jurisdiction premium comparison: **compare cost per unit of coverage under a stated benefit regime**, never headline average premiums.
