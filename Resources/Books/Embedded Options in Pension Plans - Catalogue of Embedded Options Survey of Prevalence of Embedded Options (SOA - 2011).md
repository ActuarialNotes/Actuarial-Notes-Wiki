An August 2011 report sponsored by the SOA Pension Section and prepared by PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP, created at the Society's request as reference material on embedded options in defined benefit pension plans. It presents a catalogue of embedded options in pension plans, draws parallels between these contract features and those in the life insurance industry and the financial markets, and gives the results of a survey on their prevalence. It is Part I of a two-part project, written for pension practitioners.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam RET-101 (SOA)|Exam RET-101]] — Outcome 3b; pp. 1-17.

## I Introduction
- The term "embedded option" comes from the finance literature: an option that is part of, and inseparable from, a contract or financial instrument.
- Category 1 options are driven primarily by employee behavior and/or employee election, such as optional forms of payment.
- Category 2 options are not driven primarily by employee behavior or election, but by the behavior of underlying economic phenomena; the effort is primarily concerned with these.

## III Catalogue of Embedded Options
- A. Greater of Benefits - valuing a lump sum (annuity) based on the greater result of using a variable interest rate and a fixed interest rate for conversions
- B. Floor-Offset Plans
- C. Cost of Living Adjustments (COLAs) with caps and floors or with a relationship that is not linear to the change in the applicable index (CPI)
- D. Cost of Living Adjustments (COLAs) that provide cost of living increases if market returns exceed a “hurdle rate”
- E. Cash Balance Plans with Caps and/or Floors on the Interest Crediting Rate
- F. Flat dollar minimum benefits and flat dollar maximum (cap/ceiling) benefits
