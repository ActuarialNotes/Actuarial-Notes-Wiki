A dotchart of four models fit to the same underlying dataset is provided below. The dots represent estimations of in-sample and out-of-sample deviance for each of the four models as estimated by WAIC.

![WAIC dotchart for four models. Each row has a filled dot for in-sample deviance and an open circle for out-of-sample deviance. model_A: filled near -24.8, open near -14.5. model_B: filled near -13.9, open near -8.2. model_C: filled near -11.9, open near -7.8. model_D: filled near -12.0, open near -6.6](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q31-waic-dotchart.png)

Determine which model has the most effective number of parameters.

- A) model_A
- B) model_B
- C) model_C
- D) model_D
- E) All four models have the same number of effective number of parameters

## Explanation

On this chart the filled dot is the in-sample deviance and the open circle is the WAIC estimate of out-of-sample deviance. WAIC is the in-sample deviance plus twice the effective number of parameters:

$$
\text{WAIC} = -2\,\text{lppd} + 2\,p_{WAIC}
$$

so the **horizontal gap between the two dots on a row is $2\,p_{WAIC}$**. The model with the widest gap has the most effective parameters — reading the chart is just a matter of comparing gap widths.

| Model | filled | open | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| model_A | ≈ -24.8 | ≈ -14.5 | ≈ 10.3 |
| model_B | ≈ -13.9 | ≈ -8.2 | ≈ 5.7 |
| model_C | ≈ -11.9 | ≈ -7.8 | ≈ 4.1 |
| model_D | ≈ -12.0 | ≈ -6.6 | ≈ 5.4 |

model_A's gap is nearly twice any other, which is exactly the picture of a flexible model: much the best in-sample fit, paid for with the largest complexity penalty. Note that this makes it a poor answer to "which model would you *choose*" — the open circles decide that — but the question asks about effective parameters, which is the gap.

The answer is **A**.
