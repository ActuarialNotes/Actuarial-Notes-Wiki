A named insured buys a motorcycle and asks his agent to add it to his personal auto policy (ISO PP 00 01 09 18), which insures his sedan. Why does he need the miscellaneous type vehicle endorsement (PP 03 23) rather than simply listing the motorcycle in the declarations?

- A) Part A excludes vehicles with fewer than four wheels, and Part B excludes injury on one
- B) Only vehicles bought before the policy began can be listed in the declarations of a PAP
- C) A motorcycle can't be a newly acquired auto unless it is reported within 14 days of purchase
- D) Part D's collision coverage applies only to vehicles that are garaged at the insured's residence

## Explanation

The unendorsed PAP is built for private passenger autos, pickups and vans. Part A excludes liability for any vehicle with fewer than four wheels (exclusion B.1), and Part B excludes injury while occupying a motorized vehicle with fewer than four wheels. The miscellaneous type vehicle endorsement makes those exclusions inapplicable to a scheduled motorcycle, motor home, ATV, dune buggy or golf cart. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — vehicles bought during the term are routinely added; the issue is the type of vehicle, not the date.
- **C** — the 14-day rule applies to a newly acquired private passenger auto, pickup or van; reporting a motorcycle within 14 days would not overcome the four-wheel exclusions.
- **D** — Part D has no garaging requirement; garaging affects rating, not coverage.
