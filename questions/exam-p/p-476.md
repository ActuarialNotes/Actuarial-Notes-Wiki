An insurer sells an annual group life and disability policy.

The joint probability distribution for death and disability is:

| Disabilities \ Deaths | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.45 | 0.09 | 0.03 | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| 1 | 0.08 | 0.06 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| 2 | 0.07 | 0.05 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.00 |
| 3 | 0.04 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.00 |

Calculate the probability of at least two disabilities, given no more than one death.

- A) $0.14$
- B) $0.17$
- C) $0.18$
- D) $0.21$
- E) $0.32$

## Explanation

Both events are read off the table as blocks of cells.

**Denominator: no more than one death.** That is the first two columns, summed over all four disability rows:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[D \leq 1] &= (0.45 + 0.09) + (0.08 + 0.06) + (0.07 + 0.05) + (0.04 + 0.02) \\
            &= 0.54 + 0.14 + 0.12 + 0.06 \\
            &= 0.86
\end{align*}
$$

**Numerator: at least two disabilities *and* no more than one death.** That is the intersection — the first two columns of the bottom two rows only:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{Dis} \geq 2, D \leq 1] &= (0.07 + 0.05) + (0.04 + 0.02) \\
                               &= 0.18
\end{align*}
$$

Dividing:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{Dis} \geq 2 \mid D \leq 1] &= \frac{0.18}{0.86} \\
                                   &= \mathbf{0.2093}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 0.18, is the numerator on its own. The unconditional probability of at least two disabilities is 0.23, so conditioning on at most one death lowers it slightly — deaths and disabilities are positively associated in this table.
