An insurer already writes workers compensation for many employers in a downtown financial district. It receives an application from a bank with 3,000 employees in a single tower there, a clean loss record, a credit mod and a strong safety program. Which underwriting concern does this account raise most directly?

- A) One event at the tower could injure many insured employees, and statutory benefits have no limit
- B) The bank's mostly clerical payroll is rated so low that the premium cannot cover its expenses
- C) The credit mod shows the bank's own loss experience is not credible enough for the insurer to rely on
- D) The employers liability limits in Part Two are too low for a bank with this many employees

## Explanation

Workers compensation pays the benefits the statute requires with **no dollar limit**, and a terrorism loss can't be excluded, so the insurer's exposure to one event is open-ended. Insurers therefore track how many insured employees sit in one building or area, and an account that adds 3,000 employees to an area where the book is already concentrated is an **accumulation** problem, however good its own record. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Low clerical rates reflect low expected losses; they are not a reason the account is unprofitable.
- **C** — A credit mod means the bank's experience is better than expected for its class, and the mod already weights that experience by its credibility.
- **D** — Part Two limits are chosen by the employer and can be raised; they don't cap the unlimited Part One exposure that makes concentration a concern.
