Given the following information:

| Report Year | Loss Costs by Report Year Lag (\$), Lag 0 | Lag 1 | Lag 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 500 | 300 | 200 |
| 2016 | 525 | 330 | 210 |
| 2017 | 550 | 365 | 220 |

## Part a (0.25 point)

Calculate the report year 2015 loss costs for a claims-made policy.

### Answer

\$1,000

### Explanation

A claims-made policy covers every claim **reported** during the policy period, regardless of when the loss occurred. Report year 2015 therefore picks up the full row for 2015 — lag 0 (occurred and reported in 2015), lag 1 (occurred in 2014) and lag 2 (occurred in 2013):

$$500 + 300 + 200 = 1{,}000$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to know that all loss costs reported during 2015 are covered by a claims-made policy, regardless of when the losses occurred.

A common mistake was assuming the claims-made policy covered only losses both reported and occurring in 2015 (i.e. \$500) without stating an assumption. A mature claims-made policy answer of \$500 was acceptable only if the first-year assumption was stated.

## Part b (0.25 point)

Calculate the accident year 2015 loss costs for an occurrence policy.

### Answer

\$1,050

### Explanation

An occurrence policy covers every claim **occurring** during the policy period, no matter when it is reported. Accident year 2015 losses appear on the diagonal: reported in 2015 at lag 0, in 2016 at lag 1, and in 2017 at lag 2:

$$500 + 330 + 220 = 1{,}050$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to know that all loss costs occurring during 2015 are covered by an occurrence policy, accounting for the various reporting lags.

A common mistake was assuming an occurrence policy covered the claims *reported* in 2015.

## Part c (0.5 point)

Compare the effect of an unexpected increase in underlying trend on the accuracy of the pricing for a claims-made policy and an occurrence policy. Briefly explain why one of the policies is impacted more than the other.

### Explanation

An unexpected increase in trend distorts the pricing of the **occurrence policy** far more.

A claims-made policy covers only claims reported in the policy period, so it is exposed to settlement lag alone — the trend period between pricing and the ultimate settlement of those claims is short, and there is no pure IBNR. An occurrence policy is exposed to both report lag and settlement lag: losses occurring in the policy period can be reported years later and settled later still. Those claims are trended over much longer, more varied periods, so an error in the trend assumption compounds over the longer development tail and the priced rate is further off.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to understand that an occurrence policy has a longer development period than a claims-made policy — because claims can still be reported well into the future — and to recognize that those claims are more affected by a change in future trend.

A common mistake was simply stating that claims-made policies are shorter-tailed than occurrence policies, without explaining why they are shorter-tailed or why that matters.

## Part d (0.25 point)

Briefly explain why the risk of reserve inadequacy is reduced for a claims-made policy relative to an occurrence policy.

### Explanation

IBNR has two components: pure IBNR (IBNYR) for losses incurred but not yet reported, and IBNER for losses reported but not yet adequately valued. A claims-made policy has **no pure IBNR** — every covered claim has already been reported by the end of the policy period — so its unpaid claim estimate covers only the development on known claims (IBNER). An occurrence policy must estimate both components, including claims that have not yet been reported and about which nothing is known, so its reserve estimate is far more uncertain.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to know that IBNR includes both pure IBNR (or IBNYR) and IBNER, and that a claims-made policy carries only IBNER while an occurrence policy carries both.

A common mistake was to state that claims-made policies have no IBNR without elaborating why that reduces reserve inadequacy risk.

## Part e (0.25 point)

Briefly describe why the investment income earned from claims-made policies is less than under occurrence policies.

### Explanation

A claims-made policy shortens the time between the collection of premium and the payment of the claim, because claims must be reported within the policy period rather than years afterward. Premium is therefore held and invested for a shorter horizon than under an occurrence policy, producing less investment income.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to understand that the investment period is shorter for a claims-made policy — less time elapses between premium collection and claim payment — and therefore less investment income is earned.

A common mistake was framing the shorter lag as being between claim occurrence and claim payment, rather than between premium collection (or the start of coverage) and claim payment.
