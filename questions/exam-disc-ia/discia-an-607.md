A retailer is choosing between the causes of loss basic form and broad form. Which loss would be covered under the broad form but **not** under the basic form?

- A) Stock soaked when a water heater on the premises cracks
- B) Stock stolen by burglars who pry open the back door
- C) Stock ruined when a nearby river overflows into the store
- D) Stock damaged when the street sewer backs up into the store

## Explanation

The broad form adds three perils to the basic form's eleven: falling objects; weight of snow, ice or sleet; and **water damage**, meaning accidental discharge of water or steam when a plumbing, heating, air conditioning or other system or appliance on the premises breaks apart or cracks. The basic form excludes leakage from such a system unless a covered cause of loss damaged it. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Theft is not a named peril in either form; only the special form covers it.
- **C** — Flood and overflow of a body of water are in the water exclusion of every causes of loss form.
- **D** — Water that backs up from a sewer or drain is also in the water exclusion of every form.
