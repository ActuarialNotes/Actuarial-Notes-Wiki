You have a sample of five independent observations, $x_1 \ldots x_5$, each with exponential distribution:

$$
f(x_i|\theta_i) = \frac{1}{\theta_i}\exp\left(-\frac{x_i}{\theta_i}\right)
$$

- You are fitting this data to a model with $\theta_i = \theta$, for all $i$, using maximum likelihood estimation:

$$
f(x_i|\theta) = \frac{1}{\theta}\exp\left(-\frac{x_i}{\theta}\right)
$$

- The five observed values are: 100, 100, 500, 800, 1000
- The deviance of the model, $D$, is equal to twice the difference between the log-likelihood of the saturated model and the log-likelihood of the fitted model.

Calculate $D$.

- A) Less than 2
- B) At least 2, but less than 4
- C) At least 4, but less than 6
- D) At least 6, but less than 8
- E) At least 8

## Explanation

The saturated model gives every observation its own parameter, and the MLE of $\theta_i$ from a single exponential observation is $\hat\theta_i = x_i$. The fitted model has one common $\theta$, whose MLE is the sample mean:

$$
\begin{align*}
\hat\theta &= \bar{x} \\
&= \frac{2500}{5} \\
&= 500
\end{align*}
$$

One observation contributes $\ell_i(\theta) = -\ln\theta - x_i/\theta$ to the log-likelihood, so

$$
\begin{align*}
\ell_{\text{sat}} &= \sum_{i=1}^{5}\left(-\ln x_i - 1\right) \\
\ell_{\text{fit}} &= \sum_{i=1}^{5}\left(-\ln 500 - \frac{x_i}{500}\right)
\end{align*}
$$

Twice the difference is

$$
D = 2\left(\ell_{\text{sat}} - \ell_{\text{fit}}\right) = 2\sum_{i=1}^{5}\left[-\ln\frac{x_i}{500} + \frac{x_i - 500}{500}\right]
$$

The second term sums to zero because $\sum x_i = 5 \times 500$. The ratios $x_i/500$ are $0.2, 0.2, 1, 1.6, 2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
D &= -2\sum_{i=1}^{5}\ln\frac{x_i}{500} \\
&= -2\left(2\ln 0.2 + \ln 1 + \ln 1.6 + \ln 2\right) \\
&= -2\left(-3.2189 + 0 + 0.4700 + 0.6931\right) \\
&= -2(-2.0557) \\
&= 4.111
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[4, 6)$, so the answer is **C**.
