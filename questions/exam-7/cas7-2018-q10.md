Given the following information as of December 31, 2017:

Incremental Paid Loss (\$000) as of (months):

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 600 | 1,300 |
| 2014 | 650 | 1,400 |
| 2015 | 550 | 1,400 |
| 2016 | 700 | 1,450 |
| 2017 | 700 | |

- Exposures are stable over the years.
- There are no calendar or accident date trends in the data.
- A simple linear regression $y = a + bx$ of the incremental loss at age 24 over the cumulative loss at age 12 was performed. The resulting regression coefficients are:
  - $a = 1{,}075$
  - $b = 0.5$
- For a simple linear regression $y = a + bx$, the standard error for the slope (b) and intercept (a) are calculated as:

$$s_b = \sqrt{\sum_i \frac{\epsilon_i^2}{(n - 2) \sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2}}$$

$$s_a = s_b \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_i x_i^2}$$

where $\epsilon_i$ are the error terms.

## Part a (2 points)

Test for significance of the 12-24 loss development factor and state the chain ladder model assumption that this test is used to validate.

### Explanation
Fitted values $\hat{y} = 1{,}075 + 0.5\,x$ and errors for the four accident years with a 24-month value:

| AY | x | Predicted $\hat{y}$ | Actual y | ε = Actual - Predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 600 | 1,375 | 1,300 | -75 |
| 2014 | 650 | 1,400 | 1,400 | 0 |
| 2015 | 550 | 1,350 | 1,400 | 50 |
| 2016 | 700 | 1,425 | 1,450 | 25 |

With $n = 4$ and $\bar{x} = 625$:

$$\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 = 12{,}500 \qquad \sum x_i^2 = 1{,}575{,}000$$

$$s_b = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2 \times 12{,}500} \times (75^2 + 50^2 + 25^2)} = 0.592$$

$$s_a = 0.592 \times \sqrt{\frac{1{,}575{,}000}{4}} = 371.5$$

- $a = 1{,}075 > 2 \times s_a = 2 \times 371.5 = 743$, so $a$ is significant.
- $b = 0.5 < 2 \times s_b = 2 \times 0.592$, so $b$ is not significant.

The 12-24 loss development factor is not significant (the constant term is).

Assumption: the test validates whether incremental losses in the next period are proportional to the losses reported to date.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate sb, including all of the required components such as ε2 and (xi - x̅)2. They were expected to recognize that because the slope was insignificant (since it was about the same as the standard error) that the LDF was statistically insignificant. Additionally, they were expected to know the underlying assumption of the chain ladder method.
Common mistakes included:

- Not stating the significance conclusion.
- Not stating the assumption.
- Not taking the square root of sb2.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Discuss how the estimated future loss emergence for a year with abnormally high losses to date would differ under the chain ladder versus additive model.

### Explanation
Sample responses:

- Abnormally high losses to date would make chain ladder's estimate abnormally high since it is very reflective to losses to date. Additive model would not get impacted as much as C-L since the later years' future development for each period are independent and are not impacted by loss to date.
- C-L will assume future loss proportional to loss to date, so abnormally high loss to date will result in abnormally high loss for the next year. Additive model loss development will not be affected, as it is not linked to loss to date.

- Additive model would not be influenced by the reported to date at an unusually high year. Chain Ladder model would have future losses in proportion to the unusually high losses to date, so the estimated future losses would be high.
- Chain ladder applies a multiplicative factor to losses to date, whereas the additive model adds a flat amount to losses known to date. The CL method would imply that since losses to date have been high, future losses will also be high, so the resulting estimate will be leveraged up. The additive model will have the same estimate of future losses regardless of level of losses reported to date.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know that the future loss emergence for a year with abnormally high losses would be higher under a chain ladder methodology compared to an additive methodology. They were also expected to be able to explain why this was true.
Common mistakes included:

- Not providing any explanation as to why the conclusion was true.
