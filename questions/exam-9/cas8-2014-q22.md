The following information is given for a one-year reinsurance treaty effective January 1, 2012.

| Risk | Effective Date | Insured Value | Loss | Loss Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | June 1, 2011 | \$200,000 | \$100,000 | March 1, 2012 |
| 2 | January 1, 2012 | 500,000 | 400,000 | June 1, 2012 |
| 3 | June 1, 2012 | 1,500,000 | 1,500,000 | September 1, 2012 |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the reinsurer's loss liability for a 30% quota share agreement written on a risks attaching basis.

### Explanation
Only risks written in 2012 are included (risks 2 and 3):

$$\text{Reinsurer's loss} = 400{,}000 \times 0.3 + 1{,}500{,}000 \times 0.3 = 570{,}000$$

### Examiner Report
Most candidates received full credit on this subpart. The common error was including risk 1 in the calculation.

## Part b (1 point)

Calculate the reinsurer's loss liability for a 5-line surplus share agreement written on a losses occurring basis with a retained line of \$100,000.

### Explanation
On a losses occurring basis the treaty is based on loss date, so all claims are considered. Reinsurer's share = reinsurer's lines / insured value, with at most 5 lines of \$100,000 ceded:

- Share 1 = 100k / 200k = 50%
- Share 2 = 400k / 500k = 80%
- Share 3 = 500k / 1.5m = 33.33%

$$\text{Reinsurer's loss liability} = \text{share} \times \text{loss} = 50\% \times 100k + 80\% \times 400k + 33.33\% \times 1.5m = 870k$$

### Examiner Report
Like part a, most candidates received full credit on this subpart. Common errors were excluding risk 1 and calculating the ceded loss as excess of the insurer's one line with a \$500K maximum.

## Part c (1 point)

The primary insurance company purchased a treaty on a risks attaching basis for the time period from January 1, 2013 to December 31, 2013 time. They then decided to purchase the same treaty but on a losses occurring basis for the time period from January 1, 2014 to December 31, 2014.

Describe a coverage issue that could arise with these two treaties. Explain how the ceding company and reinsurer can structure the treaties to avoid this issue.

### Explanation
Coverage issue results when a policy written in 2013 (and still inforce in 2014) has a loss in 2014. Both treaties would cover the loss by definition. An interlocking clause can be introduced to assign the loss to a specific treaty.

### Examiner Report
This was the most difficult subpart for candidates as about half of the candidates received full credit. Most candidates were able to identify the problem; however the most common oversight was neglecting to provide a solution to how the ceding company and reinsurer can structure the existing treaties to avoid a coverage overlap.

Simply changing the type of treaty written (e.g. just use two loss occurring treaties) was not a valid response.

Other correct solutions to the overlap included:

- The reinsurer may add a clause in the 2014 treaty to exclude claims that would be covered by 2013 treaty.
- Commute the risks attaching policy at 1/1/14 and replace it with the loss occurring treaty.
