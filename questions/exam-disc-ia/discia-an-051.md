Two bodily injury claims involve the same injury, equally clear liability and the same medical bills. According to the NAIC's description of social inflation, which difference would most likely make one claim worth more than the other?

- A) One claimant's medical bills were paid by her health insurer
- B) One claimant reduced her losses by following her doctor's advice
- C) One case would go to a jury pool more distrustful of big companies
- D) One defendant carries a lower liability limit than the other

## Explanation

The NAIC notes that the varying demographic makeup of jury pools, public distrust of large corporations, and the influences of social media and legal marketing can all influence jury verdicts and awards, which is part of why liability claim costs rise faster than general inflation. Where a case would be tried changes what it is worth. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — under the traditional collateral source rule, health insurance payments do not reduce the award, so they do not separate the two claims.
- **B** — mitigation lowers recoverable damages; it does not raise the value.
- **D** — a lower limit caps what that insurer pays, not what the claim is worth against the defendant.
