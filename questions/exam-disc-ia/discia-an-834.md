An adjuster reviewing a CGL claim writes: "A customer's guest was injured by a product we sold. Exclusion k, damage to your product, applies, so the claim is denied." Which criticism of this coverage analysis is most accurate?

- A) The analysis is correct, because any claim involving the insured's product is excluded
- B) The analysis should have started with the limits of insurance, because limits decide coverage
- C) The analysis is wrong only because a guest of a customer is not a third party
- D) The analysis skipped the insuring agreement and misread the exclusion, which removes only damage to the product itself

## Explanation

A coverage analysis works through the policy in order: declarations, the insuring agreement and its definitions, the conditions, the exclusions (read to their end, including exceptions), the endorsements, and finally the amount payable. Here the guest's bodily injury caused by an occurrence fits the Coverage A insuring agreement, and exclusion k ("damage to your product") removes only *property damage to the insured's product arising out of it*. Bodily injury the product causes is not excluded. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** reads exclusion k as reaching everything connected with the product; the products-completed operations hazard is covered.
- **B** — limits set the amount payable once a loss is covered; they come last, not first.
- **C** — the guest is a member of the public, a third party; nothing in the analysis turns on this.
