Given the following information regarding an experience rating plan:

- Reported losses and ALAE limited by basic limits and maximum single limit per occurrence (MSL) for the policy being rated as of March 31, 2016 = \$175,000.
- Company subject basic limit loss and ALAE for experience period = \$225,000.
- Expected experience ratio = 0.875.
- Expected percentage basic limit loss and ALAE for experience period unreported at March 31, 2016 = 0.425.
- Credibility = 0.35.

## Part a (1 points)

Calculate the experience modification factor.

### Answer
1.110 (an 11.0% debit)

### Explanation
The experience modification compares the risk's own **actual experience ratio** to the **expected experience ratio**, credibility-weights the difference, and applies it to unity:

$$\text{Mod} = 1 + Z \times \frac{\text{AER} - \text{EER}}{\text{EER}}$$

**Step 1 — expected unreported loss and ALAE.** Reported losses as of March 31, 2016 are immature. The plan estimates what is still unreported from the company's subject basic limit losses, scaled by the expected experience ratio so that the estimate sits on the same basis as the risk's own expected losses:

$$\text{Expected unreported} = 225{,}000 \times 0.875 \times 0.425 = \$83{,}671.88$$

**Step 2 — actual experience ratio.** The numerator is the risk's projected ultimate basic limit loss and ALAE — what has been reported plus what is expected still to come — and the denominator is the company subject basic limit loss and ALAE:

$$\text{AER} = \frac{175{,}000 + 83{,}671.88}{225{,}000} = \frac{258{,}671.88}{225{,}000} = 1.1497$$

**Step 3 — modification factor.** With $Z = 0.35$ and $\text{EER} = 0.875$:

$$\text{Mod} = 1 + 0.35 \times \frac{1.1497 - 0.875}{0.875} = 1 + 0.35(0.31389) = 1.1099$$

$$\text{Mod} \approx \mathbf{1.110}$$

The risk's own experience is worse than expected — an actual ratio of 1.150 against an expected 0.875, roughly 31% adverse — but only 35% of that difference is credible, so the risk is debited about 11% rather than 31%.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the experience rating modification factor. Therefore, candidates had to calculate the expected unreported losses and ALAE, the projected ultimate losses and ALAE (reported plus unreported), the actual experience ratio, and the experience modification factor. Additionally, candidates needed to understand the proper application of schedule rating avoiding possible overlap with experience rating.

Candidates were expected to calculate the expected unreported losses and ALAE, the projected ultimate losses and ALAE (reported plus unreported), the actual experience ratio, and the experience modification factor.

Common errors included:

- Calculating expected unreported losses (as printed).
- Not applying the expected experience ratio for the expected unreported loss calculation.
- Not using the unreported percentage or using it solely as a development factor.
- Applying the factors to reported losses rather than basic limit loss.
- Applying credibility improperly (only to the actual experience ratio).

## Part b (0.25 points)

Briefly describe a scenario in which it would be appropriate for schedule rating to be used in addition to experience rating.

### Explanation
Schedule rating belongs where a risk characteristic is expected to have a material effect on future loss experience but is **not yet reflected in the historical experience** the experience rating plan uses.

For example, the insured has just installed a new loss control or safety program that has not been in place long enough to have influenced any of the losses in the experience period. Experience rating can only recognize the improvement once several years of post-implementation experience have entered the plan, so until then a judgmental schedule rating credit is the only way to give credit for it.

The condition matters: a safety program that has been in place for many years is already reflected in the experience, and crediting it again through schedule rating would double count the same characteristic.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand that schedule rating is for individual risk characteristics that are expected to have a material impact on the future loss experience but are not adequately reflected in the prior loss experience (or experience rating plan).

Common errors included:

- Providing a reason for schedule rating that had already been contemplated in the insured's experience. For example, stating "safety program" was not sufficient; while a new safety program would be incorporated through schedule rating, an insured with a safety program that had been in place for many years would have the safety program addressed through experience rating.
