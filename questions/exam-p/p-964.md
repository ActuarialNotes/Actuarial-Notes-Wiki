The number of claims $N$ filed by a randomly chosen policyholder is Poisson with mean $\Lambda$, where the risk parameter $\Lambda$ varies across the portfolio according to a uniform distribution on $(0, 4)$.

Calculate $\text{Var}(N)$.

- A) $2.00$
- B) $2.67$
- C) $3.33$
- D) $4.00$
- E) $5.33$

## Explanation

For a Poisson conditional distribution, $E[N \mid \Lambda] = \Lambda$ and $\text{Var}(N \mid \Lambda) = \Lambda$. For $\Lambda \sim \text{Uniform}(0,4)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\Lambda] &= \frac{0+4}{2} = 2 \\
\text{Var}(\Lambda) &= \frac{(4-0)^2}{12} = \frac{4}{3}
\end{align*}
$$

Apply the law of total variance:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(N) &= E\bigl[\text{Var}(N \mid \Lambda)\bigr] + \text{Var}\bigl(E[N \mid \Lambda]\bigr) \\
              &= E[\Lambda] + \text{Var}(\Lambda) \\
              &= 2 + \frac{4}{3} \\
              &= \mathbf{3.33}
\end{align*}
$$

Note $\text{Var}(N) = 3.33 > E[N] = 2$: mixing Poisson distributions over a random parameter makes claim counts **overdispersed** relative to any single Poisson. Choice (A) keeps only the within-group term $E[\Lambda] = 2$ and drops the between-group term $\text{Var}(\Lambda) = \tfrac{4}{3}$; both halves of the decomposition are needed.
