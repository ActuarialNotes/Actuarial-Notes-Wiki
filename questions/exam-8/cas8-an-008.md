A manufacturer's workers compensation policy is experience rated, and its experience modification for the coming year is 1.25. Which statement best describes what the modification says about this risk?

- A) Its expected losses next year are 25% above those of the average risk in its class
- B) Its actual losses over the experience period were 25% above the losses expected for it
- C) Its past losses exceeded expected, and the debit recovers those losses over the coming year
- D) It is a bad risk that the insurer should non-renew if the debit continues
- E) Its class rate is 25% inadequate and should be raised for every risk in the class

## Explanation

Fisher et al. (Chapter 1) define the experience modification as a factor applied to manual premium, with standard premium equal to the mod times manual premium. A mod above 1.0 (a debit mod) means the insured's experience is worse than average for its class. The mod measures the risk's **expected loss relative to other risks in its class**: how much its past experience predicts about its future loss potential. A mod of 1.25 estimates the risk's coming-year losses at 25% above its class average. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** confuses the mod with the ratio of actual to expected losses. The mod gives that ratio only partial credibility, so a 1.25 mod comes from an actual-to-expected ratio above 1.25 unless the risk is fully credible.
- **C** is the misconception Fisher et al. single out: experience rating is not a way to charge back past losses. It is a prospective estimate of loss potential.
- **D** contradicts the study note directly: a risk with a debit mod should not be viewed as a "bad" risk.
- **E** misreads the level. The mod adjusts one insured within its class; the class rate is set for the class's average risk.
