The following charts present the posterior distribution of $\theta$.

![Five copies of the same posterior density of theta on 0 to 1, each with a different region shaded. The density is piecewise linear: it rises from 0 at theta = 0 to a knee at theta = 0.2 (half the peak height), rises more gently to its peak at theta = 0.8, then falls steeply to 0 at theta = 1.0. Plot 1 shades from about 0.62 up to the peak at about 0.81. Plot 2 shades from about 0.47 to about 0.68. Plot 3 shades from about 0.35 to about 0.65. Plot 4 shades from about 0.75 to 1.0, taking in the whole falling right-hand side. Plot 5 shades from about 0.53 to about 0.83](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2018f-q26-hpdi-plots.png)

Determine which of the shaded regions in the above plots represents the 30% highest posterior density interval (HPDI).

- A) Plot 1
- B) Plot 2
- C) Plot 3
- D) Plot 4
- E) Plot 5

## Explanation

The 30% HPDI is the **narrowest** interval holding 30% of the posterior probability: every point inside it has higher density than every point outside. It must therefore contain the mode and reach out from it towards the side where the density falls off more slowly.

Reading the plots, the density rises from 0 at $\theta = 0$ to a knee at $\theta = 0.2$ that is half the peak height, rises more gently to the peak at $\theta = 0.8$, then drops steeply to 0 at $\theta = 1$. Call the knee $a$ and the peak $2a$; the area must be 1:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= \tfrac{1}{2}(0.2)(a) + \tfrac{1}{2}(0.6)(a + 2a) + \tfrac{1}{2}(0.2)(2a) \\
&= 1.2a \\
a &= 0.833
\end{align*}
$$

So $f(0.8) = 1.667$, and the density falls six times faster to the right of the mode ($-8.33$ per unit) than it rises to the left ($+1.39$ per unit). The HPDI therefore extends mostly to the left of 0.8.

- **Plots 2 and 3** leave out the mode, so points near 0.8 with higher density are excluded. Plot 2 is the central 30% interval, the 35th to 65th percentile ($\approx [0.46, 0.68]$).
- **Plot 4** includes the right tail, where the density falls to 0, while excluding points just left of 0.75 with density of about 1.6.
- **Plot 5** is centred correctly but too wide. On $[0.53, 0.83]$ the endpoint densities are 1.292 and 1.417, giving an area of $0.399 + 0.046 = 0.446$.

Plot 1 runs from about 0.62 to just past the mode at 0.81, where $f(0.62) = 1.417$ and $f(0.81) = 1.583$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Area} &= 0.18 \cdot \frac{1.417 + 1.667}{2} + 0.01 \cdot \frac{1.667 + 1.583}{2} \\
&= 0.2775 + 0.0163 \\
&= 0.294
\end{align*}
$$

It holds about 30% and sits on the highest part of the density. The answer is **A**.
