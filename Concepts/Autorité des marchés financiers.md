The **Autorité des marchés financiers** (AMF) is Quebec's integrated financial regulator, supervising insurance, securities, deposit institutions and distribution in a single body. Elsewhere in Canada these functions sit in separate agencies: [[OSFI]], a provincial superintendent and a securities commission.

The AMF's role for an insurer writing in Quebec depends on where the insurer is incorporated:

| Insurer | Prudential supervision | Role of the AMF |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Provincially chartered in Quebec | AMF | Prudential and market-conduct supervision |
| Federally incorporated | [[OSFI]] | Licenses it to operate in the province |

The AMF issues its own sound commercial practices and governance guidelines. They parallel OSFI's [[Corporate Governance]] and [[ORSA]] expectations but do not simply copy them.

## Quebec automobile insurance

Quebec auto insurance is a hybrid. Bodily injury is covered by the public, no-fault **SAAQ** scheme, funded by driver's licence and vehicle registration levies. Property damage is written by private insurers under the Direct Compensation agreement. A Quebec auto rate filing therefore concerns property damage and optional coverages only, which is one reason Quebec auto premiums are the lowest in Canada.

> [!example]- Average Quebec auto premiums are far below Ontario's. Does that show Quebec's private insurers are more efficient? {Example}
> - A Quebec private auto premium buys property damage and optional coverages. Bodily injury is covered by the SAAQ and paid for through licence and registration fees.
> - An Ontario private premium buys bodily injury tort exposure, [[Statutory Accident Benefits|accident benefits]] and property damage.
> - Bodily injury and accident benefits are the largest part of the Ontario premium, so the two premiums buy different products.
> - A fair comparison adds the SAAQ levies back to the Quebec figure, and even then the schemes differ in benefit generosity and access to [[Tort Litigation|tort]].
> - The comparison does not show that Quebec insurers are more efficient. Premiums from two jurisdictions are compared as cost per unit of coverage under a stated benefit regime.

## Insurance law in Quebec

The **Civil Code of Québec** governs the insurance contract, in place of a common-law *Insurance Act*. Quebec is a civil law jurisdiction, so a [[Court Case|court case]] from another province is persuasive at best, and doctrines such as [[Duty of Good Faith|good faith]] arise from articles of the Code rather than from case law.

Quebec has its own [[Guaranty Funds|guaranty fund]] arrangements. [[PACICC]] covers its members across Canada, and Quebec insurers participate.
