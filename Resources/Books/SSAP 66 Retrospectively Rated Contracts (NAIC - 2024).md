Statement of Statutory Accounting Principles No. 66 of the NAIC's Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual. It establishes statutory accounting principles for retrospectively rated contracts, which it applies to property and casualty, life insurance and accident and health contracts; retrospective reinsurance contracts are addressed in SSAP No. 62R.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objective D2; NAIC Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual, 2025, SSAP No. 66, paragraphs 1-5, 7.a., 8, 9.a. and 10-14, available in Appendix IV of the COPLFR P&C Practice Note (as the content outline states it)

## Scope of Statement
- The statement applies to property and casualty contracts, life insurance contracts and accident and health contracts.
- Retrospective reinsurance contracts are not within its scope and are addressed in SSAP No. 62R.

## Summary Conclusion
- A [[Retrospective Rating|retrospectively rated contract]] has a final policy premium calculated from the insured's loss experience during the policy term, including loss development after the term, under a formula in the policy or required by law; such contracts are also called loss sensitive contracts.
- Amounts due from and to insureds under retrospectively rated contracts meet the definitions of assets and liabilities in SSAP Nos. 4 and 5R and are admitted assets to the extent they conform to the statement.
- Retrospective premium adjustments are estimated from experience to date, for property and casualty contracts either by actuarially accepted methods under filed and approved plans (one net asset or liability) or by reviewing each retrospectively rated risk individually (an asset or liability for each risk).
- Assumptions used in estimating adjustments are consistent with those used for claim and loss reserves, including IBNR, and contingent commissions; contingent commissions and related expenses are adjusted in the same period.
- Estimated adjustments for the expired portion of the policy period are an immediate adjustment to premium; for property and casualty entities, accrued additional retrospective premiums are a receivable and accrued return premiums are part of the change in unearned premium liability.
