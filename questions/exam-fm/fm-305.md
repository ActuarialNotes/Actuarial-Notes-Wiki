A loan of 50,000 is based on an annual effective interest rate of 6%.

You are given:

- i) The loan is to be repaid with quarterly payments of 2125 and a final drop payment.
- ii) The first payment is due 1.5 years after the loan is taken out.

Calculate the number of payments of 2125 to be made.

- A) 28
- B) 29
- C) 30
- D) 31
- E) 32

## Explanation

The quarterly effective rate $j = i^{(4)}/4$ satisfies

$$1.06 = \left(1 + \frac{i^{(4)}}{4}\right)^4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad j = \frac{i^{(4)}}{4} = 1.06^{1/4} - 1 = 0.014674$$

The first payment is at 1.5 years, the end of quarter 6. Valued one quarter earlier, at the end of quarter 5, the payments form an annuity-immediate, so the loan is accumulated for **5** quarters:

$$
\begin{align*}
50{,}000(1.014674)^5 &= 2125\,a_{\overline{n}|0.014674} \\
53{,}777.71 &= 2125\,a_{\overline{n}|0.014674}
\end{align*}
$$

Solving for $n$:

$$n = \frac{-\ln\left(1 - \dfrac{53{,}777.71 \times 0.014674}{2125}\right)}{\ln 1.014674} = 31.86$$

So there are **31 payments** of 2125, followed by a smaller final drop payment. (Equivalently, accumulate 6 quarters to 54,566.84 and use an annuity-due, $2125\,\ddot{a}_{\overline{n}|}$, which gives the same $n$.)
