OSFI **Guideline B-9** (February 2013) on **earthquake exposure**. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objectives C1 and C5. Earthquake is the one peril in Canada capable of exhausting an insurer's capital in a single event, which is why it gets its own guideline and its own [[Earthquake Exposure Risk Margin|margin]] in the [[MCT]].

## The five key principles

1. **Earthquake exposure risk management.** Insurers should have a sound and comprehensive earthquake exposure risk management policy. For a foreign branch, OSFI looks to the **Chief Agent** to oversee it.
2. **Earthquake exposure data.** Data must be appropriately captured — location, construction, occupancy, height, and the coverage terms. Model output is only as good as the exposure data behind it, and incomplete data biases the PML *downward*.
3. **Earthquake models.** Models should be used with a sound knowledge of their limitations: the treatment of secondary perils (fire following, tsunami, liquefaction), demand surge, uncertainty in the hazard module, and the differences between vendor models.
4. **PML estimates.** A PML estimate should properly reflect the **total expected ultimate cost** to the insurer, Canada-wide — gross and net of reinsurance, and inclusive of loss adjustment expenses and reinstatement premiums.
5. **Financial resources and contingency plans.** The insurer must be able to meet the PML, from a defined stack:
   - **1. Capital and surplus**
   - **2. Earthquake reserves** — the earthquake premium reserve and earthquake reserve component
   - **3. Reinsurance coverage** — and its reinstatement
   - **4. Capital market financing** — catastrophe bonds and similar

A senior officer should **regularly report to senior management** the details of the insurer's earthquake exposure.

## Why the exam likes it

The financial-resources stack is directly examinable: given a PML and a set of resources, does the insurer have enough, and in what order do the resources respond? It also connects across the syllabus — the PML drives the [[MCT]] earthquake margin (§4.5); the exposure aggregation logic is the same one in [[OSFI Concentration]]; and the contingency plan is a [[FCT|financial condition testing]] scenario with [[Ripple Effect|ripple effects]] (reinstatement cost, capital depletion, rating downgrade).
