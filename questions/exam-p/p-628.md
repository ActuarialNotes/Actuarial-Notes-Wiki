In a given month, a group of policies are up for renewal. The random variable $R$ denotes the fraction of those policies that are actually renewed. $R$ has a probability density function of the form

$$
f(r) = \begin{cases}
(\alpha + 1)r^\alpha, & \text{for } 0 < r < 1 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $\alpha$ is a positive parameter. The expected value of $R$ is 0.75.

Calculate the probability that more than half of the policies up for renewal in a given month will actually be renewed.

- A) $0.50$
- B) $0.58$
- C) $0.70$
- D) $0.75$
- E) $0.88$

## Explanation

First use the mean to find $\alpha$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.75 &= \int_0^1 r\,(\alpha + 1)r^\alpha\,dr = \int_0^1 (\alpha + 1)r^{\alpha + 1}\,dr \\
     &= \left.\frac{\alpha + 1}{\alpha + 2}r^{\alpha + 2}\right|_0^1 \\
     &= \frac{\alpha + 1}{\alpha + 2}
\end{align*}
$$

Solving $0.75(\alpha + 2) = \alpha + 1$ gives $\alpha = 2$, so $f(r) = 3r^2$. Then

$$
\begin{align*}
P(R > 0.5) &= \int_{0.5}^{1} 3r^2\,dr \\
           &= \left. r^3 \right|_{0.5}^{1} \\
           &= 1 - 0.125 \\
           &= \mathbf{0.875}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 0.75, is the mean $E(R)$ handed back as if it were a probability. The two are different questions, and for this density they differ substantially: $f(r) = 3r^2$ piles mass toward $r = 1$, so the chance of clearing the halfway mark (0.875) is well above the average renewal fraction (0.75).
