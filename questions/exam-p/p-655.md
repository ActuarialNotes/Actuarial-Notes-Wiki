The number of earthquakes a homeowner experiences is Poisson distributed, at a constant rate of 2 for every 30 years.

Calculate the standard deviation of the number of earthquakes the homeowner experiences in the next ten years.

- A) $0.258$
- B) $0.471$
- C) $0.667$
- D) $0.816$
- E) $1.414$

## Explanation

The Poisson rate scales with the length of the exposure period. Over ten years,

$$
\lambda = \frac{2}{30}(10) = \frac{2}{3}
$$

For a Poisson distribution the variance equals the mean, so the variance over these ten years is also $2/3$, and

$$
\text{SD} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}} = \mathbf{0.816}
$$

Choice (C), 0.667, is $\lambda = 2/3$ itself — the mean and variance, reported without taking the square root. Because $\lambda < 1$ the root *increases* the value rather than shrinking it, so a standard deviation above the variance is exactly what to expect here.
