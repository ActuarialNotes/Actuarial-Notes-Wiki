Which statement best describes how risk treatment has evolved from the traditional approach to enterprise risk management?

- A) Insurance was abandoned, because ERM transfers hazard risks through the capital markets instead
- B) Treatment widened from insuring and controlling pure risks to managing all risks together, even exploiting some
- C) Loss control replaced retention, because ERM requires every risk to be reduced as far as possible
- D) Operational and strategic risks became transferable through standard insurance policies

## Explanation

The traditional risk manager handled pure risks, mainly by buying insurance and running loss control. Under ERM, all risks, hazard, financial, operational and strategic, are treated as one portfolio, natural hedges are recognized, and some risks are kept or pursued for competitive advantage. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Insurance remains the prevalent transfer mechanism for hazard risks; ERM adds other tools, it does not abandon insurance.
- **C** — ERM still uses retention, and it may choose to exploit a risk rather than reduce it.
- **D** — Operational and strategic risks have long lacked transfer markets, which is why they were mostly avoided or retained; ERM did not make them insurable.
