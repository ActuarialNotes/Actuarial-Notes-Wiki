## [F-001] Equation of value written with a(t_0)/a(t_k) without the compound-interest condition; contradicts SOA's simple-interest convention
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-09-28T14:47Z/aa33
- date: 2026-09-28
- severity: major
- status: open
- locus: formula block 'sum_k C_k^+ a(t_0)/a(t_k) = sum_k C_k^- a(t_0)/a(t_k)' (lines 22-24)
- claim: 'More generally, the Equation of Value at time t_0 equates all inflows and outflows: sum C_k^+ a(t_0)/a(t_k) = sum C_k^- a(t_0)/a(t_k)', for any accumulation function.
- evidence: SOA's notation note (NOTE p.2, read as page image) states: 'If an examination question specifies simple interest, the accumulation function for each cash flow is given by a(t) = 1 + ti, with t measured from the moment that cash flow occurs.' Finan Remark 4.3 (p.31) contrasts exactly this with the a(t)/a(s) approach of §2 (p.16): 'According to the SOA/CAS, simple interest is generally understood to mean that the linear function starts all over again from the date of each deposit or withdrawal.' SOA sample solution 112 (solutions PDF p.32) applies it: Gomer's deposit at time 3 accumulates by 'accumulation function from time 3 is 1 + yt', i.e. 1000(1 + 2y) at time 5, not 1000 a(5)/a(3). Recomputation: 100 deposited at t=1 at 10% simple, valued at t=3 — SOA convention 100(1 + 0.1*2) = 120.00; the page's a(t)/a(t_k) with a(t) = 1 + it (the form the linked [[Accumulation Function]] page lists for simple interest) gives 100(1.3/1.1) = 118.18. The formula is right for compound interest and any force of interest, but the page states it without that condition, and an FM student applying it to a simple-interest question gets a different number from SOA's. SYL Topic 1 b) (p.2) asks for these calculations 'using simple or compound interest', so the simple case is examinable.
- source_rank: 1
- proposed_action: Maintainer: state that the a(t_0)/a(t_k) form holds under compound interest / a force of interest, and that under simple interest each cash flow is moved by 1 + i|t_0 - t_k| from its own date (NOTE p.2).
