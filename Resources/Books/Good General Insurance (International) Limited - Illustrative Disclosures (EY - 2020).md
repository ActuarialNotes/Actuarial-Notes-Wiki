EY's illustrative disclosures meeting the requirements of IFRS 17 and IFRS 9 for groups of insurance contracts accounted for under the premium allocation approach (PAA). They are presented as extracts from the full financial statements of Good General Insurance (International) Limited, a fictitious non-life insurer in the fictitious country of Euroland, for the year ended 31 December 2023, with commentary explaining how the requirements of IFRS have been implemented; the figures come from a model of hypothetical transactions.

## Abbreviations and key

## Introduction
- The publication is not a full set of illustrative financial statements: it focuses on the new presentation and disclosure requirements of [[IFRS 17]] and IFRS 9 for a non-life entity applying the [[Premium Allocation Approach|PAA]], which Good General adopted for the annual period commencing 1 January 2023.
- Good General disaggregates its insurance disclosures by four major product lines: personal accident insurance, marine insurance, property insurance and liability reinsurance assumed (on a quota share basis, treaty and facultative).
- The disclosures in Note 11, the roll-forward of the net asset or liability for insurance and reinsurance contracts, require significantly more information than is currently presented and are expected to be one of the areas requiring most effort from preparers.
- The standards applied are those in issue at 30 June 2020, including the final amendments to IFRS 17 issued 25 June 2020.
- The preparation approach
    - The model includes onerous and non-onerous groups, some in a foreign currency, quota share [[Reinsurance Contracts Held|reinsurance held]] on marine business with a loss-recovery component on reinsured onerous contracts, profit commission on liability reinsurance treated as a non-distinct investment component, and assets for insurance acquisition cash flows.
    - Insurance finance income and expense is disaggregated between profit or loss and OCI for personal accident insurance only (IFRS 17.88).
- Key new disclosures since February 2019 publication
    - The update for the June 2020 amendments adds the accounting for assets for insurance acquisition cash flows and for a loss-recovery component on initial recognition of an onerous group of underlying contracts.

## Notes to the financial statements
- 1 Changes in accounting policies and disclosures
    - 1.1 New and amended standards and interpretations
- 2 Summary of significant accounting policies
    - 2.1 Insurance and reinsurance contracts classification
    - 2.2 Insurance and reinsurance contracts accounting treatment
    - 2.3 Financial assets
    - 2.4 Recognition of interest income
- 3 Insurance and financial risk
    - 3.1 Insurance risk
    - 3.2 Financial risk
- 4 Capital
    - 4.1 Capital management objectives, policies and approach
- 5 Significant judgements and estimates
    - 5.1 Insurance and reinsurance contracts
    - 5.2 Financial assets
- 6 Insurance service expense
    - The breakdown of [[Insurance Service Expenses|insurance service expenses]] by major product line for 2023: incurred claims and other expenses, amortisation of insurance acquisition cash flows, losses on onerous contracts and reversals of those losses, changes to liabilities for incurred claims, impairment and reversal of impairment of assets for insurance acquisition cash flows, and insurance acquisition cash flows recognised when incurred.
- 7 Total investment income and net insurance financial result
- 8 Equity and debt instruments at fair value through profit or loss
- 9 Debt instruments measured at fair value through other comprehensive income
- 10 Debt instruments measured at amortised cost
- 11 Insurance and reinsurance contracts
    - The breakdown of groups of insurance contracts issued and reinsurance contracts held in an asset position and in a liability position, by product line, for 2023 and 2022.
    - 11.1 Roll-forward of net asset or liability for insurance contracts issued showing the liability for remaining coverage and the liability for incurred claims
        - Disclosure is disaggregated by major product line, based on how the company is managed; the commentary notes that IFRS 17.95–96 require judgement on aggregation, by type of contract, geographical area or reportable segment.
        - 11.1.1 Personal accident insurance
        - 11.1.2 Marine insurance
        - 11.1.3 Property insurance
        - 11.1.4 Liability reinsurance issued
        - The roll-forwards show the [[Liability for Remaining Coverage|liability for remaining coverage]] (excluding the [[Loss Component|loss component]], and the loss component), the [[Liability for Incurred Claims|liability for incurred claims]] (estimates of the present value of future cash flows, and the risk adjustment) and assets for insurance acquisition cash flows; the expected timing of derecognition of assets for insurance acquisition cash flows is also disclosed in time bands.
    - 11.2 Roll-forward of net asset or liability for reinsurance contracts held showing the assets for remaining coverage and the amounts recoverable on incurred claims
        - 11.2.1 Marine insurance
        - The roll-forward has no loss component column, since reinsurance contracts held cannot be onerous, but includes a loss-recovery component column for amounts recognised where reinsurance covers onerous underlying contracts.
