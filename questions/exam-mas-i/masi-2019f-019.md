You are testing the following hypotheses about a random variable, $X$:

- $H_0$: $X$ is uniformly distributed on $[0,16]$
- $H_1$: $X$ is uniformly distributed on $[8,16]$

You used a single observation and the best critical region with power of 0.95 to evaluate this hypothesis.

Calculate the probability of a Type I error.

- A) Less than 0.40
- B) At least 0.40, but less than 0.50
- C) At least 0.50, but less than 0.60
- D) At least 0.60, but less than 0.70
- E) At least 0.70

## Explanation

By the Neyman–Pearson lemma, the best critical region consists of the points where the likelihood ratio $f_0(x)/f_1(x)$ is smallest. Here

$$
\frac{f_0(x)}{f_1(x)} =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1/16}{0} = \infty, & 0 \le x < 8 \\[2ex]
\dfrac{1/16}{1/8} = \dfrac{1}{2}, & 8 \le x \le 16
\end{cases}
$$

A point in $[0, 8)$ can never be in the best critical region: it adds Type I error without adding any power, since $H_1$ puts no probability there. So the region $C$ is a subset of $[8, 16]$, where the ratio is constant, and its size is fixed by the power requirement:

$$
\begin{align*}
\Pr(X \in C \mid H_1) &= \frac{\text{length}(C)}{8} \\
                      &= 0.95 \\
\text{length}(C) &= 7.6
\end{align*}
$$

The Type I error is the probability of that same region under $H_0$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\alpha &= \Pr(X \in C \mid H_0) \\
&= \frac{7.6}{16} \\
&= 0.475
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[0.40, 0.50)$, so the answer is **B**.
