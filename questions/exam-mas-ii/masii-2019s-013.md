The following study was conducted to examine the effect of three treatments on wood strength.

There are five wood planks. Each is cut into three pieces, and one of the three treatments is randomly assigned to each piece. The change in wood strength is measured and graphed as follows, where each line represents the measurements of the strength of a single plank:

![Profile plot of wood strength against Treatment X, Y and Z, with one line per plank: five roughly parallel upward-sloping lines separated vertically, the lowest starting near 8 and the highest near 16 at Treatment X and all ending between 12 and 21 at Treatment Z](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019s-q13-plank-profiles.png)

You are given the following observations from the graph:

I. Wood strength is not affected by treatment.
II. Variation between planks is noticeable, suggesting a random effect associated with treatment for each plank.
III. Variation between planks is noticeable, suggesting a random effect associated with the intercept for each plank.

Determine which of the preceding statements are true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II, and III
- E) The correct answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

Read the profile plot the way a mixed-model diagnostic is read — the *level* of each line is the plank's intercept, the *slope* of each line is the plank's treatment effect.

**I is false.** Every plank rises from Treatment X to Treatment Z. A consistent upward shift across all five planks is exactly the signature of a real treatment effect.

**II is false.** A random effect on *treatment* means a random slope: the lines would fan out or cross, with each plank responding to treatment differently. Here the lines are close to parallel, so there is little evidence that the treatment effect varies plank by plank.

**III is true.** The lines are clearly separated vertically — some planks are simply stronger than others at every treatment. That plank-to-plank level shift is a random effect on the intercept.

Only statement III, so the answer is **C**.
