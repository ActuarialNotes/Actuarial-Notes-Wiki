A restaurant owner takes out a 120-month loan of 10,000 today. To pay off the loan, the owner will make end-of-month payments of $P$ for each of the first 30 months, $1.1P$ for each of the next 40 months, and $1.2P$ for each of the final 50 months.

The monthly payments are based on an annual nominal interest rate of 6% compounded monthly.

Determine which one of the following is an equation of value that can be used to calculate $P$.

- A) $10{,}000 = P\ddot{a}_{\overline{30}|0.005} + 1.1P\ddot{a}_{\overline{40}|0.005} + 1.2P\ddot{a}_{\overline{50}|0.005}$
- B) $10{,}000 = Pa_{\overline{30}|0.005} + 1.1Pa_{\overline{40}|0.005} + 1.2Pa_{\overline{50}|0.005}$
- C) $10{,}000 = Pa_{\overline{30}|0.005} + (1.005)^{-30}\left(1.1Pa_{\overline{40}|0.005}\right) + (1.005)^{-40}\left(1.2Pa_{\overline{50}|0.005}\right)$
- D) $10{,}000 = P\ddot{a}_{\overline{30}|0.005} + (1.005)^{-30}\left(1.1P\ddot{a}_{\overline{40}|0.005}\right) + (1.005)^{-70}\left(1.2P\ddot{a}_{\overline{50}|0.005}\right)$
- E) $10{,}000 = Pa_{\overline{30}|0.005} + (1.005)^{-30}\left(1.1Pa_{\overline{40}|0.005}\right) + (1.005)^{-70}\left(1.2Pa_{\overline{50}|0.005}\right)$

## Explanation

Since the annual nominal interest rate is 6%, compounded monthly, the monthly effective interest rate is $\dfrac{6\%}{12} = 0.5\%$ and the monthly discount factor is $\dfrac{1}{1.005}$.

The payments are made at the end of each month, so each block of payments is an annuity-immediate, valued one month before its first payment and then discounted to today. The 10,000 borrowed today is the present value of a 30-month annuity-immediate with monthly payments of $P$ each, followed by a 40-month annuity-immediate with monthly payments of $1.1P$ deferred by 30 months, followed by a 50-month annuity-immediate with monthly payments of $1.2P$ deferred by $30 + 40 = 70$ months. Therefore,

$$10{,}000 = Pa_{\overline{30}|0.005} + (1.005)^{-30}\left(1.1Pa_{\overline{40}|0.005}\right) + (1.005)^{-70}\left(1.2Pa_{\overline{50}|0.005}\right)$$
