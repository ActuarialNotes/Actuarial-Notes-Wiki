A family-owned manufacturer's risk manager shows that retaining its product liability exposure would cost less, on average, than the premium for insuring it. The owners decide to keep buying the insurance anyway, because a single large verdict would worry them constantly and could force the company to close. Which risk management objectives outweigh economy in this decision?

- A) Reduction of anxiety and survival
- B) Externally imposed obligations and continued growth
- C) Stability of earnings and social responsibility
- D) Economy and continued operation

## Explanation

The owners accept a higher expected cost, which goes against **economy**, for two reasons. The constant worry about a large verdict is the pre-loss objective of **reduction of anxiety**, and the risk that one verdict could close the company is the post-loss objective of **survival**. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — No law or contract is said to require the coverage, so externally imposed obligations are not the reason, and growth is not mentioned.
- **C** — Stability of earnings and social responsibility could be served by insurance, but the stated reasons are worry and the chance of closing, not steady earnings or the effect on others.
- **D** — Economy is the objective the decision overrides, since retention was cheaper on average; it cannot be one of the objectives that outweighs itself.
