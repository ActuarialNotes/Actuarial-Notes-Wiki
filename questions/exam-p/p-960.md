Ground-up losses in the current year follow an exponential distribution with mean 1000. All losses are subject to an ordinary deductible of 500.

Next year every loss is expected to increase by 10%, while the deductible stays at 500.

Calculate the percentage increase in the insurer's expected payment per loss.

- A) $10.0\%$
- B) $11.0\%$
- C) $12.5\%$
- D) $15.1\%$
- E) $18.2\%$

## Explanation

For an exponential loss with mean $\theta$ and ordinary deductible $d$, the expected payment per loss is $E[(X-d)_+] = \theta e^{-d/\theta}$.

**Current year** ($\theta = 1000$):

$$
\begin{align*}
E[(X - 500)_+] &= 1000\,e^{-0.5} \\
               &= 1000(0.606531) \\
               &= 606.53
\end{align*}
$$

**Next year.** Scaling an exponential scales its mean, so $X' = 1.1X$ is exponential with $\theta' = 1100$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[(X' - 500)_+] &= 1100\,e^{-500/1100} \\
                &= 1100\,e^{-0.454545} \\
                &= 1100(0.634736) \\
                &= 698.21
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Increase} &= \frac{698.21 - 606.53}{606.53} \\
                &= \mathbf{15.1\%}
\end{align*}
$$

A 10% increase in losses drives a 15.1% increase in insurer payments. This is the **leveraged impact of inflation**: because the deductible is a fixed dollar amount, it absorbs a shrinking share of each inflated loss, so the insurer's layer grows faster than the loss itself. Answering 10% (choice A) is the standard error.
