For each of the following stakeholders, describe the importance of having accurate unpaid claim estimates:

i. Internal Management
ii. Investors
iii. Regulators

## Part a (0.5 point)

i. Internal Management

### Explanation

Management uses the unpaid claim estimate to drive pricing, underwriting and strategic decisions. If reserves are understated, the indicated rate level looks better than it is and management continues to write business at inadequate rates until the deficiency emerges — by which point the damage is done. If reserves are overstated, management may over-correct by raising rates or tightening underwriting guidelines, losing profitable business it did not need to shed. Accurate estimates also underpin decisions about how much reinsurance to buy and where to deploy capital.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to know the importance of accurate estimates of unpaid claims and how under-reserving or over-reserving could impact different aspects of a company.

In some cases, candidates did not receive full credit based upon the amount of description provided.  Full credit was awarded for situation where two briefly describe items were provided.

Candidates were expected to know how under-reserving or over-reserving could impact internal management.

Common mistakes include:

- Providing one brief point when the question prompt specified describe

## Part b (0.5 point)

ii. Investors

### Explanation

Investors rely on the financial statements to judge the company's earnings and financial strength, and the unpaid claim estimate is the largest liability on a P&C insurer's balance sheet. An inaccurate estimate distorts reported income and surplus, making the company look stronger or weaker than it truly is, which in turn misprices the stock and misdirects capital. Investors also use reserve levels in assessing what dividends the company can sustainably pay.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to know how under-reserving or over-reserving could impact investors.

A common mistake was providing one brief point when the question prompt specified describe.

## Part c (0.5 point)

iii. Regulators

### Explanation

The regulator's principal concern is solvency — protecting policyholders by identifying troubled insurers early enough to intervene. Understated reserves overstate surplus and mask a weakening financial position, so the regulator may not act until the insurer is already insolvent and the guaranty fund must absorb the shortfall. Accurate estimates are also needed for the regulator's review of rate filings, since the indicated rate level depends directly on the estimated ultimate claim costs.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to know how under-reserving or over-reserving could impact regulators.

Common mistakes include:

- Providing one brief point when the question prompt specified describe
- Specifying that regulators would use the information to determine the appropriate reserve level
