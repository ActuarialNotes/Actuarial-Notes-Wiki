In a group of 35 patients, 9 have high blood pressure, 9 have high cholesterol, and 15 have high blood pressure or high cholesterol.

From these 35 patients, a doctor needs to select a group of 8 patients for a test in which exactly 4 have high blood pressure only, exactly 1 has high cholesterol only, and exactly 2 have both high blood pressure and high cholesterol.

Calculate the number of different groups the doctor can select for this test.

- A) $90$
- B) $270$
- C) $5{,}400$
- D) $47{,}628$
- E) $238{,}140$

## Explanation

First split the 35 patients into the four disjoint categories the selection refers to. The addition rule gives the overlap:

$$
\begin{align*}
|B \cap C| &= |B| + |C| - |B \cup C| \\
           &= 9 + 9 - 15 = 3
\end{align*}
$$

so the four groups are

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{both} &= 3 \\
\text{blood pressure only} &= 9 - 3 = 6 \\
\text{cholesterol only} &= 9 - 3 = 6 \\
\text{neither} &= 35 - 15 = 20
\end{align*}
$$

The specification names 4 + 1 + 2 = 7 patients, but the group has **8** — so one patient must come from the "neither" category. That implicit slot is the crux of the question.

Multiplying the independent choices:

$$
\begin{align*}
\binom{6}{4}\binom{6}{1}\binom{3}{2}\binom{20}{1} &= (15)(6)(3)(20) \\
                                                  &= \mathbf{5400}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 90, is $\binom{6}{4}\binom{6}{1} = 90$ — stopping partway. Omitting the $\binom{20}{1}$ factor gives 270, which is choice (B): that is the count for a group of seven, and it is what you get by never noticing the eighth patient.
