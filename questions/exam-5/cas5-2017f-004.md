Given the following information for an insurance company:

- All policies are annual.
- For all claims reported in one year, 40% of the ultimate loss is from claims occurring in the same year, 35% from the prior year and 25% from the 2nd prior year.
- Annual report year loss cost trend = 3%.
- The company writes policies uniformly through the year.
- Exposure levels are constant.
- For report year 2013, the loss cost per exposure from claims occurring in 2013 = \$200.

## Part a (1 points)

Calculate the loss cost per exposure for the following:

i. Occurrence policy effective January 1, 2016

ii. Claims-made policy effective January 1, 2018 with retroactive date of January 1, 2017

### Answer
i. \$560.42; ii. \$434.73

### Explanation
**Setting up the report year / report lag grid.** The \$200 given is only the *lag 0* piece of report year 2013 — the claims reported in 2013 that also occurred in 2013 — which is 40% of that report year's total:

$$\text{RY2013 total} = \frac{200}{0.40} = \$500$$

Splitting by the given weights, the three report-lag components of RY2013 are

$$\text{lag } 0 = 0.40(500) = 200, \qquad \text{lag } 1 = 0.35(500) = 175, \qquad \text{lag } 2 = 0.25(500) = 125$$

Loss costs trend at 3% per **report** year, so the cost of the claims reported in year $RY$ at lag $\ell$ is

$$C(RY, \ell) = \text{(lag } \ell \text{ base)} \times 1.03^{\,RY - 2013}$$

**i. Occurrence policy effective January 1, 2016.** An occurrence policy covers claims that *occur* during the policy period, no matter when they are reported. Claims occurring in 2016 surface across three report years — at lag 0 in 2016, lag 1 in 2017 and lag 2 in 2018:

| Occurring | Reported | Lag | Loss cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 2016 | 0 | $200 \times 1.03^{3} = 218.55$ |
| 2016 | 2017 | 1 | $175 \times 1.03^{4} = 196.96$ |
| 2016 | 2018 | 2 | $125 \times 1.03^{5} = 144.91$ |

$$\text{Occurrence loss cost} = 218.55 + 196.96 + 144.91 = \$560.42$$

**ii. Claims-made policy effective January 1, 2018, retroactive date January 1, 2017.** A claims-made policy covers claims *reported* during the policy period — so all of report year 2018 — but the retroactive date excludes claims that occurred before it. Of the three lags reported in 2018, the lag 2 component occurred in **2016**, before the retroactive date, and is not covered:

| Reported | Lag | Occurring | Covered? | Loss cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 0 | 2018 | Yes | $200 \times 1.03^{5} = 231.85$ |
| 2018 | 1 | 2017 | Yes | $175 \times 1.03^{5} = 202.87$ |
| 2018 | 2 | 2016 | **No** — before the retroactive date | — |

$$\text{Claims-made loss cost} = 231.85 + 202.87 = \$434.73$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to aggregate loss costs from report year information for an occurrence policy, calculate a claims-made loss cost, and understand how a retroactive date restricts coverage. Common errors included trending incorrectly for the time period or the reporting lag; failing to work out the reporting lag correctly across occurrence periods; and failing to recognize that the loss cost given in the problem covered only one occurrence year *and* one report year, so the full report year cost had to be grossed up from it first.

## Part b (0.5 points)

A customer is switching from a claims-made policy to an occurrence policy effective January 1, 2016. Calculate the total loss cost per exposure that would provide complete coverage without overlap for this customer.

### Answer
\$1,028.93

### Explanation
The switch leaves a **gap**, and the gap is what tail coverage fills.

- The expiring **claims-made** policy covered claims *reported* through 12/31/2015. It pays nothing on a claim reported later, whenever it occurred.
- The new **occurrence** policy covers claims *occurring* from 1/1/2016 onward. It pays nothing on a claim that occurred earlier, whenever it is reported.

Uncovered, therefore, are claims that **occurred before 1/1/2016 but are reported after 12/31/2015** — the tail. Given a maximum report lag of two years, those are:

| Occurring | Reported | Lag | Loss cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 2016 | 1 | $175 \times 1.03^{3} = 191.23$ |
| 2014 | 2016 | 2 | $125 \times 1.03^{3} = 136.59$ |
| 2015 | 2017 | 2 | $125 \times 1.03^{4} = 140.69$ |

$$\text{Tail} = 191.23 + 136.59 + 140.69 = \$468.51$$

Complete coverage without overlap is the new occurrence policy from part a(i) plus that tail:

$$560.42 + 468.51 = \$1{,}028.93$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to organize the data by occurrence and report year, trend the loss costs to the right periods, and price coverage across differing policy characteristics. Common errors included miscalculating the tail portion of the coverage, and failing to recognize that complete coverage consists of the occurrence policy *plus* tail coverage rather than the occurrence policy alone.
