Given the following information as of December 31, 2017:

**Cumulative Reported Claims Gross of Reinsurance (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 200 | 400 | 600 | 720 |
| 2015 | 240 | 480 | 720 |  |
| 2016 | 300 | 600 |  |  |
| 2017 | 350 |  |  |  |

|  |  |
|---|---|
| 1.10 | 48-ultimate reported claim development factor |
| 20% | Percentage ceded by the company under a quota share treaty for each accident year |

| Accident Year | Stop Loss Attachment Point (\$000) |
|---|---|
| 2014 | 700 |
| 2015 | 750 |
| 2016 | 900 |
| 2017 | 1,000 |

- The aggregate stop loss treaty is applied after the quota share for each year.
- The stop loss treaty covers all losses above the attachment point.

## Part a (1.25 points)

Estimate the ultimate claims net of all reinsurance for accident years 2014 through 2017 using the development technique.

### Explanation
**Gross development** (volume-weighted factors):

$$\text{12–24: } \frac{1{,}480}{740} = 2.000 \qquad \text{24–36: } \frac{1{,}320}{880} = 1.500 \qquad \text{36–48: } \frac{720}{600} = 1.200 \qquad \text{48–ult: } 1.100$$

**Net of reinsurance.** The 20% quota share applies first, then the aggregate stop loss caps each year's net claims at its attachment point:

| AY | Reported | CDF | Gross ultimate | After quota share (× 0.8) | Stop loss attachment | Net ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 720 | 1.100 | 792.0 | 633.6 | 700 | 633.6 |
| 2015 | 720 | 1.320 | 950.4 | 760.3 | 750 | 750.0 |
| 2016 | 600 | 1.980 | 1,188.0 | 950.4 | 900 | 900.0 |
| 2017 | 350 | 3.960 | 1,386.0 | 1,108.8 | 1,000 | 1,000.0 |

Net ultimate claims (\$000): 633.6, 750, 900 and 1,000 for 2014–2017.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge in estimating ultimate claims using the development technique, application of quota share and stop loss reinsurance arrangements, and the impact reinsurance can have on claim development patterns.

Candidates were expected to develop claims to ultimate for each accident year given a reported claims triangle using the development technique, net of the described quota share and aggregate stop loss reinsurance treaties.

Common errors included:

- Failing to apply either the quota share or stop loss reinsurance treaty.
- Developing claims to ultimate using age-to-age factors rather than age-to-ultimate.
- Not including the given tail factor within claim development calculations.
- Calculating the estimated ultimate from the point of view of the reinsurer.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Calculate the ceded IBNR for accident year 2017.

### Explanation
$$\text{Gross IBNR} = 1{,}386.0 - 350 = 1{,}036.0$$

Net reported claims are 350 × 0.8 = 280 (below the attachment point), so

$$\text{Net IBNR} = 1{,}000 - 280 = 720 \qquad \text{Ceded IBNR} = 1{,}036.0 - 720 = 316.0$$

That is 207.2 ceded to the quota share (20% × 1,036) plus 108.8 to the stop loss (1,108.8 - 1,000).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate ceded IBNR for accident year 2017.

Common errors included:

- Calculating the ceded ultimate rather than ceded IBNR.
- Only calculating the ceded IBNR for one of the reinsurance treaties.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe the relationship between gross and net tail factors for each of the following reinsurance arrangements:

- i. Stop Loss
- ii. Quota Share

### Explanation
- **i. Stop loss:** the net tail factor is smaller than the gross tail factor. Once a year's net claims reach the attachment point, further development is ceded, so net claims stop developing (or develop less) while gross claims keep developing.
- **ii. Quota share:** the net tail factor equals the gross tail factor. A quota share cedes the same proportion of every claim at every maturity, so net claims develop exactly as gross claims do.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of how different reinsurance arrangements would impact claims development factors.

Common errors included:

- Stating that the net tail factor will be greater than the gross tail factor in the presence of stop loss reinsurance, when the opposite is true.
- For stop loss, stating that the net tail factor will equal 1.0. This is not appropriate since the net tail factor would only be 1.0 if claims are guaranteed to hit the stop loss attachment prior to the oldest maturity date in the reported claims triangle. Since claims may not hit the attachment point until maturities beyond that of the latest age in the triangle, a tail factor above 1.0 would be appropriate to reflect this. While the net tail factor will typically be smaller than the gross tail factor, stating that the net tail factor will always equal 1.0 is incorrect.
- For stop loss, describing the expected impact to development of ceded claims from the perspective of the reinsurer, rather than expected impact to development of net claims from the perspective of the insurer.
- Stating that the net tail factor in the presence of quota share reinsurance is obtained by adjusting the gross tail factor based on the percentage of claims being ceded under the treaty.
- Vagueness in response, failing to clearly distinguish between gross and net.
