An insurance company has determined that the number of auto claims in a year is modeled by a Poisson random variable, $X$. The probability that there is at least one claim in a year is 0.368.

Calculate the second moment of $X$.

- A) $0.211$
- B) $0.421$
- C) $0.459$
- D) $0.669$
- E) $1.000$

## Explanation

Take the complement to reach the pmf at zero, which is where $\lambda$ is easiest to extract:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X = 0] &= 1 - 0.368 = 0.632 = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^0}{0!} = e^{-\lambda} \\
\lambda &= -\ln(0.632) = 0.4589
\end{align*}
$$

For a Poisson distribution both the mean and the variance are $\lambda$, so

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= \text{Var}(X) + \left[E(X)\right]^2 = \lambda + \lambda^2 \\
       &= 0.4589 + 0.2106 \\
       &= \mathbf{0.6694}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 0.459, is $\lambda$ itself — the mean and the variance, but not the second moment about the origin. Choice (A), 0.211, is $\lambda^2$, the other half of the sum. Both halves are needed: $E[X^2] = \text{Var}(X) + \left[E(X)\right]^2$ collapses to $\lambda + \lambda^2$ only because the Poisson's mean and variance coincide.
