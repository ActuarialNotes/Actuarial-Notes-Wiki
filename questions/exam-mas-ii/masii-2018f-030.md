You are given the following procedures:

- I. Gibbs algorithm
- II. Metropolis-Hastings algorithm
- III. Restricted Maximum Likelihood

Determine which of the above procedures can be used to update posterior distributions.

- A) None can be used
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I can be used.** Gibbs sampling is a Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm. It draws each parameter in turn from its full conditional posterior distribution (often available in closed form through conjugate priors), and the resulting chain samples from the joint posterior.

**II can be used.** Metropolis-Hastings proposes a move and accepts it with a probability based on the ratio of the posterior densities at the proposed and current points. It needs the posterior only up to a constant — likelihood times prior — which makes it the general-purpose posterior sampler. Gibbs sampling is a special case of it.

**III cannot be used.** Restricted maximum likelihood is a frequentist method for estimating the variance components of a linear mixed model. It maximises the likelihood of the residuals left after removing the fixed effects, avoiding the downward bias of ordinary ML variance estimates. It produces point estimates. There is no prior and no posterior.

I and II only, so the answer is **B**.
