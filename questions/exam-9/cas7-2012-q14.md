The Chief Risk Officer for an insurance company is considering U.S. Treasury interest rate models for the company's Enterprise Risk Management process.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Provide the formula for the Vasicek model and briefly describe the process it models.

### Explanation
$$dr_t = (b - a r_t)\,dt + s\,dw_t$$

This is the form of the Vasicek model. It models a mean-reverting Brownian motion process for the (continuous) short rate $r$, where $b$ is the mean term and $a$ corresponds to the autocorrelation; the long-run mean of the rate is $b/a$.

### Examiner Report
For the a. part, in addition to the formula, a brief description of the Vasicek model using at least 2 key words such as "mean reverting", "Brownian motion", "short rate", "continuous" was required to receive full credit.  It was not sufficient to only say that the Vasicek Model models US treasury interest rates since this is defined in the problem.

Some candidates used alternative letters for r, a, b, etc. when defining the formula.  Full credit was provided as long as the formula was correct.
Common errors included:

- Calling the Vasicek model a discrete distribution rather than a continuous distribution
- Not including a dt in the first term (the dt shows that this is a continuous distribution, which is a key point in the model)
- Using r in the second term
- Using a(b-rt)dt as the first term, as the b term is not multiplied by a
- Saying that the model measured interest rates, as this was defined in the problem
- Using just 1 key word in the description

## Part b (0.5 points)

Describe the key difference between the Cox, Ingersoll, Ross (CIR) model and the Vasicek model.

### Explanation
The CIR model is

$$dr_t = (b - a r_t)\,dt + s\sqrt{r_t}\,dw_t$$

The key difference is the $\sqrt{r}$ term in the volatility: it makes the variation depend on the level of the rate, so CIR cannot produce negative rates, and volatility increases with the rate, which is seen empirically.

### Examiner Report
For the b. part, the key difference is the addition of the square root of r term in the formula which does not allow the CIR model to produce negative rates.  Partial credit was given for a number of alternative differences between the CIR model and the Vasicek model, as long as the difference was accurate.
One common error was that candidates took the square root of s rather than r.

## Part c (0.75 points)

Provide and support a recommendation as to which of the two models, Vasicek or CIR, is more appropriate to use in the current environment of historically low interest rates.

### Explanation
Recommend the CIR model. Because the square root of the rate is used in the variability term, volatility is linked to the rate: in the current environment of low interest rates there is limited volatility. For the same reason CIR rates can never be negative — important when rates are historically low, since the Vasicek model could otherwise produce negative rates.

Alternatively:

CIR is more appropriate as it is the more robust model. Because interest rates are so low, there is an increased risk of Vasicek producing a negative rate, which is impossible; CIR doesn't have this issue.

### Examiner Report
For the c. part, candidates must include discussion about the low interest rate environment when selecting the appropriate model.  Only partial credit was awarded if the low interest rate environment was not discussed.
