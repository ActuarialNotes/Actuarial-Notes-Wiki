An insured has two primary liability policies covering the same \$1,200,000 loss: Policy X with a \$500,000 limit (an ISO CGL) and Policy Y with a \$1,500,000 limit. Both permit contribution by equal shares. How much does Policy X pay?

- A) \$300,000
- B) \$500,000
- C) \$600,000
- D) \$700,000

## Explanation

Under the CGL's method of sharing, if all the other insurance permits contribution by equal shares, each insurer contributes equal amounts until it has paid its limit or none of the loss remains. Half the loss would be \$600,000, but Policy X stops at its limit:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Share}_X &= \min\left(\frac{1{,}200{,}000}{2},\ 500{,}000\right) \\
&= 500{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

Policy Y pays the remaining \$700,000. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** uses contribution by limits (one quarter of \$1,200,000, since \$500,000 is a quarter of the \$2,000,000 of total limits), which applies only if some other insurance does not permit equal shares.
- **C** splits the loss in half without stopping at Policy X's limit.
- **D** is Policy Y's share.
