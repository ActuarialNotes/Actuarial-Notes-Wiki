The time in days, $X$, that an individual suffers from a common cold is continuous and uniformly distributed on $[a, b]$. The mean of $X$ is 8.50, and the variance of $X$ is 0.75.

Calculate the probability that a person who has suffered from a common cold for the past 7.5 days will continue to suffer from that cold for at least an additional 1.5 days.

- A) $0.33$
- B) $0.40$
- C) $0.50$
- D) $0.60$
- E) $0.83$

## Explanation

The mean and variance of a uniform on $[a, b]$ pin down the endpoints:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{a + b}{2} &= 8.5 \implies a = 17 - b \\
\frac{(b - a)^2}{12} &= 0.75 \implies (b - a)^2 = 9 \implies b - a = 3
\end{align*}
$$

Substituting, $b - (17 - b) = 3$, so $2b = 20$, $b = 10$ and $a = 7$.

The person has already suffered 7.5 days and asks about lasting to at least 9 days. Conditional probability for a uniform is a ratio of interval lengths:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 9 \mid X > 7.5] &= \frac{(10 - 9)/(10 - 7)}{(10 - 7.5)/(10 - 7)} \\
                      &= \frac{1.0}{2.5} \\
                      &= \mathbf{0.40}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.33, is the unconditional $P[X > 9] = 1/3$, which ignores the 7.5 days already elapsed. Unlike the exponential, the uniform is not memoryless: knowing the cold has lasted 7.5 days shrinks the remaining support from $[7, 10]$ to $[7.5, 10]$ and raises the chance of reaching day 9.
