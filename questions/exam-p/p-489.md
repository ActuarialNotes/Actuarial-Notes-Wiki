The amount of loss under an insurance policy is uniformly distributed on $[10, 60]$. The insurer will reimburse the full amount of the loss up to a maximum benefit. The expected benefit per loss is equal to 31.

Calculate the maximum benefit under the policy.

- A) $35$
- B) $38$
- C) $40$
- D) $52$
- E) $56$

## Explanation

Let $b$ be the maximum benefit. The payment is $\min(X, b)$: the full loss when $X \leq b$, and the cap $b$ when $X > b$. The loss is uniform on $[10,60]$, so its density is $1/50$.

Split the expectation at $b$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\min(X,b)] &= \int_{10}^{b}\frac{x}{50}\,dx + \int_{b}^{60}\frac{b}{50}\,dx \\
             &= \frac{b^2 - 100}{100} + b\left(\frac{60-b}{50}\right) \\
             &= \frac{-b^2 + 120b - 100}{100}
\end{align*}
$$

Setting that equal to 31:

$$
\begin{align*}
3100 &= -b^2 + 120b - 100 \\
b^2 - 120b + 3200 &= 0 \\
(b-40)(b-80) &= 0
\end{align*}
$$

The root $b = 80$ lies above the largest possible loss of 60, where the cap would be inactive and the expected benefit would just be the mean 35. So the only feasible answer is

$$
b = \mathbf{40}
$$

Checking the discarded root against the support is the essential step. Choice (A), 35, is the uncapped mean $E[X] = (10 + 60)/2$. The cap can only pull the *expected benefit* below that mean (here to 31); the maximum benefit itself must be at least 31, since $E[\min(X, b)] \leq b$.
