A random variable $X$ follows a binomial distribution:

$$
X \sim \text{Binomial}(n, \theta)
$$

The prior probability distribution of $\theta$ is:

$$
p(\theta) = 2\cos^2(4\pi\theta)
$$

An actuary uses the Metropolis algorithm to sample from the posterior distribution of $\theta$. At each iteration of the algorithm let $\theta_{curr}$ and $\theta_{prop}$ denote the current and proposed value of $\theta$.

Determine the acceptance probability of $\theta_{prop}$.

- A) $\min\left\{\dfrac{\theta_{curr}^{x}(1-\theta_{curr})^{n-x}}{\theta_{prop}^{x}(1-\theta_{prop})^{n-x}},\, 1\right\}$
- B) $\min\left\{\dfrac{\theta_{prop}^{x}(1-\theta_{prop})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{prop})}{\theta_{curr}^{x}(1-\theta_{curr})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{curr})},\, 1\right\}$
- C) $\min\left\{\dfrac{\theta_{curr}^{x}(1-\theta_{curr})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{curr})}{\theta_{prop}^{x}(1-\theta_{prop})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{prop})},\, 1\right\}$
- D) $\min\left\{\dfrac{\theta_{prop}^{x}(1-\theta_{prop})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{curr})}{\theta_{curr}^{x}(1-\theta_{curr})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{prop})},\, 1\right\}$
- E) $\min\left\{\dfrac{\theta_{curr}^{x}(1-\theta_{curr})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{prop})}{\theta_{prop}^{x}(1-\theta_{prop})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{curr})},\, 1\right\}$

## Explanation

The **Metropolis** algorithm (as opposed to Metropolis-Hastings) uses a symmetric proposal, so the proposal densities cancel and the acceptance probability is the ratio of the unnormalized posterior at the proposed value to that at the current value:

$$
\alpha = \min\left\{\frac{L(\theta_{prop})\,p(\theta_{prop})}{L(\theta_{curr})\,p(\theta_{curr})},\ 1\right\}
$$

The binomial likelihood contributes $\theta^{x}(1-\theta)^{n-x}$ (the $\binom{n}{x}$ cancels) and the prior contributes $2\cos^2(4\pi\theta)$ (the factor 2 cancels):

$$
\alpha = \min\left\{\frac{\theta_{prop}^{x}(1-\theta_{prop})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{prop})}{\theta_{curr}^{x}(1-\theta_{curr})^{n-x}\cos^2(4\pi\theta_{curr})},\ 1\right\}
$$

Two checks dispose of the distractors: the **proposed** value belongs in the numerator (ruling out A, C and E), and each $\theta$ must appear consistently — likelihood and prior evaluated at the *same* point (ruling out D, which mismatches them). Option A additionally drops the prior entirely.

The answer is **B**.
