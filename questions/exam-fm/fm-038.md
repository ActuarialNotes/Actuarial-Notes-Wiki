Joe must pay liabilities of 2000 due one year from now and another 1000 due two years from now. He exactly matches his liabilities with the following two investments:

**Mortgage I:** A one year mortgage in which $X$ is lent. It is repaid with a single payment at time one. The annual effective interest rate is 6%.

**Mortgage II:** A two-year mortgage in which $Y$ is lent. It is repaid with two equal annual payments. The annual effective interest rate is 7%.

Calculate $X + Y$.

- A) 2600
- B) 2682
- C) 2751
- D) 2825
- E) 3000

## Explanation

Because only **Mortgage II** provides a cash flow at time 2, it must be considered first. Its two equal payments are $Y/a_{\overline{2}|0.07} = 0.553092Y$, at times 1 and 2. To provide exactly 1000 at time 2:

$$0.553092Y = 1000 \implies Y = 1808.02$$

This also provides $0.553092 \times 1808.02 = 1000$ at time 1.

**Mortgage I** must provide $2000 - 1000 = 1000$ at time 1, so the amount lent is:

$$X = \frac{1000}{1.06} = 943.40$$

$$X + Y = 943.40 + 1808.02 = 2751.42$$
