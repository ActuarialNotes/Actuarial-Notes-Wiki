A large manufacturer's broker drafts a manuscript property policy for it, which an insurer accepts. In a later dispute a term proves ambiguous. Compared with the same dispute under a standard ISO form, what is the most likely difference?

- A) The court will construe the ambiguity even more strongly against the insurer
- B) The insured may be unable to rely on construing ambiguity against the drafter
- C) The court will resolve the ambiguity using ISO's published interpretation
- D) The common policy conditions will decide it, because the policy is modular

## Explanation

Courts construe ambiguous wording against the drafter because standard forms are contracts of adhesion. When the insured's own broker drafted the manuscript wording, that rule cannot be invoked in the policyholder's favour, and the broker's knowledge is imputed to the insured. Some courts also limit the pro-insured rules for a sophisticated commercial insured. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — gets the effect backwards: the insured, not the insurer, wrote the wording.
- **C** — a manuscript form is custom designed for one insured, not an ISO form, so no ISO interpretation applies.
- **D** — manuscript versus preprinted is a separate question from modular versus self-contained.
