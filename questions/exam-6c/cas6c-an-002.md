Every Canadian P&C insurer files the same quarterly and annual P&C returns, whether its primary regulator is OSFI or a province. Comments on the returns' forms go to the Forms Committee of the Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators (CCIR).

Which description of the CCIR is most accurate?

- A) The industry association of P&C insurers that lobbies governments on insurers' behalf
- B) The federal regulator responsible for the solvency of every insurer licensed in Canada
- C) The fund that pays the claims of policyholders when a P&C insurer becomes insolvent
- D) The body that licenses insurance agents and brokers in every province and territory
- E) An association of federal, provincial and territorial regulators that works to harmonize regulation

## Explanation

The CCIR describes itself as "an association of insurance regulators" and a forum for Canadian insurance regulators to strengthen regulatory oversight of the industry. Its members are the provincial and territorial regulators and the federal Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and its mission is to find solutions to common regulatory issues and promote **harmonization**. The uniform P&C returns are an example: all P&C insurers complete the same returns, although each jurisdiction may apply conditions of its own, and their forms are maintained through the CCIR's Forms Committee. Each member still regulates under its own statute. The answer is **E**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — that describes the Insurance Bureau of Canada, the insurers' own trade association. The CCIR's members are regulators, not insurers.
- **B** — that is OSFI, and only for federally regulated insurers. OSFI is one *member* of the CCIR, and provincially incorporated insurers are supervised by their province.
- **C** — that is PACICC, the P&C industry's guarantee fund.
- **D** — intermediary licensing belongs to each province's own regulator. Those regulators coordinate through the Canadian Insurance Services Regulatory Organizations (CISRO), a separate body.
