An article in the June 2022 issue of the Society of Actuaries Health Section's Health Watch by Greg Fann. It argues that the aim of ACA risk adjustment is to foster markets where health plans compete on quality, efficiency and value rather than on risk selection, and examines whether the risk adjustment methodology bridges the gap between the premiums the ACA's rating rules allow and the premiums that would apply without them.

## Introduction
- Actuarial Standard of Practice No. 12 (Risk Classification) says the actuary should select risk characteristics related to expected outcomes; in a loosely regulated environment health plans are theoretically indifferent to enrollment mix if rating factors promote actuarial equity, the "indifference ideal".

## The World of ACA
- In the ACA, some rating factors are regulatorily prescribed (age) and others are not allowed (health status, gender), creating a rating gap between premium rates under the regulatory environment and those that would be offered without it.
- Risk adjustment is a mechanism designed to bridge that gap, and the federal government assumes responsibility for developing the factors and associated adjustments.
- In the phrase "risk adjustment", risk arises from premium rates being detached from risk characteristics by rating regulations.

## Bridging the Gap
- A two-plan, two-enrollee example shows a $200 transfer is inequitable when the ACA fixes age rating at a 3:1 ratio; a $50 transfer would then be required.
- The methodology must be designed to align with rating rules; risk adjustment need not be perfect, only designed to correctly bridge the gap.

## ACA Risk Adjustment Application
- The risk score is the Plan Liability Risk Score; the defunding of cost-sharing reduction (CSR) payments in October 2017 increased the plan liability of on-exchange silver plans.
- Risk adjustment is the final step in the process, not an input into the rating formula.

## Allowable Rating Factors
- Allowable rating factors are predicated on actuarial value, not metal level; from 2018 silver plan CSR 87 and CSR 94 variants had actuarial values in line with platinum plans.

## Risk Adjustment and CSR Enrollees
- The aim is to offset all variations in plan actuarial risk due to risk selection beyond the premiums plans are able to collect.
- HHS publicly recognized the platinum-level nature of the CSR 87 and CSR 94 variants and in 2017 proposed applying platinum coefficients to them, but did not follow through after CSR defunding.
- In October 2021 HHS raised the issue in Appendix A of a Technical Paper.

## Premium Misalignment
- Metal level risk selection was practiced before 2018: silver plan premiums declined relative to bronze and gold premiums from 2014 to 2017 (Figure 1).
- Risk adjustment results have effectively been used as an input into metal level experience rating formulas.

## State Action
- Colorado, Texas, New Mexico, Pennsylvania and Virginia have addressed premium alignment through rules or guidance, while a majority of states passively allow flexibility of rating factors.
- Figure 2 shows the 2022 premium relationships of gold and silver plans across the states.

## Risk Adjustment Modifications
- The 2021 HHS Technical Paper discusses several variations of a proposal to reflect platinum level liability for silver CSR 87 and CSR 94 enrollees as early as plan year 2024.
- HHS cites a lack of evidence of higher induced demand associated with the receipt of CSRs; the article disputes the comparison used.
- The risk adjustment transfer formula includes a 12 percent credit for silver CSR 87 and CSR 94, described by actuaries as a "happy accident".

## Two Realities
- Where rating rules are strictly enforced, inequities arise when enrollment mix differs among risk characteristics that rating rules constrain but risk adjustment does not account for; where they are not, plans compete on risk selection.
- The ideal ACA environment is one where rating rules are enforced, risk adjustment reinforces them, and health plans are generally indifferent to the populations they enroll.
