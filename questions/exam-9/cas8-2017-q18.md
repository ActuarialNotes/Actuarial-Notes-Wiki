A homogenous group of property risks exhibit the following risk profile:

| Exposure Distribution | Losses as a percent of the Maximum Possible Loss (MPL) |
|---|---|
| 80% | 0% |
| 6% | 25% |
| 8% | 50% |
| 4% | 75% |
| 2% | 100% |

## Part a (2 points)

Plot the exposure curve, $G(x)$. Label the axes and the points on the curve.

### Explanation
First restate the exposure distribution as a loss distribution, conditional on a loss occurring (80% of exposures have no loss):

| Loss (% of MPL) | Probability |
|---|---|
| 25% | 6%/(1 - 80%) = 30% |
| 50% | 8%/(1 - 80%) = 40% |
| 75% | 4%/(1 - 80%) = 20% |
| 100% | 2%/(1 - 80%) = 10% |

Then $E(\text{loss}) = 52.5\%$, and $G(x)$ is the limited expected loss at $x$ divided by the expected loss:

$$G(0\%) = 0 \qquad G(25\%) = \frac{25\%}{52.5\%} = 0.476$$

$$G(50\%) = \frac{25\% \times 30\% + 50\% \times 70\%}{52.5\%} = 0.81$$

$$G(75\%) = \frac{25\% \times 30\% + 50\% \times 40\% + 75\% \times 30\%}{52.5\%} = 0.952 \qquad G(100\%) = 1$$

![Exposure curve G(x) plotted against x as a percent of MPL, both axes from 0 to 1. A smooth concave curve rises through the labelled points (0, 0), (0.25, 0.476), (0.5, 0.81), (0.75, 0.95) and (1, 1).](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas8-2017-q18-exposure-curve.png)

Alternatively, work with the unconditional distribution, since the ratio $G(x) = E[X;x]/E[X]$ is unaffected by the rescaling:

$$E[X] = 0.8(0) + 0.06(0.25) + \dots = 0.105$$

$$E[X;0.25] = 0.8(0) + 0.25(0.06 + 0.08 + 0.04 + 0.02) = 0.05 \qquad E[X;0.50] = 0.8(0) + 0.25(0.06) + 0.50(0.08 + 0.04 + 0.02) = 0.085$$

$$E[X;0.75] = 0.8(0) + 0.25(0.06) + 0.50(0.08) + 0.75(0.04 + 0.02) = 0.1$$

giving $G(0) = 0$, $G(0.25) = 0.05/0.105 = 0.476$, $G(0.5) = 0.81$, $G(0.75) = 0.952$ and $G(1) = 1$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to restate the provided exposure distribution to a loss distribution, calculate the points on the exposure curve, graph the points, and correctly label the x- and y- axes on the graph.
Common mistakes included:

- Not restating with respect to the loss distribution.  If they did not restate the loss distribution, then they did not get credit for the points on the graph.  Credit was still given to the points (0,0) and (1,1)
- Candidates often missed the axis labels.  Credit was not given for % of losses, % exposure, cumulative distribution, cumulative exposure distribution, F(x)
- Credit was not given for plotting a stepwise function, or not connecting the points

## Part b (0.75 points)

Determine the parameters $b$ and $g$ for the 2 parameter MBBEFD distribution that fits this exposure curve using the following information:

**Calculated value of parameter $b$**

| Parameter $g$ | $\mu$ = 40.0% | 42.5% | 45.0% | 47.5% | 50.0% | 52.5% | 55.0% | 57.5% | 60.0% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50.0 | 0.0023 | 0.0017 | 0.0013 | 0.0009 | 0.0005 | 0.0003 | 0.0002 | 0.0001 | 0.0001 |
| 25.0 | 0.0096 | 0.0073 | 0.0055 | 0.0041 | 0.0022 | 0.0015 | 0.0010 | 0.0006 | 0.0004 |
| 16.7 | 0.0239 | 0.0181 | 0.0137 | 0.0103 | 0.0057 | 0.0039 | 0.0026 | 0.0017 | 0.0010 |
| 12.5 | 0.0483 | 0.0365 | 0.0276 | 0.0209 | 0.0118 | 0.0081 | 0.0055 | 0.0036 | 0.0023 |
| 10.0 | 0.0877 | 0.0659 | 0.0497 | 0.0375 | 0.0212 | 0.0147 | 0.0100 | 0.0067 | 0.0044 |
| 8.3 | 0.1498 | 0.1116 | 0.0836 | 0.0628 | 0.0354 | 0.0245 | 0.0168 | 0.0113 | 0.0075 |
| 7.1 | 0.2470 | 0.1817 | 0.1347 | 0.1004 | 0.0561 | 0.0389 | 0.0267 | 0.0181 | 0.0121 |
| 6.3 | 0.4000 | 0.2892 | 0.2116 | 0.1561 | 0.0861 | 0.0594 | 0.0408 | 0.0277 | 0.0185 |
| 5.6 | 0.6446 | 0.4557 | 0.3277 | 0.2385 | 0.1291 | 0.0885 | 0.0605 | 0.0411 | 0.0276 |
| 5.0 | 1.0469 | 0.7190 | 0.5055 | 0.3616 | 0.1910 | 0.1297 | 0.0882 | 0.0597 | 0.0400 |

### Explanation
$g = 1/p$, where $p$ is the probability of a total loss given a loss: $p = 0.1 = 0.02/(1 - 0.8)$, so $g = 10$.

$$\mu = \frac{1}{G'(0)} \qquad G'(0) = \frac{1 - F(0)}{E(X)} = \frac{1 - 0.8}{0.105} = 1.905 \qquad \mu = \frac{1}{1.905} = 0.525$$

Table lookup at $g = 10.0$, $\mu = 52.5\%$: $b = 0.0147$.

If part (a) was not rescaled, full credit was also accepted for $p = 0.02$, $g = 50$, with $\mu = 0.525$ and table lookup $b = 0.0003$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to recall and calculate the formula for g, calculate µ and look up these values in a table.

Common mistakes include:

- Most candidates did not correctly calculate the conditional mean; if they did not get part (a), credit was still given for µ =0.105 (instead of 0.525).  This µ is not on the table provided, but if a candidate explicitly assumed µ equals some number on the table, they received partial credit for looking up the b value.
- Many candidates used p = 0.02 (instead of 0.1).  If they did not rescale in part (a), they still received credit for g=(1/p)=50 in part (b).

## Part c (0.5 points)

A reinsurer uses this exposure curve to price a property excess of loss treaty where the underlying limit is \$100 million. Calculate the proportion of total losses in the layer \$25 million excess of \$25 million.

### Explanation
$$G\left(\frac{\text{Limit} + \text{Retention}}{\text{MPL}}\right) - G\left(\frac{\text{Retention}}{\text{MPL}}\right) = G\left(\frac{50}{100}\right) - G\left(\frac{25}{100}\right) = G(0.5) - G(0.25) = 0.81 - 0.476 = 0.334$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to write the formula for the total losses in a layer and identify the correct points on their exposure curve.
Common mistakes included:

- Calculation errors were common in part (a), but if part (a) was used in part (c) full credit was given.  A common answer that was given full credit was G(0.5)-G(0.25) = 0.94 – 0.86
= 0.08
- Many candidates only wrote the formula without trying to identify the correct points on the exposure curve
