A bicycle rental shop is in an ISO general liability classification marked with a plus sign in the premium base column. The shop occasionally adjusts the brakes on bicycles customers own. What does the plus sign mean for this account?

- A) Products-completed operations coverage is included in the premises rate and erodes the general aggregate limit
- B) Products-completed operations losses are excluded, because the classification charges no separate products-completed operations rate
- C) A separate products-completed operations rate must be added to the premium, with its own separate aggregate limit
- D) The account must be rated on two premium bases, one for rentals and one for repairs

## Explanation

A plus-sign ("dagger") classification has negligible products-completed operations exposure, so ISO includes that coverage in the premises and operations loss cost at no additional charge. The classification states that products-completed operations are subject to the **general aggregate** limit, so there is no separate products-completed operations aggregate. A claim from the brake work is covered. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — The coverage is included, not excluded; the CGL's definition simply moves such losses out of the products-completed operations hazard and under the general aggregate.
- **C** — That describes a standard (non-dagger) class, which has separate rates and its own products-completed operations aggregate.
- **D** — The plus sign concerns the products rate, not the number of premium bases.
