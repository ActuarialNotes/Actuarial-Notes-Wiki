In a large city, 40% of the intersections are safe, while all others are dangerous. The numbers of accidents occurring annually at individual safe and dangerous intersections are Poisson distributed with means 0.5 and 1.0, respectively.

No accidents occurred during 2004 at Thirty-Fourth Street and Vine Street, an intersection within the city.

Calculate the probability that Thirty-Fourth Street and Vine is a safe intersection.

- A) $0.24$
- B) $0.40$
- C) $0.46$
- D) $0.52$
- E) $0.62$

## Explanation

For a Poisson distribution the probability of zero accidents is $e^{-\lambda}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[N=0 \mid S] &= e^{-0.5} = 0.60653 \\
P[N=0 \mid D] &= e^{-1.0} = 0.36788
\end{align*}
$$

Apply Bayes' theorem with priors 0.40 and 0.60:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S \mid N=0] &= \frac{(0.4)(0.60653)}{(0.4)(0.60653) + (0.6)(0.36788)} \\
              &= \frac{0.24261}{0.24261 + 0.22073} \\
              &= \frac{0.24261}{0.46334} \\
              &= \mathbf{0.5236}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 0.40, is the prior — the answer if the accident-free year told you nothing. It should rise, since safe intersections are more likely to have a clean year, and the shift from 0.40 to 0.52 is substantial because the two Poisson means differ by a factor of two. Choice (A), 0.24, is the numerator, the joint probability rather than the conditional one.
