A short CAS study note covering MAS-I learning objectives A1–A5 — the [[Poisson Process]] and the mixture ideas that go with it. It is the syllabus's primary treatment of the process variants an exam question is most likely to test.

**Available from:** the CAS study-note library (`studynotes_poissonprocesses.pdf`), and as an SOA Exam MLC study note.

## What it covers

- The [[Poisson Process]] as a counting process: independent and stationary increments, and the $\text{Poi}(\lambda t)$ distribution of counts
- [[Interarrival Time|Interarrival]] and waiting times: i.i.d. [[Exponential Distribution|exponential]] gaps, the [[Gamma]] distribution of the time to the $n$-th event, and the memoryless property
- The [[Nonhomogeneous Poisson Process]]: a rate $\lambda(t)$ that varies with time, and the integrated rate $m(t)$ that replaces $\lambda t$
- **Thinning and decomposition**: splitting a process by event type — a claim exceeding a [[Deductible]], a claim of a given cause — gives independent Poisson processes
- **Superposition**: independent Poisson processes add, with rates adding
- The [[Compound Poisson Process]]: a random number of random-sized claims, with $E[S(t)] = \lambda t\,E[X]$ and $\text{Var}(S(t)) = \lambda t\,E[X^2]$
- **Mixture distributions** and the [[Mixed Poisson Process]]: a rate that varies across risks, producing overdispersion — and, with a [[Gamma]] mixing distribution, the [[Negative Binomial Distribution]]
