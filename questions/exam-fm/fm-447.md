A perpetuity-immediate with level annual payments of $k$ has a Macaulay duration of 32.25 years based on an annual effective interest rate of $i$. At the same annual effective interest rate, a perpetuity-due with level annual payments of $1.02k$ has a modified duration of $X$ years.

Calculate $X$.

- A) 30.28
- B) 30.89
- C) 31.25
- D) 31.88
- E) 32.22

## Explanation

The size of a level payment has no effect on duration, so neither $k$ nor $1.02k$ matters. For the perpetuity-immediate, payments of 1 at times $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ have present value $a_{\overline{\infty}|} = \dfrac{1}{i}$ and time-weighted present value $(Ia)_{\overline{\infty}|} = \dfrac{1}{i} + \dfrac{1}{i^{2}}$, so

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{MAC} &= \frac{\dfrac{1}{i} + \dfrac{1}{i^{2}}}{\dfrac{1}{i}} \\
        &= \frac{1+i}{i} \\
        &= 32.25
\end{align*}
$$

Since $\dfrac{1+i}{i} = \dfrac{1}{d}$,

$$
\begin{align*}
d &= \frac{1}{32.25} \\
  &= 0.031008 \\
i &= \frac{d}{1-d} \\
  &= 0.032
\end{align*}
$$

For the perpetuity-due, the payments are at times $0, 1, 2, \ldots$, so the present value is $\ddot{a}_{\overline{\infty}|} = \dfrac{1}{d} = \dfrac{1+i}{i}$, while the time-weighted present value is unchanged (the payment at time 0 has weight 0):

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{MAC} &= \frac{\dfrac{1}{i} + \dfrac{1}{i^{2}}}{\dfrac{1}{d}} \\
        &= \frac{\dfrac{1+i}{i^{2}}}{\dfrac{1+i}{i}} \\
        &= \frac{1}{i} \\
        &= \frac{1}{0.032} \\
        &= 31.25
\end{align*}
$$

The modified duration is the Macaulay duration divided by $1+i$:

$$
\begin{align*}
X &= D_{MOD} \\
  &= \frac{31.25}{1.032} \\
  &= 30.281
\end{align*}
$$
