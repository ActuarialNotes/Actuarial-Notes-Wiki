A summary of the OECD's work on private health insurance and of its development in OECD countries. It introduces the work of the OECD Insurance Committee in this field, gives a general overview of the role of private health insurance alongside predominantly publicly financed health care, and illustrates innovations in insurance products, services and institutions that various OECD countries have tried in order to improve their private health insurance systems.

## 1 Health is a key component of the social and economic agenda
- Health expenditures run from 8% to 10% of GDP in OECD countries; on average 74% of health care is financed by public resources, and in the US, the country that relies most on private health insurance, 60% of health expenditures are still publicly financed.
- Private insurance plays its role at two levels: financing, where the insurer reimburses the cost of care or provides compensation, and providing care, as in managed care.
