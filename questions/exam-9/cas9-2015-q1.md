Given the information below describing a three-security financial market, one rational investor's complete portfolio and investors' utility:

| Security | Expected Return | Standard Deviation | Portfolio Allocation |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 4% | 0% | 43% |
| Y | 10% | 20% | 30% |
| Z | 15% | 30% | 27% |

- Investors assign utility to their portfolios by the formula: $U = E(r) - \frac{1}{2}A\sigma^2$.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Describe the interpretation of the risk aversion index when $A < 0$.

### Explanation
1. When A < 0, the investor is considered to be "risk-seeking", meaning he seeks to invest for risk, regardless of expected return and therefore does not care for optimizing his mean variance return.
2. A is a measure of investors' risk appetite. When A < 0, it means this investor is risk seeking. His utility increases by taking on more risk.

### Examiner Report
Majority of candidates scored well.

Candidate was expected to be able to interpret the meaning of different values for A. They needed to mention that the investor is "risk seeking" or "risk lover" to obtain full credit.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Calculate the correlation coefficient of the returns of securities Y and Z.

### Explanation
Given $W_Y = 30\%$ and $W_Z = 27\%$.

The weight of the risky portfolio is: $y = 30\% + 27\% = 57\%$

Y's weight in the risky portfolio is: $\frac{30\%}{57\%} = \frac{10}{19}$

We also know the portion of Y in the risky assets should be:

$$W_Y = \frac{R_Z\,\sigma_Z^2 - R_Z\,\sigma_{YZ}}{R_Y\,\sigma_Z^2 + R_Z\,\sigma_Y^2 - (R_Y + R_Z)\,\sigma_{YZ}} \quad \text{(as printed)}$$

Given $r_t = r_x = 4\%$:

- $R_y = 6\%$, $R_z = 11\%$
- $\sigma_y^2 = 0.2^2$, $\sigma_z^2 = 0.3^2$

$$\frac{10}{19} = \frac{0.06 * 0.3^2 - 0.11\sigma_{YZ}}{0.06 * 0.3^2 + 0.11 * 0.2 - (0.06 + 0.11)\sigma_{YZ}} \quad \text{(as printed)}$$

Solve for $\sigma_{YZ} \rightarrow \sigma_{YZ} = 0.0118$

$$0.0118 = \rho_{YZ}\,\sigma_Y\,\sigma_Z \;\rightarrow\; \rho_{YZ} = \frac{\sigma_{YZ}}{\sigma_Y\sigma_Z} = 0.1966$$

Alternatively:

$$\text{Risk portfolio} = \frac{r_p\;r_f}{A\,r_p^2} = 0.57 = \frac{.1237 - .04}{A\sigma_p^2} \quad \text{(as printed)}$$

$$r_p = \left(\frac{.3}{.57} * .1\right) + \left(\frac{.27}{.57} * .15\right) = .1237$$

Correlation coeff of Y,Z $\Rightarrow$ $\text{Cov}(Y,Z) = D_{rz}\,r_r r_z = D(.2)(.3)$ (as printed)

$$.5263 = \frac{r_z^2 E(R_y) - E(R_z)\text{Cov}(Z,Y)}{r_z^2 E(R_y) + E(R_z)r_y^2 - [E(R_y) + E(R_z)]\text{Cov}(Z,Y)} = \frac{.3^2(.06) - (.11)\text{Cov}(Z,Y)}{(.3^2 * .06) + (.2^2 * .11) - (.11 + .06)\text{Cov}(Y,Z)}$$

$$.5263 = \frac{.0054 - .11\,\text{Cov}(Z,Y)}{.0098 - .17\,\text{Cov}(Z,Y)}$$

$0.00516 - 0.0895\,\text{Cov} = .0054 - .11\,\text{Cov}$, so $\text{Cov}(Z,Y) = 0.0117$

$0.0117 = D(.2)(.3) \Rightarrow$ Corr Coeff $= 0.195$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the formula for the weight of securities in the optimal risky portfolio and determine the correlation coefficient.  The most common errors were due to miscalculation.

## Part c (1 point)

Calculate the value of A for the investor.

### Explanation
To maximize utility, we invest $y^* = \dfrac{E(r_p) - r_t}{A\sigma_p^2}$ in risky assets.

$$E(r_p) = \frac{30}{57} * 0.1 + \frac{27}{57} * 0.15 = 0.1237$$

$$\sigma_p^2 = w_y^2\,\sigma_y^2 + W_z^2\,\sigma_z^2 + 2w_yw_z\sigma_{yz}$$

$$\sigma_p^2 = \left(\frac{30}{57}\right)^2 * 0.2^2 + \left(\frac{27}{57}\right)^2 * 0.3^2 + 2 * \frac{30}{57} * \frac{27}{57} * 0.1966 * 0.2 * 0.3 = 0.0372$$

$$Y^* = \frac{57}{100} = \frac{0.1237 - 0.04}{A * 0.00372} \quad \text{(0.00372 as printed)}$$

$\rightarrow A = 3.95$

Alternatively:

$$\sigma_p^2 = w_y^2\,r_y^2 + w_z^2\,\sigma_z^2 + 2\,\text{Cov}(Z,Y)\,w_y\,w_z = 5.265^2 + .2^2 + .3^2 + .4737^2 + 2(.5263)(.4737) \quad \text{(as printed)}$$

Cov $= 0.037108$ (as printed)

$$0.57 = \frac{.1237 - .04}{A * .037108} \qquad A = 3.957$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the formula for the optimal weight of the risky portfolio. They were expected to be able to calculate the value of A given the correlation coefficient calculated in
part b.  The most common errors were due to misinterpreting the formula and miscalculation.
