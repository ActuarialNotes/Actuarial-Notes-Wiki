Let $R$ be the proportion of right-handed babies among all newborns in a town each year. $R$ has a beta distribution with density function

$$
f(r) = \begin{cases}
k\left[r^{\alpha-1}(1-r)^{\beta-1}\right], & \text{for } 0 < r < 1 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $\alpha = 13$, $\beta = 2$, and $k$ is a positive constant.

Calculate $P[R > 0.9]$.

- A) $0.208$
- B) $0.240$
- C) $0.415$
- D) $0.479$
- E) $0.575$

## Explanation

Substituting the parameters, the density is proportional to $r^{12}(1-r)$. Find $k$ by normalizing:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= k\int_0^1 \left(r^{12} - r^{13}\right)dr \\
  &= k\left(\frac{1}{13} - \frac{1}{14}\right) = \frac{k}{182} \\
k &= 182
\end{align*}
$$

Now integrate the tail:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[R > 0.9] &= 182\int_{0.9}^{1}\left(r^{12} - r^{13}\right)dr \\
           &= 182\left(\frac{r^{13}}{13} - \frac{r^{14}}{14}\right)\Bigg|_{0.9}^{1} \\
           &= 182\left[\left(\frac{1}{13} - \frac{1}{14}\right) - \left(\frac{0.9^{13}}{13} - \frac{0.9^{14}}{14}\right)\right] \\
           &= \mathbf{0.4154}
\end{align*}
$$

With $\alpha = 13$ and $\beta = 2$ the distribution has mean $\alpha/(\alpha+\beta) = 13/15 = 0.867$ and is skewed left toward 1, so a substantial probability above 0.9 is expected — which rules out choices (A) and (B) on inspection. The integer parameters are what make this integrable by hand; a general beta would need the incomplete beta function.
