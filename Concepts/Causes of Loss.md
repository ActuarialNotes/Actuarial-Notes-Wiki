A **Cause of Loss**, or peril, is what produces a loss, such as fire, windstorm, theft or flood. A property policy states which causes of loss it covers in one of two ways: a named perils policy lists the perils it covers, and an open perils policy covers every risk of direct physical loss that it doesn't exclude or limit.

The two approaches differ in what they cover and in who must prove what:

| | Named perils | Open perils |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| What is covered | Only the perils the policy lists, less its exclusions | Every risk of direct physical loss, less its exclusions and limitations |
| Burden of proof | The insured must show that a listed peril caused the loss | The loss is covered unless the insurer shows that an exclusion or limitation applies |
| ISO forms | Basic and broad causes of loss forms; HO-3 personal property | Special causes of loss form; HO-3 dwelling and other structures |

The burden of proof is a large part of the open perils form's value to the insured. Open perils is not all risks, because its exclusions are long.

## The basic, broad and special forms

The ISO commercial property program puts the choice in a separate causes of loss form, basic, broad or special, which is attached to a coverage form such as the [[Building and Personal Property Coverage Form|building and personal property coverage form]]. The form chosen determines which perils are covered.

1. **Basic form.** Fire, lightning, explosion, windstorm or hail, smoke, aircraft or vehicles, riot or civil commotion, vandalism, sprinkler leakage, sinkhole collapse and volcanic action.
2. **Broad form.** The basic perils, plus falling objects, the weight of snow, ice or sleet, and water damage from the accidental discharge or leakage of water or steam from a plumbing, heating or air conditioning system or appliance that breaks apart or cracks. It also adds an additional coverage for collapse.
3. **Special form.** Risks of direct physical loss unless excluded or limited, so it covers every peril the broad form lists plus theft and other perils no list names.

The special form adds exclusions for causes that are gradual or within the insured's control, such as wear and tear, rust, settling, mechanical breakdown, faulty workmanship, insects, and dishonest acts of the insured or its employees. It also places special limits on theft of items such as furs and jewelry. To pay a theft loss, each property class with a special limit is capped at its limit, property with no special limit is paid in full, and the results are added. Each peril's definition and every exclusion are worked through on [[Causes of Loss Basic and Broad Forms]] and [[Causes of Loss Special Form]].

> [!example]- How much of this store's year of losses does each causes of loss form cover? {Example}
> A retail store has five losses in a year, and deductibles and limits are ignored:
>
> | Loss | Amount ($) |
> | :--- | ---: |
> | Fire | 40,000 |
> | Roof collapse from the weight of snow | 25,000 |
> | Water damage from a burst water heater | 8,000 |
> | Burglary of inventory | 15,000 |
> | Flood from a nearby river | 60,000 |
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Fire is a basic peril, so the basic form pays $\$40{,}000$.
> > - The broad form adds the weight of snow and the accidental discharge of water, so it pays $40{,}000 + 25{,}000 + 8{,}000 = \$73{,}000$.
> > - Only the special form covers theft, because no list names it, so it pays $73{,}000 + 15{,}000 = \$88{,}000$.
> > - Flood is excluded under all three forms, so the $\$60{,}000$ flood loss is uninsured unless the store buys [[Flood Insurance|flood insurance]].

## Exclusions in every form

All three forms exclude ordinance or law, earth movement, governmental action, nuclear hazard, utility service failure, war, water and fungus or rot. The water exclusion covers flood, overflow of a body of water, surface water, sewer or drain backup and underground water, not all water damage. A sudden discharge from a plumbing system is a broad-form peril and is covered under the broad and special forms.

These exclusions apply whatever other cause contributes to the loss, concurrently or in any sequence. Many carry an exception for an ensuing loss: if an excluded cause results in a covered cause of loss, the form pays for the loss the covered cause does. The earth movement exclusion's exception covers direct loss by fire, explosion or theft that results from earth movement, while the damage done by the earth movement itself, such as a cracked foundation, stays excluded unless an earthquake endorsement is added.

> [!example]- What does the insurer pay for this earthquake and the fire that follows it? {Example}
> An earthquake does $\$50{,}000$ of structural damage to a house insured under an HO-3 with a $\$1{,}000$ deductible and no earthquake endorsement. It ruptures a gas line, and the fire that follows does $\$180{,}000$ of damage to the dwelling.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Earth movement is excluded, so the $\$50{,}000$ of shaking damage is not covered.
> > - The exclusion's exception covers direct loss by an ensuing fire, so the fire damage is covered as it would be from any other cause.
> > - The payment is $180{,}000 - 1{,}000 = \$179{,}000$, and the homeowner bears the earthquake damage.

## Homeowners perils

The [[Homeowners Policy|HO-3]] covers the dwelling and other structures on an open perils basis. Personal property (Coverage C) is covered for sixteen named perils:

1. Fire or lightning.
2. Windstorm or hail.
3. Explosion.
4. Riot or civil commotion.
5. Aircraft.
6. Vehicles.
7. Smoke.
8. Vandalism.
9. Theft.
10. Falling objects.
11. The weight of ice, snow or sleet.
12. Accidental discharge of water or steam.
13. Sudden tearing apart of a heating or water system.
14. Freezing.
15. Sudden damage from artificially generated electrical current.
16. Volcanic eruption.

A loss covered for the dwelling, such as paint spilled by a guest, may not be covered for the furniture it ruins.

## Causes of loss in underwriting

Fire is the cause of loss that dominates commercial property underwriting, judged by the building's construction, its occupancy, its fire protection and the hazards next door. Windstorm, hail, flood and earthquake are catastrophe perils, which can damage many insured locations from one event, so they are evaluated by location and by how much value is concentrated in one area ([[Catastrophe Risk]]). Theft, water damage and collapse depend on the occupancy, the property's condition and its security. Flood and earthquake are usually insured separately.

> [!trap] Covering theft under the basic or broad form
> Neither the basic nor the broad form lists theft, so a burglary of a building's contents is covered only under the special form. Vandalism is named in all three forms, but it covers only the damage, such as the door burglars break, not the property they take.
