A building is insured for \$750,000 with a 90% coinsurance percentage and a \$2,500 deductible. The declarations also show the agreed value optional coverage with an agreed value of \$750,000, in effect at the time of loss. By then the building's value has risen to \$1,000,000. A fire causes a \$200,000 loss. How much does the insurer pay?

- A) \$147,500
- B) \$164,166.67
- C) \$197,500
- D) \$200,000

## Explanation

Under agreed value, the coinsurance condition does not apply. The insurer pays no more than the proportion the limit bears to the agreed value, here $C/A = 750{,}000/750{,}000 = 1$, so the loss is paid in full less the deductible:

$$
\text{Paid} = 200{,}000 - 2{,}500 = 197{,}500
$$

The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Divides the limit by the full value at the time of loss: $R = 750{,}000/1{,}000{,}000 = 0.75$, giving \$147,500.
- **B** — Applies the coinsurance condition, which the agreed value option suspends: $R = 750{,}000/900{,}000$, giving \$164,166.67.
- **D** — Forgets the deductible, which still applies under agreed value.
