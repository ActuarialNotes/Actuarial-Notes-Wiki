In the supplemental material, you have been given a case study, "Systolic Blood Pressure Case Study", showing the results of different treatment options and the description of how that study was set up. You have also been asked to evaluate three statements regarding the results of the study.

- I. None of the treatments significantly reduce systolic blood pressure.
- II. The age of the patient has a significant effect on the change in blood pressure expected over a one-year time period.
- III. The age of the patient interacts significantly with the treatment.

Determine which of the above statements are true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I is false.** Treatment is overwhelmingly significant in Model 1 ($F = 2521.7$, $p < 0.0001$), and the exploratory box plot shows that treatments 0 and 1 both sit clearly below zero — change in systolic is negative, meaning blood pressure falls. Reading the same thing off Model 1 at the mean age of about 61:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Treatment 0:} \quad -12.487 + 0.1657(61) &= -2.38 \\
\text{Treatment 1:} \quad -12.487 + 0.655 + 0.1657(61) &= -1.72
\end{align*}
$$

Both reduce systolic blood pressure, so it is not true that *none* of the treatments do. (Treatment 2 raises it, at $+2.65$.)

**II is true.** Age carries $t = 33.7$ with $p \approx 0$ in Model 1 and $F = 1136.9$ with $p < 0.0001$. Each additional year of age adds about 0.166 to the expected change — comfortably significant.

**III is false.** This is what Models 3 and 4 are for. Neither interaction coefficient is close to significant ($p = 0.9817$ and $p = 0.6540$ in Model 3), and the joint F-test on the interaction is $F = 0.1305$ with $p = 0.8777$. Adding the interaction also *worsens* AIC — Model 3's 7345.4 against Model 1's 7327.4, both fitted by REML. The treatment effect does not vary with age.

Only statement II, so the answer is **B**.
