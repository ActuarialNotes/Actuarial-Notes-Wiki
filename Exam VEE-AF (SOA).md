# VEE Accounting and Finance

**VEE Accounting and Finance** is one of the *Validation by Educational Experience* (VEE) requirements, and both bodies ask for it. It is required for the [[Associate of the Society of Actuaries (ASA)|ASA]] and for the [[Associate of the Casualty Actuarial Society (ACAS)|ACAS]], and the SOA's guidelines call it *VEE–Accounting and Corporate Finance*. A VEE is not an exam. It is credit for university coursework, or for an approved alternative, that the VEE Administration Committee has approved for the topic. A candidate applies for it once they have passed two SOA, CAS or CIA actuarial exams. A CAS candidate may claim it directly through the CAS or through the SOA. Credit obtained through the CAS may not be recognized by other actuarial organizations, such as the SOA.

> [!question]- About the requirement
>
> While this material may be entirely covered in a course or courses that cover both topics, it is more likely that separate courses will be used for accounting and for corporate finance. If distinct courses are used, the application should indicate which are for the accounting topic and which are for the corporate finance topic. The typical corporate finance program covers the topics below in one semester with an introductory course. Should the topics be split across two semesters or courses, both will be required. Most of the topics listed below should be covered.
>
> The topics are those of the SOA's *Guidelines for Approval of VEE Courses and Educational Experiences*, effective November 1, 2024, the guidelines the CAS's VEE page also points to. The guidelines also set out transition credit for the retired *VEE Corporate Finance* topic: a candidate who earned it through an approved course before July 1, 2019 receives credit for the complete Accounting and Finance requirement on claiming it.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Accounting
> 1. Describe the basic principles of [[Corporate Taxation|corporate taxation]] and the taxation of [[Taxation of Investments|investments held]] by institutions.
> 2. Explain why companies are required to produce [[Annual Report|annual reports]].
> 3. Explain fundamental [[Accounting Standards|accounting concepts]] and terms, and describe the main sources of [[Accounting Regulation|accounting regulation]].
> 4. Explain the structure and purpose of the [[Income Statement|income statement]], [[Balance Sheet|balance sheet]], and [[Cash Flow Statement|statement of cash flows]]. and the interactions between them.
> 5. Construct [[Financial Statements|simple statements]] of [[Financial Position|financial position]] and profit or loss.
> 6. Calculate and interpret [[Financial Ratio|financial ratios]].

> [!example]- Corporate Finance
> 1. Explain the characteristics of various forms of [[Equity Capital|equity capital]] from the point of view of the issuer and the investor.
> 2. Explain the characteristics of various forms of [[Long-Term Debt|long-term debt capital]] from the point of view of the issuer and the investor.
> 3. Explain the characteristics of various forms of [[Short-Term Financing|short- and medium-term financing]] from the point of view of the issuer and the investor.
> 4. Calculate [[Weighted Average Cost of Capital|weighted-average cost of capital]].
> 5. Explain the main methods of [[Capital Budgeting|capital budgeting]].
> 6. Calculate a project’s [[Internal Rate of Return|investment return]].
