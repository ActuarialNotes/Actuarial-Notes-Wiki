Three plots of likelihood, prior, and posterior densities for the parameter $\theta$ are provided below. At least one of these plots was produced from a Bayesian model.

![Three stacked density plots over theta from -5 to 30, each with a dotted prior, a dashed likelihood and a solid posterior. In all three the prior is flat, a low rectangle from theta = 0 to theta = 30 and zero outside it. Plot I: the likelihood is a hump centred at about 5, while the posterior is a taller, narrower hump centred at about 22, where the likelihood is essentially zero. Plot II: the posterior is a tall hump centred at about 0, with much of its mass below 0, while the likelihood is a hump centred at about 10. Plot III: the likelihood is a hump centred at about 5 and the posterior is a taller hump also centred at about 5, near zero below 0](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2018f-q28-prior-likelihood-posterior.png)

Determine which of these three plots could have been produced by a Bayesian model.

- A) Plot I only
- B) Plot II only
- C) Plot III only
- D) Plots I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

A Bayesian posterior is proportional to the likelihood times the prior:

$$
p(\theta \mid y) \propto p(y \mid \theta)\, p(\theta)
$$

In all three plots the prior is flat on $[0, 30]$ and zero elsewhere. That has two consequences. First, the posterior must be zero wherever the prior is zero. Second, inside $[0, 30]$ the flat prior multiplies the likelihood by a constant, so it cannot pull the posterior anywhere: the posterior must sit where the likelihood is.

**Plot I** is impossible. The likelihood peaks at about 5, but the posterior peaks at about 22, where the likelihood is essentially zero. Nothing in a flat prior could move it there.

**Plot II** is impossible. The posterior peaks at about 0 with much of its mass **below** 0, where the prior density is zero, and it sits far from the likelihood's peak at 10.

**Plot III** is consistent. The posterior is centred at about 5, where the likelihood peaks, and it vanishes below 0 where the prior does.

Only Plot III could come from a Bayesian model, so the answer is **C**.
