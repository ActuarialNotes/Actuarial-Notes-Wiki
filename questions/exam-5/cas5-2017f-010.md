Given the following:

| Territory | True Relativity | Univariate Indicated Relativity | Loss & ALAE (\$000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.50 | 0.46 | 3,680 |
| 2 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 8,000 |
| 3 | 1.20 | 1.28 | 11,636 |

| Earned Exposures (000) — Territory | Class A | Class B | Class C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 150 | 70 | 110 |
| 2 | 105 | 115 | 110 |
| 3 | 70 | 180 | 125 |

| Class | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charged Factor | 0.85 | 1.15 | 1.00 |

## Part a (0.5 points)

Explain why the univariate indicated relativities are different from the true relativities.

### Explanation
A univariate analysis measures each territory using only that territory's own raw experience. In doing so it **assumes the exposures are distributed the same way across the other rating variables in every territory** — here, that each territory has the same class mix. That assumption is violated by this data:

| Territory | Class A | Class B | Class C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 45.5% | 21.2% | 33.3% |
| 2 | 31.8% | 34.8% | 33.3% |
| 3 | 18.7% | 48.0% | 33.3% |

Territory 1 is heavily weighted toward **Class A**, the cheapest class at a factor of 0.85, while Territory 3 is weighted toward **Class B**, the most expensive at 1.15. This is **exposure correlation** between territory and class.

The consequence is double-counting. Territory 1's raw loss experience is low partly because it *is* a low-risk territory and partly because it is full of low-risk classes; the univariate method attributes all of it to territory, so the indicated 0.46 falls below the true 0.50. The same effect works in reverse for Territory 3, where the concentration of Class B pushes the indication up to 1.28 against a true 1.20.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify that univariate indicated relativities assume a uniform distribution of exposures across the other rating variables, **and** to demonstrate that this assumption is violated by the data provided. The common mistake was stating the uniform-distribution assumption in general terms without discussing it against the actual earned exposure distribution in the question.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Calculate territory relativities using the adjusted pure premium method, keeping territory 2 as the base level.

### Answer
Territory 1: 0.4795; Territory 2: 1.0000; Territory 3: 1.2316

### Explanation
The adjusted pure premium method removes the class distortion by re-expressing each territory's exposures in **class-equivalent** units — weighting each exposure by its charged class factor — before dividing losses by them.

**Step 1 — adjusted exposures.** Multiply each class's exposures by its charged factor and sum within the territory:

| Territory | Calculation | Adjusted Exposures (000) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $150(0.85) + 70(1.15) + 110(1.00)$ | 318.0 |
| 2 | $105(0.85) + 115(1.15) + 110(1.00)$ | 331.5 |
| 3 | $70(0.85) + 180(1.15) + 125(1.00)$ | 391.5 |

Territory 1's adjusted exposures fall below its 330 raw exposures (its class mix is cheap), while Territory 3's rise above its 375 — exactly the correction the univariate method omits.

**Step 2 — adjusted pure premiums.**

| Territory | Loss & ALAE (\$000) | ÷ Adjusted Exposures | Adjusted Pure Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3,680 | 318.0 | 11,572 |
| 2 | 8,000 | 331.5 | 24,133 |
| 3 | 11,636 | 391.5 | 29,722 |

**Step 3 — relativities to the base, territory 2.**

$$\text{Terr 1} = \frac{11{,}572}{24{,}133} = 0.4795, \qquad \text{Terr 2} = 1.0000, \qquad \text{Terr 3} = \frac{29{,}722}{24{,}133} = 1.2316$$

These sit much closer to the true relativities of 0.50, 1.00 and 1.20 than the univariate indications of 0.46, 1.00 and 1.28 — the adjustment has stripped out most of the class effect that the univariate method had absorbed into territory.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to apply the adjusted pure premium method: adjusting exposures for the average class factor by territory, calculating the adjusted pure premiums and the relativities, and rebasing to the same base territory. The common mistake was applying the average class factor by territory incorrectly, producing incorrect adjusted exposures within the territory.
