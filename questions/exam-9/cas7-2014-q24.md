The following information (\$000,000) is available for an insurer:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Earned premium | 1,000 |
| Expected losses | 200 |
| Acquisition expenses | 150 |
| Beginning surplus | 2,000 |
| Risk-free rate | 3% |
| Investment rate of return | 5% |

The insurer is considering a reinsurance program for accident year 2015:

| | No Reinsurance | Reinsurance |
|---|---|---|
| Reinsurance premium | 0 | 450.0 |
| Reinsurance commission | 0 | 67.5 |
| Expected recovery | 0 | 110.0 |
| Probability of distress | 3.00% | 0.40% |

Assume the following:

- All premiums are collected at the beginning of the year.
- Losses are paid in full at the end of the year.
- All expenses are paid at the beginning of the year.
- There is no growth or change in the insured portfolio.
- The insurer cannot operate in a financially distressed state.
- Regulatory considerations are negligible.
- The firm value is the risk-adjusted present value of all future earnings.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate the insurer's expected underwriting profit both with and without reinsurance.

### Explanation
w/o Reins: U/W Profit = EP – Loss – Expense = 1000 – 200 – 150 = 650
w/ Reins: U/W Profit = (1000 – 450) – (200 – 110) – (150 – 67.5) = 377.5

Alternatively:

Without Reinsurance: U/W Profit = 1000 – 200 – 150 = 650 M With Reinsurance: U/W Profit = 650 – 450 + 67.5 + 110 = 377.5 M

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate underwriting profit with and without reinsurance. In order to obtain full credit, the candidate had to show the correct underwriting profit with and without reinsurance as well as the components of premium, expense/commission, and losses for the reinsurance portion. 76% of candidates received full credit on this part.
Common errors included the following:

- Including investment income in the underwriting profit (about 10% of candidates)

- Calculation errors
- Discounting losses

## Part b (2.5 points)

Calculate the change in firm value of the insurer at the beginning of 2015 if the insurer purchases the reinsurance program.

### Explanation
Firm value is the risk-adjusted present value of a level perpetuity of annual earnings, $V = E \times M$, where the discount factor reflects both the risk-free rate $r$ and the probability of distress $d$ (the firm cannot operate once distressed):

$$D = \frac{1 - d}{1 + r}, \qquad M = \frac{D}{1 - D}$$

Earnings are underwriting profit (part a) plus investment income at 5% on invested assets — surplus plus premium collected, less expenses (and, with reinsurance, less the reinsurance premium plus the commission).

**Without reinsurance:**

$$E = 650 + (2{,}000 + 1{,}000 - 150) \times 5\% = 792.5\text{M}$$

$$D = \frac{0.97}{1.03}, \qquad M = \frac{D}{1 - D} = 16.167, \qquad V = 792.5 \times 16.167 = 12{,}812.08\text{M}$$

**With reinsurance:**

$$E = 377.5 + (2{,}000 + 1{,}000 - 450 + 67.5 - 150) \times 5\% = 500.875\text{M}$$

$$D = \frac{0.996}{1.03}, \qquad M = \frac{D}{1 - D} = 29.294, \qquad V = 500.875 \times 29.294 = 14{,}672.69\text{M}$$

$$\text{Change in firm value} = 14{,}672.69 - 12{,}812.08 = 1{,}860.61\text{M}$$

Other published samples reach 1,855.51 to 1,866 by rounding $D$ differently.

### Examiner Report
Candidates needed to compare firm value with and without reinsurance, which required that the same calculations be performed for each scenario. Candidates needed to understand all the components of the method. Full credit required that they accurately show calculations for invested assets, investment income, total earnings, the discount and perpetuity factors, calculation of firm value using those components, and the difference in firm value for the two scenarios. 69% of candidates received full credit.
Common errors included the following:

- Calculation errors
- Incorrect calculation of invested assets (omitting surplus or another component)
- Use of an incorrect investment rate or risk-free rate in a particular calculation
