Why are most property-casualty insurance policies described as unilateral contracts?

- A) Only the insurer's promise is enforceable; the insured just meets conditions
- B) Only the insured may cancel the policy before its expiration date
- C) The insurer drafts the policy and offers it on a take-it-or-leave-it basis
- D) The insurer's payment depends on an uncertain event that may not occur

## Explanation

In a unilateral contract only one party makes an enforceable promise. Under most insurance policies only the insurer makes a legally enforceable promise, to pay covered claims; the insured makes few, if any, and instead must fulfil conditions such as paying premiums and reporting accidents to keep the policy in force. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — the insurer can also cancel; under the ISO Common Policy Conditions it may do so on 10 days' notice for nonpayment and 30 days for any other reason.
- **C** — describes a contract of adhesion.
- **D** — describes an aleatory contract.
