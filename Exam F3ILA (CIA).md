# Exam F3ILA
**F3ILA – Risk Management** is an exam of the individual life and annuities track of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Fellowship (FCIA) pathway.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The aim of this exam is for candidates to develop the following skills:
>
> 1. Understand the main principles and techniques of enterprise risk management (ERM) and economic capital modelling that are relevant to life insurance.
>
> 2. Apply these principles and techniques within the context of life insurance.
>
> 3. Understand how ERM and economic capital modelling links to wider business processes (e.g., business planning, pricing, estimating reserves, and capital setting).
>
> 4. Create and evaluate hypothetical scenarios, including using judgement to assess the implications of possible actions and to develop appropriate proposals or recommendations for life insurers.
>
> Exams are administered as an open-book, four-hour exam requiring analysis in the context of a problem and submission of written responses to specific questions. Candidates will complete the exam through the CIA learning management system. The ranges of weights (in parentheses) attached to the examination topics further below apply to most of the administered exams. Candidates should also recognize that questions often cover multiple learning objectives, including communication.

> [!example]- ERM concept, framework, and process {10%}
>
> 1. Apply the concept of [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]]. (D3)
> 2. Apply the framework for [[Risk Management|risk management]] and control within an [[Insurer|insurer]]. (D3)
> 3. Describe regulatory requirements related to [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]] including [[ORSA|own-risk and solvency assessment]] ([[ORSA]]). (A2)
> 4. Describe the perspectives of [[Rating Agency|credit-rating agencies]]. (B2)
> 5. Reflect [[Data Issues|data issues]] in general and special considerations with respect to [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]]. (C3)
> 6. Demonstrate how to determine and articulate: (C3)
>    - a. [[Risk Appetite|Risk appetite]],
>    - b. [[Risk Identification|Risk identification]],
>    - c. [[Risk Assessment|Risk assessment]],
>    - d. [[Risk Monitoring|Risk monitoring]],
>    - e. [[Risk Appetite|Risk limits]],
>    - f. [[Risk Mitigation|Risk mitigation]],
>    - g. [[Risk Capacity|Risk capacity]],
>    - h. [[Risk Tolerance|Risk tolerances]],
>    - i. [[Desired Risk Profile|Desired risk profile]],
>    - j. [[Risk]] objectives, and
>    - k. [[Risk Reporting|Risk reporting]].
> 7. Assess the implications of financial and other [[Risk|risks]] and opportunities for strategic planning. (C4)
> 8. Describe the [[Risk Management|risk management]] control cycle, including the relevance of [[External Influence|external influences]] and [[Emerging Risk|emerging risks]]. (C2)
> 9. Utilize methods to identify [[Risk|risks]] and their causes and implications. (D4)

> [!example]- Risk categories and risk identification {10%}
>
> 1. Explain what is meant by [[Risk|risk]] and uncertainty, including different definitions and concepts of [[Risk|risk]]. (B2)
> 2. Describe [[Risk Taxonomy|risk taxonomy]], including an awareness of how [[Individual Risk Model|individual risks]] might be categorized in different ways. (B1)
> 3. Describe and evaluate common [[Risk|risks]] faced by [[Life Insurer|life insurers]] including: (B2)
>    - a. [[Market Risk|Market risk]],
>    - b. [[Economic Risk|Economic risk]],
>    - c. [[Interest Rate Risk|Interest rate risk]],
>    - d. [[Foreign Exchange Risk|Foreign exchange risk]],
>    - e. [[Basis Risk|Basis risk]],
>    - f. [[Credit Risk|Credit risk]],
>    - g. [[Counterparty Risk|Counterparty risk]],
>    - h. [[Liquidity Risk|Liquidity risk]],
>    - i. [[Insurance Risk|Insurance risk]],
>    - j. [[Operational Risk|Operational risk]],
>    - k. [[Operational Risk|Legal risk]],
>    - l. [[Regulatory Risk|Regulatory risk]],
>    - m. [[Political Risk|Political risk]],
>    - n. [[Agency Risk|Agency risk]],
>    - o. [[Reputational Risk|Reputational risk]],
>    - p. [[Strategic Risk|Strategic risk]],
>    - q. [[Demographic Risk|Demographic risk]],
>    - r. [[Moral Hazard|Moral hazard]], and
>    - s. Other [[Emerging Risk|emerging risks]].
> 4. Analyze the relationship between [[Systemic Risk|systemic risk]] vs. non-systemic and specific [[Risk|risk]] vs. concentration of [[Risk|risk]]. (B4)
> 5. Identify, categorize, and evaluate potential sources of [[Operational Risk|operational risk]], such as regulatory, marketplace, technology and [[Expense Risk|expense risks]]. (C3)
> 6. Identify, categorize, and evaluate potential sources of [[Risk|risk]] in products including (but not limited to) [[Mortality|mortality]], morbidity, and [[Lapse|lapse]]. (C3)

> [!example]- Economic capital modelling and aggregation of risks {25%}
>
> 1. Describe the extent to which each of the [[Risk|risks]] in 3.3 (above) can be amenable to quantitative analysis. (B2)
> 2. Demonstrate an understanding of the use of [[Correlation|correlation]] measures.
>    - a. Describe [[Enterprise-Wide Risk Aggregation|enterprise-wide risk aggregation]] techniques that incorporate the use of [[Correlation|correlation]]. (C2)
> 3. Apply different [[Correlation|correlation]] measures based on their [[Relative Merit|relative merits]] and implications. (C4)
> 4. Apply copulas as part of the process of [[Modelling Multivariate Risk|modelling multivariate risks]] and evaluate different types of copulas for a given situation. (C5)
> 5. Reflect the importance of the tails of [[Probability Distributions|distributions]] and [[Tail Correlation|tail correlations]]. (C4)
> 6. Describe how [[Extreme-Value Theory|extreme-value theory]] can be used to help [[Model Risk|model risks]] that have a [[Low Probability|low probability]]. (C2)
> 7. Demonstrate how model and [[Parameter Risk|parameter risk]] can be incorporated into an [[Economic Capital|economic capital]] model. (C4)
> 8. Use [[Economic Capital|economic capital]] models in the overall [[ERM Decision-Making Process|ERM decision-making process]]. (C4)
>    - a. Describe the development and use of models for [[Decision-Making|decision-making]] purposes in [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]]. (B2)
>    - b. Demonstrate how the [[Decision-Making Process Consider|decision-making process considers]] the [[Organization's Risk Appetite|organization’s risk appetite]] and [[Corporate Governance|corporate governance]] and builds on the results of [[Stochastic Modelling|stochastic modelling]], scenario analysis, [[Stress Testing|stress testing]], and analysis of model and [[Parameter Risk|parameter risk]]. (D4)
>    - c. Evaluate different types of models for a given purpose. (D5)

>
> 1. Using common [[Risk Measure|risk measures]] (such as VAR, TVAR, and [[Probability|probability]] of ruin), determine [[Risk Exposure|risk exposures]] and tolerances using these measures. (C3)
> 2. Understand the key considerations in deriving and applying [[Economic Capital Modelling|economic capital modelling]] techniques using deterministic and stochastic models. (D2)
> 3. Recommend a specific choice of model based on the results of both quantitative and qualitative analysis of financial and [[Insurance Data|insurance data]]. (D5)
> 4. Describe approaches, including parameterization and [[Model Validation|validation]], for the assessment of [[Regulatory Capital|capital requirements]] for the following [[Risk|risk]] types: (C2)
>    - a. Catastrophe.
>    - b. [[Underwriting]] and pricing.
>    - c. Reserving.
>    - d. [[Credit]].
>    - e. Climate-related.
>    - f. Liquidity; and
>    - g. Operational.
> 5. Describe the practical considerations (including [[Data Availability|data availability]], parameterization, and [[Model Validation|validation]] procedures) that should be borne in mind when [[Undertaking Capital Modelling|undertaking capital modelling]]. (D2)
> 6. Calculate [[Regulatory Capital|regulatory capital]] requirement. (C4)
> 7. Demonstrate understanding of [[Regulator Guidance|regulator guidance]] and context for different approaches to [[Responsible Investment|responsible investment]] and, specifically, the integration of environmental, social, and [[Corporate Governance|governance]] (ESG) factors in the [[Investment Process|investment process]]. (C3)
> 8. Demonstrate an understanding of the underlying issues that constitute factors within each of the ESG areas. ([[C3]])
> 9. Demonstrate an understanding of the [[ESG Market|ESG market]]: relevance, size, scope, key drivers and challenges, and [[Risk|risks]] and opportunities. (C3)

> [!example]- Stress and scenario testing {15%}
>
> 1. Describe the use of scenario analysis and [[Stress Testing|stress testing]] in the [[Risk Measurement Process|risk measurement process]], including the advantages and disadvantages of each. (B2)
> 2. Describe the [[Appointed Actuary's Responsibility|appointed actuary’s responsibilities]] related to [[FCT|financial condition testing]] ([[FCT]]). (A2)
> 3. Explain the primary categories of [[Risk|risk]] associated with [[Climate Change|climate change]] from a [[Life Insurer's|life insurer’s]] perspective, including:
>    - a. [[Transition Risk|Transition risk]]; (A2)
>    - b. [[Physical Risk|Physical risk]] and [[Environmental Risk|environmental risks]]; and (A2)
>    - c. [[Liability Risk|Liability risk]]. (A2)
> 4. Describe the actuary’s role with respect to [[Climate-Change Risk|climate-change risk]]. (B2)

> [!example]- Capital management {10%}
>
> 1. Apply key elements of [[ORSA]] and [[FCT]], specifically: [[Risk Identification|risk identification]] and assessment, quantification of [[Risk|risk]] to [[Regulatory Capital|capital requirements]], [[Board Oversight|board oversight]] and [[Senior Management Responsibility|senior management responsibility]], monitoring, reporting, and [[Internal Control|internal controls]]. (D4)
> 2. Demonstrate an understanding of [[Capital|capital]] calculations:
>    - a. Utilize the concept of economic measures of value and [[Capital|capital]] and their uses in [[Corporate Decision-Making Process|corporate decision-making processes]]. (D5)
>    - b. Evaluate different [[Risk Measure|risk measures]] and [[Economic Capital|capital assessment]] approaches. (D5)
>    - c. Demonstrate the ability to develop a [[Capital|capital]] model for a [[Representative Financial Firm|representative financial firm]]. (D6)
> 3. Compare techniques for [[Allocating Capital|allocating capital]] across an organization. (C4)

> [!example]- Asset/liability management {10%}
>
> 1. For an asset/liability management ([[Asset-Liability Management|ALM]]) model for a block of [[Life Insurance Policy|life insurance policies]] or [[Annuity Contract|annuity contracts]] and a given [[Portfolio|asset portfolio]]:
>    - a. Select and use appropriate assumptions and scenarios (C3);
>    - b. Incorporate dynamic behavior of both assets and liabilities (B2);
>    - c. Incorporate, explain, and apply [[Risk-Mitigation Strategy|risk-mitigation strategies]], including [[Hedging|hedging]] and [[Immunization|immunization]] (C3); and
>    - d. Analyze and evaluate results, including actual vs. [[Projected Difference|projected differences]]. (C5)
> 2. Analyze the application of [[Asset-Liability Management|ALM]] and [[Liability-Driven Investment Principle|liability-driven investment principles]] to [[Investment Policy Statement|investment policy]] and [[Asset Allocation|asset allocation]]. (C5)
> 3. Describe and assess [[Performance Measurement|performance measurement]] methodologies and [[Hedge Risk Metrics|hedge risk metrics]] for [[Portfolio|asset portfolios]] with regards to [[Asset-Liability Management|ALM]].(C5)
> 4. Apply [[Constraint|constraints]] that prevent the optimization of [[Portfolio|asset portfolios]] considering: (C3)
>    - a. Performance objectives of the [[ALM Strategy|ALM strategy]];
>    - b. [[Investment Policy Restriction|Investment policy restrictions]];
>    - c. Available assets;
>    - d. Liquidity; and
>    - e. Regulations.
