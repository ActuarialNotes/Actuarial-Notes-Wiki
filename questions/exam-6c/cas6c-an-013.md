On April 1, a P&C insurer issues a group of one-year contracts, measured under the premium allocation approach. Expected premium of 2,000 is received at issue, and directly attributable acquisition cash flows of 300 are paid at issue. The insurer has elected to expense acquisition cash flows when incurred. The risk is spread evenly over the coverage period, so expected premium receipts are allocated on the basis of the passage of time. Ignore discounting.

Calculate the insurance revenue for this group for the financial year ending December 31.

- A) 500
- B) 1,200
- C) 1,275
- D) 1,500
- E) 2,000

## Explanation

Under the PAA, insurance revenue for a period is the amount of expected premium receipts allocated to that period, on the passage of time unless the expected pattern of release of risk differs significantly. The CIA's educational note on the LRC (section 5.2, footnote 7) notes this is what is commonly called "earned premium."

$$
\text{Insurance revenue} = 2{,}000 \times \frac{9}{12} = 1{,}500
$$

The acquisition cash flows are an **insurance service expense** (expensed in full under the election), not a deduction from revenue. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — 500 is the premium for the three months of coverage still to come. That is the unexpired portion that stays in the LRC, not revenue.
- **B** — 1,200 deducts the 300 of acquisition cash flows from revenue. They are reported as an expense, not netted against revenue.
- **C** — 1,275 deducts nine months of amortized acquisition cash flows. Under deferral that amortization would also be an insurance service expense, so revenue would still be 1,500.
- **E** — 2,000 is the written premium. Revenue is recognized only for the coverage provided in the period.
