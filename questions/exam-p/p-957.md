An insurer models its annual loss ratio $X$ with a beta distribution having $\alpha = 3$ and $\beta = 2$.

Calculate $P(X > 0.8)$.

- A) $0.082$
- B) $0.181$
- C) $0.240$
- D) $0.328$
- E) $0.410$

## Explanation

Both parameters are integers, so the density is an ordinary polynomial. First the normalizing constant:

$$
\begin{align*}
B(3,2) &= \frac{\Gamma(3)\Gamma(2)}{\Gamma(5)} \\
       &= \frac{2! \cdot 1!}{4!} \\
       &= \frac{2}{24} = \frac{1}{12}
\end{align*}
$$

So $f(x) = 12\,x^2(1-x)$ for $0 < x < 1$. Integrate over the tail:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(X > 0.8) &= \int_{0.8}^{1} 12\left(x^2 - x^3\right) dx \\
           &= 12\left[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_{0.8}^{1} \\
           &= 12\left[0.083333 - 0.068267\right] \\
           &= 12(0.015067) \\
           &= \mathbf{0.181}
\end{align*}
$$

There is roughly an 18% chance the loss ratio exceeds 80%. Every Exam P beta question uses integer parameters for exactly this reason — the beta function reduces to factorials and the integral is elementary.
