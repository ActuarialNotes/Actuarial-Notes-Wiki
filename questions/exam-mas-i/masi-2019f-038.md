A modeler creates a cubic spline model with Property Claim Frequency as the response variable and Age of Building Construction as a continuous predictor. The modeler puts knots in at Age = {10, 20, 30, 50}.

The modeler believes that she is overfitting on the ends of the distribution and decides to impose an additional constraint that the curve before the first knot and after the last knot will be linear.

Calculate the number of degrees of freedom used by this new model.

- A) 3
- B) 4
- C) 5
- D) 6
- E) 7

## Explanation

A cubic spline with $K$ knots uses $K + 4$ degrees of freedom. It needs a full cubic (4 coefficients) in the first region, and then one extra term per knot: continuity of the curve and of its first and second derivatives leaves only the cubic coefficient free to change at each knot.

Requiring the curve to be linear before the first knot and after the last knot makes it a *natural* cubic spline. Linearity in an end region removes that region's quadratic and cubic terms, which is two constraints at each end. With $K = 4$ knots:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{df}_{\text{cubic}} &= K + 4 \\
&= 8 \\
\text{df}_{\text{natural}} &= 8 - 2 - 2 \\
&= 4
\end{align*}
$$

This matches the rule that a natural cubic spline with $K$ knots has $K$ degrees of freedom (counting the intercept). The answer is **B**.

*Note: the CAS answer key accepted both **B** and **D** for this question.* D (6) is what you get by counting the linearity requirement as a single constraint at each end, $8 - 2$.
