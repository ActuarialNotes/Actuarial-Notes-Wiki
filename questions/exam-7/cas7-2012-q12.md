Given the following financial information for an insurance company (in \$000,000):

| | Actual 2011 | Projections 2012 | 2013 | 2014 | 2015 | 2016 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net Income | 180 | 190 | 200 | 211 | 222 | 234 |
| Beginning Equity | 2,000 | 2,108 | 2,222 | 2,342 | 2,468 | 2,602 |
| Ending Equity | 2,108 | 2,222 | 2,342 | 2,468 | 2,602 | 2,742 |

- The expected market return on equity for peer insurance companies is 10%.
- The risk-free rate based on US Treasury bill rates is 2%.
- The equity beta, based on peer companies, is 0.85.
- The company's business plan contemplates a 60% plowback ratio.
- CAPM is an appropriate model for determining the company's risk-adjusted discount rate.

Determine the value of this company as of December 31, 2011 based on the dividend discount model and the projections for 2012 through 2016.

### Explanation
Cost of equity from CAPM:

$$k = r_f + \beta\,(r_m - r_f) = 0.02 + 0.85 \times (0.10 - 0.02) = 0.088$$

With a plowback ratio $p = 0.6$, the company pays $1 - p = 40\%$ of net income as dividends. ROE (net income / beginning equity) is a stable 9% throughout the projection period:

| Year | Net income | Dividends = NI × 0.4 | ROE = NI / Beg. equity | PV of dividend = Div / $(1+k)^t$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 190 | 76 | 190/2,108 = 9% | 69.85 |
| 2013 | 200 | 80 | 9% | 67.58 |
| 2014 | 211 | 84.4 | 9% | 65.53 |
| 2015 | 222 | 88.8 | 9% | 63.37 |
| 2016 | 234 | 93.6 | 9% | 61.39 |
| Total | | | | 327.72 |

Select ROE = 0.09, so the terminal growth rate is

$$g = p \times \text{ROE} = 0.6 \times 0.090 = 0.054$$

Terminal value at the end of the forecast horizon, discounted to December 31, 2011:

$$\text{PV(TV)} = \frac{93.6 \times 1.054}{(0.088 - 0.054)(1.088)^5} = 1{,}903.24$$

$$\text{Value} = 327.72 + 1{,}903.24 = 2{,}230.96$$

(Discounting the five dividends in a single expression, the other sample gets 327.73 and a value of 2,230.97.)

### Examiner Report
Many candidates used the free cash flow to equity method to calculate dividends when the question calls for the dividend discount model.  While this produces similar results, the approach is incorrect.

Many candidates assumed that the initial or final ROE's were appropriate for the firm over the forecast horizon rather than evaluating trends in ROE over the six year period.
