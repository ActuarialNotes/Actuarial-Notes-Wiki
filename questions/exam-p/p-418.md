A policyholder purchases car insurance for two years. In a given year, the policyholder's number of car accidents is zero with probability 0.9, exactly one with probability 0.08, and exactly two with probability 0.02. The number of accidents in the first year is independent of the number in the second.

Calculate the probability that the policyholder has one accident in each year, given that the policyholder has a total of exactly two accidents.

- A) $0.006$
- B) $0.042$
- C) $0.151$
- D) $0.262$
- E) $0.960$

## Explanation

Let $M$ and $N$ be the accident counts in the two years. A total of exactly two can happen in three disjoint ways, and independence lets each be a product:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[M=0, N=2] &= (0.9)(0.02) = 0.018 \\
P[M=1, N=1] &= (0.08)(0.08) = 0.0064 \\
P[M=2, N=0] &= (0.02)(0.9) = 0.018
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
P[M + N = 2] = 0.018 + 0.0064 + 0.018 = 0.0424
$$

The conditional probability is the middle case's share of that total:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[M=1, N=1 \mid M+N=2] &= \frac{0.0064}{0.0424} \\
                       &= \mathbf{0.151}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A) is the numerator 0.0064 on its own. The split-one-each outcome is the *least* likely of the three routes to a two-accident total, because a single year producing two accidents (0.02) is more probable than a year producing one (0.08) squared — a good check that the conditional probability should come out well below $1/3$.
