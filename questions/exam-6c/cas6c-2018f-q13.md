The following information is available for two property and casualty insurance companies operating in Canada as at December 31, 2017. All amounts are in thousands of dollars (\$000s).

| Direct Written Premium for PACICC Protected Policies | Alberta | Other Jurisdictions | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company A | 200,000 | 100,000 | 300,000 |
| Company B | 100,000 | 0 | 100,000 |
| Total Participating Insurers | 10,000,000 | 20,000,000 | 30,000,000 |

- Amount advanced by PACICC to and on behalf of insureds of Company B: 100,000
- Amount recovered by Company B from third parties after liquidation: 50,000

## Part a (0.75 points)

Identify three main causes of property and casualty insurer insolvency in Canada.

### Explanation
- Foreign parent
- Rapid growth
- Alleged fraud

Alternatively:

- Deficit loss reserve and inadequate pricing
- Foreign ownership
- Rapid growth

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the main causes of P&C insurer insolvency in Canada and be able to calculate the annual PACICC assessment for a company.

Candidates were expected to know the main causes of Canadian P&C insurer failure.
A common error included:

- Listing company characteristics that play a role in insolvency but were not among the main causes of Canadian P&C insurer insolvency for example, "new entrance," "poor management," or "international exposure."

## Part b (1 point)

Calculate the annual PACICC assessment to be borne by Company A.

### Explanation
Amount advanced, net of recoveries: $100{,}000 - 50{,}000 = 50{,}000$

Company A's share of Alberta direct written premium: $200{,}000 / 10{,}000{,}000 = 0.02$

$$0.02 \times 50{,}000 = 1{,}000$$

This is not capped by 1.5% of 200,000, which is 3,000. The assessment borne by A is **1,000** (in thousands).

Alternatively:

$$X = (\text{amount advanced} - \text{recovery}) \times \frac{\text{DWP}_A}{\text{DWP}_{\text{all}}} \quad \text{(only in Alberta)}$$

$$(100{,}000 - 50{,}000) \times \frac{200{,}000}{10{,}000{,}000} = 1{,}000$$

$$\text{Assessment}_A = \min(1.5\% \times \text{DWP}, X) = 1{,}000$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the formula for a company's annual PACICC assessment.
Candidates were also expected to be able to identify and utilize the relevant jurisdiction in calculating a company's share of the shortfall.

Common errors included:

- Calculating market share using data from "other jurisdictions" or "total." The inputs to the formula should be that of the participating jurisdiction – Alberta in this case.
- Excluding DWP of company B from the market DWP.  The DWP of all participating insurers of the relevant participating jurisdiction should be included in the market DWP.
- Not demonstrating that the result is less than the maximum annual levy.
- Using the total DWP for Company A to calculate the maximum instead of its DWP from the relevant participating jurisdiction.
