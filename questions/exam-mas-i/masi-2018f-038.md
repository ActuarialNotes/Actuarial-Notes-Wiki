You are given a series of plots of a single data set containing two variables:

![Five scatter plots, Plot I to Plot V, of the same two-variable data set: an upward-sloping cloud of about 25 points, x from 0 to 1 and y from about 0.03 to 0.5, each plot with a PC1 line (solid) and a PC2 line (dashed) crossing near the centre of the cloud. Plot I: PC1 is a straight line along the cloud (slope about 0.34); PC2 is a straight line crossing it steeply downward (slope about -3). Plot II: the same two straight lines with the labels swapped, the dashed PC2 along the cloud and the solid PC1 crossing it steeply. Plot III: PC1 along the cloud as in Plot I; PC2 vertical. Plot IV: PC1 horizontal at y of about 0.24; PC2 vertical at x of about 0.5. Plot V: PC1 a concave curve rising through the cloud; PC2 a decreasing convex curve.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masi-2018f-q38-pca-plots.png)

Determine which of above plots accurately represent the $1^{\text{st}}$ and $2^{\text{nd}}$ principal components (PC1 and PC2, respectively) of this dataset.

- A) I
- B) II
- C) III
- D) IV
- E) V

## Explanation

The principal components must satisfy two properties: **PC1 points in the direction of maximum variance** (the elongation of the scatter), and **PC2 is orthogonal to PC1** (the next direction of variance). They are straight lines (linear combinations of the variables).

- Plot I satisfies both: PC1 lies along the positive-sloping cloud and PC2 is perpendicular to it. ✓
- Plot II has PC1 and PC2 reversed (PC1 is not along the maximum-variance direction).
- Plot III has PC1 along the cloud but a vertical PC2, which is not orthogonal to the sloped PC1.
- Plot IV's lines are orthogonal (PC1 horizontal, PC2 vertical), but its PC1 is not the direction of maximum variance: the cloud is elongated along its upward slope, not along the horizontal axis.
- Plot V draws the components as curves, but principal components are linear.

Only Plot I is correct, so the answer is **A**.
