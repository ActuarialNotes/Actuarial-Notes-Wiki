Given the following data:

| Calendar / Accident Year | Earned Premium ($000s) |
|---|---|
| 2011 | 100 |
| 2012 | 150 |
| 2013 | 150 |
| 2014 | 200 |

Reported Claims ($000s):

| Calendar / Accident Year | 12 Months | 24 Months | 36 Months | 48 Months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 26 | 40 | 48 | 51 |
| 2012 | 40 | 43 | 51 | |
| 2013 | 44 | 67 | | |
| 2014 | 69 | | | |

- The a priori expected claim ratio for all accident years is 51%.
- There is no development after 48 months.

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate ultimate claims for accident year 2014 using the reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique. Justify all selections.

### Explanation
Age-to-age factors from the reported claims triangle:

| AY | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 1.538 | 1.200 | 1.063 |
| 2012 | 1.075 | 1.186 | |
| 2013 | 1.523 | | |

**Justification.** The $12$–$24$ factor for AY 2012 ($1.075$) sits far below the other two, which look consistent with one another; treating it as an anomaly and selecting the average of AY 2011 and AY 2013 gives $1.531$. At $24$–$36$ there are only two points and nothing to distinguish them, so a straight average is used, and $36$–$48$ has a single observation.

| | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selection | 1.531 | 1.193 | 1.063 |
| CDF | 1.941 | 1.268 | 1.063 |

Applying the Bornhuetter-Ferguson formula to accident year 2014, with earned premium of $\$200$k and the a priori claim ratio of $51\%$:

$$U = 69{,}000 + 0.51 \times 200{,}000 \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{1.941}\right) = 118{,}450$$

Alternatively, a candidate unwilling to discard a third of a small data set could select the straight average of all three $12$–$24$ factors, $1.379$, giving a CDF of $1.748$ and

$$U = 69{,}000 + 0.51 \times 200{,}000 \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{1.748}\right) = 112{,}600$$

Both were accepted; what mattered was that the selection was justified.

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to know how to calculate ultimate losses using the BF method given a reported loss triangle, the earned premium for the year, and an expected loss ratio. The candidate was also asked to justify all selections, which in the context of this question, applies mainly to their selection of LDFs at each age.

To obtain full credit, candidates were expected to do the following:

- Calculate the loss development factor triangle
- Provide some reasonable justification for their LDF selections
- Calculate the CDF
- Apply the CDF using the BF method to calculate ultimate loss The most common mistake candidates made was in their justification, either by just restating the method used or by omitting any kind of justification. Credit was given for a wide variety of answers as long as the justification supported the selection.
In general, candidates performed very well on this part of the question.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Discuss the applicability of the Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique when cumulative claim development factors are less than 1.00.

### Explanation
The Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique can be read as a credibility weighting of the development technique and the expected claims technique, with weight $Z = 1/\text{CDF}$ on the development result. A CDF below $1.00$ gives $Z > 1$, which is not a valid credibility weight, so that interpretation breaks down.

In practice the method can still be used: cap the CDFs at $1.00$, apply the method as it stands and accept the odd percentage-reported figures, or fall back on a different technique. Any of these positions earned credit if it was supported.

### Examiner Report
The majority of the candidates who answered this question performed well. The most common answer was discussing how the credibility weight interpretation of the BF method was no longer applicable and how this should be handled. Candidates could argue the BF method was no longer applicable, was applicable as is, or was applicable with adjustments provided the response was well supported.
A common mistake was mentioning that because LDFs <1 cause negative IBNR the BF method should not be used. This is not unique to the BF method and further discussion was needed for full credit.
