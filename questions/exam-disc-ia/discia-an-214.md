Two problems arise at a company's 401(k) plan. Participants sue the plan's investment committee, alleging it kept high-fee funds when cheaper identical funds were available. Separately, the plan's bookkeeper is found to have stolen \$50,000 from the plan. Which coverage responds to each?

- A) D&O insurance for the suit, and fiduciary liability insurance for the theft
- B) Fiduciary liability insurance for both
- C) The ERISA bond for both
- D) Fiduciary liability insurance for the suit, and the ERISA bond for the theft

## Explanation

ERISA makes a fiduciary personally liable for plan losses caused by a breach, such as imprudent investment choices or excessive fees, and fiduciary liability insurance protects the fiduciaries against such claims. ERISA separately requires everyone handling plan funds to be bonded against loss from fraud or dishonesty, and that bond pays the plan when someone steals.

- **A** sends the suit to D&O, which usually excludes ERISA claims, and the theft to the fiduciary policy, which does not protect the plan against dishonesty.
- **B** treats fiduciary liability as crime insurance. It covers honest but imprudent decisions, not theft.
- **C** treats the bond as liability insurance for the fiduciaries, which it is not.
