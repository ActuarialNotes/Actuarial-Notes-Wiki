To discourage traffic violations, county C charges each driver a fine of 1 for the driver's first ticket of this year, 2 for the driver's second ticket of this year, and generally $n$ for the driver's $n$th ticket of this year.

The number of traffic tickets a certain driver in county C receives this year is Poisson distributed with mean 4.

Calculate the expected value of the total fine this driver is charged for tickets this year.

- A) $6$
- B) $8$
- C) $10$
- D) $12$
- E) $14$

## Explanation

Let $N$ be the number of tickets, Poisson with mean 4. If the driver gets $N$ tickets, the total fine is $1 + 2 + \cdots + N$, which is the familiar sum of the first $N$ integers:

$$
\text{Total fine} = \frac{N(N+1)}{2}
$$

The fine is *quadratic* in $N$, so its expectation needs the second moment, not just the mean:

$$
\begin{align*}
E\left[\frac{N(N+1)}{2}\right] &= \tfrac{1}{2}E[N^2] + \tfrac{1}{2}E[N]
\end{align*}
$$

Get $E[N^2]$ from the variance. A Poisson distribution has variance equal to its mean, so $\text{Var}(N) = 4$ and

$$
\begin{align*}
E[N^2] &= \text{Var}(N) + \bigl(E[N]\bigr)^2 \\
       &= 4 + 16 = 20
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
E\left[\frac{N(N+1)}{2}\right] &= \tfrac{1}{2}(20) + \tfrac{1}{2}(4) \\
                               &= \mathbf{12}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 10, is $\tfrac{1}{2}E[N]\bigl(E[N]+1\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{2}(4)(5)$ — plugging the mean into the fine formula instead of taking the expectation of the formula. Because the fine grows quadratically, that substitution understates the answer: the occasional very bad year contributes disproportionately.
