Each of three people is holding a biased coin that has probability 0.25 of landing heads. They toss the coins simultaneously. After each round of tosses, if the outcomes are all heads or all tails, they will continue to another round; otherwise, they will stop.

Calculate the probability that there will be exactly five rounds of tosses.

- A) $0.0003$
- B) $0.0029$
- C) $0.0206$
- D) $0.0438$
- E) $0.1030$

## Explanation

Find the probability that a single round leads to **continuation** — all three coins matching:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{continue}] &= P[\text{all heads}] + P[\text{all tails}] \\
                   &= (0.25)^3 + (0.75)^3 \\
                   &= 0.015625 + 0.421875 \\
                   &= 0.4375
\end{align*}
$$

Exactly five rounds means the first four rounds all continued and the fifth did not. Rounds are independent, so

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{exactly 5 rounds}] &= (0.4375)^4(1 - 0.4375) \\
                           &= (0.036636)(0.5625) \\
                           &= \mathbf{0.0206}
\end{align*}
$$

The stopping rule is what makes this geometric: each round independently ends the process with probability 0.5625.

Choice (D), 0.0438, is $(0.5625)^4(0.4375)$ — the continue and stop probabilities swapped. $(0.4375)^4 = 0.0366$ on its own, four continuations without requiring the fifth round to stop, is the probability of *at least* five rounds, and is not an option. Note also that "all tails" is far more likely than "all heads" with a biased coin, and dropping that term would give a wildly different continuation probability.
