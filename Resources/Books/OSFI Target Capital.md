## I. Capital in OSFI's risk assessment

OSFI's process starts with the **inherent risk** in each significant activity and the **quality of risk management** applied to it, giving a **net risk** rating and a direction for each activity. Those are combined by relative importance into the **Overall Net Risk (ONR)**, and OSFI then sets a **Composite Risk Rating** after considering earnings, capital and liquidity against the ONR.

Regulatory capital is one input, not the assessment. OSFI's Capital Assessment Criteria also weigh the **adequacy of capital to support the risk profile and business plan including risks not fully captured by the capital guidelines**, the ability to **access capital at reasonable rates**, the **quality of capital**, and the strength of the insurer's **capital management policy**.

## II. Regulatory capital — the three levels

The [[Insurance Companies Act]] requires federally regulated insurers to maintain adequate capital, and branches an adequate margin of assets in Canada over liabilities in Canada. The Capital Guidelines — MICAT for mortgage insurers, **[[MCT]]** for other P&C insurers, LICAT for life insurers — provide the framework within which the Superintendent assesses adequacy. (For P&C the requirement is the *minimum capital required*; for life it is the **[[Base Solvency Buffer|base solvency buffer]]**.)

- **Minimum.** If capital resources approached or fell below the minimum, OSFI "would be very concerned about the ongoing viability of the insurer and/or the level of risk to policyholders and creditors."
- **[[Supervisory Target Capital Ratio|Supervisory target]].** Set above the minimum to give an early signal, so that intervention is timely and there is a reasonable expectation that action can succeed. Falling to or below it indicates **material safety and soundness concerns** and generally attracts **stage 1 early-warning intervention**.
- **[[Internal Target Capital Ratio|Internal target]].** Industry-wide minimums and supervisory targets rest on simplifying assumptions and are **not tailored to an individual insurer's risk profile**, so an insurer "should not unduly rely on these regulatory capital measures" and must set its own target through its **[[ORSA]]**. Internal targets must be **above** supervisory targets; OSFI must be **notified when they change**, and **promptly informed** if the insurer falls below one, with a plan to restore capital within a relatively short period. Parent or head-office guarantees and potential future capital injections do not substitute for capital held.

## IV. Capital management policy

Capital is managed to maintain financial strength, absorb losses in adverse conditions, allow for growth, and — **in extreme cases such as imminent failure or insolvency — provide sufficient assets to transfer or run off policyholder obligations and pay creditor claims**. The policy should assign clear roles and responsibilities and state capital adequacy goals relative to risk, including the internal targets, supported by the ORSA and the business plan. Board expectations are in the [[OSFI Corporate Governance|Corporate Governance Guideline]].
