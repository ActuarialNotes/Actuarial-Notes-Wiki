## Adoption of the 2020 Agreement Respecting Multi-Jurisdictional Pension Plans
- The mission of CAPSA is to facilitate an efficient and effective pension regulatory system in Canada, and it developed the 2020 Agreement to coordinate and harmonize [[Canadian Pension Legislation|pension regulation]] across Canada.
- Alberta, British Columbia, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Quebec, Saskatchewan and Canada signed the 2020 Agreement, which governs the administration and regulation of multi-jurisdictional pension plans between their jurisdictions effective July 1, 2020.
- It replaces the 2016 Agreement, the earlier 1968 reciprocal agreement and similar federal-provincial bilateral agreements, as far as they apply between the signatories; the 1968 agreement and bilateral agreements remain in effect for Manitoba and Newfoundland and Labrador.
- Funding: ongoing funding of a multi-jurisdictional plan applies only the major authority's legislated funding rules (section 6(2)), and a major authority with legislated annuity discharge funding rules may apply them in place of a minor authority's (sections 6(5) and (6)).
- Asset allocation on plan wind up or other major plan events is amended to accommodate legislative changes that eliminate traditional solvency funding requirements for some plans (sections 10 to 16).
- A change of major authority may be cancelled if the existing major authority's jurisdiction again has the plurality of active plan members before the change takes effect (sections 5(3.1), (6) and (6.1)).
- The waiting period before a government can withdraw from the agreement is shortened from three years to eighteen months (sections 21(2), (3) and (4)).
- CAPSA is working on a Commentary Guide for the 2020 Agreement and administrative procedures to facilitate its implementation.
