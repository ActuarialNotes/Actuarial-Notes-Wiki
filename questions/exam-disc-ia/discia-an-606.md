A distributor's BPP covers its building and business personal property at one described location, with a 70% coinsurance percentage. Twenty days after buying a second warehouse across town, which it has not yet reported to the insurer, a fire destroys the warehouse. What does the BPP pay for the warehouse?

- A) Up to \$250,000, because the newly acquired property extension covers buildings for 30 days
- B) Up to \$100,000, because a newly acquired location is covered only for business personal property
- C) The full building limit, because the building item covers any building the insured owns
- D) Nothing, because the coverage extensions do not apply under this coinsurance percentage

## Explanation

Coverage for a newly acquired building comes only from the newly acquired or constructed property extension. Every BPP coverage extension applies only if the declarations show a coinsurance percentage of **80% or more** or a value reporting period symbol. At 70% there is no extension, and the warehouse is not the building described in the declarations, so nothing is paid. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — This would be right with 80% or higher coinsurance: \$250,000 per building for up to 30 days. It overlooks the extension's precondition.
- **B** — \$100,000 is the extension's limit for business personal property, not buildings, and it too needs 80% coinsurance.
- **C** — The building item covers the building described in the declarations, not every building the insured owns.
