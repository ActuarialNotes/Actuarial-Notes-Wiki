A state regulates homeowners rates under a prior approval law with a 30-day deemer provision. An insurer files a rate increase, and the insurance department neither approves nor disapproves it. When may the insurer use the new rates?

- A) On the day it files them, because the department retains the right to disapprove them later
- B) Within a set period before filing, as long as it files them afterward
- C) Never, because a prior approval law requires the department's written approval
- D) Once 30 days have passed without a denial, when the filing is deemed approved

## Explanation

Under prior approval, rates must be filed with and approved by the department before they are used, but approval can come through a **deemer** provision: the filing is treated as approved if it is not denied within the stated number of days. After 30 days without a denial, the rates may be used.

- **A** describes file and use, where the insurer files and uses the rates without waiting.
- **B** describes use and file, where rates are filed within a set period after they take effect.
- **C** ignores the deemer, which is exactly what the department's silence triggers.
