Frank, the frequentist, fits a model to claim frequency using frequentist methods. Bob, a Bayesian, fits a model to claim frequency using MCMC.

- Frank reports a 95% confidence interval for claim frequency of (1.45, 2.75).
- Bob reports a 95% percentile interval for claim frequency of (1.50, 2.40).

You are provided with the following three statements:

- I. Frank's analysis indicates that claim frequency is between 1.45 and 2.75 with probability 95%.
- II. 95% of Bob's posterior sample is between 1.50 and 2.40.
- III. The value of the 96th percentile of Bob's posterior sample is not within Bob's interval.

Determine which of the statements above are true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I is false.** This is the classic misreading of a frequentist confidence interval. To Frank the claim frequency is a fixed unknown constant, not a random variable, so it is not "between 1.45 and 2.75 with probability 95%." The 95% refers to the *procedure*: intervals built this way cover the true value in 95% of repeated samples.

**II is true.** A 95% percentile interval is constructed as the interval between the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the posterior sample, so by construction 95% of the sampled values fall inside it.

**III is false.** Bob's interval runs from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentile, and $2.5 < 96 < 97.5$, so the 96th percentile lies *inside* the interval.

Only statement II is true, so the answer is **B**.
