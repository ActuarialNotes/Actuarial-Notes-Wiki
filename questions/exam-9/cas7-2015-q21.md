## Part a (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two benefits of using copulas to express correlation from joint loss distributions.

### Explanation
Any two distinct benefits, e.g.:

- A copula can join any marginal distributions, regardless of the family they come from.
- It can reflect correlation that varies across the distribution — e.g. risks loosely correlated at low percentiles but strongly correlated in the tail — rather than a single linear correlation.
- It imposes correlation between the marginals without assumptions about causality, and gives flexibility in quantifying tail exposure.
- Dependencies can be reported to external parties (e.g. a rating agency) without disclosing the underlying loss distributions.
- It facilitates simulation of joint events.

### Examiner Report
The vast majority of candidates received full credit; almost all candidates received at least some credit.
In order to receive full credit candidates needed to provide two distinct benefits of using copulas.
Some candidates did not receive full credit when the second benefit they listed was essentially the same as the first benefit.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Identify a copula that is more appropriate than the normal copula for modeling insurance loss at the portfolio level, and briefly describe a feature of this copula that makes it more appropriate than the normal distribution for modeling insurance loss data.

### Explanation
- The **heavy right tail (HRT) copula**: light left tail and heavy right tail, so it reflects the increase in correlation of insurance losses in the right tail (during extreme events); or
- The **Gumbel copula**: a heavier right tail than the normal copula, which better reflects the skewness of insurance losses.

### Examiner Report
Over 95% of candidates received full credit on this part.  The candidate needed to recall one copula that is more appropriate for modeling insurance than the Normal copula and briefly describe why it is better. The most common error for this problem was candidates who mistakenly selected Frank's Copula as having a fatter tail than the Normal, which is incorrect. Other candidates only named a copula and failed to describe a feature of the chosen copula that would make it more appropriate than a Normal one.
