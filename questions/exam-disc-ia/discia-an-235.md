A plaintiff free of fault proves \$500,000 of damages against two defendants. Defendant A is 80% at fault and is insolvent and uninsured. Defendant B is 20% at fault and insured. The state applies joint and several liability. How much can the plaintiff collect from B?

- A) \$100,000
- B) \$250,000
- C) \$400,000
- D) \$500,000

## Explanation

Under joint and several liability each defendant is liable for the full extent of the injuries, so the plaintiff may collect the whole judgment from any one of them:

$$
P_B = 500{,}000 - P_A = 500{,}000 - 0 = \mathbf{500{,}000}
$$

B can then seek contribution from A, but against an insolvent defendant that right is worth nothing, so B's insurer bears the whole loss.

- **A** is B's own 20% share, which is what B would pay under several (proportionate) liability.
- **B** splits the damages equally between the two defendants, which no rule here does.
- **C** is A's 80% share, the amount B pays in place of A, but it leaves out B's own share.
