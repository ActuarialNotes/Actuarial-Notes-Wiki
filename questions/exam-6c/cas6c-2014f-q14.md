The following information is available for a property and casualty insurance company that writes both personal and commercial automobile business. All amounts are in thousands dollars (\$000s).

| Assets | Balance Sheet Value | Asset Risk Factor |
|---|---|---|
| Bond | 200,000 | 0% |
| Preferred Stock | 50,000 | 15% |
| Common Stock | 50,000 | 15% |
| Mortgage Loans | 50,000 | 5% |
| Agent Balances Receivables | 8,000 | 5% |
| Reinsurance Recoverables | 40,000 | 10% |

| Factor | Commercial Automobile | Personal Automobile |
|---|---|---|
| Deficiency Factor | 1.05 | 1.08 |
| Discount Factor | 0.89 | 0.88 |
| Baseline Capital Factor for Loss Reserve | 0.38 | 0.38 |
| Baseline Capital Factor for Premium | 0.36 | 0.39 |
| Company Size Factor | 0.95 | 0.95 |
| Company Stability Factor | 1.10 | 1.15 |
| Company Profit Factor | 1.02 | 1.00 |

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Spread of risk factor | 16% |
| Potential market depreciation of fixed income securities after 120 bps interest rate rise | 29,000 |
| Liquid assets | 350,000 |
| Gross PML | 30,000 |
| Credit Risk | 5.4 |
| Net written premium for commercial automobile | 11,000 |
| Net written premium for personal automobile | 25,000 |
| Carried reserve for commercial automobile | 21,000 |
| Carried reserve for personal automobile | 77,000 |
| Diversification factor for loss reserves | 0.9357 |
| Diversification factor for premium | 0.9083 |
| Growth factor | 1.0 |
| Adjusted surplus (APHS) | 55,000 |

Calculate the following under A. M. Best's Capital Adequacy Ratio (BCAR) rules:

## Part a (1.5 points)

Asset risk

### Explanation
$$\text{Investment Risk} = (200 \times 0\% + 50 \times 15\% + 50 \times 15\% + 50 \times 5\%) \times (1 + \text{Spread of Risk Factor}) = 17.5 \times 1.16 = 20.3$$

$$\text{PML/Liquid Assets} = 30/350 = 8.57\% < 10\% \text{ of minimum Catastrophe Exposure}$$

$$\text{Interest Rate Risk} = \text{Potential Market Depreciation} \times \%\,\text{Catastrophe Exposure} = 29 \times 10\% = 2.9$$

Minimum Reinsurance Dependence Required Capital $= 5.4$

$$\text{Asset Risk} = \text{Investment Risk} + \text{Interest Rate Risk} + \text{Credit Risk} = 20.3 + 2.9 + 5.4 = 28.6$$

## Part b (1.25 points)

Underwriting risk

### Explanation
Loss and LAE Reserve Required Capital:

$$21 \times 1.05 \times 0.89 \times 0.38 \times 0.95 \times 1.1 + 77 \times 1.08 \times 0.88 \times 0.38 \times 0.95 \times 1.15 = 38.17$$

(Company Profit Factor is not used for Loss and LAE Reserve Risk)

Growth Factor $= 1$

$$\text{Loss and LAE Reserve Risk} = 38.17 \times 1 \times 0.9357 = 35.72$$

Net Premiums Written Required Capital:

$$11 \times 0.36 \times 0.95 \times 1.02 + 25 \times 0.39 \times 0.95 \times 1.0 = 13.1$$

(Company Stability Factor is not used for Net Premiums Written Risk)

Growth Factor $= 1$

$$\text{Net Premiums Written Risk} = 13.1 \times 1 \times 0.9083 = 11.90$$

Assume Business Risk $= 0$

$$\text{Underwriting Risk} = 35.72 + 11.90 = 47.62$$

## Part c (0.75 points)

BCAR ratio assuming there is no business risk and that the risk covariance can reduce capital by 35%.

### Explanation
$$\text{Gross Required Capital} = 28.6 + 35.72 + 11.90 = 76.22$$

$$\text{Net Required Capital} = 76.22 \times (1 - 35\%) = 49.54$$

$$\text{BCAR} = \text{APHS}/\text{NRC} = 55/49.54 = 111\%$$

### Examiner Report
- Candidates are expected to understand the composition of the asset risk and identify the associated figures given in the question for calculation. Candidates are also expected to understand and calculate the underwriting risk as well as BCAR ratio formula.
- Candidates generally did better in parts b and c than part a.
- Common mistakes include
  - Missing the spread of risk factor in the calculation of the investment risk;
  - Failing to consider the minimum catastrophe exposure;
  - Failing to consider credit risk in asset risk calculation.
