Let $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ represent three independent losses with common density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{6}e^{-x/6}, & \text{for } 0 < x < \infty \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Let $W$ represent the minimum of the losses.

Calculate $E(W)$.

- A) $1/6$
- B) $1/3$
- C) $1/2$
- D) $2$
- E) $6$

## Explanation

Each loss is exponential with mean 6, so its distribution function is $F(x) = 1 - e^{-x/6}$.

The minimum exceeds $x$ exactly when all three losses do, and independence lets those probabilities multiply:

$$
\begin{align*}
F_{\min}(x) &= 1 - \bigl[1 - F(x)\bigr]^3 \\
            &= 1 - \left(e^{-x/6}\right)^3 \\
            &= 1 - e^{-x/2}
\end{align*}
$$

That is again an exponential distribution, now with mean 2. So

$$
E(W) = \mathbf{2}
$$

The general rule is worth remembering: the minimum of $n$ independent exponential variables is exponential with the **rates added**, so with three identical rates of $1/6$ the minimum has rate $1/2$ and mean $6/3 = 2$. Choice (E), 6, is the mean of a single loss; choice (A), $1/6$, is the rate rather than the mean.
