Each of ten homeowners independently has the same probability of experiencing at least one loss this year. Eight of these ten homeowners are insured.

Three of the ten homeowners experience at least one loss.

Calculate the probability that at least two of these three homeowners are insured.

- A) $0.384$
- B) $0.467$
- C) $0.700$
- D) $0.896$
- E) $0.933$

## Explanation

The key observation is that the common loss probability is never needed. Because every homeowner has the *same* probability of a loss, whether or not they are insured, the three who suffer losses are a simple random sample of size 3 from the ten.

The population splits into 8 insured and 2 uninsured, so the count of insured among the three is hypergeometric. "At least two" means exactly 2 or exactly 3:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \geq 2] &= \frac{\binom{8}{2}\binom{2}{1}}{\binom{10}{3}} + \frac{\binom{8}{3}\binom{2}{0}}{\binom{10}{3}} \\
            &= \frac{(28)(2)}{120} + \frac{(56)(1)}{120} \\
            &= \frac{56 + 56}{120} \\
            &= \mathbf{0.9333}
\end{align*}
$$

Since 80% of homeowners are insured, having at least two of three insured is very likely. The only outcome against it is that both uninsured homeowners are among the three, with probability $\binom{2}{2}\binom{8}{1}/\binom{10}{3} = 8/120 = 0.0667 = 1 - 0.9333$.
