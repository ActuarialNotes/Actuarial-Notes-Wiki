## Part a (1 point)

Two techniques for setting reinsurance risk loads are the swap technique and the option technique. Describe the investment strategies that underlie each.

### Explanation
**Swap technique:** the reinsurer takes the allocated assets out of the target investment and invests the funds (premium and allocated assets) in the risk-free investment instead — exchanging the higher, risky target return for the lower risk-free return, so the funds grow at the risk-free rate until losses are paid. The advantage is the reduction in risk; the disadvantage is the loss of investment income in excess of the risk-free rate. Its IRR equation is

$$A(1 + IRR) = (P + A)(1 + r_f) - w_l$$

**Option technique:** the reinsurer leaves the funds in the target investment and also purchases a put option, which gives it the right to sell the investment at a price that provides a rate of return equal to the risk-free rate. If the actual return is below the risk-free rate the reinsurer exercises the option, so the option eliminates the downside risk of the investment strategy while keeping the upside. The disadvantage is the cost of the option, which reduces the funds to invest. Its IRR equation is

$$A(1 + IRR) = \left(\frac{P + A}{1 + r}\right)(1 + i) - w_l$$

where $r$ is the per-unit cost of the option and $i$ is the put-protected return.

### Examiner Report
1. Swap: For full credit, candidates must (at least) state exchange of risky asset for risk-free asset (or some notion of exchanging a higher risky return for lower risk free return).
2. Option: For full credit, candidate must (at least) state that (a) the insurer invested in target investment and (b) bought a put option to (c) guarantee at least a risk free return

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly describe the two constraints that must be satisfied in both the swap and option techniques.

### Explanation
- **Safety constraint:** the funds available to pay claims at the end of the year must be at least equal to the safety level — an amount of loss chosen at a high percentile of the loss distribution — so the reinsurer can pay losses up to that level: $(1 + y_t)F > S$.
- **Variance constraint:** the return on the combined reinsurance contract and investment strategy must be no more volatile than the direct investment of the allocated assets in the target (risky) investment alone: the standard deviation of the IRR $\le \sigma_y$.

### Examiner Report
1. In general, the candidate must state both sides of the constraint and be specific about what is being constrained.
2. For example:
    1. Funds available must be greater than some safety level at the end of the period (acceptable).
    2. Must be greater than safety level (unacceptable).
3. A lot of candidates did not get credit for the variance constraint portion of the question. Some common mistakes are listed below:
    1. Variance of losses less than variance of returns on target investment - incorrect since this is only true for the swap technique but not the option technique, but the constraint should apply to both techniques
    2. Variance of investments less than variance of target investment – it is unclear whether the candidate included the investment in the reinsurance policy (which would be correct), or just the investment in the financial instruments (which would be incorrect).
    3. Variance of IRR less than variance of return on target investment – this was a tough call, because this would have been taken verbatim from the paper. However, the paper would have set the context for this wording. At the end of the day, we decided to not award credit for this formulation of the answer because the candidate has not made it clear that the IRR is the IRR of the combined investment + reinsurance policy, and not just the IRR of the investment alone (to be consistent with the grades awarded in pt. 2 above).
4. Candidates who only wrote down the relevant formulas with no explanations whatsoever of the formulas also did not receive credit for their answers as the question specifically requested candidates to "Briefly ***describe***".

## Part c (0.5 points)

A reinsurer has calculated indications for allocated asset and risk load amounts for all four combinations of financial techniques and constraints. Identify the steps the reinsurer would go through to select the risk load for this treaty.

### Explanation
1. Within each technique, choose the constraint that requires the higher assets as the dominant constraint — that asset level ensures both constraints are met.
2. Calculate the risk load for each technique using the chosen assets.
3. Compare the risk loads of the two techniques and select the smaller one — the most economically efficient way of meeting all the constraints, and the more competitive premium.

### Examiner Report
1. There are two common mistakes made in this part:
    1. doing the steps in the incorrect order (i.e. selecting technique first and then comparing the results of the constraints), or
    2. selecting the min assets (between techniques) after selecting the constraint that results in the max assets for each technique.
