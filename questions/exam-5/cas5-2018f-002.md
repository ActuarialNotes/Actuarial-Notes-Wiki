Given the following premium and loss information:

**Premium Transactions**

| Policy | Original Effective Date | Original Expiration Date | Transaction Effective Date | Full-Term Premium (\$) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | July 1, 2016 | June 30, 2017 | July 1, 2016 | 800 | Start of New Policy |
| A | July 1, 2016 | June 30, 2017 | April 1, 2017 | 400 | Additional Premium for Endorsement |
| B | April 1, 2017 | March 31, 2018 | April 1, 2017 | 1,000 | Start of New Policy |
| C | October 1, 2017 | September 30, 2018 | October 1, 2017 | 500 | Start of New Policy |
| C | October 1, 2017 | September 30, 2018 | April 1, 2018 | N/A | Policy Canceled |

**Loss Transactions**

| Policy | Accident Date | Payment Date | Loss Payment (\$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | October 1, 2016 | October 15, 2016 | 500 |
| A | January 1, 2017 | January 15, 2017 | 200 |
| B | October 1, 2017 | January 15, 2018 | 500 |
| C | January 1, 2018 | January 15, 2018 | 750 |

- Each claim is closed on the payment date.

## Part a (0.75 point)

Calculate the 2017 calendar year earned premium.

### Answer

\$1,375

### Explanation

Earn each transaction over the portion of its term that falls in calendar year 2017.

- **Policy A (base):** annual policy 7/1/2016–6/30/2017, so half of the \$800 term falls in 2017:

$$800 \times \tfrac{6}{12} = 400$$

- **Policy A (endorsement):** the \$400 is a *full-term* premium, so it must be prorated for the 4/1/2017–6/30/2017 period the endorsement is actually in effect — 3 months of the 12-month term, all inside 2017:

$$400 \times \tfrac{3}{12} = 100$$

- **Policy B:** 4/1/2017–3/31/2018, so 9 months are earned in 2017:

$$1{,}000 \times \tfrac{9}{12} = 750$$

- **Policy C:** 10/1/2017–9/30/2018, so 3 months are earned in 2017. The cancellation is effective 4/1/2018 and does not affect CY 2017:

$$500 \times \tfrac{3}{12} = 125$$

$$\text{CY 2017 EP} = 400 + 100 + 750 + 125 = 1{,}375$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to earn the Policy A, B and C premium transactions for calendar year 2017 based on their effective and expiration dates, prorate the full-term Policy A endorsement premium for the partial year the endorsement was in effect, exclude the Policy C cancellation (effective after CY 2017), and sum the result.

Common mistakes included not prorating the full-term endorsement premium for Policy A, and earning the Policy A base premium and endorsement premium over the same time period. No credit was given for assuming the endorsement premium was already prorated — the question identifies it as a full-term premium.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Calculate the 2017 policy year loss ratio evaluated at December 31, 2018.

### Answer

100%

### Explanation

Policy year 2017 contains only policies **effective** in 2017: Policy B (4/1/2017) and Policy C (10/1/2017). Policy A was effective 7/1/2016, so it — and its endorsement — belong to policy year 2016.

**Premium.** Policy B contributes its full \$1,000 term premium. Policy C was canceled effective 4/1/2018, having run 6 months of its 12-month term:

$$1{,}000 + 500 \times \tfrac{6}{12} = 1{,}000 + 250 = 1{,}250$$

**Losses.** All losses on PY 2017 policies, regardless of when they were paid:

$$500 \ (\text{Policy B}) + 750 \ (\text{Policy C}) = 1{,}250$$

$$\text{PY 2017 Loss Ratio} = \frac{1{,}250}{1{,}250} = 100\%$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to determine that only Policies B and C belong to policy year 2017, sum the premium and loss transactions for those policies, adjust the Policy C premium for the mid-term cancellation, and calculate the loss ratio.

Common mistakes included not recognizing the Policy C cancellation, and performing a calculation that more closely resembled a calendar year than a policy year aggregation.
