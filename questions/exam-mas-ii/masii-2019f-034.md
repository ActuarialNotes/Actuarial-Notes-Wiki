Assume our data $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ come from a normal distribution with an unknown mean $\mu$ and known standard deviation, $\sigma$. We assume a flat prior for $\mu$.

We use the Metropolis algorithm to sample from the posterior distribution of $\mu$ using a standard normal distribution as a proposal distribution. The chain is allowed to run for 3,000 iterations.

Calculate the probability that the $1000^{th}$ draw is greater than the $999^{th}$ draw.

- A) Less than 0.30
- B) Greater than or equal to 0.3 but less than 0.45
- C) Greater than or equal to 0.45 but less than 0.60
- D) Greater than or equal to 0.60 but less than 0.75
- E) Greater than or equal to 0.75

## Explanation

The key fact about a Metropolis chain is that a **rejected proposal repeats the current value**. So each step has three outcomes, not two:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(\text{next} > \text{current}) &= P(\text{propose up}) \times P(\text{accept}) \\
P(\text{next} < \text{current}) &= P(\text{propose down}) \times P(\text{accept}) \\
P(\text{next} = \text{current}) &= P(\text{reject})
\end{align*}
$$

By iteration 999 the chain has long since converged, so it is at stationarity. The proposal is symmetric and the target is symmetric about its mean, so upward and downward moves are equally likely to be accepted:

$$
P(\text{next} > \text{current}) = \frac{a}{2}
$$

where $a$ is the acceptance rate. That alone caps the answer at 0.5, eliminating D and E, and makes C possible only for an implausible acceptance rate of at least 90%.

Now consider the scale mismatch. With a flat prior the posterior is

$$
\mu \mid \mathbf{x} \sim \text{Normal}\!\left(\bar{x},\ \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right),
$$

whose standard deviation $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$ shrinks with the sample size, while the proposal has a fixed standard deviation of 1. For any reasonable $n$ the proposal is far wider than the posterior, so most proposals land in the tails and are rejected — the acceptance rate is well below 0.6. (Even a well-tuned one-dimensional random-walk Metropolis targets an acceptance rate near 0.44, giving about 0.22 here.)

So the probability is less than 0.30, and the answer is **A**.

*Note: the CAS final answer key accepts **A**, **B** and **C** for this question.*
