# Exam GI-201
**GI 201 – Operational, Financial, Regulatory and Legal** is an exam of the Society of Actuaries' Fellowship (FSA) pathway. Its syllabus covers the Nov 2026/Mar 2027 sittings.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- General Insurance Company Operations {5–10%}
> The candidate will understand the structure and functions of a general [[Insurer|insurance company]].
>
> 1. Describe the various core and supporting functions of a general [[Insurer|insurance company]].
> 2. Differentiate among the various classifications of [[Insurer|insurers]].
> 3. Describe the marketing and distribution of general [[Insurance Policy|insurance products]].
> 4. Understand the processes for [[Underwriting|underwriting]] and [[Ratemaking|pricing general insurance policies]].
> 5. Describe the activities and challenges in the [[Claims Handling|claim handling process]].
> 6. Describe the goals of [[Risk Control|insurer risk]] control activities.
> 7. Explain why [[Premium Audit|premium audits]] are conducted.
> 8. Demonstrate knowledge of general [[Insurance Risk|insurance risks]], perils, product characteristics / issues, and contracts.
> 9. Describe and compare the different types of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] and [[Insurance-Linked Securities|risk mitigation instruments]].
> 10. Demonstrate knowledge of the issues regarding [[International Insurance|international business]] for [[Insurer|insurance companies]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - General Insurance Company Operations, SOA, May 2025 (1a-g)
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, 2022 (1d)
>     - Ch. 29: Manual Ratemaking
> - IAA Risk Book: Non-Life Perils and Coverages, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, sections 3-7, and 8.1-8.2 (1h)
> - IAA Risk Book: Reinsurance, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, sections 2-4, 7 and 11 (1i)
> - Excerpts from Business Law for Insurance Professionals, Assignment 3, pp. 3.29-3.39 (begin on pp. 3.29 with “Financial Considerations in International Business”) (1j)

> [!example]- Regulatory Environment {20–32%}
> The candidate will understand the [[Insurance Regulation|regulatory environment]] for general insurance.
>
> 1. Describe the functions and goals of the different forms of [[Insurance Regulation|insurance regulation]].
> 2. Describe the protections provided by [[Solvency|solvency]] and [[Solvency Regulation|insolvency regulation]].
> 3. Understand the issue of discrimination and [[Insurance|insurance]].
> 4. Discuss the issues regarding [[Usage-Based Insurance|usage-based insurance]] and telematics in [[Personal Auto Policy|automobile insurance]].
> 5. Describe the development of general insurance programs controlled by government or [[Government and Industry Insurance Programs|collective insurance industry organizations]] and their mechanisms of operation.
> 6. Discuss the issues with the use of [[Credit-Based Insurance Scoring|credit scores]] by [[Insurer|insurers]].
> 7. Understand the function and regulation of price [[Optimization|optimization]].
> 8. Outline the function and regulation of [[Captive|captives]].
> 9. Understand the functions and [[Insurance Regulation|regulatory environment]] for [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - The General Insurance Regulatory Environment, SOA, May 2025 (2a-b)
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, 2022 (2a)
>     - Ch. 28: Rate Regulation
> - IAA Risk Book: Non-Life Perils and Coverages, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, sections 8.5-8.7 (2a, 2c)
> - Insurance and the Discrimination Laws: Motor and Travel Insurance, Edmunds, T., House of Commons Library Standard Note SN04601, Mar 2013 (report dated Oct 2015), pp. 4- (2c)
> - Usage Based Insurance and Telematics, SOA, May 2025 (2d)
> - IAA Risk Book: Non-Life Perils and Coverages, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, sections 8.3-8.4 (2e)
> - Government Provision of General Insurance, SOA, May 2025, sections 1-3, 5, 6, and 9 (2e)
> - Private Flood Insurance and the National Flood Insurance Program, CRS Report R45242, Congressional Research Service, Updated Jan 2023, pp. 1-21 (excluding the section “NFIP Reauthorization and Legislation” on pp. 5-6, and Table 1 on pp. 9) (2e)
> - Issues Paper: Use of Credit Scores by Insurers Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators, Credit Scoring Working Group, Jun 2011, pp. 5-16 (2f)
> - Price Optimization White Paper, Casualty Actuarial and Statistical (C) Task Force, National Association of Insurance Commissioners, Nov 2015, pp. 6-16 (paragraphs 15-49) (2g)
> - Captive Insurance, SOA, May 2025 (2h)
> - IAA Risk Book: Reinsurance, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, section 10 (2i)

> [!example]- Legal Environment {15–25%}
> The candidate will understand [[Tort Law|tort law]] and [[Insurance Law|insurance law]] with respect to general insurance.
>
> 1. Describe and interpret the key elements of [[Tort Law|tort law]] and the underlying principles of [[Insurance Law|insurance law]].
> 2. Discuss the issues of [[Social Inflation|tort trends]] and [[Tort Reform|tort reform]] as it applies to the general [[Insurance Industry|insurance industry]].
> 3. Understand the effects of [[Transnational Tort Litigation|transnational tort litigation]].
> 4. Understand [[Mass Tort|mass torts/class action suits]] and discuss their effect on the general [[Insurance Industry|insurance industry]].
> 5. Understand the use of [[No-Fault Insurance|no-fault insurance]].
> 6. Understand the [[International Legal Environment|international legal environment]] as it relates to general insurance.
> 7. Discuss the influence of the U.S. [[Transnational Tort Litigation|tort law environment]] in other countries.
> 8. Discuss the issue of [[Social Inflation|social inflation]] and its effect on general insurance.
> 9. Describe and interpret [[Court Case|legal cases/issues]] included in the [[Syllabus Resource|syllabus resources]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - Excerpts from Business Law for Insurance Professionals, Assignments 1 & 2 (3a, 3i)
> - IAA Risk Book: Non-Life Perils and Coverages, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, section 8.10 (3a)
> - Tort Law – Topics for General Insurance Actuaries, SOA, May 2025, sections 1-5 (excluding 5.1 & 5.5) Note: Regarding the table in 5.4 (pp. 35-37), candidates are only expected to be familiar with the cases BMW of North America v. Gore and State Farm Mutual Auto. v. Campbell. (3b-d, 3i)
> - Government Provision of General Insurance, SOA, Section 10, Appendix – No-Fault Insurance (3e)
> - Excerpts from Business Law for Insurance Professionals, Assignment 3, 3.11-3.29 (begin on pp. 3.11 with “Legal Systems”, end before “Financial Considerations…” on pp. 3.29) (3f)
> - Tort Law – Topics for General Insurance Actuaries, SOA, May 2025, section 6 (excluding 6.2.1-6.2.9) (3i)
> - Social Inflation: Navigating the Evolving Claims Environment, The Geneva Association, Dec 2020 (pp. 6-17, 19-34 & 36-38) (3h-i)

> [!example]- Financial Economics {5–8%}
> The candidate will understand elements of [[Financial Economics|financial economics]] relevant to general insurance.
>
> 1. Demonstrate knowledge of [[Behavioral Finance|behavioral finance]].
> 2. Demonstrate knowledge of [[Asset-Liability Management|asset liability management strategies]] for general [[Insurer|insurance companies]].
> 3. Calculate an [[Financial Economics|underwriting profit margin]] using different models under a [[Financial Economics|financial economics]] approach.
>
> **Readings:**
> - Behavioral Finance, J. Ritter, Pacific-Basin Finance Journal, Volume 11, Issue 4, Sep 2003, sections 1-3 (pp. 429-434) (4a)
> - ALM Strategies for General Insurers, SOA, May 2025 (4b)
> - Ratemaking: A Financial Economics Approach, D’Arcy, S. and Dyer, M., 1997 Proceedings of the Casualty Actuarial Society, Volume LXXXIV, sections 1, 2, 4 & 6 Note: Sections 3 and 5 are not required reading but may be beneficial as background reading. (4c)

> [!example]- Financial Reporting {10–20%}
> The candidate will understand elements of [[Financial Reporting|financial reporting]] for general [[Insurer|insurance companies]].
>
> 1. Demonstrate knowledge of the different types of general [[Insurance Contract Liabilities|insurance liabilities]].
> 2. Understand and apply the concepts of [[Insurance-reinsurance|insurance/reinsurance]] accounting.
> 3. Understand the different [[Financial Reporting|financial reporting standards]] for general [[Insurer|insurers]].
> 4. Understand key terms and considerations related to [[Unpaid Claims|claim liabilities]].
> 5. Understand the reporting of [[Financial Asset Classification|financial instruments]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - IAA Risk Book: Liabilities for General Insurance, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023 (pp. 2-22) (5a)
> - General Insurance Financial Reporting Topics, Society of Actuaries, 5th Edition, 2021: (5b-c)
>     - Ch. 1: Accounting Concepts for General Insurance
>     - Ch. 2: Accounting for Insurance Contracts (excluding 2.3.3-2.3.4, 2.5.1-2.5.2.1, 2.7.2-2.7.4 & 2.8 after the Insight box on pp. 36)
>     - Ch. 4: Accounting for Reinsurance Contracts (excluding 4.4.2, 4.6.3-4.6.5, 4.8.3 & 4.9)
> - Fundamentals of General Insurance Actuarial Analysis, Friedland, J., 2nd Edition, 2022 (5d)
>     - Ch. 24: Claim Liabilities
> - General Insurance Financial Reporting Topics, Society of Actuaries, 5th Edition, 2021: (5e)
>     - Ch. 3: Accounting for Financial Instruments (excluding 3.4.3)

> [!example]- Financial Health Measurement {15–20%}
> The candidate will understand the analysis of a general [[Financial Health|insurer’s financial health]].
>
> 1. Understand the various considerations for [[Key Financial Measures|performance measurement]] of a general [[Insurer|insurer]].
> 2. Understand and apply [[Financial Health|financial health]] ratios.
> 3. Demonstrate knowledge of [[Solvency Monitoring|solvency monitoring]] for general [[Insurer|insurance companies]].
> 4. Understand and apply the [[Solvency II|EU Solvency II capital requirement]].
> 5. Demonstrate knowledge of [[ORSA]].
> 6. Discuss the function of [[Rating Agency|credit rating agencies]] and their influence on general [[Insurer|insurers]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - General Insurance Financial Reporting Topics, Society of Actuaries, 5th Edition, 2021:
>     - Ch. 10: Performance Measurement for General Insurers (excluding 10.4.3, and 10.8.3.1-10.9.1) (6a)
>     - Ch. 11: Financial Ratios for Insurers (excluding 11.6.2, 11.7.1, 11.8, and 11.10-11.11) (6a-b)
>     - Ch. 12: Solvency Monitoring (excluding 12.5.3, 12.9.2-12.9.3, 12.10.2, 12.11 and 12.13) (6c-e)
> - IAA Risk Book: Reinsurance, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, section 9 (6a, c-d, f)
> - IAA Risk Book: Own Risk Solvency Assessment, Insurance Regulation Committee, International Actuarial Association, May 2023, pp. 3-19 (excluding sections 10.1 & 11) (6e)
> - NAIC Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (ORSA) Guidance Manual, NAIC, as of Dec 2022, pp. 1-5 & 7-16 (6e)
> - Deriving Value from ORSA: Board Perspective, International Actuarial Association, Apr 2015, pp. 1-8 (6e)
> - General Insurance Financial Reporting Topics, Society of Actuaries, 5th Edition, 2021: (6f)
>     - Ch. 13: General Insurance Financial Ratings (excluding sections 13.4.3-13.4.6.4, 13.5.2 and the first paragraph on page 376 after the Insight box)

> [!example]- Responsibilities of the Actuary {5–10%}
> The candidate will understand the standards of practice and professionalism required of the [[Actuary|actuary]].
>
> 1. Describe, interpret and apply [[International Actuarial Association|professionalism principles]] as defined by the [[International Actuarial Association]] ([[International Actuarial Association|IAA]]).
> 2. Describe, interpret and apply Standards of Practice as defined by the [[International Actuarial Association|IAA]].
> 3. Demonstrate knowledge of the general [[Statement of Actuarial Opinion|insurance actuarial opinion]] and the role of the [[Appointed Actuary|appointed actuary]].
> 4. Describe and apply the concept of [[Materiality|materiality]].
>
> **Readings:**
> - IAA Risk Book: Professional Standards, Insurance Regulation Committee, IAA, May 2023, pp. 2-7 (7a)
> - International Standard of Actuarial Practice 1, General Actuarial Practice, IAA, Revision adopted Dec 2018 (7b)
> - International Standard of Actuarial Practice 4, IFRS 17 Insurance Contracts, IAA, Nov 2019, pp. 1-9 (excluding sections 2.8 and 2.10) (7c)
> - General Insurance Financial Reporting Topics, Society of Actuaries, 5th Edition, 2021 (7c)
>     - Ch. 14: The General Insurance Actuarial Opinion (excluding 14.2.6.1-14.3.6, 14.4 & 14.6-14.8)
> - IAA Risk Book: Materiality, Proportionality and Similar Terms, Insurance Regulation Committee, IAA, Apr 2023, pp. 2-10 (7d)
