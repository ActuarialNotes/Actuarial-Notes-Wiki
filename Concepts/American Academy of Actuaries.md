The **American Academy of Actuaries** (the Academy, AAA) is the U.S. professional association for actuaries of every practice area. It speaks for the profession on public policy and sets its U.S. conduct, qualification and practice standards.

Established in 1965, with about 20,000 members, it serves the public and the U.S. profession. It sets no exams: the FCAS, ACAS and FSA come from the CAS and SOA, and its own credential is MAAA. The other half of its work is nonpartisan actuarial input to Congress, the NAIC and regulators. Its Canadian counterpart is the [[Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)]], whose Standards of Practice and Rules of Professional Conduct govern the Canadian [[Appointed Actuary]].

The U.S. standards rest on four pillars:

| Pillar | Governed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| Conduct | The Code of Professional Conduct |
| Qualification | The U.S. Qualification Standards (USQS) |
| Practice | The [[Actuarial Standards of Practice]], set by the Actuarial Standards Board (ASB) |
| Discipline | The Actuarial Board for Counseling and Discipline (ABCD) |

## The Code of Professional Conduct

The Code is shared by the U.S.-based actuarial organizations. Precept 2 (act only when qualified) and Precept 3 (comply with applicable standards of practice) make the qualification and practice standards binding.

## The U.S. Qualification Standards

The current edition of the USQS took effect on January 1, 2022. The general standard requires at least 30 hours of continuing education a year, including at least 3 on professionalism, at least 1 on bias topics and at least 6 in organized activities, with no more than 3 counted from general business skills.

The Specific Qualification Standard for NAIC P&C opinions adds:

1. Basic education in coverages, ratemaking, statutory accounting, reserves and reinsurance.
2. Three years of responsible experience reviewed by an actuary qualified to sign.
3. 15 hours a year of directly relevant continuing education, at least 6 of them with outside actuaries or professionals.

A shortfall can be made up early in the next year before the actuary signs, but those hours then cannot count toward that year's requirement.

> [!example]- Does this opining actuary's continuing education meet the USQS? {Example}
> An FCAS will sign a P&C Statement of Actuarial Opinion in February 2026. Her 2025 continuing education log is:
>
> | Activity | Hours |
> | :--- | ---: |
> | CAS reserving seminar, in person | 9 |
> | NAIC statutory accounting webinar | 3 |
> | Reading ASOP No. 36 and the COPLFR practice note | 4 |
> | In-house reserving meetings, no outside speakers | 6 |
> | Professionalism webinar | 2 |
> | Leadership course (general business skills) | 5 |
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Hours directly relevant to the opinion are $9 + 3 + 4 + 6 = 22$, at least $15$.
> > - Of those, the hours with outside interaction are $9 + 3 = 12$, at least $6$, so the specific standard is met.
> > - For the general standard only $3$ of the $5$ business-skills hours count, so the countable hours are $9 + 3 + 4 + 6 + 2 + 3 = 27$, below $30$.
> > - Organized hours, $9 + 3 + 2 = 14$, clear the floor of $6$.
> > - Professionalism is $2$, below $3$, and bias topics are $0$, below $1$.
> > - She is $3$ hours short, of which at least $1$ must be professionalism and $1$ bias. She may make them up early in 2026 before she signs, but those hours then cannot count toward 2026.
> > - Until she does, Precept 2 bars her from issuing the opinion, however ample her reserving hours.

## The Actuarial Standards Board

The ASB is housed within the Academy's structure but is independent in writing standards. The P&C opinion rests on ASOP No. 36 (opinions on P&C reserves, revised effective October 1, 2024), [[ASOP 43 - Property Casualty Unpaid Claim Estimates (ASB - 2007)|ASOP No. 43]], [[ASOP 23 - Data Quality (ASB - 2016)|ASOP No. 23]] and ASOP No. 41 (actuarial communications).

## The Actuarial Board for Counseling and Discipline

The ABCD gives guidance, investigates complaints and counsels actuaries. It does not discipline anyone. It recommends discipline to the membership organizations, which decide under their own procedures.

## The Academy and the Statement of Actuarial Opinion

The NAIC writes the Academy's standards into the definition of who may sign a U.S. P&C Statement of Actuarial Opinion ([[Appointed Actuary Responsibilities]]). A U.S. Qualified Actuary must:

1. Meet the USQS specific standard.
2. Hold an Accepted Actuarial Designation.
3. Belong to an organization that requires the Academy's Code and the USQS and participates in the ABCD.

Academy membership itself is not required, since CAS membership satisfies the third test, but the Academy's standards are in the definition either way. Its Casualty Practice Council can also evaluate an actuary without an accepted designation and find them qualified for particular lines of business.

## Practice notes

The Academy's Committee on Property and Liability Financial Reporting (COPLFR) publishes an annual practice note on P&C loss reserve opinions, with illustrative wording ([[SAO Language]]). A practice note is nonbinding. Departing from its wording is not a violation, while departing from ASOP No. 36 may be.

> [!example]- Which of these rules bind the opining actuary, and who enforces each? {Example}
> An Appointed Actuary drafting a year-end opinion consults (a) the NAIC Annual Statement Instructions for the opinion, (b) ASOP No. 36, (c) the COPLFR practice note and (d) the USQS.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - (a) is binding as regulation. States require the annual statement to follow the instructions, and the domiciliary commissioner enforces them.
> > - The instructions also settle conflicts: insurance laws and regulations "shall at all times take precedence over the actuarial standards."
> > - (b) is binding professionally, through Precept 3. A breach is a matter for the ABCD, which may recommend discipline to her membership organizations.
> > - (c) is not binding. Different wording is fine if the NAIC's required elements and ASOP No. 36's disclosures are present.
> > - (d) is binding professionally through Precept 2, and as regulation because the NAIC's Qualified Actuary definition incorporates the USQS.
> > - Where (a) and (b) conflict, she follows the law and discloses the departure from the standard, as the ASOPs themselves provide.

> [!trap] Treating a practice note as a standard
> The COPLFR practice note is guidance. An opinion worded differently from it breaks no rule, but one that leaves out a disclosure ASOP No. 36 requires does.
