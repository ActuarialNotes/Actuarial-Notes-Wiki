The following information is available for a property and casualty insurance company as at December 31, 2017. All amounts are in thousands of dollars (\$000s).

| | 2017 | 2016 |
|---|---|---|
| Net Written Premiums | 140,000 | 130,000 |
| Net Earned Premiums | 135,000 | 120,000 |
| Net Income After Taxes | 8,500 | 8,000 |
| Other Comprehensive Income | 300 | 500 |
| Dividends | 2,000 | 1,500 |
| Total Assets | 120,000 | ? |
| Total Liabilities | ? | 50,000 |
| Equity | ? | 60,000 |

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate the following ratios as at December 31, 2017:

- i. Return on equity
- ii. Return on assets after tax
- iii. Overall net leverage

### Explanation
- Assets 2016 = Equity 2016 + Liabilities 2016 $= 50{,}000 + 60{,}000 = 110{,}000$
- Equity 2017 = Equity 2016 + Net Income + Other comprehensive income - Dividends $= 60{,}000 + 8{,}500 + 300 - 2{,}000 = 66{,}800$
- Liabilities 2017 = Assets 2017 - Equity 2017 $= 120{,}000 - 66{,}800 = 53{,}200$

Then:

- i. Return on equity = Net income / Equity $= 8{,}500 / 66{,}800 = 12.72\%$
- ii. Return on assets after tax = Net income / Average assets $= 8{,}500 / (110{,}000/2 + 120{,}000/2) = 7.39\%$
- iii. Overall net leverage = (Net Written Premium + Net liabilities) / Equity $= (140{,}000 + 53{,}200) / 66{,}800 = 2.89$

Alternatively, with return on equity on average equity:

Return on equity = Net income / Average Equity $= 8{,}500 / (60{,}000/2 + 66{,}800/2) = 13.41\%$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the three requested ratios, and the component parts (equity, assets, and liabilities) needed to calculate these ratios.
Common mistakes included:

- Incorrect calculation of the 2017 equity
- Incorrect calculation of the 2017 liabilities

## Part b (1 point)

Using the ratios calculated in part a. above, assess the financial health of the company as at December 31, 2017.

### Explanation
- Return on equity is above 5.4%
- Return on assets after tax is above 2.6%
- Overall net leverage is below 500%

The company is in good financial health.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how to interpret the ratios calculated in part a.
Common mistakes included:

- Incorrectly identifying the target "healthy" financial ratios
