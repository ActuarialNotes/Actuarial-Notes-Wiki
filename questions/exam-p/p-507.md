The random variable $X$ has density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
cx^5e^{-0.01x}, & \text{for } x > 0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $c$ is a constant.

Calculate $E(X)$.

- A) $100$
- B) $200$
- C) $300$
- D) $500$
- E) $600$

## Explanation

Recognising the family turns this into a one-line problem. A gamma density with shape $\alpha$ and scale $\beta$ has the form

$$
f(x) \propto x^{\alpha - 1}e^{-x/\beta}
$$

Matching the exponents against $x^5e^{-0.01x}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\alpha - 1 &= 5 \implies \alpha = 6 \\
\frac{1}{\beta} &= 0.01 \implies \beta = 100
\end{align*}
$$

The mean of a gamma distribution is $\alpha\beta$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E(X) &= 6(100) \\
     &= \mathbf{600}
\end{align*}
$$

The normalizing constant $c$ never has to be found. Without the recognition, the same answer requires evaluating $\int_0^\infty cx^6e^{-0.01x}dx$ and $\int_0^\infty cx^5e^{-0.01x}dx$ by repeated integration by parts. Choice (D), 500, uses $\alpha = 5$ — reading the exponent as $\alpha$ rather than $\alpha - 1$.
