Six people are seated randomly around a circular table. Two of the six people are left-handed.

Calculate the probability that the two left-handed people are seated next to each other.

- A) $1/6$
- B) $1/5$
- C) $1/3$
- D) $2/5$
- E) $3/5$

## Explanation

The quickest route skips the counting entirely. Seat one left-handed person anywhere; by symmetry it does not matter where. That person has exactly **two** neighbouring seats, and the other left-hander is equally likely to occupy any of the remaining **five** seats:

$$
P[\text{adjacent}] = \frac{2}{5} = \mathbf{0.4}
$$

The formal count agrees. Around a circular table, seatings are counted up to rotation, so there are $5! = 120$ distinct arrangements of six people. Treating the two left-handers as a single block gives $4!$ arrangements of the five resulting units, times 2 for the block's internal order:

$$
\frac{2(4!)}{5!} = \frac{48}{120} = \frac{2}{5}
$$

Choice (B), 1/5, uses only one neighbouring seat instead of two — around a circle every seat has neighbours on both sides. The answer is notably large because with only six seats, two of the five available positions are adjacent.
