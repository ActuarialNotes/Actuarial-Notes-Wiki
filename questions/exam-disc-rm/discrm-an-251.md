An insurer has a surplus share treaty with a retained line of \$200,000 and 5 lines. It insures a building valued at \$900,000, which suffers a \$180,000 fire loss. How much of the loss does the reinsurer pay?

- A) \$0
- B) \$40,000
- C) \$140,000
- D) \$180,000

## Explanation

The cession percentage depends on how far the building's value exceeds the retained line:

$$
\begin{align*}
s &= \frac{\min(900{,}000 - 200{,}000,\ 5 \times 200{,}000)}{900{,}000} \\
&= \frac{700{,}000}{900{,}000} \\
&= 77.8\% \\
\text{Ceded} &= 0.778 \times 180{,}000 \\
&= 140{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **C**. The insurer keeps \$40,000.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Treats the treaty as excess of loss. The loss is below \$200,000, but a surplus share pays its percentage of every loss on the risk.
- **B** — Is the insurer's retained share, $\frac{200{,}000}{900{,}000} \times 180{,}000$.
- **D** — Cedes the whole loss, as if the insurer retained nothing.
