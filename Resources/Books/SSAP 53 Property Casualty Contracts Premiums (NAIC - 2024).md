Statement of Statutory Accounting Principles No. 53 of the NAIC's Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual. It establishes general statutory accounting principles for the recording and recognition of premium revenue for property and casualty contracts, including unearned premium, earned but unbilled and uncollected premium, advance premiums and the premium deficiency reserve.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objectives C1–C7; NAIC Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual, 2025, SSAP No. 53, paragraphs 1-18, available in Appendix IV of the COPLFR P&C Practice Note (as the content outline states it)

## Scope of Statement
- The statement covers the recording and recognition of premium revenue for property and casualty contracts as defined in SSAP No. 50; specific requirements for title, mortgage guaranty and financial guaranty insurance, reinsurance, SSAP No. 65 contracts and retrospectively rated contracts are in their own statements.

## Summary Conclusion
- Written premium is the contractually determined amount charged to the policyholder for the effective period of the contract; for workers' compensation contracts with premium that varies with the insured's activities it may be recorded on an installment basis.
- Written premiums for other contracts are recorded as of the contract's effective date, with an unearned premium reserve established for the unexpired coverage.
- Premiums are recognized as earned using the daily pro-rata or monthly pro-rata method, or over the period of risk in proportion to the insurance protection provided when that differs significantly from the contract period; additional premiums for endorsements are recorded on the endorsement's effective date.
- Earned but Unbilled Premium
    - Insurers estimate audit premiums (earned but unbilled premium) and record them as an adjustment to premium; ten percent of the amount in excess of collateral held per policy is a nonadmitted asset.
- Earned but Uncollected Premium
    - Premium for the extended period during which the insurer is at risk is recorded as direct and assumed written premium and charged to expense when uncollectible.
- Advance Premiums
    - Premiums paid before the effective date are a liability and are not income until due, and are not included in written premium or the unearned premium reserve.
- Premium Deposits on Perpetual Fire Deposits
    - At least 90% of the gross amount of such a deposit is charged as a liability.
- Premium Deficiency Reserve
    - A [[Premium Deficiency|premium deficiency reserve]] is recognized when anticipated losses, loss adjustment expenses, acquisition and maintenance costs exceed the unearned premium reserve and future installment premiums; deficiencies are not offset by anticipated profits in other policy groupings.
- Disclosures
    - Direct premiums written through managing general agents or third party administrators are disclosed in aggregate, with detail on each when the total is 5% of surplus or more, and the amount of any premium deficiency reserve is disclosed.
