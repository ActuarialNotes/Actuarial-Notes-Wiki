FSCO's October 2014 consumer brochure defining the coverages in an Ontario automobile policy. Three pages, and the shortest reading on the [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] syllabus — read for objective A1. It is the glossary the rest of the Ontario auto material assumes.

## First-party benefits — the [[Statutory Accident Benefits|Statutory Accident Benefits Schedule]]

| Benefit | What it pays |
|---|---|
| **Income replacement** | Basic weekly benefit up to **\$400**, commencing after one week, when the insured cannot work as a result of the accident |
| **Medical and rehabilitation** | Reasonable and necessary medical and rehabilitation expenses listed in the SABS and not covered by OHIP or a disability plan |
| **Attendant care** | An aide or attendant to look after a seriously injured insured |
| **Caregiver** | Reimburses the cost of hiring someone to care for dependants when a [[Catastrophic Impairment\|catastrophic injury]] stops the insured providing full-time care |
| **Housekeeping and home maintenance** | Additional expense of someone completing the insured's usual duties after a catastrophic injury |
| **Death and funeral** | Lump sum to spouse and dependants; lump sum for funeral expenses |
| **Indexation** | Automatic CPI adjustment of the income replacement, non-earner, attendant care and medical/rehabilitation benefits |

**[[Minor Injury Guideline|Minor injury]]** — sprains, strains, contusions, lacerations and whiplash. A minor injury caps medical and rehabilitation benefits at **\$3,500**, regardless of optional increased benefits purchased.

**[[Catastrophic Impairment|Catastrophic injury]]** — a serious, life-threatening injury defined in regulation (loss of use of limbs, complete loss of eyesight, and the other listed impairments). A higher benefit tier applies, and the determination is made by assessment.

## Third-party and physical damage coverages

- ****Third party liability**** — pays claims against the insured for injury, death or property damage, and the cost of settling them. Ontario's statutory minimum is **\$200,000**; higher limits are optional.
- **Collision or upset** — loss when the vehicle collides with another object or rolls over.
- **Comprehensive** — named perils such as falling or flying objects, and vandalism.
- **Specified perils** — fire; theft or attempted theft; lightning, windstorm, hail or rising water; earthquake; explosion; riot or civil disturbance; falling aircraft; and the standing, sinking, burning, derailment or collision of a transport carrying the vehicle.
- **All perils** — collision or upset plus comprehensive, and additionally theft by a household member or by an employee who drives, services or repairs the vehicle.
- **[[Uninsured Automobile Coverage|Uninsured automobile]]** — protection when the at-fault driver is uninsured or unidentified.
- **[[Tort Threshold and Deductible|Tort deductible]]** — the amount deducted from a settlement or court award for pain and suffering.

## Other defined terms

**Exclusions** · **Premium** · **[[Risk Classification Restrictions|Risk classification]]** (grouping risks with similar characteristics to set rates — vehicle type and use, driving record, age, gender, marital status, [[Territorial Rating|territory]]) · **Tort** · **Underwriting** (selecting or rejecting risks).
