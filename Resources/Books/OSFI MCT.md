The **[[MCT|Minimum Capital Test]]** guideline, effective 1 January 2024 — the risk-based capital test for federally regulated P&C insurers, and the single most examined reading in Domain C. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objectives C2–C4.

> [!warning]- Assigned scope
> Candidates are **not** responsible for sections 1.2.2, 2.1.1.1, 2.1.2, 2.1.3, Appendix 2-A, 3, 4.6, 5.2.2, 5.3.4.1, 5.3.4.2, 6.1.1, 6.2.1, 6.2.2, 6.2.3 or 6.3; **nor for the risk factors relating to insurance, market or credit risk**. Candidates will not be penalised for answers based on the final version of the paper.
> The exam tests the *structure* of the test and what drives each margin — not the numerical factors.

## The ratio

$$\text{MCT ratio} = \frac{\text{[[Capital Available]]}}{\text{Minimum capital required}}$$

where minimum capital required is derived from capital required computed at the target level for each risk. Federally regulated insurers must maintain **at least 100%**. OSFI has set an industry-wide **[[Supervisory Target Capital Ratio|supervisory target]] of 150%**, a cushion that supports early intervention. Each insurer must additionally set an **[[Internal Target Capital Ratio|internal target]]** above the supervisory target through its [[ORSA]] (see [[OSFI Target Capital]]), and must tell OSFI immediately if it expects to fall below it. The Superintendent may set an alternative supervisory target for an individual insurer.

## The mechanics to hold on to

- **Capital required is built risk by risk, then aggregated with a diversification credit** between insurance risk and market risk — the [[Base Solvency Buffer|base solvency buffer]] construction. Credit and operational risk do not receive the same benefit.
- **Insurance risk has two margins**: one on the liability for incurred claims (reserve risk) and one on **unexpired coverage** (premium risk), the latter driven by expected loss ratios — which is where [[CIA IFRS 17 - LRC]] §8 connects.
- **Reinsurance changes the answer twice**: it reduces the insurance risk margin, and it creates a **credit risk** exposure to the reinsurer. [[Unregistered Reinsurance|Unregistered]] reinsurance receives credit only to the extent it is collateralised.
- **Earthquake** (§4.5) has its own margin built from the [[Probable Maximum Loss|PML]], the earthquake reserves, reinsurance and capital market financing — see [[OSFI Earthquake]]. There is also a **nuclear reserve** requirement.
- **Operational risk** is formula-based off premium, liabilities and growth, and is *added* after the diversification credit — deliberately, since operational risk does not diversify with underwriting risk.
- **§1.1.5 interpretation of results** is the section that says what the ratio does and does not mean: it is a point-in-time, standardised measure that cannot capture every insurer-specific risk, which is precisely why [[ORSA]] and [[FCT]] exist alongside it.
