**Insurance Policy Structure** is the way a policy is physically put together: which documents make it up, who drafted them, and where each kind of provision is found. Knowing the structure tells a reader where to look for the [[Policy Provisions|provisions]] that decide a claim.

A policy is classified two separate ways:

| Question | Choices |
| :--- | :--- |
| Who drafted the wording? | **Preprinted** standard forms, or a **manuscript** written for one insured |
| How is it assembled? | **Self-contained**, one document holding every insuring agreement, or **modular**, assembled from a declarations page, coverage forms, conditions forms and [[Endorsement|endorsements]] |

Every policy is made up of its declarations, its form or forms, and its endorsements.

## Preprinted and manuscript forms

Standard forms are drafted for insurers' use by insurance industry service organizations such as the American Association of Insurance Services (AAIS), Insurance Services Office (ISO) and the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI), and are printed for use with many insureds. A **manuscript** form or policy is custom designed for one insured. More often, a standard form is customized only a little, through endorsements.

Manuscript wording can meet a particular risk management goal, but it can lose the insured the protections courts give a standard form, such as construing ambiguous wording against the drafter ([[Insurance Contract Characteristics#Contract of adhesion|contract of adhesion]]) when the insured or its broker helped write it. An endorsed standard form keeps that protection.

## Self-contained policies

A self-contained policy is a single document containing all the insuring agreements between insurer and insured. It is often preprinted and standardized for a large number of insureds, and it is tailored through endorsements. The ISO homeowners form HO 00 03 is built this way: one form carries the definitions, the Section I property coverages and the Section II liability coverages, with their exclusions and conditions, and the declarations page and endorsements are attached to it.

## Modular policies

A modular policy is assembled from separate standardized forms. A **coverage form** is one of the primary forms used to construct the contract, and generally holds the insuring agreement, coverage conditions, exclusions and definitions.

The ISO commercial package is the example: one set of Common Policy Declarations and the [[Common Policy Conditions]] (IL 00 17), which apply to every coverage part, plus coverage parts that are each built from forms. A coverage part is one line of coverage within the policy, such as commercial property or commercial general liability, made up of its own declarations, its coverage form or forms, its conditions and its endorsements. A coverage part's own conditions apply to that part only.

A commercial property coverage part combines its declarations, the [[Building and Personal Property Coverage Form]] (CP 00 10), a causes of loss form, and the [[Commercial Property Conditions]]. The BPP refers to the causes of loss form for both the covered causes of loss and the exclusions, so looking for them in CP 00 10 finds only a cross-reference. A commercial general liability coverage part is added the same way around the [[Commercial General Liability|CGL coverage form]] (CG 00 01). A package policy combines several coverages in this way, usually general liability and property.

> [!example]- How do the documents of this commercial package policy group by coverage part? {Example}
> A retailer's commercial package policy contains these documents:
>
> 1. Common Policy Declarations.
> 2. IL 00 17 Common Policy Conditions.
> 3. Commercial Property declarations.
> 4. CP 00 10 Building and Personal Property Coverage Form.
> 5. A Causes of Loss – Special Form.
> 6. Commercial Property Conditions.
> 7. Commercial General Liability declarations.
> 8. CG 00 01 Commercial General Liability Coverage Form.
> 9. An endorsement adding the retailer's landlord as an additional insured on the CGL.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - The Common Policy Declarations and IL 00 17 apply to the whole policy. IL 00 17 applies to all coverage parts included in the policy.
> > - The commercial property coverage part is its declarations, CP 00 10, the causes of loss form and the Commercial Property Conditions. The BPP refers to the causes of loss form for what is covered and excluded, so neither form works alone.
> > - The commercial general liability coverage part is its declarations, CG 00 01 and the additional insured endorsement, which changes only that part.
> > - The policy is modular. Each part was assembled from standard forms, and a different insured could take the same CGL part with a different property part.

## Endorsements

An endorsement is a form that changes or adds to the provisions of one or more other forms in the policy. It may broaden or restrict coverage, clarify how coverage applies to an unusual exposure, add insureds or add locations. Under the Common Policy Conditions the policy's terms can be amended or waived only by an endorsement the insurer issues.

## Where each provision is found

The **declarations** are the front page or pages: the named insured, address, policy period, locations, limits and other information that varies from insured to insured. **Definitions**, **insuring agreements**, **exclusions** and **conditions** are usually in the coverage form. Exclusions can also be in a causes of loss form, and conditions in a separate conditions form that applies to a whole coverage part or the whole policy. The categories themselves, and what each does, are on [[Policy Provisions]].

> [!example]- Where in the retailer's package policy is each of these answers found? {Example}
> 1. How many days' notice the insurer must give before cancelling the whole policy for nonpayment.
> 2. The limit of insurance on the retailer's building.
> 3. Whether flood damage to the building is covered.
> 4. How a deductible is applied to a property loss.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - The cancellation notice is in IL 00 17, the Common Policy Conditions: 10 days for nonpayment of premium, 30 days for any other reason. Cancellation applies to the policy as a whole, so it sits in the common conditions.
> > - The building limit is in the commercial property declarations, because limits vary from insured to insured.
> > - Flood is answered by the causes of loss form, which the BPP points to for its covered causes of loss and exclusions.
> > - The deductible is in CP 00 10, section D, Deductible, within the coverage form ([[Property Insurance Limits and Deductibles]]).

> [!trap] Looking for common conditions in the coverage form
> In a commercial package, cancellation, changes, premiums and transfer of rights sit in IL 00 17 and apply to every coverage part. The conditions in a coverage form, such as the CGL's, apply only to that coverage part.
