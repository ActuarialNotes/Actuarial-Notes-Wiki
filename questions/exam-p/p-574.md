A gardener models his strawberry ($S$) / blueberry ($B$) harvest with the following joint probability distribution. Note that three of the entries are smudged and cannot be read.

| $S$ \ $B$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.07 | 0.06 | 0.06 | 0.05 | 0.01 |
| 2 | 0.07 | 0.10 | 0.08 | 0.05 | 0.03 |
| 3 | 0.04 | 0.05 | 0.06 | 0.05 | 0.04 |
| 4 | 0.01 | 0.02 | ? | ? | ? |

Calculate the variance of the strawberry harvest.

- A) $1.03$
- B) $1.04$
- C) $1.07$
- D) $1.09$
- E) Cannot be determined based on information provided.

## Explanation

The variance of $S$ needs only the **marginal** distribution of $S$ — the row totals. Three rows are fully legible:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S=1] &= 0.07+0.06+0.06+0.05+0.01 = 0.25 \\
P[S=2] &= 0.07+0.10+0.08+0.05+0.03 = 0.33 \\
P[S=3] &= 0.04+0.05+0.06+0.05+0.04 = 0.24
\end{align*}
$$

The fourth row is incomplete, but its total follows from the requirement that all probabilities sum to 1:

$$
P[S=4] = 1 - 0.25 - 0.33 - 0.24 = 0.18
$$

That is why choice (E) is wrong — the individual smudged cells are unrecoverable, but their *sum* is not, and only the sum matters here.

Now the moments:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[S]   &= 1(0.25) + 2(0.33) + 3(0.24) + 4(0.18) = 2.35 \\
E[S^2] &= 1(0.25) + 4(0.33) + 9(0.24) + 16(0.18) = 6.61
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(S) &= 6.61 - (2.35)^2 \\
              &= 6.61 - 5.5225 \\
              &= \mathbf{1.0875}
\end{align*}
$$

Recognising that a marginal variance never needs the interior cells is the entire insight; anyone who tries to reconstruct the three missing entries individually will conclude the problem is unsolvable.
