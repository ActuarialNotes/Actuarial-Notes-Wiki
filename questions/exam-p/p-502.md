A medical insurance policyholder just experienced a hospitalization, resulting in an undetermined financial loss that is exponentially distributed. The policyholder has Plan A, which reimburses the first two units for each hospitalization. The policyholder could have chosen Plan B, which reimburses the first ten units for each hospitalization.

The probability that this hospitalization is completely covered under Plan A is 0.15.

Calculate the probability that this hospitalization would have been completely covered had the policyholder chosen Plan B.

- A) $0.200$
- B) $0.351$
- C) $0.443$
- D) $0.556$
- E) $0.750$

## Explanation

Complete coverage under a plan means the loss does not exceed that plan's limit. Let $\lambda$ be the exponential rate.

**Plan A** covers the loss fully when $X \leq 2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.15 &= P[X \leq 2] = 1 - e^{-2\lambda} \\
e^{-2\lambda} &= 0.85
\end{align*}
$$

**Plan B** covers it fully when $X \leq 10$. Rather than solving for $\lambda$, note that $10 = 5(2)$, so

$$
\begin{align*}
e^{-10\lambda} &= \left(e^{-2\lambda}\right)^5 = (0.85)^5
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \leq 10] &= 1 - (0.85)^5 \\
             &= 1 - 0.44371 \\
             &= \mathbf{0.5563}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 0.750, is $5(0.15)$ — scaling the probability with the limit, which the exponential does not do. Each additional two units of coverage multiplies the *uncovered* probability by 0.85, so five such blocks leave $0.85^5 = 0.44$ uncovered rather than nothing. Choice (C), 0.443, is that uncovered probability itself.
