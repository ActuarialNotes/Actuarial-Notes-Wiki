An amusement park has two roller coasters. This year, the numbers of accidents occurring on the first and second roller coasters are Poisson distributed with means $\lambda_1 = 0.5$ and $\lambda_2$, respectively. The probability that at least one accident occurs on the second roller coaster is twice the probability for the first roller coaster.

Calculate $\lambda_2$.

- A) $1.00$
- B) $1.19$
- C) $1.23$
- D) $1.55$
- E) $2.00$

## Explanation

For a Poisson variable with mean $\lambda_j$, the probability of at least one accident is the complement of zero accidents:

$$
P[X_j \geq 1] = 1 - \frac{e^{-\lambda_j}\lambda_j^0}{0!} = 1 - e^{-\lambda_j}
$$

The stated relationship is between these *probabilities*, not between the means:

$$
1 - e^{-\lambda_2} = 2\left(1 - e^{-\lambda_1}\right)
$$

Substituting $\lambda_1 = 0.5$ and solving:

$$
\begin{align*}
e^{-\lambda_2} &= 2e^{-0.5} - 1 \\
\lambda_2 &= -\ln\left(2e^{-0.5} - 1\right) \\
          &= -\ln(0.21306) \\
          &= \mathbf{1.546}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 1.00, is $2\lambda_1$ — doubling the mean rather than the probability. Doubling the probability requires *more* than doubling the mean, because $1 - e^{-\lambda}$ is concave: it rises quickly at first and then flattens as it approaches 1. Note the answer must stay below $-\ln(2e^{-0.5}-1)$'s ceiling; had $2(1-e^{-0.5})$ exceeded 1 the problem would have had no solution at all.
