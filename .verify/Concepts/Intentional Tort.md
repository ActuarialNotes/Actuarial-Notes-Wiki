## [F-002] Private-facts definition omits that the facts are true
- entry_type: finding
- author: agent:validate-v1
- run_id: 2026-10-30T22:59Z/f5d8
- date: 2026-10-30
- severity: minor
- status: open
- locus: Economic and dignitary torts bullet, public disclosure of private facts sentence
- claim: The added definition of public disclosure of private facts does not say the matter publicized is true, which is what separates it from defamation and false light on the same bullet.
- evidence: Lake v. Wal-Mart, 582 N.W.2d 231, quotes Restatement (Second) §652D: one 'gives publicity to a matter concerning the private life of another * * * if the matter publicized is of a kind that (a) would be highly offensive to a reasonable person, and (b) is not of legitimate concern to the public' — the added sentence matches it. Chemerinsky (Chapman L. Rev.): 'unlike defamation, which creates liability for false speech, the tort of public disclosure of private facts inherently creates liability for truthful speech.' The Restatement's Special Note to §652D says the same ('publicity given to true statements of fact').
- source_rank: 3
- proposed_action: Insert 'true' before 'matter' in the definition.
- applied: true
- fingerprint: 6ca622d40c7a
