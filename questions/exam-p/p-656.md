A policyholder experiences two sports injuries, each resulting in three possible outcomes: no hospital stay, a short hospital stay, or a long hospital stay.

The joint probability function for $i$ short hospital stays and $j$ long hospital stays is

$$
p(i, j) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{2!}{i!\,j!\,(2 - i - j)!}(0.2)^i(0.1)^j(0.7)^{2-i-j}, & 0 \le i + j \le 2 \\[6pt]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the probability that at least one of the two injuries results in a long hospital stay.

- A) $0.05$
- B) $0.15$
- C) $0.17$
- D) $0.19$
- E) $0.36$

## Explanation

"At least one long stay" means $j \ge 1$, which covers three cells:

$$
\begin{align*}
p(0,1) &= \frac{2!}{0!\,1!\,1!}(0.2)^0(0.1)^1(0.7)^1 = 0.14 \\
p(0,2) &= \frac{2!}{0!\,2!\,0!}(0.1)^2 = 0.01 \\
p(1,1) &= \frac{2!}{1!\,1!\,0!}(0.2)(0.1) = 0.04
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
P[j \ge 1] = 0.14 + 0.01 + 0.04 = \mathbf{0.19}
$$

The complement confirms it in one line. Each injury independently produces a long stay with probability 0.1, so

$$
P[\text{at least one long stay}] = 1 - (0.9)^2 = 0.19
$$

Choice (E), 0.36, is that same complement computed with the wrong outcome probability: $1 - (0.8)^2 = 0.36$ uses 0.2, the *short*-stay probability, in place of 0.1.
