A patient must undergo hospitalization and surgery. The hospitalization and surgery charges are modeled by random variables uniformly distributed on the intervals $[0, c]$ and $[0, 3c-18]$, respectively, where $c$ is a constant larger than 6.

The standard deviation of the hospitalization charge is $4\sqrt{3}$.

Calculate the standard deviation of the surgery charge.

- A) $2.8$
- B) $7.3$
- C) $10.4$
- D) $15.6$
- E) $20.8$

## Explanation

A uniform distribution on an interval of length $L$ has variance $L^2/12$, so its standard deviation is $L/\sqrt{12}$.

**Find $c$.** The hospitalization interval has length $c$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{c}{\sqrt{12}} &= 4\sqrt{3} \\
c &= 4\sqrt{3}\sqrt{12} = 4\sqrt{36} = 24
\end{align*}
$$

**Apply the same formula to the surgery charge**, whose interval has length $3c - 18$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{SD} &= \frac{3(24) - 18}{\sqrt{12}} \\
          &= \frac{54}{\sqrt{12}} \\
          &= \mathbf{15.5885}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 20.8, is $3(4\sqrt{3}) = 20.78$ — tripling the standard deviation, which would be right only if the surgery interval were $[0, 3c]$. Because standard deviation is linear in interval length, the $-18$ subtracts exactly $18/\sqrt{12} = 5.2$ from that.
