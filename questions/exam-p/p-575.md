An insurer's quarterly profits are independent and normally distributed with mean 8 and standard deviation 24. A quarterly loss counts as a negative profit.

Let $Z$ represent the standard normal random variable.

Determine the probability that the insurer makes an overall positive profit in a given four-quarter period.

- A) $P[Z > -3.00]$
- B) $P[Z > -1.50]$
- C) $P[Z > -0.67]$
- D) $P[Z > -0.33]$
- E) $P[Z > -0.17]$

## Explanation

The four-quarter total $S$ is a sum of independent normal variables, hence normal. Means add by a factor of 4, but **variances** add, so the standard deviation grows only by $\sqrt{4} = 2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[S] &= 4(8) = 32 \\
\text{SD}(S) &= \sqrt{4}(24) = 48
\end{align*}
$$

Standardizing the break-even point:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S > 0] &= P\left[Z > \frac{0 - 32}{48}\right] \\
         &= \mathbf{P[Z > -0.67]}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), $-0.33$, comes from scaling the standard deviation by 4 instead of 2, giving $32/96$. A single quarter's figures, $-8/24$, give the same $-0.33$. The gain from pooling is exactly the ratio $4/\sqrt{4} = 2$: the four-quarter $z$-score is twice the quarterly one, which is what makes a full year more reliably profitable than any single quarter.
