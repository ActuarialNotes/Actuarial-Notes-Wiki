You are given the following information about two insurance policies:

i) Losses covered by each policy follow a normal distribution with mean 8000.
ii) Losses covered by Policy I have standard deviation $k$.
iii) Losses covered by Policy II have standard deviation $1.5k$.

The $p$th percentile of losses covered by Policy I is equal to the 81st percentile of losses covered by Policy II.

Calculate $p$.

- A) $83.2$
- B) $85.7$
- C) $88.2$
- D) $90.6$
- E) $93.2$

## Explanation

Both distributions share the mean 8000, so work with distances from it measured in standard deviations.

The 81st percentile of the standard normal is $z_{0.81} = 0.8779$, so Policy II's 81st percentile sits at

$$
8000 + 0.8779(1.5k) = 8000 + 1.3168k
$$

For Policy I, whose standard deviation is $k$, that same loss amount is $1.3168$ standard deviations above the mean:

$$
\begin{align*}
p &= 100\,\Phi(1.3168) \\
  &= \mathbf{90.6}
\end{align*}
$$

The unknown $k$ cancels entirely — only the ratio 1.5 of the two standard deviations matters. Policy II is more variable, so a fixed loss amount sits further out in Policy I's tighter distribution, which is why $p$ exceeds 81. Choice (A), 83.2, is close to what a small ratio would give; the shift from 81 to 90.6 reflects the full 50% increase in spread.
