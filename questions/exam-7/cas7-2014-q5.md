An insurance company has 1,000 exposures uniformly distributed throughout the accident year. The a priori ultimate loss is \$800 per exposure unit.

The expected loss payment pattern is approximated by the following loglogistic function where $G$ is the cumulative proportion of ultimate losses paid and $x$ represents the average age of reported losses in months.

- $G(x) = \dfrac{x^{\omega}}{x^{\omega} + \theta^{\omega}}$
- $\omega = 2.5$
- $\theta = 24$

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the expected losses paid in the first 36 months after the beginning of the accident year.

### Explanation
Exposures are written uniformly through the year, so at 36 months the average age of the accident year's losses is $36 - 6 = 30$ months.

$$G(30) = \frac{30^{2.5}}{30^{2.5} + 24^{2.5}} = 0.636$$

$$\text{Expected paid by 36 months} = 1{,}000 \times \$800 \times 0.636 = \$508{,}800$$

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to know the average age of the losses, the initial ultimate loss estimate, and the application of the two to calculate estimated paid losses.
The most common mistake was candidates inappropriately calculating the average age.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Assume the actual cumulative paid losses at 36 months after the beginning of the accident year are \$650,000. Estimate the ultimate loss for the accident year using assumptions based upon the Cape Cod method.

### Explanation
The Cape Cod estimate adds the expected unpaid (from the a priori ultimate of $1{,}000 \times \$800 = \$800{,}000$) to the actual paid:

$$\text{Ultimate} = \text{Paid} + \text{Expected Unpaid} = 650{,}000 + (1 - 0.636) \times 800{,}000 = \$941{,}200$$

Alternatively:

Using truncation: $G(66) = 0.926$, so truncate at twice 3 years = 6 years.

$$\text{Ultimate} = 650{,}000 + (0.926 - 0.636) \times 800{,}000 = \$882{,}000$$

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to know to how to calculate the estimated unpaid loss, and add the actual paid losses to this amount to arrive at the ultimate.
The most common mistake was candidates using the wrong initial Ultimate loss.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Estimate the ultimate loss for the accident year based on the loglogistic payment model and the actual payments through 36 months, disregarding the a priori expectation.

### Explanation
Ignoring the a priori, the LDF-style estimate divides actual paid by the expected percent paid:

$$\text{Ultimate} = \frac{650{,}000}{0.636} = \$1{,}022{,}013$$

Alternatively:

Using truncation at 6 years:

$$\text{Ultimate} = 650{,}000 \times \frac{0.926}{0.636} = \$946{,}384$$

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to know to divide the actual paid loss by the estimated percent paid to date, which ignores the a-priori loss estimate.

## Part d (1 point)

Calculate a reserve estimate for the accident year by credibility-weighting the two estimates of ultimate loss in parts b. and c. above using the Benktander method.

### Explanation
The Benktander credibility weight is the expected percent paid, $z = G(30) = 0.636$.

$$U_{GB} = z \times U_{LDF} + (1 - z) \times U_{CC} = 0.636 \times 1{,}022{,}013 + (1 - 0.636) \times 941{,}200 = 992{,}597$$

$$R_{GB} = 992{,}597 - 650{,}000 = \$342{,}597$$

Alternatively:

Weight the reserves directly:

$$R_{GB} = z \times R_{LDF} + (1 - z) \times R_{CC} = 0.636 \times (1{,}022{,}013 - 650{,}000) + (1 - 0.636) \times (941{,}200 - 650{,}000) = \$342{,}597$$

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to appropriately weight the ultimate loss estimates from parts a. and b., per the Benktander method.
The most common mistake made on this part of the question was not subtracting paid loss from ultimate loss in order to determine the reserve estimate.
Some candidates used a different method that produced the correct answer, but did not utilize the answers in parts b and c, as the question specified.  Credit was not awarded for these responses.
