A 35-year-old parent has a 20-year mortgage and two young children. She wants the largest death benefit her budget allows until the mortgage is paid off, and she would like the option of keeping some coverage for life later without having to prove she is still in good health. Which policy best fits these needs?

- A) A 20-year level term policy that is convertible to cash value coverage
- B) A whole life policy with premiums payable to age 65
- C) A 10-year nonrenewable term policy, bought again when it expires
- D) A universal life policy funded at the lowest premium the insurer allows

## Explanation

Term insurance pays only on death within the term and generally builds no cash value, so it buys the largest death benefit for the premium. A 20-year term matches the mortgage, and a conversion privilege lets the insured convert to cash value coverage during the conversion period without new evidence of health, at the higher premium of the permanent policy.

- **B** buys lifetime coverage with a cash value, so its level premium starts far higher than term for the same face amount, and the limited-payment schedule raises it further. It gives much less death benefit for her budget.
- **C** fails the second need: a nonrenewable policy can't be continued, so after 10 years she must apply, and qualify, for a new policy at an older age and possibly worse health.
- **D** is a cash value policy whose charges come out of the account. Funding it at the minimum risks the account falling too low and the coverage ending, and it still buys less protection per dollar than term.
