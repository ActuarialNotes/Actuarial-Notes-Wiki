A company has liabilities of 1000 and 750 due at the end of years two and four, respectively. The company creates an investment portfolio that produces asset cash flows of $X$ immediately and $Y$ at the end of year three. The portfolio is constructed to match the present value and duration of the company's payment obligations, using an annual effective interest rate of 6%.

Calculate $Y$.

- A) 1000
- B) 1385
- C) 1650
- D) 1667
- E) 1768

## Explanation

Let $PV$ be the present value of the liabilities minus the present value of the assets, as a function of the interest rate $i$. Matching present values makes $PV = 0$, and with the present values equal, matching durations makes the derivative $PV' = 0$ as well:

$$
\begin{align*}
PV &= 1000(1+i)^{-2} + 750(1+i)^{-4} - X - Y(1+i)^{-3} \\
   &= 0 \\
PV' &= -2000(1+i)^{-3} - 3000(1+i)^{-5} + 3Y(1+i)^{-4} \\
    &= 0
\end{align*}
$$

The second equation involves only $Y$. At $i = 0.06$:

$$
\begin{align*}
3Y(1.06)^{-4} &= 2000(1.06)^{-3} + 3000(1.06)^{-5} \\
2.37628Y &= 1679.24 + 2241.77 \\
         &= 3921.01 \\
Y &= 1650.06
\end{align*}
$$
