A large lot of circuit boards contains 10% that are defective. A test for defective boards is correct with 80% probability if the board is defective and correct with only 40% probability if the board is not defective.

A randomly selected board is tested and the test indicates that the board is defective.

Calculate the probability that the board is defective.

- A) $2/25$
- B) $1/10$
- C) $2/17$
- D) $4/31$
- E) $2/11$

## Explanation

The two accuracy figures are stated as "correct", so they must be translated into probabilities of a *positive* result.

For a defective board, "correct" means the test says defective:

$$
P[+ \mid D] = 0.8
$$

For a good board, "correct" means the test says **not** defective, so a positive result is the error:

$$
P[+ \mid D^c] = 1 - 0.4 = 0.6
$$

That flip is the whole difficulty of the question. Now apply Bayes' theorem with the 10% prior:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[D \mid +] &= \frac{(0.1)(0.8)}{(0.1)(0.8) + (0.9)(0.6)} \\
            &= \frac{0.08}{0.08 + 0.54} \\
            &= \frac{0.08}{0.62} \\
            &= \mathbf{\frac{4}{31}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 2/17, is what you get by taking $P[+ \mid D^c] = 0.4$ without the flip. The test is barely informative — it produces false positives on 60% of good boards — so the posterior of 0.129 is only slightly above the 0.10 prior.
