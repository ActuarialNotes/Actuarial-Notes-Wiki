# Exam 6I

The **International Regulation and Financial Reporting Exam 6I** is the international version of the CAS's Exam 6, which every [[Associate of the Casualty Actuarial Society (ACAS)|ACAS]] candidate sits in one version — [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Canada]], [[Exam 6U (CAS)|United States]], International or [[Exam 6T (CAS)|Taipei]]. It is a 4-hour computer-based exam, sat at Pearson VUE centres. It covers the regulatory systems and the [[IFRS|International Financial Reporting Standards]] used outside the United States and Canada, including [[IFRS 17]], [[Solvency II]], [[Takaful]] (Islamic) regulation and the regulation specific to China. Candidates should know how to apply its frameworks and concepts to specific examples from four markets: China, Hong Kong, Malaysia and Singapore.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The appointment is 4.5 hours: a 4-hour exam, a 15-minute scheduled break, and 15 minutes for the tutorial, the confidentiality agreement and the end-of-exam survey. Items may be multiple choice, multiple selection, point and click, fill in the blank, matching, constructed response or spreadsheet.
>
> Every item is written to one of four cognitive levels: Remember (5–10%), Understand and Apply (65–85%), Analyze and Evaluate (10–20%) and Create (0–5%).
>
> The Fall 2026 window was October 19–27, 2026, with registration closing September 29, 2026. The fee was $850, or $680 for full-time students (CAS exam page, read 2026-10-17). The CAS offers registered candidates a digital study kit free of charge. The syllabus's supplemental readings aren't covered on the exam.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- A. Regulation of Insurance {15–20%}
> Candidates will be able to understand the role of the [[Insurance Industry|insurance business]] as a supplier of a [[Vital Service|vital service]].
>
> 1. Understand [[Market Conduct Regulation|marketplace regulation]] within a [[Risk-Based Capital|risk-based capital]] framework.
> 2. Describe the necessity, operations, and [[Risk Transfer|risk transfer]] processes of [[Government and Industry Insurance Programs|mainstream government and industry programs]], and how these programs interact with the [[Voluntary Private Insurance Market|voluntary private insurance sector]] in disaster, catastrophe and [[Health Insurance|health insurance]], including programs for catastrophes, [[Terrorism Insurance|terrorism]], and agriculture.
> 3. Understand [[Market Discipline|market discipline]] in insurance imposed by regulators (e.g., [[Financial Reporting|financial reporting]], product and [[Rate Regulation|rate filing]]).
> 4. Understand conditions of effective [[Insurance Supervision|insurance supervision]].
> 5. Understand [[Market Conduct Regulation|marketplace conduct]] and related issues (e.g., [[Professional Code|professional codes]] of conduct for marketing and [[Distribution Activity|distribution activities]], [[Consumer Protection|consumer regulation]]).
>
> **Readings:** Avraham · Atreya et al. · Chaplin et al. · Dror and Piesse · Eling Europe · Eling Discipline · Fang and Xu · Feldblum · FDC · Frees · Government Insurers Study Note · IAIS ICP1, ICP2 · Klein · Kousky · Kurniandi · MAPFRE · McAneney et al. · OCED · Swartz · Takaful · Tipping · Webel

> [!example]- B. Solvency {15–25%}
> 1. Understand [[Solvency|solvency components]].
> 2. Understand [[Capital Adequacy|capital adequacy]] components of [[Solvency Regulation|solvency regulation]].
> 3. Understand [[ORSA|own risk and solvency assessment (ORSA)]] and [[Enterprise Risk Management|enterprise risk management (ERM)]] in general.
> 4. Understand mainstream global [[Solvency Assessment|solvency assessment]].
> 5. Calculate and compare [[Capital Measures|capital measures]] (e.g., available [[Capital|capital]], [[Regulatory Capital|required capital]]) under different [[Financial Reporting|financial reporting]] or [[Solvency Standard|solvency standards]]. This task does not apply to the [[October 2026 Administration|October 2026 administration]].
>
> **Readings:** Geneva · IAA Solvency · IAA ISAP · IAA ORSA · IAA Risk Book · IAA Stress · MAPFRE · New Zealand

> [!example]- C. Financial Reporting {35–40%}
> Candidates will be able to understand the key principles underlying [[IFRS 17]] including the [[Financial Statement Presentation|financial statement presentation]].
>
> 1. Understand the various [[Risk-Based Capital|risk-based capital]] frameworks, their implementation, and their purpose in [[Aligning Insurer|aligning insurers]] with [[Economic Capital|economic capital-based decision making]].
> 2. Compare and contrast key principles and concepts of major [[Accounting Frameworks|financial reporting practices]] around the world (e.g., [[GAAP|USGAAP]], [[IFRS 17]]).
> 3. Describe the key components of [[IFRS 17|IFRS 17 reporting]].
> 4. Evaluate the [[Financial Health|financial health]] of an [[Insurance Entity|insurance entity]] based on various [[Solvency Assessment|solvency frameworks]].
>
> **Readings:** Cai et al. · IFRS Study Note · EY IFRS 17 · EY PAA Eligibility · EY Illustrative Financials · IAA ISAP · IAA Stress

> [!example]- D. International Reinsurance {5–15%}
> The candidates will be able to understand [[Reinsurance Accounting|reinsurance accounting]] terminologies and practices.
>
> 1. Understand the impact of [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] and [[Risk Transfer|risk transfer]] on the [[Financial Health|financial health]] and [[Capital|capital]] of an [[Insurer|insurer]].
> 2. Understand the [[Main Cause|main cause]] of [[Reinsurance Credit Risk|reinsurance failure]] and associated impact.
> 3. Understand [[Commutations|reinsurance commutation]] basics, including commutation impact evaluation.
> 4. Understand the basics of [[Reinsurance Accounting|reinsurance accounting]].
> 5. Understand [[Reinsurance Accounting|reinsurance accounting]] and its impact on [[Financial Statements|financial statements]].
> 6. Evaluate [[Risk Transfer|risk transfer]] (e.g., [[Risk Transfer Testing|risk transfer test]], calculating [[Insurance Premium|premium]] and loss) in a [[Reinsurance|reinsurance contract]] utilizing numerical techniques (e.g., [[Expected Reinsurer Deficit|ERD]], [[10-10 Rule|10-10 rule]]) while understanding their limitations.
>
> **Readings:** Blanchard & Klann · Cedar & Thompson · EY IFRS 17 · EY Illustrative Financials · IFRS Example · IFRS Study Note · Klann · Ollodart · Pocket Guide · Ruhm et. al.

> [!example]- E. Professional Responsibilities of the Actuary in Financial Reporting {10–20%}
> The candidates will be able to apply [[Professional Responsibilities of the Actuary|professional responsibilities of an actuary]] as defined by standards of practice, regulators, and [[Insurance Law|insurance laws]] for [[Financial Reporting|financial reporting]] from an [[International Viewpoint|international viewpoint]].
>
> 1. Describe the oversight role of the [[Actuarial Function|actuarial function]] as part of the [[Three Lines of Defense|lines of defense]] of [[Effective Risk|effective risk]] management of an [[Insurer|insurance company]] (e.g., signing [[Statement of Actuarial Opinion|statements of opinion]], [[Financial Statements|financial statements]]).
> 2. Describe the role of [[Actuarial Professional Standards|actuarial professional standards]] and their relationships with regulations.
> 3. Understand the [[Principles of Professionalism|professional principles]] upon which the profession is founded and describe how these [[Principles Guide|principles guide]] an [[Actuary's Professional Conduct|actuary’s professional conduct]] and the outcomes of their work.
> 4. Describe common [[International Standards of Actuarial Practice|actuarial standards of practice]] applicable to general actuarial work and to [[Financial Reporting|financial reporting]].
>
> **Readings:** IAA Professionalism · IAA ISAP · IAA Risk Book
