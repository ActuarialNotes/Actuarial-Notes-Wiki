A restaurant's oven was bought four years ago for \$18,000. An oven of like kind and quality costs \$24,000 today, and the oven's useful life is 12 years. Measuring actual cash value as replacement cost less straight-line depreciation, what is the oven's ACV?

- A) \$6,000
- B) \$8,000
- C) \$12,000
- D) \$16,000

## Explanation

ACV starts from today's replacement cost and deducts depreciation for the part of the useful life used up.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{ACV} &= 24{,}000 \times \left(1 - \frac{4}{12}\right) \\
&= 16{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — \$6,000 is the rise in price since purchase, not a value of the oven.
- **B** — \$8,000 is the depreciation, not what is left after it.
- **C** — \$12,000 depreciates the original purchase price instead of today's replacement cost.
