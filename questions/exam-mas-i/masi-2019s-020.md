A sample of losses has the following observations:

$$
\{400, 600, 900, 1100, 1200\}
$$

A rectangular kernel with bandwidth 300 is used to estimate the probability density function (pdf) of the losses at $x = 800$. The resulting estimate is $\tilde{f}_R(800)$.

A triangular kernel with bandwidth 300 is used to estimate the pdf of the losses at $x = 800$. The resulting estimate is $\tilde{f}_T(800)$.

The absolute difference in the estimates is denoted $z$, where $z = \left|\tilde{f}_R(800) - \tilde{f}_T(800)\right|$.

Calculate $z$.

- A) Less than 0.0001
- B) At least 0.0001, but less than 0.0002
- C) At least 0.0002, but less than 0.0003
- D) At least 0.0003, but less than 0.0004
- E) At least 0.0004

## Explanation

A kernel density estimate averages one kernel per observation. With bandwidth $b$, the rectangular kernel centred at $y$ is a uniform density on $[y - b, y + b]$, and the triangular kernel is a tent on the same interval:

$$
\begin{align*}
k^R_y(x) &= \frac{1}{2b}, \qquad y - b \le x \le y + b \\
k^T_y(x) &= \frac{b - |x - y|}{b^2}, \qquad y - b \le x \le y + b
\end{align*}
$$

At $x = 800$ with $b = 300$, the observations within 300 are 600 (distance 200), 900 (distance 100) and 1100 (distance exactly 300, on the edge of the closed interval). 400 and 1200 are 400 away and contribute nothing.

Rectangular — each of the three contributes $1/600$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\tilde{f}_R(800) &= \frac{1}{5}\left(\frac{3}{600}\right) \\
&= 0.001
\end{align*}
$$

Triangular — 600 contributes $(300 - 200)/300^2$, 900 contributes $(300 - 100)/300^2$, and 1100 contributes $(300 - 300)/300^2 = 0$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\tilde{f}_T(800) &= \frac{1}{5}\left(\frac{100 + 200 + 0}{90{,}000}\right) \\
&= 0.000667
\end{align*}
$$

The difference:

$$
\begin{align*}
z &= |0.001 - 0.000667| \\
&= 0.000333
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[0.0003, 0.0004)$, so the answer is **D**.
