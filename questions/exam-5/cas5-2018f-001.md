An insurer is considering changing the exposure base for boat owners line of business from boat-years to the insured value of the boat.

The insurer offers the following coverages for boat owners:

- Liability coverage pays for damages to another boat or injuries of people not on the insured's boat.
- Physical damage coverage pays for damages to the insured's boat caused by common risks, such as sinking, fire, storms, theft, and collision.

## Part a (2.5 points)

Using three criteria for a good exposure base, evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed change in exposure base for both liability and physical damage coverages and provide a recommendation for the preferred exposure base.

### Explanation

The three criteria for a good exposure base are that it be **proportional to expected loss**, **practical** (objective, easy to obtain and verify, and inexpensive to administer), and **considerate of historical precedence**.

**Liability coverage**

- *Proportional to expected loss:* No. Insured value measures what the insured's own boat is worth, but liability losses are damages to other boats and injuries to people not on the insured's boat. A more expensive boat does not cause proportionally larger third-party damage, so the relationship breaks down.
- *Practical:* Weak. The value of a boat is not easy to obtain and verify objectively — market value, original purchase price and replacement cost all differ, and a value supplied by the insured is subject to manipulation. Boat-years, by contrast, is objective and verifiable.
- *Considerate of historical precedence:* No. Changing the base requires system, rating-algorithm and statistical-reporting changes, and it disrupts insureds through premium swings that are unrelated to any change in their risk.

**Physical damage coverage**

- *Proportional to expected loss:* Yes. Repair and replacement costs rise with the value of the boat, so insured value has a logical, direct relationship to physical damage severity.
- *Practical:* Same weakness as liability — the value is harder to obtain and verify than a simple count of boat-years, and it is open to manipulation by the insured.
- *Considerate of historical precedence:* Same as liability — the change is costly for the insurer to implement and disruptive to insureds.

**Recommendation**

Continue to use boat-years. Insured value fails the proportionality criterion outright for liability coverage, and although it satisfies proportionality for physical damage, it is less practical than boat-years and abandons historical precedence for both coverages. The limited benefit on the physical damage side does not justify the implementation cost and premium disruption.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to:

- List and define the 3 criteria of a good exposure base
- Justify how insured value meets each of these criteria for both Liability and Property damage coverages
- Recommend and justify an appropriate exposure base that meets these criteria

Common mistakes include:

- Not accurately listing or defining 3 criteria of a good exposure base
- Not evaluating the differences between Liability and Property Damages Coverages
- Not recommending an exposure base or recommending a base that was not supported by the justification
