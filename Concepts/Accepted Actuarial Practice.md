**Accepted Actuarial Practice** is the body of practice a Canadian actuary is required to follow. It is defined by the [[Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)]]'s **[[Standards of Practice]]**, supplemented by [[Educational Note|educational notes]] and other guidance, and by generally accepted practice where the standards are silent. The phrase matters because it is the statutory hook: the [[Insurance Companies Act]] requires [[Insurance Contract Liabilities]] to be valued "in accordance with accepted actuarial practice."

- **The statutory link is what gives the CIA's standards legal effect.** Parliament did not write a valuation method into the Act; it referred to the profession's own standards, so the CIA's Standards of Practice become binding on federally regulated insurers by reference — with the addition of "as modified by any direction of the Superintendent."
- **It is not a single document.** The hierarchy runs: **Rules of Professional Conduct** (conduct), **Standards of Practice** (binding practice requirements), **educational notes and research** (non-binding guidance on how to comply), and **generally accepted practice** where none of these speak.
- **Deviation is permitted with disclosure.** An actuary may depart from the standards where circumstances warrant, provided the departure and the reason are disclosed. What is not permitted is departing silently.
- **It evolves.** Accepted practice in 2026 is not what it was in 2020: [[IFRS 17]] replaced [[Margin for Adverse Deviations|margins for adverse deviations]] with the [[Risk Adjustment for Non-Financial Risk|risk adjustment]], and [[FCT]] replaced DCAT. An actuary applying yesterday's practice today is not complying, and the continuing professional development requirement exists for this reason.
- **The [[Appointed Actuary]] cannot delegate the judgement.** Relying on another actuary's work, or on management's view, is permitted with disclosure — but the opinion given is the Appointed Actuary's, and accepted actuarial practice governs it.

> [!example]- Is This Accepted Actuarial Practice? {Example}
> Assess each.
>
> 1. Selecting a development factor at the low end of a reasonable range because management prefers a lower reserve.
> 2. Using a method not described in any CIA standard or educational note, because the line of business is novel.
> 3. Using the [[Premium Allocation Approach|PAA]] for a three-year contract without documenting the eligibility demonstration.
> 4. Setting the [[Risk Adjustment for Non-Financial Risk|risk adjustment]] by a cost-of-capital method rather than a confidence-level method.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > 1. **Not accepted practice.** Selecting within a range is legitimate; selecting *because management prefers the answer* is not — the basis for the selection must be the evidence. It is also a Rules of Professional Conduct issue (integrity), not merely a technical one, and if the effect is material it engages the [[Duty to Report]].
> > 2. **Potentially accepted practice.** The standards do not enumerate permitted methods. A novel line may require a novel approach, and the actuary must satisfy the general requirements: the method must be appropriate to the work's use, the assumptions supportable, and the approach and its limitations **disclosed**. Novelty is not the problem; undisclosed novelty is.
> > 3. **Not accepted practice**, and this one is a documentation failure rather than a judgement failure. [[IFRS 17]] permits the PAA beyond one year only where the insurer *demonstrates* the results would not differ materially from the [[General Measurement Model|GMM]]. Without the demonstration on file, the eligibility conclusion is unsupported — and the auditor and the regulator will both ask for it.
> > 4. **Accepted practice.** IFRS 17 does not prescribe a technique; it requires the equivalent **confidence level** to be disclosed. A cost-of-capital risk adjustment translated into a disclosed percentile complies fully. Choosing a method the standard permits, and disclosing what the standard requires, is exactly what accepted actuarial practice means.
