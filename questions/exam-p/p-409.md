A repair cost, in hundreds of dollars, is equally likely to be any integer from 3 to 12 inclusive.

Calculate the variance of the repair cost.

- A) $6.75$
- B) $8.25$
- C) $9.92$
- D) $11.92$
- E) $14.38$

## Explanation

The range $\{3, 4, \ldots, 12\}$ contains

$$n = 12 - 3 + 1 = 10$$

equally likely values. For a discrete uniform distribution over $n$ consecutive integers:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X) &= \frac{n^2 - 1}{12} \\
              &= \frac{100 - 1}{12} \\
              &= \frac{99}{12} \\
              &= \mathbf{8.25}
\end{align*}
$$

The variance depends only on **how many** values there are, not where the range sits — shifting a distribution never changes its spread. (The mean does move: $E[X] = (3+12)/2 = 7.5$.)

Choice (A) is the *continuous* uniform variance $(b-a)^2/12 = 81/12$; choice (D) uses $n = 12$ instead of counting the ten values.
