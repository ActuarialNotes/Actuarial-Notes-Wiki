A retailer rates its risks on scales of 1 to 5 and multiplies likelihood by impact. Shoplifting has likelihood 5 and impact 1. A flood at its only distribution center has likelihood 1 and impact 5. Both score 5. What does plotting them on a risk map show that the score hides?

- A) That both risks call for the same treatment, because their scores are equal
- B) That the flood costs five times as much as shoplifting in an average year
- C) That neither risk needs attention, because both score below the scale's midpoint
- D) That shoplifting suits retention with loss control, while the flood suits transfer

## Explanation

The map places each risk by likelihood and impact separately. Shoplifting is frequent and small, the quadrant for retention with loss control; the flood is rare and severe, the quadrant for transfer by insurance. A single score hides that difference. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Equal scores do not mean equal treatment; the score is the product that hides where each risk sits.
- **B** — The ratings are ordinal, so a score is a ranking device, not an expected loss in dollars.
- **C** — A flood that could close the only distribution center may threaten the business; a low product score does not make a severe risk unimportant.
