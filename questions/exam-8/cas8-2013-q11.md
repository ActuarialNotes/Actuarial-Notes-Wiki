Assume that workers compensation rate adequacy in a particular state has improved for several successive years.

## Part a (0.25 points)

Briefly describe the impact this improvement will have on the statewide off-balance.

### Explanation
Lower the off-balance.

### Examiner Report
Candidates needed to state that the off-balance would decrease. Stating the off-balance would be 1.0 or move to 1.0 was not given credit.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Discuss the effect that the off-balance impact will have on the state's indicated rate level.

### Explanation
The off-balance reduces the degree of the indicated rate level change.

### Examiner Report
Candidates needed to demonstrate that they understood that if rate adequacy was improving indicated rate levels would be decreasing, but the off-balance would make the decrease less than expected.

Partial credit was given for stating that the off-balance masks the indicated rate change.

Common answers that did not receive credit included:

*True statements that do not answer the question*

- Off-balance near 1 implies rates are adequate.
- Rate level is based on manual premium which the off-balance does not impact
- There is no change in the off-balance between the experience and prospective period

*Incorrect statement*

- As the off-balance decreases so does standard premium therefore the indicate rate level increases

## Part c (0.5 points)

Discuss the effect on the off-balance factor and the state's premium adequacy over time if inadequate rates are approved.

### Explanation
The off-balance will go up and reduce the degree of premium inadequacy.

### Examiner Report
Most candidates correctly answered that the off-balance factor would increase. To receive full credit candidates also needed to clearly state that the increasing off-balance reduces premium inadequacy. Common answers that did not receive full credit were: Off-balance offsets inadequate rates and premium adequacy is not impacted.

## Part d (0.75 points)

Fully explain why the experience rating off-balance is frequently a credit.

### Explanation
Larger risks that go into the experience rating usually perform better than small risks that are not eligible for experience rating. The off-balance factor is assumed to be 1 for these small risks when calculating the experience rating off-balance, resulting in a credit.

### Examiner Report
To earn full credit candidates needed to clearly state three key pieces of information:

1. There are only certain risks that qualify or receive more credibility
2. Risks that qualify and/or receive more credibility are large
3. These risks have better experience or perform better

Stating that risks have a credit mod or better risk instead of better experience would not have earned credit.
