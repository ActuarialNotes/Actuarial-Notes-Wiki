A bakery leases its shop. At its own expense it installed a walk-in cooler built into the walls and new flooring, neither of which it may remove when the lease ends. The landlord insures the building. The bakery's BPP shows limits for Your Business Personal Property only. A fire destroys the cooler and the flooring. Under which part of the bakery's BPP, if any, is this damage covered?

- A) Your Business Personal Property, as the bakery's use interest in improvements and betterments
- B) Building, because the cooler and flooring are permanently attached to the structure
- C) Personal Property of Others, because the landlord owns the building they are attached to
- D) None of it, because only the landlord's building policy can cover property attached to the building

## Explanation

Improvements and betterments are fixtures, alterations, installations or additions made part of a building the insured occupies but does not own, which it made at its own expense and cannot legally remove. The tenant's use interest in them is listed under **Your Business Personal Property**, so the bakery's BPP limit covers them. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Building coverage is for the building described in the declarations, which the bakery neither owns nor insures; it has no Building limit. Being attached does not move a tenant's improvements into Building coverage under the tenant's policy.
- **C** — Personal Property of Others is property of others in the insured's care. The tenant's interest in improvements it paid for is the tenant's own business personal property.
- **D** — The BPP gives the tenant's use interest in improvements and betterments its own place in business personal property precisely so a tenant can insure what it paid for.
