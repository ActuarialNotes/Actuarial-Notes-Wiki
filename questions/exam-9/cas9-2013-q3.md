Given the following information:

- The expected risk premium of the market index is 8%.
- The standard deviation of the market index is 20%.
- A single-factor model is used to construct an optimal risky portfolio with two components:
  - i. A passive portfolio containing the market index
  - ii. An active portfolio containing some combination of three stocks, with the following estimated parameters:

| Stock$_i$ | $\alpha_i$ | $\sigma^2(e_i)$ | $\beta_i$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.06 | 0.16 | 0.8 |
| 2 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 1.0 |
| 3 | 0.15 | 0.24 | 1.2 |

Calculate the portion of the optimal risky portfolio that is invested in the active portfolio.

### Explanation
| Stock | $W_i^0 = \frac{\alpha_i}{\sigma^2 e_i}$ | $W_i = \frac{W_i^0}{\Sigma W_i}$ | $W_i^* = W_i \times W_A^*$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | .375 = .06/.16 | 0.25 = .375/1.5 | = .1922 = (.25 × .7687) |
| 2 | .5 | 0.333 | .2562 |
| 3 | .625 | 0.4167 | .3203 |
| Sum | 1.5 | 1 | |

1. We calculate initial weights $W_i^0 = \frac{\alpha_i}{\sigma^2 e_i}$
2. We then normalize those weights so that they add up to 1: $W_i = \frac{W_i^0}{\Sigma W_i}$
3. Use those weights to calculate $\alpha_A = \Sigma w_i\alpha_i = (.25) \times .06 + (.333).1 + (.4167).15 = .1108$

   $\sigma^2 e_A = \Sigma w_i^2\sigma^2 e_i = (.25)^2(.16) + (.333)^2(.2) + (.4167)^2(.24) = .0739$
4. Calculated the initial weight in active portfolio: $W_A^0 = \frac{\alpha_A/\sigma^2_A}{E(R_m)/\sigma^2_m} = \frac{.1108/.0739}{.08/(.2)^2} = .7497$
5. Calculate: $W_A^* = \frac{W_A^0}{1 + (1 - \beta)W_A^0} = \frac{.7497}{1 + (1 - 1.033).7497} = .7687$
6. The portion of the optimal risky portfolio invested in Active is $W_A^* = .7687$

### Examiner Report
This question was a straight-forward application of the methodology found on p 266 of the BKM textbook.

The most common mistake candidates made was to be either totally or partially unfamiliar with the method. A couple of other common mistakes are mentioned below.

There are seven steps (formulae) needed to calculate the weight of the active portfolio.

**Step 1:** Compute the initial position of each security in the active portfolio: $w_i^0 = \alpha_i/\sigma^2(e_i)$ [see table below]

**Step 2:** Scale the initial positions so that their sum is unity: $w_i = w_i^0/\Sigma w_i^0$ [see table below]

| Stock | $\alpha_i$ (given) | $\sigma^2(e_i)$ (given) | $\beta_i$ (given) | $w_i^0$ (Step 1) | $w_i$ (Step 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.06 | 0.16 | 0.8 | 0.3750 | 0.2500 |
| 2 | 0.10 | 0.2 | 1.0 | 0.5000 | 0.3333 |
| 3 | 0.15 | 0.24 | 1.2 | 0.6250 | 0.4167 |
| $\Sigma$ | | | | 1.5000 | 1.0000 |

- Risk premium of the market index ($R_M$): 0.08 (given)
- Standard deviation of the market index ($\sigma_M$): 0.20 (given)

**Step 3:** Compute the alpha of the active portfolio:

$$\alpha_A = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i\alpha_i = [.25 \times .06 + .333 \times .10 + .417 \times .15] = 0.110833$$

**Step 4:** Compute the residual variance of the active portfolio:

$$\sigma^2(e_A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i^2\sigma^2(e_i) = [.25^2 \times .16 + .333^2 \times .20 + .417^2 \times .24] = 0.073889$$

A common mistake in Step 4 was failing to square the weights.

**Step 5:** Compute the initial weight of the active portfolio:

$$w_A^0 = \left[\frac{\alpha_A/\sigma^2(e_A)}{E(R_M)/\sigma_M^2}\right] = \left[\frac{0.110833/0.073889}{0.08/0.20^2}\right] = 75.0\%$$

**Step 6:** Compute the beta of the active portfolio:

$$\beta_A = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i\beta_i = [.25 \times 0.8 + .333 \times 1.0 + .417 \times 1.2] = 1.033333$$

**Step 7:** Adjust the initial weight of the active portfolio:

$$w_A = \frac{w_A^0}{1 + (1 - \beta_A)w_A^0} = \frac{0.75}{1 + ((1 - 1.033333) \times .75)} = 0.769231$$

A common mistake in Step 7 was to put $(1 + \beta_A)$ in the denominator rather than $(1 - \beta_A)$. While the point of this step is to adjust for $\beta_A$ being different from unity, the graders chose to only take a quarter-point off for this mistake.
