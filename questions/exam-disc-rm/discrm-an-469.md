Using the traditional risk management matrix, which of a bakery chain's exposures is best suited to treatment by insurance?

- A) A rare fire that would destroy its central bakery, a loss it could not absorb
- B) Frequent minor scrapes to its delivery vans, costing a few hundred dollars each
- C) Occasional loss of small kitchen utensils, of little value each time
- D) A proposed fireworks display, where serious injuries are frequent in the industry

## Explanation

Insurance fits exposures with a **low frequency** and a **high severity** of loss: the loss is too large to retain, and its rarity keeps the premium small next to it. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — High frequency and low severity points to retention with loss control; insuring small, frequent losses costs more in premium than it saves.
- **C** — Low frequency and low severity points to retention.
- **D** — High frequency and high severity points to avoidance.
