A court enters a \$2,000,000 bodily injury judgment against an insured whose CGL has a \$1,000,000 each occurrence limit. The court also awards \$160,000 of prejudgment interest, calculated on the full judgment. The insurer spent \$70,000 on defense counsel and pays its limit immediately after the judgment, so no post-judgment interest is owed by it. How much does the insurer pay in total?

- A) \$1,070,000
- B) \$1,150,000
- C) \$1,230,000
- D) \$2,230,000

## Explanation

Damages are capped at the limit. Supplementary payments are paid in addition to the limits: all expenses the insurer incurs (defense counsel), and prejudgment interest awarded on *that part of the judgment the insurer pays*.

$$
\begin{align*}
I_{\text{pre}} &= 160{,}000 \times \frac{1{,}000{,}000}{2{,}000{,}000} \\
&= 80{,}000 \\
\text{Total} &= 1{,}000{,}000 + 70{,}000 + 80{,}000 \\
&= 1{,}150{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** leaves out prejudgment interest entirely.
- **C** pays prejudgment interest on the full judgment, including the part above the limit.
- **D** pays the whole judgment, ignoring the each occurrence limit.
