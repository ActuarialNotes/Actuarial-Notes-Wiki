Given the following information for two proportional casualty treaties as of December 31, 2011:

| Treaty ID | Inception Date | Treaty Coverage Basis | Written Premium | Aggregate Reported Loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Jan 1, 2009 | Loss Occurring | \$80,000 | \$50,000 |
| B | Jul 1, 2009 | Policies Written | 100,000 | 20,000 |

| Age of Accident Year (in months) | Age to Ultimate LDF |
|---|---|
| 36 | 1.25 |
| 24 | 2.00 |
| 12 | 3.00 |

- Both treaties have a one-year term.
- All underlying policies are one-year, occurrence-based policies with effective dates uniformly distributed over the year.
- Treaty A commission, brokerage and internal expense is 5% of premium.
- Treaty B commission, brokerage and internal expense is 20% of premium.
- The loss development factors apply to all exposures in the applicable accident year.

## Part a (2 points)

Estimate the ultimate losses for the two treaties combined using the Stanard-Bühlmann method.

### Explanation
Remove commission, brokerage and internal expense from the written premium:

$$A: 80{,}000 \times (1 - 0.05) = 76{,}000 \qquad B: 100{,}000 \times (1 - 0.20) = 80{,}000$$

Treaty A (loss occurring, 1/1/09–12/31/09) is all accident year 2009, 36 months mature. Treaty B covers one-year occurrence policies written 7/1/09–6/30/10, so its exposure is earned over AY 2009–2011: 1/8 in 2009 (36 months), 3/4 in 2010 (24 months) and 1/8 in 2011 (12 months).

| AY | Adjusted premium | % reported | Used-up premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2009 | 76,000 + 1/8 of B = 86,000 | 1/1.25 = 0.8 | 68,800 |
| 2010 | 3/4 of B = 60,000 | 0.5 | 30,000 |
| 2011 | 1/8 of B = 10,000 | 0.333 | 3,000 (as printed in the examiner's report) |
| Total | 156,000 | | 102,133 |

$$\text{ELR} = \frac{50\text{k} + 20\text{k}}{102{,}133} = 0.685$$

$$\text{Ultimate} = (0.685)(156{,}000) = 106{,}910$$

(With the Stanard-Bühlmann ELR, reported loss equals ELR × used-up premium in total, so reported + ELR × unused premium is the same as ELR × total premium.)

Alternatively:

A simpler treatment, placing each treaty at a single maturity. At 12/31/11 Treaty A is 36 months mature and Treaty B is 24 months mature (average written date 1/1/10):

$$\text{Used-up premium} = 76{,}000\left(\tfrac{1}{1.25}\right) + 80{,}000\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 100{,}800$$

$$\text{ELR} = \frac{70{,}000}{100{,}800} = 0.694$$

$$\text{IBNR} = 0.694\left[76{,}000\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{1.25}\right) + 80{,}000\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right] = 38{,}333.33$$

$$\text{Ultimate} = \text{IBNR} + \text{reported to date} = \$108{,}333.33$$

The examiners noted that almost all candidates lost points for not allocating the treaty premiums and losses over the three accident years 2009–2011.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Identify a situation in which the chain ladder method is preferred over the Stanard-Bühlmann method when estimating IBNR for a reinsurer with long-tail exposures.

### Explanation
The chain ladder is preferred when it is difficult to bring premium from all years to the same rate level.

Alternatively:

When the premium is difficult to adjust to current rate level, or net of the expenses, it is more appropriate to use the chain ladder.

## Part c (0.25 points)

Identify a situation in which the Stanard-Bühlmann method is preferred over the chain ladder method when estimating IBNR for a reinsurer with long-tail exposures.

### Explanation
Stanard-Bühlmann is preferred when there are fluctuations in reported losses but the ELR (to on-level earned premium) is expected to have remained constant.

## Part d (0.25 points)

Identify the key innovation of the Stanard-Bühlmann method over the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method.

### Explanation
The ELR is based on the loss data, not just judgmentally selected.

Alternatively:

Stanard-Bühlmann uses actual experience to estimate the expected loss ratio, whereas the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method uses an a priori loss ratio.
