Assess each of the models.

### Explanation
**Model 1.** Model 1 achieves a near-perfect fit on the training data, tracking the actual pure premium closely across all deciles. However, on the hold-out sample, Model 1's predictions become erratic and volatile, particularly above decile 5, where it diverges significantly from actual results. This pattern indicates that Model 1 is **overfit** — it has captured noise in the training data rather than the underlying signal, and therefore fails to generalize to new data.

**Model 2.** Model 2 shows a consistent, dampened prediction in both the training and hold-out samples. In training data, it does not match the amplitude of the actual results — it underestimates at high deciles. However, in the hold-out sample, Model 2 performs more reliably than Model 1, maintaining a steady upward trend that broadly tracks actual results. Model 2 appears to focus on the underlying signal rather than noise, suggesting it is **less overfit** and more likely to generalize well. The flat response may indicate that the model is missing some predictive variables.

Model 1 is a poor fit.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know and understand desirable/undesirable characteristics of a generalized linear model (GLM). Candidates were expected to interpret graphical information and make observations about the performance of each GLM.

Common mistakes included:

- Only listing one brief observation per model
- Evaluating the data sets as opposed to the GLMs
- Not specifying which model the commentary was intended for

- Recommending a model without any supporting commentary.  Recommendation of one model over the other was not required.
- Commenting on the possibility of the models performing a particular way due to low volume of data / loss in certain deciles. The prompt stated that all deciles had equal volume.
