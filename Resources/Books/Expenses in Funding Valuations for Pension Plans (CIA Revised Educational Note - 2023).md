A Canadian Institute of Actuaries educational note, dated June 7, 2023 (document 223110), intended to assist actuaries in selecting appropriate assumptions about expenses in funding valuations of pension plans. It is divided into two major sections: the first for going concern valuations, the second for wind-up, hypothetical wind-up and solvency valuations. It was originally published in January 2007 and updated in September 2014; the 2023 update reflects changes in actuarial practice and the Standards of Practice effective December 1, 2022.

## 1 Introduction
- The Standards of Practice require the actuary to make assumptions regarding the payment of expenses; paragraph 3210.05 says the advice should take account of expenses expected to be paid from the plan's assets.
- It is not the role of the actuary to determine whether it is appropriate to pay expenses from the plan's assets; the actuary may rely on guidance from the plan administrator on which expenses may be charged to the plan.

## 2 Going concern
- Two types of expenses are typically charged to pension funds: investment-related and administration-related.
- Provision may be made through an increase in the accrued actuarial liability, an increase in the present value of future normal actuarial costs, or both, following the funding equation PV(Future Benefits and Expenses) = Accrued Actuarial Liability + PV(Future Normal Actuarial Costs).
- Reduction in discount rate
- Explicit allowance in normal actuarial cost
- Inclusion of an explicit provision for future expenses in the accrued actuarial liabilities
- Other approaches
- Other considerations

## 3 Hypothetical wind-up, solvency and actual wind-up valuations
- Quotes Standards of Practice paragraphs 3240.03, 3240.08, 3240.13, 3320.02, 3330.20 and 3330.21 on expenses.
- Wind-up expenses usually include fees related to the actuarial wind-up report(s), fees imposed by a regulator, legal fees, administration expenses, and custodial and investment management expenses.
- Hypothetical wind-ups and solvency valuations
- Actual wind-up

## 4 Special reports
- Where reports are provided for plan conversions, mergers or other non-routine purposes, the actuary would apply the principles of the note as appropriate.
