A company tests five batteries. Each battery has a probability $p$ of being defective, where $p$ is a constant. The events of different batteries being defective are mutually independent.

The number of defective batteries out of the five batteries is a random variable, $N$, where $E(N^2) = 1.80$.

Calculate $\text{Var}(N)$.

- A) $0.80$
- B) $0.98$
- C) $1.15$
- D) $1.67$
- E) $1.80$

## Explanation

$N$ is binomial with $n = 5$, so $E[N] = 5p$ and $\text{Var}(N) = 5p(1-p)$. Write the second moment in terms of $p$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[N^2] &= \text{Var}(N) + \bigl(E[N]\bigr)^2 \\
1.80   &= 5p(1-p) + (5p)^2 \\
       &= 5p - 5p^2 + 25p^2 \\
       &= 5p + 20p^2
\end{align*}
$$

Solve the quadratic $20p^2 + 5p - 1.80 = 0$:

$$
\begin{align*}
p &= \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25 + 144}}{40} \\
  &= \frac{-5 \pm 13}{40}
\end{align*}
$$

The root $p = -0.45$ is impossible, so $p = 8/40 = 0.20$.

Then

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(N) &= 5(0.2)(0.8) \\
              &= \mathbf{0.80}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 1.80, is the given second moment mistaken for the variance. The two differ by $(E[N])^2 = 1$ here — a reminder that $E[N^2]$ exceeds the variance whenever the mean is nonzero.
