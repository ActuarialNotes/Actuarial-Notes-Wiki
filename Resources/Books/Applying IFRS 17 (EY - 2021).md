EY's guide to IFRS 17 Insurance Contracts, the IASB's comprehensive accounting standard for insurance contracts issued in May 2017 and amended in June 2020, in its June 2021 edition. It works through the standard's objective, definitions and scope, the definition of an insurance contract, level of aggregation, recognition, the general model, the premium allocation approach, reinsurance contracts held, participating contracts, modification and derecognition, presentation, disclosure and transition.

## Introduction

- The IASB issued IFRS 17 Insurance Contracts in May 2017 and amended it in June 2020; it is effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2023, and supersedes IFRS 4, an interim standard that allowed a wide variety of accounting practices.
- The standard applies a current value approach to measuring insurance contracts, recognises profit as insurers provide services, and reports underwriting results separately from financing activities.

## 1 Overview of IFRS 17

- IFRS 17 establishes principles for the recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure of insurance contracts issued, reinsurance contracts held and investment contracts with discretionary participation features.
- It combines current measurement of future cash flows with recognition of profit over the period services are provided, presents insurance service results separately from insurance finance income or expenses, and requires a policy choice whether to recognise all insurance finance income or expense in profit or loss or some in other comprehensive income.
