![[Media/Figures/Accepted_Actuarial_Practice.svg|340]]

**Accepted Actuarial Practice** is the body of practice a Canadian actuary is required to follow. It is defined by the [[Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)]]'s [[Standards of Practice]], supplemented by [[Educational Note|educational notes]] and other guidance, and by generally accepted practice where the standards are silent.

It is not a single document. Its sources, from binding to guidance, are:

1. The **Rules of Professional Conduct**, which govern the actuary's conduct.
2. The **Standards of Practice**, the binding practice requirements.
3. **Educational notes and research**, non-binding guidance on how to comply.
4. **Generally accepted practice**, where none of these speak.

> [!example]- An actuary selects a development factor at the low end of a reasonable range because management prefers a lower reserve. Is this accepted practice? {Example}
> - Selecting within a reasonable range is legitimate.
> - The basis for a selection must be the evidence, and here it is management's preference, so it is not accepted practice.
> - It is also a Rules of Professional Conduct issue of integrity, not merely a technical one.
> - If the effect is material, it engages the [[Duty to Report]].

## The statutory link

The [[Insurance Companies Act]] requires [[Insurance Contract Liabilities|insurance contract liabilities]] to be valued "in accordance with accepted actuarial practice", with the addition of "as modified by any direction of the Superintendent". Parliament did not write a valuation method into the Act. It referred to the profession's own standards, so the CIA's Standards of Practice become binding on federally regulated insurers by reference.

The [[Appointed Actuary]] may rely on another actuary's work, or on management's view, with disclosure. The opinion given is still the Appointed Actuary's, and accepted actuarial practice governs it.

## Deviation from the standards

An actuary may depart from the standards where circumstances warrant, provided the departure and the reason for it are disclosed. A departure made silently is not permitted. The standards also do not list the permitted methods, so a method no standard or educational note describes can still be accepted practice if it is appropriate to the work's use, its assumptions are supportable, and the approach and its limitations are disclosed.

> [!example]- For a novel line of business, an actuary uses a method not described in any CIA standard or educational note. Is this accepted actuarial practice? {Example}
> - The standards do not list permitted methods, and a novel line may need a novel approach.
> - The method must still be appropriate to the work's use, with supportable assumptions.
> - The approach and its limitations must be disclosed.
> - With those met, it is accepted practice. Novelty is allowed and undisclosed novelty is not.

> [!example]- An actuary sets the IFRS 17 risk adjustment by a cost-of-capital method rather than a confidence-level method. Is this accepted actuarial practice? {Example}
> - [[IFRS 17]] does not prescribe a technique for the [[Risk Adjustment for Non-Financial Risk|risk adjustment]].
> - It requires the equivalent **confidence level** to be disclosed.
> - A cost-of-capital risk adjustment translated into a disclosed percentile therefore complies, and it is accepted practice.

## Changes in accepted practice

Accepted practice changes over time. [[IFRS 17]] replaced [[Margin for Adverse Deviations|margins for adverse deviations]] with the [[Risk Adjustment for Non-Financial Risk|risk adjustment]], and the [[FCT]] replaced DCAT. An actuary applying an earlier practice today is not complying, which is why the CIA requires continuing professional development.

> [!trap] Missing documentation
> A judgement can be sound and still fail accepted practice when its support is not on file. Using the [[Premium Allocation Approach|PAA]] for a three-year contract requires a demonstration that the results would not differ materially from the [[General Measurement Model|GMM]]. Without that demonstration documented, the eligibility conclusion is unsupported, and the auditor and the regulator will both ask for it.
