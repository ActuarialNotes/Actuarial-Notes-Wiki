Which statement about a policy placed with a nonadmitted insurer through a surplus lines broker is accurate?

- A) The state guaranty fund pays its claims if the insurer becomes insolvent.
- B) The insurer must hold a license in the insured's home state.
- C) The policy must use the state's approved forms and rates.
- D) The broker checks the insurer's eligibility and remits the premium tax.

## Explanation

In the surplus lines market, the **licensed surplus lines broker** is responsible for making sure the insurer meets eligibility criteria and for remitting the surplus lines premium tax to the home state. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Guaranty fund coverage is not available for surplus lines policies; the funds protect policyholders of licensed insurers.
- **B** — A nonadmitted insurer is, by definition, not licensed in the state; it is eligible to write surplus lines there.
- **C** — Freedom from the admitted market's rate and form rules is one reason the surplus lines market can write risks admitted insurers will not.
