A building is insured for \$1,200,000 under a commercial property policy with an 80% coinsurance clause and a \$5,000 deductible. At the time of a fire, the building's value on the policy's valuation basis is \$2,000,000, and the loss is \$400,000. How much does the insurer pay?

- A) \$235,000
- B) \$295,000
- C) \$296,250
- D) \$395,000

## Explanation

The insured needed $0.80 \times 2{,}000{,}000 = 1{,}600{,}000$ of insurance. In the standard form the deductible is subtracted after the coinsurance ratio is applied:

$$
\begin{align*}
R &= \frac{1{,}200{,}000}{0.80 \times 2{,}000{,}000} \\
&= 0.75 \\
\text{Payment} &= 0.75 \times 400{,}000 - 5{,}000 \\
&= 295{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**. The insured bears \$105,000 of the loss for carrying too little insurance.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Uses 100% of value rather than 80% in the denominator: $0.60 \times 400{,}000 - 5{,}000$.
- **C** — Subtracts the deductible before applying the ratio: $0.75 \times 395{,}000$.
- **D** — Ignores the coinsurance clause, as if the building were insured to value.
