A fire makes a family's home unlivable for four months. Their mortgage payment of \$2,500 a month continues, they rent a comparable house for \$3,000 a month, and their commuting costs rise by \$200 a month; their utility bills of \$300 a month at the damaged house stop. In measuring the family's property loss exposure, what is the additional living expense, and which HO-3 coverage pays it?

- A) \$11,600, under Coverage D, loss of use
- B) \$12,800, under Coverage D, loss of use
- C) \$12,800, under Coverage C, personal property
- D) \$22,800, under Coverage A, dwelling

## Explanation

Additional living expense is the **increase** in living costs needed to keep the household's normal standard of living: the new costs less the costs that stop. The mortgage continues either way, so it is not part of the increase:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Monthly increase} &= 3{,}000 + 200 - 300 \\
&= 2{,}900 \\
\text{ALE} &= 4 \times 2{,}900 \\
&= 11{,}600
\end{align*}
$$

It is paid under Coverage D, loss of use. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** adds the rent and commuting costs but doesn't subtract the utilities that stopped.
- **C** also names the wrong coverage; Coverage C insures belongings, not living costs.
- **D** adds the continuing mortgage and treats the expense as part of the dwelling claim.
