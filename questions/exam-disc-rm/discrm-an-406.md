A furniture manufacturer's unendorsed ISO CGL policy lists only a manufacturing classification. Midway through the policy year it opened a showroom selling directly to the public, which it never reported. A customer trips in the showroom and sues. At audit, the insurer discovers the showroom. Which outcome is correct?

- A) The claim is excluded because the showroom's classification was not on the policy, and no premium is due for it
- B) The claim is covered, but the insurer can't charge any premium for the showroom until the policy renews next year
- C) The claim is covered, and at audit the insurer may add a mercantile class charged on showroom sales from its opening
- D) The policy is void from its inception because the insured never reported the showroom to the insurer

## Explanation

An unendorsed ISO CGL doesn't restrict coverage to the operations its classifications describe, so the showroom claim is covered like any other premises claim. Classification is about matching premium to exposure, and the premium audit condition lets the insurer compute premium by its rules and rates, which includes adding a better or additional classification for an operation begun during the period. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Only a classification limitation endorsement, which this policy doesn't have, excludes injury from unscheduled classifications.
- **B** — The audit is exactly where the premium for exposure that arose during the period is charged; it doesn't wait for renewal.
- **D** — A missing classification can be evidence of misrepresentation, but opening a new operation mid-term doesn't void the policy from inception.
