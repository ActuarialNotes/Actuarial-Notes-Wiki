An educational note of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Committee on Life Insurance Financial Reporting, dated November 2006, on the considerations in determining the best estimate assumption for expenses used in valuing the policy liabilities of life insurers. It sets out how an actuary assesses existing expenses through an expense study and translates them into unit expenses, and how prior experience can guide future experience. The CIA archived the note on March 11, 2025.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam CP-321 (SOA)|Exam CP-321]] — Outcomes 2c; pages 19-39 (Appendix A, Conducting an Expense Study); the course strategy guide notes that the CIA has designated the note "Archived" but it is the current version candidates should study.

## 100 Introduction
- 110 Purpose and Scope
- 120 Determining the Best Estimate Assumption for Expenses
    - The steps for completing an expense study: determine the scope; collect the expense data; check the consistency of the data with internal and external reports; determine the expenses to exclude, the expense categories and the unit expense bases; classify and allocate expenses; determine the unit expenses; and perform reasonability checks on the results.
