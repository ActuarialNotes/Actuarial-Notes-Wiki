Data from a study shows the following about the number of injuries a football player experiences in a year:

i) The probability is 0.250 that the player experiences 1 or 2 injuries.
ii) The probability is 0.036 that the player experiences 2 or 3 injuries.
iii) The probability is 0.260 that the player experiences at least 1 injury.
iv) The probability is 0.002 that the player experiences at least 4 injuries.

Calculate the probability that the football player experiences exactly 2 injuries this year.

- A) $0.009$
- B) $0.014$
- C) $0.024$
- D) $0.028$
- E) $0.048$

## Explanation

Write $p_n$ for the probability of exactly $n$ injuries. Each statement translates directly:

$$
\begin{align*}
p_1 + p_2 &= 0.250 \\
p_2 + p_3 &= 0.036 \\
p_1 + p_2 + p_3 + p_{4+} &= 0.260 \\
p_{4+} &= 0.002
\end{align*}
$$

The third statement covers every count of 1 or more, so it decomposes into the first statement's pair, plus $p_3$, plus everything from 4 up. Substituting the known pieces isolates $p_3$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.260 &= 0.250 + p_3 + 0.002 \\
p_3   &= 0.008
\end{align*}
$$

Then the second statement gives $p_2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
p_2 &= 0.036 - p_3 \\
    &= 0.036 - 0.008 \\
    &= \mathbf{0.028}
\end{align*}
$$

Nothing here needs a distributional model — the four statements are just overlapping unions of disjoint events, and the arithmetic peels them apart. Choice (A), 0.009, comes from stopping at $p_3$ having mislabelled which count was isolated.
