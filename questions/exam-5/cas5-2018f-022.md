Given the following information as of December 31, 2017:

| Calendar Year | Paid Claims (\$) | Paid ULAE (\$) |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 21,300 | 1,030 |
| 2015 | 20,900 | 1,040 |
| 2016 | 20,800 | 1,040 |
| 2017 | 21,200 | 1,090 |

| Accident Year | Paid Claims (\$) | Reported Claim Counts | Closed Claim Counts | Ultimate Claim Counts | Ultimate Claims (\$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 20,800 | 335 | 335 | 335 | 20,800 |
| 2015 | 18,000 | 300 | 270 | 330 | 21,400 |
| 2016 | 12,000 | 275 | 190 | 330 | 21,500 |
| 2017 | 5,000 | 200 | 80 | 335 | 21,800 |

| | |
|---|---|
| 60% | Percent of unallocated work that occurs when a claim is opened |
| 40% | Percent of unallocated work that occurs when a claim is closed |

- Claims are fully settled and paid by 48 months.

## Part a (0.75 point)

Estimate the provision for claims incurred but not yet reported (IBNYR) for all accident years as of December 31, 2017.

### Answer

Approximately \$14,313

### Explanation

IBNYR (pure IBNR) covers claims that have not yet been reported. The share of an accident year's ultimate claims attributable to unreported claims is the share of ultimate claim counts still unreported:

$$\text{IBNYR}_{AY} = \text{Ultimate Claims} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Reported Counts}}{\text{Ultimate Counts}}\right)$$

| AY | Ultimate Claims | Reported / Ultimate Counts | % Unreported | IBNYR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 20,800 | 335 / 335 | 0.0% | 0 |
| 2015 | 21,400 | 300 / 330 | 9.1% | 1,945 |
| 2016 | 21,500 | 275 / 330 | 16.7% | 3,583 |
| 2017 | 21,800 | 200 / 335 | 40.3% | 8,785 |
| **Total** | | | | **14,313** |

Equivalently, multiply the unreported claim counts (30, 55, 135) by the implied ultimate severity for each year (ultimate claims ÷ ultimate counts ≈ 64.85, 65.15, 65.07), which gives the same result.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to calculate the incurred but not yet reported claim provision for all accident years.

Common mistakes included calculating the total unpaid claim amount (ultimate less paid) instead of the IBNYR provision, stopping at IBNYR claim *counts* without converting to dollars, miscalculating the IBNYR or open claim counts, estimating case outstanding plus IBNER instead of IBNYR, and attempting to derive IBNYR from accident year ultimate less calendar year paid.

## Part b (1 point)

Estimate unpaid ULAE as of December 31, 2017.

### Answer

Approximately \$1,023

### Explanation

**Step 1 — ULAE ratio.** Calendar year paid ULAE to calendar year paid claims:

$$\frac{1{,}030}{21{,}300} = 4.8\%, \quad \frac{1{,}040}{20{,}900} = 5.0\%, \quad \frac{1{,}040}{20{,}800} = 5.0\%, \quad \frac{1{,}090}{21{,}200} = 5.1\%$$

Select **5.0%**.

**Step 2 — Split the unpaid claims.** Total unpaid = ultimate − paid:

| AY | Ultimate | Paid | Unpaid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 20,800 | 20,800 | 0 |
| 2015 | 21,400 | 18,000 | 3,400 |
| 2016 | 21,500 | 12,000 | 9,500 |
| 2017 | 21,800 | 5,000 | 16,800 |
| **Total** | | | **29,700** |

$$\text{Case} + \text{IBNER} = 29{,}700 - 14{,}313 = 15{,}387$$

**Step 3 — Apply the classical (Wendy Johnson) weighting.** A claim not yet reported will incur the full ULAE effort — 60% at opening plus 40% at closing. A claim already reported has had the opening work done, so only the 40% closing share remains:

$$\text{Unpaid ULAE} = 0.05 \times \left[\text{IBNYR} + 0.40 \times (\text{Case} + \text{IBNER})\right]$$

$$= 0.05 \times \left[14{,}313 + 0.40 \times 15{,}387\right] = 0.05 \times \left[14{,}313 + 6{,}155\right] = 0.05 \times 20{,}468 = 1{,}023$$

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to calculate unpaid ULAE from the given data with appropriate assumptions, correctly identifying the relationship between case reserves, IBNER and IBNYR.

Common mistakes included using a ULAE estimation method the data does not support, using the wrong denominator in the calendar year paid ULAE ratio, applying incorrect weights to the claim provisions (50%/50%, or 60% to case plus IBNER), taking 100% of pure IBNR *and* 40% of total unpaid claims without first removing pure IBNR (which weights pure IBNR at 140%), miscalculating case outstanding plus IBNER, applying the ULAE ratio to total unpaid claims, calculating unpaid ULAE for only one accident year, and mixing calendar and accident year data.
