Losses under a boat insurance policy are exponentially distributed with mean $\dfrac{400}{\ln 2}$.

For each loss, the claim payment is the amount of the loss, up to a maximum of 1000.

Calculate the expected value of a claim payment.

- A) $298$
- B) $367$
- C) $400$
- D) $475$
- E) $577$

## Explanation

Write $\beta = 400/\ln 2 = 577.08$ for the mean. The payment is $\min(X, 1000)$, and for an exponential distribution the limited expected value has a clean closed form:

$$
E[\min(X, d)] = \beta\left(1 - e^{-d/\beta}\right)
$$

This follows from splitting the integral at $d$ and integrating by parts:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\min(X,1000)] &= \int_0^{1000}\frac{x}{\beta}e^{-x/\beta}dx + \int_{1000}^{\infty}\frac{1000}{\beta}e^{-x/\beta}dx \\
                &= \beta\left(1 - e^{-1000/\beta}\right)
\end{align*}
$$

Substituting $\beta = 400/\ln 2$ makes the exponent collapse, since $1000/\beta = 2.5\ln 2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\min(X,1000)] &= \frac{400}{\ln 2}\left(1 - e^{-2.5\ln 2}\right) \\
                &= \frac{400}{\ln 2}\left(1 - 2^{-2.5}\right) \\
                &= 577.08(0.82322) \\
                &= \mathbf{475.06}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 577, is the unlimited mean $\beta$ — the answer if the 1000 cap were ignored. The cap must reduce the expectation, so anything at or above 577 is impossible; the reduction is modest here because $\beta$ is over half the cap.
