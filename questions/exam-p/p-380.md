Consider the following three mutually independent random variables:

- $X$ has a normal distribution with mean 1205 and variance 5.0
- $Y$ and $W$ have normal distributions with mean 2 and variance 0.5

Let $L = X - Y - W + 2$.

Calculate the probability that $L$ is at least 1200.

- A) $0.69$
- B) $0.89$
- C) $0.91$
- D) $0.93$
- E) $0.99$

## Explanation

A linear combination of independent normal random variables is itself normal, so $L$ only needs its mean and variance.

The mean follows the signs of the combination, and the added constant 2 shifts it:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[L] &= 1205 - 2 - 2 + 2 \\
     &= 1203
\end{align*}
$$

The variance does **not** follow the signs. Each coefficient is squared, so the minus signs become plus, and the constant contributes nothing:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(L) &= \text{Var}(X) + (-1)^2\text{Var}(Y) + (-1)^2\text{Var}(W) \\
              &= 5.0 + 0.5 + 0.5 \\
              &= 6.0
\end{align*}
$$

Standardize and evaluate:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[L \geq 1200] &= P\left[Z \geq \frac{1200 - 1203}{\sqrt{6}}\right] \\
               &= P[Z \geq -1.2247] \\
               &= \mathbf{0.8897}
\end{align*}
$$

Subtracting the variances instead of adding them — mirroring the minus signs in $L$ — would give $\text{Var}(L) = 4.0$ and a $z$ of $-1.5$, producing 0.93, which is choice (D). Variances of independent variables always add, whichever way the terms are signed.
