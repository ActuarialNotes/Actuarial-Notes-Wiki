In a population under study it is known that 40% are smokers or have below normal lung function. Among the 25% of the population that smoke 70% have below normal lung function.

Calculate the percentage of the population that have below normal lung function.

- A) $15\%$
- B) $20\%$
- C) $33\%$
- D) $55\%$
- E) $60\%$

## Explanation

Let $A$ be the event that a person smokes and $B$ that a person has below normal lung function. The given quantities are

$$
\begin{align*}
P[A \cup B] &= 0.40 \\
P[A]        &= 0.25 \\
P[B \mid A] &= 0.70
\end{align*}
$$

The addition rule relates the union to the intersection, and the intersection follows from the conditional probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[A \cup B] &= P[A] + P[B] - P[A \cap B] \\
            &= P[A] + P[B] - P[B \mid A]P[A]
\end{align*}
$$

Substituting the three known values and solving for $P[B]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.40 &= 0.25 + P[B] - (0.70)(0.25) \\
0.40 &= 0.25 + P[B] - 0.175 \\
P[B] &= 0.325
\end{align*}
$$

so about $\mathbf{33\%}$ of the population has below normal lung function.

Choice (A), 15%, is $0.40 - 0.25$: the union minus $P[A]$, which would be right only if smokers never had below normal lung function. The 0.175 overlap is counted once in $P[A]$ and once in $P[B]$, so it has to be added back.
