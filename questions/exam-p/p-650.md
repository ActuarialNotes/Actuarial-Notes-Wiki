A policyholder experiences two sports injuries this year, each resulting in three possible outcomes: no hospital stay, a short hospital stay, or a long hospital stay. Each short hospital stay results in a loss of 2. Each long hospital stay results in a loss of 20.

The joint probability function for $i$ short hospital stays and $j$ long hospital stays is

$$
p(i, j) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{2!}{i!\,j!\,(2 - i - j)!}(0.2)^i(0.1)^j(0.7)^{2-i-j}, & \text{for } 0 \le i + j \le 2 \\[6pt]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the policyholder's expected total loss from hospital stays due to sports injuries.

- A) $0.60$
- B) $1.00$
- C) $1.44$
- D) $3.92$
- E) $4.80$

## Explanation

The total loss is $2i + 20j$. Summing over the six admissible pairs:

$$
E[\text{Total Loss}] = \sum_{0 \le i+j \le 2} (2i + 20j)\,p(i,j)
$$

with probabilities $p(0,0) = 0.49$, $p(0,1) = 0.14$, $p(0,2) = 0.01$, $p(1,0) = 0.28$, $p(1,1) = 0.04$, $p(2,0) = 0.04$:

$$
\begin{align*}
&= 0(0.49) + 20(0.14) + 40(0.01) + 2(0.28) + 22(0.04) + 4(0.04) \\
&= 0 + 2.8 + 0.4 + 0.56 + 0.88 + 0.16 \\
&= \mathbf{4.80}
\end{align*}
$$

The shortcut is worth seeing. This $p(i,j)$ is a multinomial with $n = 2$ trials and outcome probabilities $0.2$, $0.1$, $0.7$, so $E(i) = 2(0.2) = 0.4$ and $E(j) = 2(0.1) = 0.2$. Expectation is linear whether or not $i$ and $j$ are independent — and they are not — so $E[2i + 20j] = 2(0.4) + 20(0.2) = 4.80$ with no summation at all.

Choice (A), 0.60, is $E(i) + E(j) = 0.6$: the expected *number* of hospital stays rather than the expected loss they generate.
