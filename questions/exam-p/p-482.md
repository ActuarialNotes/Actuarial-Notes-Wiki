A flight is delayed due to bad weather. The delay time is modeled by a random variable with a continuous uniform distribution. The expected delay time is three hours, and the standard deviation of the delay time is one hour.

Calculate the shortest possible delay time, in hours.

- A) $0.58$
- B) $1.27$
- C) $1.73$
- D) $2.31$
- E) $2.42$

## Explanation

Let the distribution be uniform on $[a, b]$. The two given facts pin down the midpoint and the length.

**Mean gives the midpoint:**

$$
\frac{a+b}{2} = 3 \implies a + b = 6
$$

**Standard deviation gives the length.** For a uniform distribution the variance is $(b-a)^2/12$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{(b-a)^2}{12} &= 1^2 \\
(b-a)^2 &= 12 \\
b - a &= 2\sqrt{3} = 3.4641
\end{align*}
$$

The shortest possible delay is the lower endpoint, which sits half the length below the midpoint:

$$
\begin{align*}
a &= 3 - \sqrt{3} \\
  &= \mathbf{1.268}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 2.42, is $3 - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, the standard deviation divided by $\sqrt{3}$ instead of multiplied by it. The correct half-width is $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$, not the standard deviation of 1 — for a uniform distribution the half-width is $\sqrt{3}$ times the standard deviation, which is exactly the relationship being tested. Choice (C), 1.73, is that half-width itself rather than the endpoint.
