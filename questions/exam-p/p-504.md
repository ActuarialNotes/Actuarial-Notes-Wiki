A construction worker experiences a back injury resulting in a loss on an insurance policy. Losses under the policy are uniformly distributed on the interval $[2, 10]$. The insurer reimburses the worker for 60% of this loss.

Calculate the 30th percentile of the construction worker's unreimbursed loss from the back injury.

- A) $1.76$
- B) $1.80$
- C) $2.64$
- D) $3.04$
- E) $4.40$

## Explanation

If the insurer pays 60%, the worker bears the other 40%. So the unreimbursed loss is

$$
Y = 0.4X
$$

where $X$ is uniform on $[2,10]$.

Scaling by a positive constant scales percentiles by the same constant, so the fastest route is to find the 30th percentile of $X$ and multiply. For a uniform distribution the $100q$th percentile is $(1-q)a + qb$:

$$
\begin{align*}
x_{0.30} &= 0.7(2) + 0.3(10) \\
         &= 1.4 + 3.0 = 4.4
\end{align*}
$$

Then

$$
\begin{align*}
y_{0.30} &= 0.4(4.4) \\
         &= \mathbf{1.76}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 4.40, is the 30th percentile of the *loss* rather than of the unreimbursed portion. Choice (C), 2.64, is $0.6(4.4)$ — the reimbursed share instead of the worker's. Reading which 60/40 side the question asks about is the whole difficulty.
