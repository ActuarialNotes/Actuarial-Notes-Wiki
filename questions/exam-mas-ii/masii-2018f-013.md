An actuary creates a Hierarchical Linear Mixed Model of patient outcomes, with random effects for clinics (Level 3) and doctors (Level 2). Doctors are clustered in clinics. The first clinic has two doctors and six patients, with patients assigned to doctors as shown in the table below.

The table provides a complete list of data for Clinic 1, which is the first among eight clinics studied.

| Clinic ID | Doctor ID | Patient ID |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| 1 | 1000 | 001 |
| 1 | 1000 | 002 |
| 1 | 1001 | 003 |
| 1 | 1001 | 004 |
| 1 | 1001 | 005 |
| 1 | 1001 | 006 |

The marginal variance-covariance matrix (rows and columns in order of patient ID) for Clinic 1 is as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
132 & 27 & 11 & 11 & 11 & 11 \\
27 & 132 & 11 & 11 & 11 & 11 \\
11 & 11 & 132 & 27 & 27 & 27 \\
11 & 11 & 27 & 132 & 27 & 27 \\
11 & 11 & 27 & 27 & 132 & 27 \\
11 & 11 & 27 & 27 & 27 & 132
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Calculate the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient for patients with the same doctor.

- A) Less than 0.1
- B) At least 0.1, but less than 0.2
- C) At least 0.2, but less than 0.3
- D) At least 0.3, but less than 0.4
- E) At least 0.4

## Explanation

In a three-level model with a random clinic effect (variance $\sigma^2_{\text{clinic}}$), a random doctor-within-clinic effect ($\sigma^2_{\text{doctor}}$) and a patient-level residual ($\sigma^2$), the marginal covariance between two patients depends only on what they share:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{same patient:} \quad \sigma^2_{\text{clinic}} + \sigma^2_{\text{doctor}} + \sigma^2 &= 132 \\
\text{same doctor:} \quad \sigma^2_{\text{clinic}} + \sigma^2_{\text{doctor}} &= 27 \\
\text{same clinic, different doctor:} \quad \sigma^2_{\text{clinic}} &= 11
\end{align*}
$$

These match the blocks of the matrix: patients 001–002 (Doctor 1000) and 003–006 (Doctor 1001) covary at 27 within their block and at 11 across blocks. (So $\sigma^2_{\text{clinic}} = 11$, $\sigma^2_{\text{doctor}} = 16$ and $\sigma^2 = 105$.)

The intraclass correlation for two patients of the same doctor — who necessarily share a clinic too — is their covariance divided by the total variance:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{ICC}_{\text{doctor}} &= \frac{\sigma^2_{\text{clinic}} + \sigma^2_{\text{doctor}}}{\sigma^2_{\text{clinic}} + \sigma^2_{\text{doctor}} + \sigma^2} \\
&= \frac{27}{132} \\
&= 0.2045
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[0.2, 0.3)$, so the answer is **C**.
