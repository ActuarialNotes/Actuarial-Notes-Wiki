During a hard market, several large commercial buyers leave the insurance market and form captives and risk retention groups to finance their liability losses. In Porter's five forces, which force does this movement illustrate?

- A) Rivalry among existing competitors
- B) Bargaining power of suppliers
- C) Threat of substitutes
- D) Threat of new entrants

## Explanation

A substitute meets the same need in a different way and caps what an industry can charge. Captives, risk retention groups and self-insurance are substitutes for commercial insurance, and buyers turn to them when insurance becomes expensive, as it does in a hard market. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Rivalry is competition among existing insurers, not buyers leaving to finance their own losses.
- **B** — An insurer's suppliers are reinsurers, capital providers and producers, not its customers.
- **D** — New entrants are new insurers competing for the same business. Buyers financing their own risk are substituting for insurance rather than entering the market as insurers.
