**The Statement of Actuarial Opinion** is the [[Appointed Actuary]]'s formal written opinion, filed with the [[Canadian Annual Return]] and included in the financial statements, on the **[[Insurance Contract Liabilities]]** an insurer carries. It states whether the amount makes appropriate provision for all policy obligations and whether the financial statements fairly present the results of the valuation.

- **The structure** of the Canadian opinion: a **scope** paragraph identifying what was valued and as at what date; a statement on the **data** — that the actuary verified their consistency with the financial statements and found them sufficient and reliable for the valuation; and the **opinion** itself, that the liabilities were valued in accordance with [[Accepted Actuarial Practice]] and make appropriate provision.
- **The four outcomes:** an **unqualified** opinion; a **qualified** opinion, where some component prevents an unreserved conclusion; an **adverse** opinion, where the amount carried does not make appropriate provision; and a **denial of opinion**, where the actuary cannot form one at all — most often because the data are inadequate.
- **Data sufficiency is a real gate, not a formality.** If data are so deficient that the valuation cannot be relied upon, the actuary cannot simply add a margin and sign — the [[Standards of Practice]] require the deficiency to be stated and, if severe enough, the opinion to be qualified or denied.
- **It is an opinion on the amount carried**, not on the actuary's own estimate. The question is whether the *reported* liability makes appropriate provision, which is why a carried figure below the range of reasonable estimates forces a qualification regardless of how the estimate was produced.
- **[[Subsequent Events]] must be considered** up to the date the opinion is signed, and events providing evidence of conditions existing at the valuation date require the liability to be adjusted.
- The opinion is supported by, and must be read with, the **[[Appointed Actuary's Report]]**, which documents the data, methods, assumptions and selections behind it.

> [!example]- Which Opinion? {Example}
> Determine the appropriate opinion in each case.
>
> 1. The carried liability is $\$412$ million; the actuary's range is $\$395$M to $\$440$M, central estimate $\$418$M.
> 2. The carried liability is $\$380$ million against the same range.
> 3. A newly acquired subsidiary's claim data cannot be reconciled to its financial statements, and it represents $22\%$ of liabilities.
> 4. The carried liability is within range, but a court decision three weeks after year end and before signing has widened a benefit definition affecting $\$30$ million of open claims.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > 1. **Unqualified.** $\$412$M is within the range, slightly below the central estimate. That is a legitimate carrying position. A careful [[Appointed Actuary's Report]] would note it is toward the low end and quantify what that implies for future development, but the opinion is clean.
> > 2. **Adverse.** $\$380$M is below the range. The amount carried does not make appropriate provision, and the opinion must say so and quantify the deficiency.
> > 3. **Qualified, or a denial**, depending on severity. At $22\%$ of liabilities, unreconcilable data are material. If the actuary can value the remaining $78\%$ and identify the affected portion, a **qualified** opinion excluding that component is appropriate. If the deficiency contaminates the whole valuation, the opinion must be **denied**. What is not available is signing unqualified with a private note in the file.
> > 4. **The liability must be adjusted before the opinion is given.** This is a [[Subsequent Events|subsequent event]] providing evidence of conditions that existed at the valuation date — the decision interprets the law as it always was, so the obligation existed at year end. If the carried amount is restated to include the $\$30$ million and remains within range, the opinion is unqualified. If management refuses to restate, the carried amount is now below the range and the opinion becomes **adverse**.
