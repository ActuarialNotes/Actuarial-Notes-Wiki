Given the following residual graphs from an over-dispersed Poisson (ODP) bootstrap model:

![Plot of residuals against accident year, 2005 to 2013, vertical axis from -4.00 to 5.00: in every accident year the residuals spread roughly evenly on both sides of zero, reaching about 3 to 4 above and -2 to -3 below](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas7-2015-q10-residuals-by-accident-year.png)

![Plot of residuals against development period, 0 to 8, vertical axis from -4.00 to 5.00: periods 0 to 4 show residuals spread widely from about -3 to 4, while periods 5 to 8 show a few residuals clustered between about 1 and 3.3, all positive](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas7-2015-q10-residuals-by-development-period.png)

## Part a (0.5 points)

For each graph, briefly discuss whether the illustrated residuals demonstrate the presence or absence of heteroscedasticity.

### Explanation
- **Accident year graph:** no heteroscedasticity — the residuals appear random around 0 with roughly constant variance.
- **Development period graph:** heteroscedasticity is present — the variance of the residuals differs by development period; from period 5 on the residuals are all above 0 and tightly grouped, unlike the earlier periods.

### Examiner Report
Candidates performed extremely well on this part. A few common mistakes were mixing up hetero- and homoscedasticity, and not including any justification for the presence/absence thereof.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Describe why it is important to adjust for heteroscedasticity when using a bootstrap model.

### Explanation
- The bootstrap samples residuals from all observed residuals to create new triangles, which assumes the residuals are independent and identically distributed.
- Heteroscedasticity violates this: residuals from different development periods do not have the same variance, so it is not appropriate to sample from the whole set.

### Examiner Report
Candidates did fairly well here.  Most candidates identified the assumption of the bootstrap model that residuals are i.i.d., but a fair number of candidates did not sufficiently demonstrate how heteroscedasticity violates this assumption. Many candidates who received full credit didn't sufficiently define heteroscedasticity explicitly in this part, but had enough detail from part a to compensate for an otherwise insufficient answer here.

## Part c (1 point)

Describe two procedures for adjusting for heteroscedasticity.

### Explanation
- **Stratified sampling** — group residuals with like variances (e.g. by development period) and, for each part of the triangle, sample only from the corresponding group.
- **Hetero-adjustment factors** — group residuals with like variances and calculate each group's standard deviation. Multiply each group's residuals by (largest group's standard deviation ÷ the group's standard deviation) so all residuals have a common variance, sample from the entire triangle, then divide the sampled residuals by the factor to undo the adjustment before building the pseudo triangles.

### Examiner Report
Candidates did well identifying and explaining the main points of stratified sampling; however, a common error was not giving enough detail regarding the hetero-adjustment factor approach.
This is understandable, given that this method simply has more detail to it than stratified sampling. It is worth noting that candidates did not need to give any formulas to receive full credit if their answer contained all the high level aspects of the approach.
