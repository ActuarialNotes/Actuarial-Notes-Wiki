The number of brake repair jobs a particular bus needs in a year is modeled by a Poisson distribution. The probability that the bus needs at least one brake repair job this year is 0.10.

Calculate the probability that the bus needs at least two brake repair jobs this year.

- A) $0.0052$
- B) $0.0100$
- C) $0.0500$
- D) $0.1054$
- E) $0.3303$

## Explanation

Recover the mean from the given probability. At least one job is the complement of none:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X = 0] &= 1 - 0.10 = 0.90 = e^{-\lambda} \\
\lambda &= -\ln(0.9) = 0.10536
\end{align*}
$$

Now take the complement of the two smallest counts:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \geq 2] &= 1 - P[X=0] - P[X=1] \\
            &= 1 - e^{-\lambda} - \lambda e^{-\lambda} \\
            &= 1 - 0.9 - (0.10536)(0.9) \\
            &= \mathbf{0.00518}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 0.1054, is $\lambda$ itself — close to the 0.10 given, which is why it looks plausible; for small $\lambda$ the probability of at least one event is approximately $\lambda$. Choice (B), 0.0100, is $(0.10)^2$, the answer if the two repair jobs were independent events each of probability 0.10. They are not: a single Poisson count governs both, and the true answer is about half that.
