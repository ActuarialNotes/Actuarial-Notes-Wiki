The following statements about the Gelman-Rubin convergence diagnostic are provided.

- I. It measures autocorrelation of samples within a chain.
- II. It aids in determining whether there are a sufficient number of chains in the sampling process.
- III. It is used to help assess convergence of multiple chains in the sampling process.

Determine which of the above statements are true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

The Gelman-Rubin diagnostic $\hat{R}$ runs several chains and compares the variance *between* the chains with the variance *within* them. If every chain is sampling the same distribution the two agree and $\hat{R} \approx 1$; a value noticeably above 1 means the chains have not converged to a common distribution.

**I is false.** Autocorrelation within a chain is measured by the autocorrelation function and summarised by the effective sample size. $\hat{R}$ compares variances across chains, not the correlation of successive draws.

**II is false.** $\hat{R}$ needs at least two chains to be computed, but its value says whether the chains agree, not whether enough of them were run.

**III is true.** Assessing whether multiple chains have converged to the same distribution is exactly what it is for.

Only III is true, so the answer is **C**.
