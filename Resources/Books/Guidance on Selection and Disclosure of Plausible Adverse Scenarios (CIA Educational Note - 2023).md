## Introduction
- The note covers when the disclosures must be included in an external user report, when to adopt the revised requirements, and the selection of scenarios for the funded status and, where applicable, the service cost.
- The required disclosures are not a comprehensive study of the plan's risks but an illustration of the sensitivity of the funded status and plan costs to certain key risks.

## 1 Summary of disclosure requirements for plausible adverse scenarios
- Paragraph 3260.13 requires the report to give the effects of the plausible adverse scenarios on the funded status and, for a going concern valuation, the service cost.

## 2 Application of disclosure requirements for plausible adverse scenarios
- Effective December 1, 2022 the scenarios may be applied to a hypothetical wind-up or solvency valuation instead of a going concern valuation, and are selected in consultation with the plan administrator or plan sponsor.
- Early adoption of the revised requirements is not permitted.
- Paragraph 3260.11 lists the risks: interest rate risk, deterioration of asset values, longevity risk and, where contributions are fixed or restricted, a lower contribution base.

## 3 Selection and disclosure of plausible adverse scenarios
- A scenario is plausible if it has a non-trivial probability of occurring within the short term (immediately to one year), generally a likelihood between 1 in 10 and 1 in 20 in the actuary's opinion.
- Guidance on selecting appropriate interest rate lower than expected
- Guidance on selecting scenario on deterioration of asset values
- Guidance on selecting increased longevity
- Guidance on fixed contributions being too low
- Guidance on reflecting risk in combination

## 4 Example of plausible adverse scenarios
- Three examples of reported results: a going concern valuation, a going concern valuation of a multi-employer pension plan, and a solvency valuation.
