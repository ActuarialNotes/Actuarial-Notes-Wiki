For each of the risk measures identified below, provide a definition and explain one significant limitation of the measure in the context of an ERM application.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Value at Risk (VaR)

### Explanation
- **Definition:** VaR at $\alpha$ is the value of the loss distribution at the $\alpha$ percentile.
- **Limitation (any one):** it is a single point, so it gives little information about the distribution and ignores the risk in the tail beyond it (and below it), which matters for risk management; or it is not sub-additive, so it does not reflect the diversification benefit.

### Examiner Report
Candidates scored well on this part. The most common error was to state a limitation without any explanation (this earned partial credit).

## Part b (0.75 points)

Tail Value at Risk (TVaR)

### Explanation
- **Definition:** TVaR is the expected value of losses above a specified percentile of the distribution.
- **Limitation (any one):** it is linear in the tail, so it does not reflect a risk-averse attitude that a loss twice as large is more than twice as bad; or it ignores the risk below $\alpha$, where losses may not be extreme but can still be significant.

### Examiner Report
Candidates also scored well on this part. Common errors included:

- Stating as a limitation that the risk measure includes losses excess of insolvency. The paper mentions this, but goes on to explain that it is not actually a limitation.
- Stating a limitation without explanation (this earned partial credit).

## Part c (0.75 points)

Risk-adjusted TVaR (RTVaR)

### Explanation
- **Definition:** RTVaR is TVaR plus some proportion $c$ of the standard deviation of the losses in the tail: $RTVaR = TVaR + c \times$ (standard deviation of losses above the percentile).
- **Limitation (any one):** it uses only the tail losses, not the whole distribution, so it is not a complete risk measure; or, being a quadratic (standard deviation) loading, it may not place enough weight on very large losses in a skewed distribution.

### Examiner Report
Candidates found this part more challenging than parts a and b. Common errors included:

- Confusion with WTVAR.
- Forgetting the constant "c" applied to the standard deviation in the formula.
- Stating a limitation without explanation (this earned partial credit).
