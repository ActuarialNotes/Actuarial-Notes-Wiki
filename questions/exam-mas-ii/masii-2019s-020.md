You are analyzing the claim counts for $n$ individual policyholders. Let $Y_i$ denote the claim count for the $i^{th}$ policyholder. You build your model as follows:

$$
\Pr(Y_i = y_i \mid P = p) = (1-p)^{y_i}p
$$

$$
f(p) = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + \beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}
$$

$$
f(\alpha) = \frac{1}{5}e^{-\alpha/5}
$$

$$
f(\beta) = \frac{1}{8}e^{-\beta/8}
$$

Assuming that $p$ is constrained to be between 0 and 1, the conditional posterior distribution of $p \mid (\alpha, \beta, y_1, \ldots, y_n)$ is proportional to $K$.

Identify which of the following could be $K$.

- A) $p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}$
- B) $p^{\alpha}(1-p)^{y_i+\beta-1}$
- C) $\frac{\beta}{8}\frac{\alpha}{5}p^{\alpha+n-1}(1-p)^{\sum y_i+\beta-1}$
- D) $p^{4+n}(1-p)^{\sum y_i+7}$
- E) None of the above could be $K$.

## Explanation

In a Gibbs sampler the conditional posterior of $p$ treats $\alpha$ and $\beta$ as **given**, so anything free of $p$ is an absorbable constant. Collect the factors that involve $p$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{likelihood} &= \prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p)^{y_i}p = p^{\,n}(1-p)^{\sum y_i} \\
\text{prior} &\propto p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
p \mid \alpha, \beta, \mathbf{y} &\propto p^{\,n}(1-p)^{\sum y_i} \cdot p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1} \\
                                 &= p^{\alpha+n-1}(1-p)^{\sum y_i + \beta - 1}
\end{align*}
$$

— that is, $\text{Beta}\!\left(\alpha + n,\ \sum y_i + \beta\right)$. Checking the options:

- **(A)** is the prior alone; it drops the data entirely.
- **(B)** carries a single $y_i$ rather than $\sum y_i$ and has $p^{\alpha}$, which only matches when $n = 1$.
- **(C)** matches the kernel exactly. The leading $\frac{\beta}{8}\frac{\alpha}{5}$ is free of $p$, so it is absorbed into the proportionality constant.
- **(D)** would require $\alpha = 5$ and $\beta = 8$ to be fixed, but they are random variables with exponential priors of means 5 and 8 — those are the prior means, not the values.

So the answer is **C**.
