Identify and explain five technical problems that make loss reserving for a reinsurer more difficult than loss reserving for a primary company.

### Explanation
1. **Reporting lags are greater for reinsurers.** This can be due to many factors, including:
   - the long pipeline through which claims data must travel to get from being reported to the cedent to being recorded by the reinsurer;
   - claims may be undervalued and therefore remain below the claims reporting threshold for some time.
2. **Claims reporting patterns differ by reinsurance line, type of contract, attachment point, etc.** The data is very heterogeneous, making it difficult to use traditional reserving methods.
3. **Industry statistics may not be useful.**
   - Schedule P does not have lines broken down into categories that are homogeneous enough.
   - ISO statistics would need to be significantly revised for the specific reinsurance specifications in order to be used.
4. **Reports received by reinsurers may be lacking important information** (e.g. they may have CY or UW data instead of AY).
5. **Data coding and IT systems** may not be able to keep up with the complex needs of reinsurers.

Alternatively:

1. Report lags are longer for reinsurance than primary: a claim first has to be reported to the primary company, which decides whether it is reportable to the reinsurer; there is also the lag of entering it in the primary's system, then reporting it to the reinsurer and entering it in theirs.
2. Increasing (upward) emergence in reinsurance: claims are reserved at modal value, or at a minimum value, until more information arrives; there is also an economic/social inflation impact.
3. Reporting patterns vary for different types of reinsurance treaties and lines of insurance; without consistency in reporting patterns, reserve analysis in larger groups is difficult.
4. IT system deficiencies: because of the delay in reporting and constant change in the market, reinsurance IT data systems are always behind and need to be updated to capture the necessary information.
5. Reinsurance requires more capital/surplus: there is more volatility and uncertainty in reinsurance, plus regulatory requirements for capital.
