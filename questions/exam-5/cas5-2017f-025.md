The following information is available for an insurer as of December 31, 2016:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Gross Reported Claims (\$000) as of 12 months | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 2,757 | 5,570 | 6,880 | 7,047 |
| 2014 | 2,345 | 4,104 | 5,121 | |
| 2015 | 2,639 | 4,677 | | |
| 2016 | 2,802 | | | |

| Accident Year | Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000) Ceded to Excess of Loss Treaty as of 12 months | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 0 | 745 | 1,332 | 1,332 |
| 2014 | 0 | 0 | 402 | |
| 2015 | 154 | 328 | | |
| 2016 | 0 | | | |

| Accident Year | Cumulative Paid Claims (\$000) Net of Excess of Loss Treaty |
|---|---|
| 2013 | 5,102 |
| 2014 | 3,834 |
| 2015 | 2,840 |
| 2016 | 1,385 |

- There is no reported claims development beyond 48 months.
- For each of accident years 2013 through 2015, the insurer maintained a stop loss reinsurance limit that applies after an excess of loss treaty.
- The stop loss limits are:

| Accident Year | Stop Loss Limit (\$000) |
|---|---|
| 2013 | 5,000 |
| 2014 | 5,000 |
| 2015 | 5,000 |
| 2016 | None |

Calculate the unpaid claims net of all reinsurance for all accident years using the reported claims development technique.

### Answer
2013: 0; 2014: 1,027; 2015: 2,160; 2016: 4,423 (\$000). Total = \$7,610K

### Explanation
The two treaties work in sequence and must be handled in that order. The excess of loss treaty is a **per-occurrence** cession that reshapes the *triangle*, so it is netted off before any development factor is selected. The aggregate stop loss is a **cap on the accident year total**, so it is applied to the finished ultimate — it cannot be developed, because a capped triangle would produce development factors that are wrong for the uncapped accident year 2016.

**Step 1 - build the triangle net of excess of loss.**

$$\text{Net reported} = \text{Gross reported} - \text{Ceded to XOL}$$

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 2,757 | 4,825 | 5,548 | 5,715 |
| 2014 | 2,345 | 4,104 | 4,719 | |
| 2015 | 2,485 | 4,349 | | |
| 2016 | 2,802 | | | |

**Step 2 - develop the net triangle.** The age-to-age factors are remarkably consistent, so any reasonable averaging gives the same selection:

| Interval | Individual factors | Selected | CDF |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12-24 | 1.750, 1.750, 1.750 | 1.750 | 2.073 |
| 24-36 | 1.150, 1.150 | 1.150 | 1.185 |
| 36-48 | 1.030 | 1.030 | 1.030 |
| 48-ult | — | 1.000 | 1.000 |

$$\text{CDF}_{12} = 1.750 \times 1.150 \times 1.030 = 2.0729, \qquad \text{CDF}_{24} = 1.150 \times 1.030 = 1.1845$$

**Step 3 - project ultimate net of the excess of loss treaty.**

| AY | Reported net of XOL | CDF | Ultimate net of XOL |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 5,715 | 1.000 | 5,715 |
| 2014 | 4,719 | 1.030 | 4,861 |
| 2015 | 4,349 | 1.1845 | 5,151 |
| 2016 | 2,802 | 2.0729 | 5,808 |

**Step 4 - apply the stop loss.** Cap each of 2013-2015 at \$5,000K; accident year 2016 has no stop loss, so it passes through uncapped.

$$\text{Ultimate net of all reinsurance} = \min(\text{ultimate net of XOL},\ \text{stop loss limit})$$

**Step 5 - cap the paid claims too, then subtract.** The given paid claims are net of the excess of loss treaty only. Accident year 2013 has already paid \$5,102K, which is above its \$5,000K stop loss, so net of *all* reinsurance the insurer has paid \$5,000K and the recovery has already been triggered.

| AY | Ultimate net of XOL | Stop loss | Net ultimate | Net paid | Net unpaid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 5,715 | 5,000 | 5,000 | 5,000 | **0** |
| 2014 | 4,861 | 5,000 | 4,861 | 3,834 | **1,027** |
| 2015 | 5,151 | 5,000 | 5,000 | 2,840 | **2,160** |
| 2016 | 5,808 | None | 5,808 | 1,385 | **4,423** |

$$\text{Total unpaid net of all reinsurance} = 0 + 1{,}027 + 2{,}160 + 4{,}423 = \$7{,}610\text{K}$$

Accident year 2013 has zero net unpaid claims because it is fully exhausted against the stop loss: every additional dollar of gross development is now the reinsurer's.

*Alternatively*, the gross and ceded triangles can be developed separately — gross ultimates of 7,047 / 5,245 / 5,978 / 6,347 less ceded ultimates of 1,332 / 402 / 763 / 0 — and the stop loss applied to the difference. That route gives essentially the same answer for 2013-2015 but rests on developing a very thin, erratic ceded excess-of-loss triangle (only three non-zero cells, with a 12-24 factor of 6.97), which is why netting first is the cleaner approach.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use the reported claims development technique to estimate ultimate claims net of the excess of loss treaty, apply the stop loss reinsurance to obtain ultimate and paid losses net of all reinsurance, and subtract paid from ultimate to get the unpaid amounts.

Common errors included not applying the stop loss limitation to the ultimate losses, to the paid losses, or to either; subtracting *undeveloped* ceded losses from developed gross ultimates; capping the net triangle at 5,000 before selecting development factors and then applying those factors to accident year 2016, which carries no aggregate stop loss; applying a stop loss limitation to accident year 2016; and using the gross development factors to develop the ceded excess of loss amounts.
