A business owner's aircraft policy has a pilot warranty requiring a current medical certificate. His certificate expired two weeks before he flew to a client meeting. During the flight the engine fails from a manufacturing defect, and the aircraft is destroyed in the forced landing. His health played no part in the accident. The policy is governed by New York law. Is the hull loss covered?

- A) No, because the pilot did not meet the warranty when he flew
- B) Yes, because the loss was caused by the engine defect and not by his health
- C) Yes, but only at actual cash value rather than the agreed value
- D) Yes, but only once the manufacturer has been shown not to be liable

## Explanation

New York courts hold that an insurer need not show a causal connection between a pilot exclusion and the loss, so when a pilot who does not meet the policy's pilot requirement is flying there is no coverage, **whether or not the shortfall caused the loss**. The expired medical certificate breaches the requirement, so the hull loss is not covered.

- **B** applies a causation test New York law does not require. Some states do require a causal connection, but this policy is governed by New York law.
- **C** confuses the settlement basis. Hull coverage on a covered total loss pays the agreed value less the deductible, but here nothing is owed.
- **D** invents a condition. The insurer's possible recovery from the manufacturer does not decide whether the policy covers the loss.
