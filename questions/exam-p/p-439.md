An insurance company's profit for one year is normally distributed with probability 0.8531 of being positive.

The company's profit the next year is normally distributed with probability 0.9192 of being positive.

The yearly profits are independent with the same mean but different standard deviations.

Calculate the probability that the insurance company earns an overall positive profit in this two-year period.

- A) $0.7842$
- B) $0.7995$
- C) $0.8849$
- D) $0.9535$
- E) $0.9929$

## Explanation

Let $X$ and $Y$ be the two years' profits, sharing the mean $\mu$ but with different standard deviations. Each given probability fixes one standard deviation as a multiple of $\mu$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.8531 = P[X > 0] &\implies \frac{-\mu}{\sigma_X} = -1.05 \implies \sigma_X = \frac{\mu}{1.05} \\
0.9192 = P[Y > 0] &\implies \frac{-\mu}{\sigma_Y} = -1.40 \implies \sigma_Y = \frac{\mu}{1.40}
\end{align*}
$$

For the two-year total $S = X + Y$, means add and variances add:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[S] &= 2\mu \\
\text{Var}(S) &= \sigma_X^2 + \sigma_Y^2 = \mu^2\left(\frac{1}{1.05^2} + \frac{1}{1.40^2}\right) \\
\sigma_S &= 1.1905\mu
\end{align*}
$$

The unknown $\mu$ cancels in the standardization:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S > 0] &= P\left[Z > \frac{0 - 2\mu}{1.1905\mu}\right] \\
         &= P[Z > -1.68] \\
         &= \mathbf{0.9535}
\end{align*}
$$

Carrying $\mu$ as an unknown throughout is the trick — it never needs a value, because every probability depends only on the ratio of mean to standard deviation. Choice (A), 0.7842, is $(0.8531)(0.9192)$, the probability that *both* years are profitable, which is a different and stronger event than a positive two-year total.
