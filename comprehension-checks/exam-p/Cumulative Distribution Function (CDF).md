A proposed function has $F(0) = 0.3$, $F(1) = 0.7$, and $F(2) = 0.5$, tending to 0 and 1 in the two tails. Why can it not be a CDF?

- A) Its values are not evenly spaced across the interval
- B) It decreases from $F(1)$ to $F(2)$, which would assign a negative probability to the interval $(1, 2]$
- C) It fails to pass through the point $(0, 0)$
- D) It never equals exactly 0.5 at the median

<!-- rationale: 1: spacing is irrelevant · 2: a CDF may take any value in $[0,1]$ at $x = 0$ · 3: $F$ does reach 0.5 here, and a CDF need not attain any particular value -->
