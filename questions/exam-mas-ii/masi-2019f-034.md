You are given the following statements comparing k-fold cross validation (with k < n) and Leave-One-Out Cross Validation (LOOCV), used on a GLM with log link and gamma error.

- I. k-fold validation has a computational advantage over LOOCV
- II. k-fold validation has an advantage over LOOCV in bias reduction
- III. k-fold validation has an advantage over LOOCV in variance reduction

Determine which of the above statements are true.

- A) None are true
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

The two methods differ in how many fits they need and in how much data each fit uses.

**I is true.** LOOCV fits the model $n$ times, once per held-out observation, while $k$-fold fits it only $k < n$ times. A gamma GLM with log link is fitted iteratively and has no one-fit shortcut like the leverage formula for least squares, so the saving is real.

**II is false.** Each $k$-fold training set holds only $(k-1)n/k$ observations, against $n - 1$ for LOOCV. Training on less data overstates the test error more, so it is LOOCV that has the lower bias.

**III is true.** LOOCV averages $n$ fits whose training sets are almost identical, so their errors are highly correlated and the average has high variance. The $k$-fold training sets overlap less, which gives a lower-variance estimate of test error.

I and III are true, so the answer is **C**.
