You are given the following information about waiting times at a subway station:

- Subway trains arrive at a Poisson rate of 20 per hour
- 30% of the trains are Express and 70% are Local
- The arrival times of each train are independent
- An Express train gets you to work in 18 minutes, and a Local train gets you there in 30 minutes
- You always take the first train to arrive and you get to the office in $X_1$ minutes from the time you arrive at the subway station
- Your coworker always takes the first Express train to arrive and he gets to the office in $X_2$ minutes from the time he arrives at the subway station

Calculate the expected value of $X_1 - X_2$.

- A) Less than -2.0
- B) At least -2.0, but less than -1.0
- C) At least -1.0, but less than 0.0
- D) At least 0.0, but less than 1.0
- E) At least 1.0

## Explanation

Train types are an independent 30/70 split of a Poisson process, so the Express trains alone form a Poisson process at $0.3(20) = 6$ per hour, and the type of whichever train comes first is independent of when it comes.

**You** wait for the first of all trains, an exponential wait with mean $60/20 = 3$ minutes, then ride whichever train it is:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X_1] &= 3 + 0.3(18) + 0.7(30) \\
&= 3 + 5.4 + 21 \\
&= 29.4
\end{align*}
$$

**Your coworker** waits for the first Express train, an exponential wait with mean $60/6 = 10$ minutes, then always rides 18 minutes:

$$
E[X_2] = 10 + 18 = 28.
$$

By linearity of expectation,

$$
E[X_1 - X_2] = 29.4 - 28 = 1.4.
$$

This is at least 1.0, so the answer is **E**.
