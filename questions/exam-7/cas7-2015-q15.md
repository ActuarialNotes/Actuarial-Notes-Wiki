Given the following forecasted information for a company (\$000):

| | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning GAAP Equity | 100,000 | 105,000 | 107,000 |
| Net Income | 10,000 | 12,000 | 13,000 |
| Minimum Capital Requirement | 105,000 | 107,000 | 110,000 |

- Investors demand 8% return on equity.

## Part a (1.25 points)

Assuming 3% growth in abnormal earnings in perpetuity, calculate the company value as of January 1, 2016.

### Explanation
With $k = 0.08$, abnormal earnings each year are $(\text{ROE} - k) \times$ beginning GAAP equity:

| | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning GAAP equity | 100,000 | 105,000 | 107,000 |
| ROE | 10/100 = 0.1 | 12/105 = 0.114 | 13/107 = 0.121 |

$$V = 100{,}000 + \frac{(0.1 - 0.08)(100{,}000)}{1.08} + \frac{(0.114 - 0.08)(105{,}000)}{1.08^2} + \frac{(0.121 - 0.08)(107{,}000)}{1.08^3} + \frac{(0.121 - 0.08)(107{,}000)(1.03)/(0.08 - 0.03)}{1.08^3}$$

$$V = 100{,}000 + 8{,}395 + 71{,}740 = 180{,}135$$

(8,395 is the three forecast years' discounted abnormal earnings and 71,740 the discounted terminal value, in \$000.)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to apply the abnormal earnings method to provide an estimate of company value. In order to receive full credit, candidates were required to show the appropriate calculation of abnormal earnings discounted at a rate of 8%. In addition, candidates were required to calculate the appropriate discounted terminal value since the abnormal earnings were projected to go into perpetuity. Many candidates performed very well. A common mistake was estimating the abnormal earnings based on the minimal capital requirement rather than beginning GAAP equity. Many candidates also failed to add the original book value to the value contemplated by the abnormal earnings.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Assuming abnormal earnings grow 3% for 3 years beyond the forecast horizon, and then decrease to \$0 for 2022 and all subsequent years, calculate the company value as of January 1, 2016.

### Explanation
The 2018 abnormal earnings grow 3% for 2019–2021 and are \$0 from 2022:

$$V_0 = 100{,}000 + \frac{(0.1 - 0.08)(100{,}000)}{1.08} + \frac{(0.114 - 0.08)(105{,}000)}{1.08^2} + \frac{(0.121 - 0.08)(107{,}000)}{1.08^3} + \frac{(0.121 - 0.08)(107{,}000)(1.03)}{1.08^4} + \frac{(0.121 - 0.08)(107{,}000)(1.03)^2}{1.08^5} + \frac{(0.121 - 0.08)(107{,}000)(1.03)^3}{1.08^6}$$

$$V_0 = 100{,}000 + 8{,}395 + 3{,}321 + 3{,}168 + 3{,}021 = 117{,}905$$

### Examiner Report
Part b is an extension of part a, and again, many candidates performed well. In order to receive full
credit, candidates were expected to adjust the 2018 abnormal earnings by 3% per annum and discount it using an 8% discount rate.
Candidates who did not receive full credit typically assumed that the abnormal earnings would gradually trend to zero linearly.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Discuss which of the two scenarios presented above is more realistic.

### Explanation
The scenario in part b is more realistic: abnormal earnings cannot be expected to continue in perpetuity.

### Examiner Report
Candidates performed very well on this question part. Candidates were expected to understand that it is not realistic to assume that abnormal earning to continue into perpetuity.
