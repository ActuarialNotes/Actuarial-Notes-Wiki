You are given Model 1 for the SCORE of STUDENT_ID $i$ in CLASS_ID $j$.

| Model | Model Specification |
|-------|---------------------|
| 1 | $SCORE_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times SEX_{ij} + \beta_2 \times SCORE\_PRE_{ij} + \beta_3 \times METHOD\_R_j + \beta_4 \times METHOD\_S_j + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$ |

The following assumptions apply to Model 1 and the models listed under A – E:

- $u_j \sim N(0, \sigma_{\text{class}}^2)$
- $\varepsilon_{ij} \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$
- All $u_j, \varepsilon_{ij}$ are mutually independent

For hierarchical models:

- Level 1 is Student
- Level 2 is Classroom

Determine which of the following four hierarchical models is equivalent to Model 1.

- A) Level 1: $SCORE_{ij} = b_{0j} + \beta_1 \times SEX_{ij} + \beta_2 \times SCORE\_PRE_{ij} + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + \beta_3 \times METHOD\_R_j + \beta_4 \times METHOD\_S_j + u_j$
- B) Level 1: $SCORE_{ij} = b_{0j} + \beta_1 \times SEX_{ij} + \beta_2 \times SCORE\_PRE_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij}$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + \beta_3 \times METHOD\_R_j + \beta_4 \times METHOD\_S_j + u_j$
- C) Level 1: $SCORE_{ij} = b_{0j} + \beta_1 \times SEX_{ij} + \beta_2 \times SCORE\_PRE_{ij} + \beta_3 \times METHOD\_R_j + \beta_4 \times METHOD\_S_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + u_j$
- D) Level 1: $SCORE_{ij} = b_{0j} + \beta_1 \times SEX_{ij} + \beta_2 \times SCORE\_PRE_{ij} + \beta_3 \times METHOD\_R_j + \beta_4 \times METHOD\_S_j + u_j$; Level 2: $b_{0j} = \beta_0 + u_j$
- E) None of (A), (B), (C), or (D) are equivalent to Model 1.

## Explanation

Written hierarchically, each equation holds only the variables measured at its level. The Level 1 (student) equation carries the student-level covariates SEX and SCORE_PRE, a classroom-specific intercept $b_{0j}$ and the student residual $\varepsilon_{ij}$. The Level 2 (classroom) equation explains that intercept with the classroom-level covariates METHOD_R and METHOD_S and the classroom random effect $u_j$.

**B** is exactly that. Substituting its Level 2 equation into Level 1 gives

$$
\begin{align*}
SCORE_{ij} &= \left(\beta_0 + \beta_3 \times METHOD\_R_j + \beta_4 \times METHOD\_S_j + u_j\right) + \beta_1 \times SEX_{ij} + \beta_2 \times SCORE\_PRE_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij} \\
&= \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times SEX_{ij} + \beta_2 \times SCORE\_PRE_{ij} + \beta_3 \times METHOD\_R_j + \beta_4 \times METHOD\_S_j + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}
\end{align*}
$$

which is Model 1.

The others fail:

- **A** puts $u_j$ in both levels. After substitution the classroom effect enters twice ($2u_j$, with variance $4\sigma^2_{\text{class}}$), so this is not Model 1.
- **C** places the classroom-level indicators METHOD_R$_j$ and METHOD_S$_j$ in the student-level equation. In a hierarchical specification, a variable measured on the classroom belongs in the Level 2 model for $b_{0j}$.
- **D** has no student-level residual $\varepsilon_{ij}$ at all, and uses $u_j$ in both levels.

The answer is **B**.
