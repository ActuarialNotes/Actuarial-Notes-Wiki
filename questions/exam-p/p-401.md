Let $X$ be a normally distributed random variable representing the amount of an individual claim of a policyholder covered by a group health policy.

You are given that $\text{Var}(X) = 250{,}000$ and $P[X < 1000] = 0.3446$.

Calculate the difference between the 90th percentile of $X$ and the median of $X$.

- A) $241$
- B) $441$
- C) $641$
- D) $822$
- E) $980$

## Explanation

The variance gives $\sigma = \sqrt{250{,}000} = 500$. Find the mean from the given probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
P\left[Z < \frac{1000 - \mu}{500}\right] &= 0.3446 \\
\frac{1000 - \mu}{500} &= -0.40 \\
\mu &= 1200
\end{align*}
$$

The $z$-score is negative because 0.3446 is below one half, which places 1000 to the left of the mean.

A normal distribution is symmetric, so its **median equals its mean**, 1200. The 90th percentile uses $z_{0.90} = 1.2816$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P_{0.9} &= 1200 + 500(1.2816) \\
        &= 1840.8
\end{align*}
$$

The difference is

$$
\begin{align*}
P_{0.9} - \text{median} &= 1840.8 - 1200 \\
                        &= \mathbf{640.8}
\end{align*}
$$

Notice the answer is just $\sigma z_{0.90} = 500(1.2816)$: because the median is the mean, the mean cancels and the given probability is never needed for the *difference*. Working that out first turns a three-step problem into one multiplication. Choice (E), 980, is $500(1.96)$ — the right structure with the two-sided 95% $z$-value substituted for $z_{0.90}$.
