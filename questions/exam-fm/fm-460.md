A geometric perpetuity-immediate provides annual payments. The first payment is 1, and each subsequent payment is 0.5% less than the previous payment. Let $M$ be the price of this perpetuity using an annual effective interest rate of 6.5%.

The price should have been calculated using an annual effective interest rate of 5.5%. Let $P$ be the correct price of this perpetuity and let $E$ be the estimate of $P$ based on the initial price, $M$, using the first-order modified approximation.

Calculate $\dfrac{E-P}{P}$, the percentage error of the estimate.

- A) $-2.78\%$
- B) $-2.04\%$
- C) $-1.92\%$
- D) $-1.24\%$
- E) $-1.06\%$

## Explanation

The present value of the geometric perpetuity-immediate, at annual effective interest rate $i$ and with payment growth rate $r = -0.005$, is

$$
\begin{align*}
P(i) &= \frac{1}{i - r} \\
     &= \frac{1}{i + 0.005}
\end{align*}
$$

The modified duration is therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{MOD}(i) &= -\frac{P'(i)}{P(i)} \\
           &= -\frac{-\dfrac{1}{(i+0.005)^{2}}}{\dfrac{1}{i+0.005}} \\
           &= \frac{1}{i + 0.005}
\end{align*}
$$

The first-order modified approximation of the present value at an interest rate $i$ near an initial interest rate $i_0$ is

$$
\begin{align*}
E &= P(i_0)\left[1 - (i - i_0)D_{MOD}(i_0)\right] \\
  &= \frac{1}{i_0 + 0.005}\left(1 - \frac{i - i_0}{i_0 + 0.005}\right)
\end{align*}
$$

Using $i_0 = 0.065$ (so $M = P(0.065) = 1/0.07$) and $i = 0.055$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E &= \frac{1}{0.07}\left(1 - \frac{0.055 - 0.065}{0.07}\right) \\
  &= \frac{1}{0.07}\left(\frac{8}{7}\right) \\
  &= 16.326531
\end{align*}
$$

The correct price is

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \frac{1}{0.055 + 0.005} \\
  &= 16.666667
\end{align*}
$$

so the percentage error is

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{E - P}{P} &= \frac{16.326531 - 16.666667}{16.666667} \\
                &= -0.0204 \\
                &= -2.04\%
\end{align*}
$$
