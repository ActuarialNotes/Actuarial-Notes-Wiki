## Part a (0.5 points)

Provide an argument for using credit-based insurance scores according to the "Actuarial Standard of Practice No. 12: Risk Classification".

### Explanation
Clear evidence that claims experience of insureds can be predicted using credit rating.

- Proof exists that using credit-score in rating can better rate specific insured's risk, otherwise overall premium is not less, just redistributed.

Alternatively:

Credit shows significant differences in expected loss costs, which helps make rate not excessive, and not unfairly discriminatory

### Examiner Report
Candidates did well on this section.  Most candidates identified the correlation between credit scores and loss experience.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Provide a counter-argument to the answer in part a. above.

### Explanation
Counter-argument to the first answer in part a:

Evidence of claims experience is only for claims free, severity of claims is similar for all credit scores.

- Evidence that it just may be that higher credit-scored insureds pay out some claims out of pocket
- Need more proof

Alternatively, counter-argument to the alternative answer in part a:

Credit reports contain many errors, using these erroneous reports would not produce a fair rate

### Examiner Report
Candidates did not read the question carefully.  Many candidates provided a response to part b which was not a counter-argument to the answer provided in part a.
For example many candidates stated in part b that using credit score was discriminatory but did not use this as an argument in part a.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two guidelines the Insurance Bureau of Canada provides in its code of conduct for insurers' use of credit information.

### Explanation
- Credit scores must be up to date
- Consider special life circumstances (so divorce, immigration, etc.)

Alternatively:

- If insured thinks they are punished for credit information, can write to insurer and insurer should consider
- Confidentiality
  - Keep info private, only necessary parties see it

### Examiner Report
Many guidelines were mentioned in the syllabus and most candidates earned full credit for this part.

## Part d (0.75 points)

Explain how changes made in 2010 by the Financial Services Commission of Ontario addressed credit-based risk rating in auto insurance.

### Explanation
- Officially defined credit information
- Made it illegal for insurers to refuse to quote, bind or renew only on terms of credit info
- Insureds must give permission for insurers to use their credit info, and insurers can't refuse to quote, bind or renew due to credit info alone.

Alternatively:

- Defined credit
- Cannot terminate policy, refuse quote due to credit alone
- Cannot require consent of credit before quoting

### Examiner Report
Generally candidates did well on part d.  The most common error was to provide a Financial Services Commission of Ontario regulation that was not a change made during 2010.
