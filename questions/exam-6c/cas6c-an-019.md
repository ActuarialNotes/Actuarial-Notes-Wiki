A P&C insurance company based in the United Kingdom plans to insure risks in Canada through a Canadian branch, writing business in Ontario and Alberta.

Which statement correctly describes its position as an insurer in Canada?

- A) It may incorporate under Ontario's statute instead, and then needs neither federal registration nor any other licence
- B) Once registered federally, it needs no provincial licence, because federal registration covers every province
- C) It must be registered federally under the Insurance Companies Act, and must also obtain a provincial licence before operating
- D) Under the Insurance Companies Act, its regulatory reporting is based on the location of the risk rather than the location of the insurance
- E) Its capital adequacy is measured by the MCT ratio of capital available to minimum capital required, like a Canadian company's

## Explanation

The introduction to the P&C return instructions says: "Foreign P&C insurance companies must be registered federally under the ICA to insure in Canada a risk. Regardless of the jurisdiction of incorporation, all insurers must first obtain a licence in a province or territory before operations can commence." The answer is **C**. Solvency registration is federal, and licensing to operate is provincial.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — a foreign company insuring in Canada must register federally. A provincial charter would create a different, Canadian company, and it would still need a licence.
- **B** — federal registration does not replace provincial licensing, which the instructions require of every insurer.
- **D** — the instructions state the reverse: under Part XIII of the ICA, regulatory reporting of a foreign entity's business is based on the location of the **insurance** rather than the location of the risk, so its returns reflect the risks insured in Canada.
- **E** — foreign companies operating on a branch basis are assessed under the Branch Adequacy of Assets Test (BAAT) in the MCT guideline. It measures net assets available in Canada against the margin required, not capital available against minimum capital required.
