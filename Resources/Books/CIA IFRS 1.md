## The asymmetries to remember

- **Reinsurance held is an asset measured in its own right**, not a deduction from the gross liability. Its grouping, boundary and measurement approach are determined independently of the underlying contracts — so a cedant can be on the PAA gross and the GMM on a reinsurance treaty, or the reverse.
- **The risk adjustment on reinsurance held represents risk transferred *away*.** It is the amount of non-financial risk the cedant has passed to the reinsurer, and it therefore reduces the net risk adjustment rather than adding to it.
- **Non-performance risk.** The FCF of reinsurance held must allow for the reinsurer's default and for disputes; there is no equivalent adjustment on the gross side. This is where **[[Unregistered Reinsurance|unregistered]]** reinsurance and collateral come into the accounting, and it connects to the [[MCT|MCT]]'s credit risk treatment.
- **Loss-recovery component.** When an onerous group recognises a [[Loss Component|loss component]], the matching reinsurance held recognises income immediately through a loss-recovery component — the standard's answer to an accounting mismatch that would otherwise report a loss on the gross group and no offsetting gain.
- **Reinstatement premiums** are presented differently depending on whether the original contract contemplated them.
- **Risk-attaching treaties** are the classic PAA-eligibility problem: the coverage period runs to the expiry of the last underlying policy attaching, so it typically exceeds a year.
