Which practice, if it were the insurer's general business practice, breaches the NAIC model act or its property/casualty model regulation?

- A) Sending a reservation of rights letter while investigating a coverage question
- B) Asking a first-party claimant for a properly executed proof of loss
- C) Denying a claim in writing and citing the policy exclusion relied on
- D) Marking a partial payment check "final" when the limit hasn't been paid

## Explanation

The regulation bars an insurer from indicating on a payment that it is "final" or "a release" unless the policy limit has been paid or the claimant has agreed to a compromise settlement. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — A reservation of rights is a legitimate way to investigate without waiving a coverage defense.
- **B** — The regulation's deadlines run from receipt of a properly executed proof of loss, so asking for one is expected. Requiring both a formal proof and duplicate verification of the same information is what the act prohibits.
- **C** — That is exactly what the regulation requires of a denial.
