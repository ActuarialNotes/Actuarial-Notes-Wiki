An insurer is doing bootstrapping using a GLM with a log-link function and an over-dispersed Poisson error function on the following dataset of incremental reported loss values (\$000).

| Year | Age 1 | Age 2 | Age 3 | Age 4 | Age 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50 | 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 250 | 450 | 200 | 50 | 0 |
| 3 | 300 | 500 | 250 | -20 | 0 |
| 4 | 250 | 1,000 | 100 | -50 | |
| 5 | 300 | 500 | 250 | | |
| 6 | 350 | 400 | | | |
| 7 | 300 | | | | |

## Part a (0.25 points)

Identify an issue with the data that would cause model failure if left unadjusted.

### Explanation
The incremental values in Age 4 are negative — the sum of the Age 4 column is negative, which the log-link ODP model cannot fit.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify the negative incremental values in the triangle, which the majority did. The most common error was to identify the zeros in the triangle.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Identify two additional issues with the data that may impact modeling results.

### Explanation
Two of:

- **Outliers** — Year 4, Ages 2 and 3 have values that appear to be outliers.
- **Year 1 is inconsistent with the other years** — a large increase in exposure from Year 1 to Year 2 (Year 1 has a different exposure level, or is the first year and very thin), or missing data (the zeros) in Year 1 from Age 3 on.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify two additional issues that may impact modeling results (although they would not cause model failure). These were the outlier in Year 4, Age 2 and the data inconsistency between Year 1 and other years, perhaps due to a change in exposure or missing data. Most candidates could identify at least one of the issues. Common errors included misinterpreting the zeros in Age 5 as missing data (rather than claim closure) and identifying the negative incremental value in Age 4 (as it should have been identified in part a instead).

## Part c (0.75 points)

For each of the three issues identified in part a. and part b. above, briefly describe how the data might be adjusted to improve modeling results.

### Explanation
- **Negative incremental values:** add a positive constant to every value in the triangle (e.g. 50) to eliminate the negatives, solve the GLM, then subtract the constant from the modeled results.
- **Outliers in Year 4:** exclude them from the triangle, or treat them as missing and estimate them from surrounding values, or exclude Year 4 from the Age 1–2 / Age 2–3 factors and the residual calculations.
- **Year 1 exposure change:** if earned exposure data is available, divide the whole triangle by exposures and model pure premium (or loss ratio) development instead of total loss development.
- **Thin / missing data in Year 1:** remove the Year 1 row from the triangle.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to suggest adjustments to the data to improve modeling results, to address the three issues identified in parts a and b. See the sample answers above for acceptable suggestions. Most candidates provided full-credit solutions for how to adjust the data for outliers (Year 4) and missing/thin data (Year 1).
The most common error was related to the negative incremental values issue; candidates described adding a value to each entry of the triangle before modeling, but they failed to mention subtracting the value back out from the modeled results. This response was considered incomplete because model output produced this way would be biased.
