For two stock companies:

| | Company A | Company B |
|---|---|---|
| Beginning Surplus | \$10,000,000 | \$5,000,000 |
| Ending Surplus | \$15,000,000 | \$6,000,000 |
| Stockholder Dividends | \$1,000,000 | \$300,000 |
| Capital Paid-In | \$3,000,000 | \$100,000 |
| Market to Book Value | 0.90 | 2.00 |

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the market rates of return for each of company A and company B.

### Explanation
The actual rate of return on surplus is the change in surplus plus stockholder dividends less paid-in capital, over beginning surplus; the market rate of return divides it by the market-to-book ratio.

$$\text{Actual ROR} = \frac{S_{end} - S_{beg}}{S_{beg}} + \frac{\text{SH divid}}{S_{beg}} - \frac{\text{Cap paid in}}{S_{beg}}$$

Company A:

$$\text{Actual ROR} = \frac{15 - 10}{10} + \frac{1}{10} - \frac{3}{10} = 0.3$$

$$\text{Market ROR} = \frac{0.3}{MV:BV} = \frac{0.3}{0.9} = 0.333$$

Company B:

$$\text{Actual ROR} = \frac{6 - 5}{5} + \frac{0.3}{5} - \frac{0.1}{5} = .24\% \text{ (as printed)}$$

$$\text{Market ROR} = \frac{2.4}{2} \text{ (as printed)} = 0.12$$

Alternatively:

$$\text{Co A: } \frac{15M - 10M + 1M - 3M}{10M} \times \frac{1}{0.9} = 33.33\%$$

$$\text{Co B: } \frac{1M - 5M + 300K - 100K}{5M} \text{ (as printed)} \times \frac{1}{2} = 12\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how to calculate market rate of return from its basic building blocks. In order to obtain full credit, candidates were expected to correctly set up an equation to calculate the market rate of return and calculate the correct figures for both companies.
Candidates generally understood how to calculate the return on surplus but most did not correctly apply the MV/BV ratio to convert this figure to the market rate of return.
Common errors included:

- multiplying instead of dividing by the MV/BV ratio
- not converting the return on book value to a return on market value figure
- converting the wrong items to market value
- calculating the return on surplus as a dollar value instead of a rate of return, as the question directs
- dividing by ending surplus instead of beginning surplus

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly compare the investors' view of the future for each company.

### Explanation
Market to book value: Company A 0.9, Company B 2.00.

- Given that people are paying 2x the book value to own Co. B, their view of the companies' future must be good.
- Co. A's mkt value is .9 of the book value so investors must be less interested in owning Co. A stock.

Alternatively:

Currently Company B is trading at a premium to Book Value indicating investors are optimistic about the firm's prospects. Company A on the other hand is trading at a discount to Book Value indicating investors are not optimistic about the firm's prospects.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to evaluate the historical information regarding the two firms in question, identify the appropriate metric(s) that would be most pertinent for highlighting the future outlook of each firm, and ultimately make a judgment on the outlook for each firm.
For full credit, candidates needed to select a metric(s) that was indicative of the future outlook for each firm from the point of an investor, describe what the metric entailed and then translate the result into an evaluation of future outlook of the two firms.
Most candidates who lost points on this part were unable to translate the selected performance metric to the future outlook of each firm. Many candidates were able to identify the MV/BV ratio as a key indicator for future performance, but subsequently lost points by failing to convey the result with respect to each firm. Another common mistake came from misidentifying metrics that would be used by investors in evaluating future outlook and failing to understand the underlying concept behind the selected metric.
Some candidates referenced the Fama French 3-factor model in support of a more optimistic view with regards to the ability to earn excess return with Company A. Candidate responses which listed the relevant assumptions and then successfully identified the BV/MV trigger were awarded full credit.
