A caterer signs a contract to serve at a museum's annual gala. The contract requires the caterer to indemnify the museum for any claims arising from the caterer's use of the museum's premises. From the museum's point of view, which risk treatment technique does this clause use?

- A) Loss prevention
- B) Retention
- C) Separation
- D) Noninsurance risk transfer

## Explanation

A hold-harmless or indemnity clause shifts the financial consequences of certain claims from one party to another by contract, without an insurance policy. For the museum, that is **noninsurance risk transfer**. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Loss prevention lowers the chance of a loss, such as safe cabling and floor mats; the clause does not change how likely an injury is.
- **B** — Retention means the museum pays its own losses; the clause moves them to the caterer.
- **C** — Separation divides an exposure among locations or units so one event damages less; nothing is being divided.

The transfer is only as good as the caterer's ability to pay, which is why such contracts often also require the caterer to carry insurance.
