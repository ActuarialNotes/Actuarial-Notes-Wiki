A P&C insurer's Appointed Actuary values the liability for remaining coverage using a method that differs from the one illustrated in a CIA educational note for her situation. She has documented why her method suits the insurer's business. A peer reviewer writes that "departing from the educational note is a departure from the standards of practice and must be disclosed as such in the report."

Which critique of the peer reviewer's comment is most valid?

- A) An educational note is not binding: the actuary may use another method, but remains responsible for its being accepted actuarial practice
- B) The comment is correct: an educational note's method is the only accepted practice for the situation it describes
- C) The comment is correct, because educational notes bind members once the Actuarial Guidance Council approves them for distribution
- D) An educational note binds only Fellows of the CIA, so the comment applies only if the actuary is a Fellow
- E) Educational notes never go out of date, so an older note's method must be used even after practice has changed

## Explanation

Every CIA educational note opens with the same statement, quoted here from the note on IFRS 17 and the liability for remaining coverage:

> The actuary should be familiar with relevant educational notes. Educational notes are not binding; rather they are intended to illustrate the application of the standards of practice. A practice that an educational note describes for a situation is not necessarily the only accepted practice for that situation... Responsibility for ensuring that work is in accordance with accepted actuarial practice lies with the actuary.

Using a different, well-supported method is therefore not in itself a departure from the standards. The actuary must still be familiar with the note and must be able to show that her method is accepted actuarial practice. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — contradicts the statement that the practice a note describes "is not necessarily the only accepted practice."
- **C** — approval for distribution under the CIA's due process for guidance material other than standards of practice does not make a note binding. Only the standards of practice are.
- **D** — no such distinction exists. The note's non-binding status does not depend on the actuary's designation.
- **E** — the CIA warns the opposite: "as accepted actuarial practice evolves, an educational note may no longer appropriately illustrate the application of standards."
