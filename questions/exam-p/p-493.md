An insurer sells a group life and disability policy.

The joint probability distribution for death and disability is given in the table below.

| Disabilities \ Deaths | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.51 | 0.09 | 0.04 | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| 1 | 0.08 | 0.06 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| 2 | 0.04 | 0.03 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.00 |
| 3 | 0.03 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.00 |

Calculate the probability of at least two disabilities given no more than one death.

- A) $0.12$
- B) $0.14$
- C) $0.15$
- D) $0.25$
- E) $0.75$

## Explanation

Both events are blocks of cells in the first two columns (no more than one death).

**Denominator: no more than one death**, summed over all four disability rows:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[D \leq 1] &= (0.51 + 0.09) + (0.08 + 0.06) + (0.04 + 0.03) + (0.03 + 0.02) \\
            &= 0.60 + 0.14 + 0.07 + 0.05 \\
            &= 0.86
\end{align*}
$$

**Numerator: at least two disabilities *and* no more than one death** — the first two columns of the bottom two rows:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{Dis} \geq 2, D \leq 1] &= (0.04 + 0.03) + (0.03 + 0.02) \\
                               &= 0.12
\end{align*}
$$

Dividing:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{Dis} \geq 2 \mid D \leq 1] &= \frac{0.12}{0.86} \\
                                   &= \mathbf{0.1395}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.12, is the numerator alone — the joint probability rather than the conditional one. The cells outside the first two columns never enter either calculation, so identifying the right block before adding anything saves most of the work.
