A purse contains two coins, one fair and one two-headed. One of the coins is selected at random and tossed twice. Both tosses result in heads.

Calculate the probability that a third toss of the selected coin will result in heads.

- A) $1/2$
- B) $9/16$
- C) $5/8$
- D) $4/5$
- E) $9/10$

## Explanation

Let $F$ be the event that the fair coin was selected, so $P[F] = P[F'] = 1/2$. Write $HH$ for two heads and $HHH$ for three.

The tosses are independent *given which coin was chosen*, but not unconditionally — the two heads are evidence about which coin is in play. Expand both the two-head and three-head probabilities over the two coins:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[HH]  &= P[HH \mid F]P[F] + P[HH \mid F']P[F'] \\
       &= \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) + (1)^2\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) \\
       &= \tfrac{1}{8} + \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{5}{8}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
P[HHH] &= \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^3\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) + (1)^3\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) \\
       &= \tfrac{1}{16} + \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{9}{16}
\end{align*}
$$

The third toss being a head, given the first two were, is then

$$
\begin{align*}
P[HHH \mid HH] &= \frac{P[HHH]}{P[HH]} \\
               &= \frac{9/16}{5/8} \\
               &= \mathbf{\frac{9}{10}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A) is the answer if the coin were known to be fair. Choices (B) and (C) are the two intermediate quantities $P[HHH]$ and $P[HH]$ reported on their own. The two heads make the two-headed coin four times as likely as the fair one — a posterior of $4/5$ versus $1/5$ — and the answer $9/10$ is exactly $\tfrac{4}{5}(1) + \tfrac{1}{5}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)$, which is a useful cross-check.
