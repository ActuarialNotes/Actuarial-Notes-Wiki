An insured purchases a $400,000 policy on a property valued at $500,000.

- The coinsurance requirement for the policy is 90% of property value.
- No deductible applies.

## Part a (0.25 points)

Calculate the coinsurance penalty for a $300,000 loss.

### Explanation
400000/(0.9*500000) = 0.889
(1-0.889)*300000 = 33,300

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to calculate a coinsurance penalty.
A common mistake was a calculation error.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Calculate the maximum coinsurance penalty.

### Explanation
0.9*500000 = 450000
400000*(1-0.889) = 44,400

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to calculate a coinsurance penalty.
A common mistake was a calculation error.

## Part c (0.25 points)

Calculate the coinsurance apportionment ratio, assuming the property is valued at $425,000 instead of $500,000.

### Explanation
a = min (F / (c * V), 1) = min (400,000/(425,000 * 0.9), 1) = 1

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to correctly calculate a coinsurance apportionment ratio.
A common mistake was stating that there would be no insurance penalty, but not writing that the apportionment ratio = 1.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Briefly describe two issues associated with underinsured properties.

### Explanation
Insured's will not be fully insured for a loss Expected losses are higher for underinsured policies when partial losses are possible

Alternatively:

Premium will not be equitable for underinsured vs. fully insured policies Premium will not be adequate for underinsured policies

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate an understanding of the issues associated with underinsurance.
Common mistakes included:
- Stating that premium would be inequitable but didn't specify that this meant underinsured properties with respect to fully insured properties
- Stating "loss not covered for underinsured policies" vs. "loss not fully covered for underinsured policies"
- "Insurer needs to be careful when inflation causes property value to increase" – this is an issue for both underinsured and fully insured properties, and was not awarded credit
