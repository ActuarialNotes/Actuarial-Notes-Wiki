The following information is available for the valuation of the premium liabilities as at December 31, 2018. All amounts are in thousands of dollars (\$000s).

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Net written premium in the last 12 months | 100,000 |
| Gross unearned premium | 65,000 |
| Net unearned premium | 55,000 |
| Selected general expense ratio | 28% |
| Portion of expenses related to policy servicing cost | 25% |
| Expected reinsurance premium | 4,000 |
| Gross present value losses and loss adjustment expenses (LAE) | 51,900 |
| Projected net undiscounted loss ratio including LAE | 80% |
| Discount rate | 3% |
| MfAD for claims development | 6% |
| MfAD for recovery from reinsurance ceded | 0.5% |
| MfAD for investment return rates | 0.25% |
| Contingent commission | 2,000 |
| Unearned commission | 2,700 |
| MCT risk factor for premium liabilities | 15% |
| Initial Deferred Policy Acquisition Expense (DPAE) | 6,500 |

The cumulative accident year claim payment pattern is as follows:

| Age (Months) | % Paid |
|---|---|
| 12 | 40% |
| 24 | 100% |

## Part a (2.5 points)

Calculate the net premium liabilities as at December 31, 2018.

### Explanation
$$\text{Adjusted net unearned premium} = 55{,}000 - 4{,}000 = 51{,}000$$

$$\text{Undiscounted losses and LAE} = 51{,}000 \times 80\% = 40{,}800$$

Present values, paid at mid-year and adjusted for the average accident date of the unexpired risk:

$$\text{PV(loss+LAE)@3\%} = 40{,}800 \times (0.6 \times 1.03^{-0.5} + 0.4 \times 1.03^{-1.5}) \times 1.03^{(0.5-1/3)} = 39{,}694$$

$$\text{PV(loss+LAE)@2.75\%} = 40{,}800 \times (0.6 \times 1.0275^{-0.5} + 0.4 \times 1.0275^{-1.5}) \times 1.0275^{(0.5-1/3)} = 39{,}783$$

(The weights 0.6 and 0.4 are as printed; the results 39,694 and 39,783 correspond to paying 40% in the first year and 60% in the second, as the payment pattern gives.)

PfADs:

- Claims PfAD $= 39{,}694 \times 6\% = 2{,}382$
- Interest rate PfAD $= 39{,}783 - 39{,}694 = 89$
- Reinsurance ceded PfAD $= (51{,}900 - 39{,}694) \times 0.5\% = 61$

$$\text{APV} = 39{,}694 + 2{,}382 + 89 + 61 = 42{,}226$$

$$\text{Maintenance expenses} = 65{,}000 \times 28\% \times 25\% = 4{,}550$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Premium liabilities} &= \text{APV} + \text{Maintenance expenses} + \text{Future reinsurance cost} + \text{Contingent commission} \\
&= 42{,}226 + 4{,}550 + 4{,}000 + 2{,}000 \\
 &= 52{,}776
\end{aligned}$$

Alternatively, with discount factors:

$$\text{Undiscounted losses and LAE} = (55{,}000 - 4{,}000) \times 80\% = 40{,}800$$

$$\text{Discount factor @3\%} = (0.6 \times 1.03^{-0.5} + 0.4 \times 1.03^{-1.5}) \times 1.03^{(0.5-1/3)} = 0.9729$$

$$\text{Discount factor @2.75\%} = (0.6 \times 1.0275^{-0.5} + 0.4 \times 1.0275^{-1.5}) \times 1.0275^{(0.5-1/3)} = 0.9751$$

$$\text{APV} = 40{,}800 \times 0.9751 + 40{,}800 \times 0.9729 \times 0.06 + (51{,}900 - 40{,}800 \times 0.9729) \times 0.005 = 42{,}226$$

$$\text{Premium liabilities} = 42{,}226 + 65{,}000 \times 0.28 \times 0.25 + 4{,}000 + 2{,}000 = 52{,}776$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the net premium liabilities calculation.
Common errors included:

- Not including all components (most commonly contingent commissions) in the final formula
- Not calculating maintenance expenses correctly
- Stopping the calculation at the APV point and not completing the final steps to derive the premium liabilities number

## Part b (0.5 points)

Determine whether the initial DPAE should be reduced.

### Explanation
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Equity in UPR} &= \text{Net UPR} + \text{Unearned commission} - \text{Premium liabilities} \\
&= 55{,}000 + 2{,}700 - 52{,}776 \\
 &= 4{,}924
\end{aligned}$$

Max DPAE $= 4{,}924$.

Since initial DPAE (6,500) is greater than Max DPAE, the DPAE must be reduced.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to calculate the maximum DPAE and the effect on initial
DPAE.
Common errors included:

- Using the wrong unearned commission amount
- Not mentioning the right action to take based on the maximum DPAE calculation

## Part c (0.75 points)

Fully explain the impact on the return on equity (ROE) of a reduction of the initial DPAE.

### Explanation
ROE = Net income / Equity.

When DPAE is reduced 2 things occur:

- Equity is reduced because DPAE is held as an asset. EQ = A – L. If Assets decrease, Equity decreases. This makes ROE go up.
- As DPAE goes down, expenses increase on the income statement which reduces net income. This makes ROE go down.

The overall effect is uncertain.

Alternatively:

The reduction in DPAE means that more of the premium acquisition expense must be recognized in the period. Therefore NI goes down. DPAE is categorized as asset. Thus, reducing asset will reduce equity by a similar amount then the NI reduction. Net effect would be a decrease in ROE.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how a change in DPAE can affect net income, equity, and ROE.
Common errors included:

- Stating that a decrease in equity will result in a decrease of ROE
- Assuming there is no impact on net income
- Failing to explain how a change in DPAE impacts equity or net income

## Part e (0.5 points)

Calculate the capital required at target for premium liabilities in the MCT.

### Explanation
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Margin for Premium liabilities} &= \max(\text{Premium liabilities excluding PfAD},\ 30\% \times \text{NWP}) \times 15\% \\
&= \max(52{,}776 - 2{,}382 - 89 - 61,\ 30\% \times 100{,}000) \times 15\% \\
&= 7{,}537
\end{aligned}$$

Alternatively:

- Premium liabilities excl. PfAD $= 39{,}694 + 4{,}550 + 4{,}000 + 2{,}000 = 50{,}244$
- $30\% \times \text{NWP} = 30\% \times 100{,}000 = 30{,}000$
- $\max(50{,}244,\ 30{,}000) \times 15\% = 7{,}537$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the margin for premium liabilities used for the MCT calculation.
Common errors included:

- Not excluding PfADs from the premium liabilities
- Not including contingent commissions, future reinsurance premium, and maintenance expenses in premium liabilities
- Not applying the maximum correctly
- Omitting the 30% x NWP calculation
