An insurer's retention model predicts the following:

| % Change in Premium | Retention Ratio — First Renewal | Retention Ratio — Second Renewal |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 85% | 0% |
| 5% | 75% | 0% |

The following information is known:

| Value | Item |
|---|---|
| 0.00% | Discount rate |
| \$0 | Fixed expenses |
| \$0 | Implementation costs |
| \$1,000 | Premium per policy |
| \$800 | Loss & LAE per policy |

- Senior management will consider only the following at renewal:
  - i. No rate change
  - ii. +5% rate change

## Part a (1.25 points)

Select the rate change that will maximize the insurer's profit.

### Answer
Select the +5% rate change (lifetime profit 387.50 vs 370.00)

### Explanation
This is a **lifetime value** comparison: the rate change that maximizes profit is the one maximizing profit over the customer's whole expected life with the insurer, not the profit on a single term. Two features of the data simplify it — the discount rate is 0%, so no present-valuing is needed, and the **second renewal retention is 0% under both scenarios**, so the customer's life ends after the first renewal in either case.

The retention ratio must be applied to the *net* result of the renewal term — to premium and losses together — not to premium alone.

**Scenario 1 — no rate change:**

| Term | Premium | Loss & LAE | Profit | Retention | Expected Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current | 1,000 | 800 | 200 | 1.00 | 200.0 |
| 1st renewal | 1,000 | 800 | 200 | 0.85 | 170.0 |
| **Total** | | | | | **370.0** |

**Scenario 2 — +5% rate change:**

| Term | Premium | Loss & LAE | Profit | Retention | Expected Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current | 1,000 | 800 | 200 | 1.00 | 200.0 |
| 1st renewal | 1,050 | 800 | 250 | 0.75 | 187.5 |
| **Total** | | | | | **387.5** |

$$387.5 > 370.0$$

**Select the +5% rate change.** The 10-point drop in retention is more than paid for by the wider margin on the policies that do renew: the extra \$50 of premium falls straight to profit, lifting the renewal margin from \$200 to \$250.

The same conclusion follows if profit is expressed as a percentage of premium rather than in dollars — $370/2{,}000 = 18.5\%$ versus $387.5/2{,}050 = 18.9\%$ — or if only the renewal term is considered, $170/1{,}000 = 17.0\%$ versus $187.5/1{,}050 = 17.9\%$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand retention ratio, lifetime value, optimized pricing and the CAS Ratemaking Principles.

Candidates were expected to calculate and compare the profit under each scenario while considering the rate change and retention ratio.

Stating an assumption regarding total number of policies in the book was acceptable but not required.

Candidates received credit for using profit or for using profit as a percentage of premium as the basis for recommendation.

Common mistakes included:

- Applying the retention rate only to premium and not loss.
- Not applying the rate increase to the 1st year renewal premium.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly evaluate the selected rate change with respect to the *Statement of Principles Regarding Property Casualty Insurance Ratemaking*, citing one relevant principle.

### Explanation
Any one of the four CAS ratemaking principles, applied to the selection made in part a. For example:

**Principle 2 — "A rate provides for all costs associated with the transfer of risk."** Selecting a rate purely because it maximizes profit does not, on its own, demonstrate compliance: the principle requires the rate to be built from the expected costs of the risk transfer, and this selection was driven by a retention model rather than by an estimate of future costs. If the +5% rate is charged without any indication that costs have risen, the strategy conflicts with the principle. On the other hand, the rate is not necessarily improper — a \$1,000 premium against \$800 of loss and LAE leaves a margin that must still cover variable expenses and provide an underwriting profit and risk load for the inherent variability of the business, so the loaded rate could well be a fair provision for all costs.

Alternatively, **Principle 4** — "A rate is reasonable and not excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory if it is an actuarially sound estimate of the expected value of all future costs associated with an individual risk transfer" — requires all three components to be evaluated. Here the rate is applied uniformly to every insured, so it is not unfairly discriminatory; whether it is excessive or inadequate cannot be settled without knowing the line of business, market conditions and the remaining cost provisions.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to reference one of the 4 CAS Ratemaking Principles and make appropriate comments on how Part a is evaluated based on the quoted principle.

Common mistakes included:

- Referencing the 4th principle but failing to evaluate all components of the principle.
- Referring to affordability issues, which is not part of the CAS Ratemaking Principles.
