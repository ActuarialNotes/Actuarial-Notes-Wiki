A competitor begins using telematics data to offer lower prices to the safest drivers in a market. An insurer that keeps pricing without such data is most at risk of which outcome?

- A) Adverse selection, as its safer drivers leave and its book worsens
- B) Moral hazard, as its own policyholders begin to drive less carefully
- C) Lower expense ratios, because it avoids the cost of devices and analytics
- D) Higher retention, because drivers prefer not to be monitored

## Explanation

As underwriting becomes more granular, an insurer that does not use the new data is left with a pool from which competitors have taken the better risks. Its average risk worsens while its prices still assume the old mix: **adverse selection**. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Moral hazard is a change in the insured's behavior because of insurance; nothing about the competitor's pricing makes this insurer's drivers less careful.
- **C** — Avoiding the technology cost may save expense, but it is not the main risk, and the savings can be outweighed by worse losses.
- **D** — Some drivers may avoid monitoring, but the safest drivers have a reason to accept it for a lower price, which is what drains the insurer's book.
