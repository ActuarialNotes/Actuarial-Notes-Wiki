Each picture below represents a two-dimensional space where observations are classified into two categories. The categories are representing by (as printed) light and dark shading. A classification tree is to be constructed for each space.

Determine which space can be modeled with no error by a classification tree.

- I. ![Space I: a square in which the dark category fills an inverted L along the left side and the top, and the light category fills a rectangle in the lower right. The boundary between them is one vertical line (about 40% of the way across from the left) running from the bottom up to one horizontal line (about 40% of the way down from the top) that runs to the right edge.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/srm-q26-space-i.png)
- II. ![Space II: a square in which the dark category fills the top and a thin strip along the left edge, and the light category fills the lower right. The boundary between them is a smooth curve: it rises steeply near the left edge, bends sharply, then slopes gently upward to the right edge, about a third of the way down from the top.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/srm-q26-space-ii.png)
- III. ![Space III: a square divided by one straight diagonal line running from the left edge (about 70% of the way down) up to the top edge (about 75% of the way across). The dark category is the triangle in the upper left above the line, and the light category fills the rest of the square.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/srm-q26-space-iii.png)

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II, and III
- E) The correct answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D).

## Explanation

Each step must divide the one the (as printed) existing regions into two parts, using either a vertical or a horizontal line. Only I can be created this way.
