At Jack's death, his estate of 298,000 is to be divided between Charity A and Charity B.

Charity A will receive 198,000 and use it to purchase a perpetuity-due that makes a payment of $X$ every month.

Charity B will receive 100,000 and use it to purchase a perpetuity-due that makes a payment of $X$ every two months.

The payments of $X$ for each charity are based on an annual effective interest rate of $i$.

Calculate $i$.

- A) 21.5%
- B) 24.0%
- C) 24.5%
- D) 26.8%
- E) 27.4%

## Explanation

Let $v$ denote the discount factor applicable to a period of one month and let $X$ be the constant periodic amount received by both charities. A perpetuity-due paying $X$ each month has present value $\dfrac{X}{1-v}$, so for Charity A,

$$198{,}000 = \frac{X}{1-v}$$

Since $v^{2}$ is the discount factor applicable to a two-month period, for Charity B,

$$100{,}000 = \frac{X}{1-v^{2}}$$

Dividing the first equation by the second gives

$$
\begin{align*}
1.98 &= \frac{1-v^{2}}{1-v} \\
1.98 &= 1 + v \\
v &= 0.98
\end{align*}
$$

The yearly discount factor is $0.98^{12} = 0.784717$, so the annual effective interest rate is

$$
\begin{align*}
i &= \frac{1}{0.784717} - 1 \\
  &= 0.274345 \\
  &\approx 27.4\%
\end{align*}
$$
