The lifetimes of light bulbs are independent and exponentially distributed with common mean 1000 hours. A lamp has a single bulb and is continuously on. Each light bulb is replaced immediately after it burns out.

Calculate the standard deviation of the time, in hours, until the failure of the fifth bulb.

- A) $71$
- B) $158$
- C) $1000$
- D) $2236$
- E) $5000$

## Explanation

Because each bulb is replaced the instant it fails, the time until the fifth failure is the **sum** of five independent lifetimes.

An exponential distribution has variance equal to the square of its mean, so each bulb contributes

$$
\text{Var} = 1000^2 = 1{,}000{,}000
$$

Independent variances add:

$$
\text{Var(total)} = 5(1{,}000{,}000) = 5{,}000{,}000
$$

Taking the square root:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{SD(total)} &= \sqrt{5{,}000{,}000} \\
                 &= 1000\sqrt{5} \\
                 &= \mathbf{2236}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 5000, adds the standard deviations rather than the variances — that would require perfectly correlated lifetimes. Choice (C), 1000, is a single bulb's standard deviation. The total time is a gamma distribution with shape 5 and scale 1000, whose mean is 5000 and standard deviation $1000\sqrt{5}$: five times the mean but only $\sqrt{5}$ times the spread.
