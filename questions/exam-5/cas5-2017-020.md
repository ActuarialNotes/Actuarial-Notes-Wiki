Given the following data evaluated as of December 31, 2016:

**Cumulative Paid Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 300 | 550 | 647 | 700 |
| 2014 | 500 | 979 | Not Provided | |
| 2015 | 400 | 825 | | |
| 2016 | 450 | | | |

**Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 500 | 660 | 700 | 700 |
| 2014 | 750 | 900 | 1,150 | |
| 2015 | 640 | 810 | | |
| 2016 | 700 | | | |

- There is no development on paid or reported claims after 48 months.
- The reported claim development technique projects IBNR that is \$50,000 lower than the IBNR projected by the paid claim development technique for accident year 2016.
- Age-to-age development factors are selected using an all year simple average.

Calculate the total claims paid in calendar year 2016 for all accident years.

### Answer
\$1,029,000

### Explanation
Calendar year 2016 payments are the differences between the latest diagonal (December 31, 2016) and the diagonal before it (December 31, 2015). Three of those four differences can be read straight off the paid triangle; the accident year 2014 cell at 36 months is missing, and the whole point of the question is to recover it from the stated \$50,000 IBNR relationship.

Call the missing accident year 2014 paid amount at 36 months $x$.

**Step 1 — reported development, which is fully observable.**

| AY | 12–24 | 24–36 | 36–48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | $660/500 = 1.320$ | $700/660 = 1.0606$ | $700/700 = 1.000$ |
| 2014 | $900/750 = 1.200$ | $1{,}150/900 = 1.2778$ | |
| 2015 | $810/640 = 1.2656$ | | |
| **All-year simple average** | **1.2619** | **1.1692** | **1.000** |

With no development after 48 months, the 12-to-ultimate reported CDF for accident year 2016 is

$$\text{CDF}_r = 1.2619 \times 1.1692 \times 1.000 = 1.4754$$

$$\text{Ultimate}_r = 700 \times 1.4754 = 1{,}032.8$$

IBNR is ultimate minus **reported**, not minus paid:

$$\text{IBNR}_r = 1{,}032.8 - 700 = 332.8$$

**Step 2 — paid development, carrying $x$ symbolically.**

| AY | 12–24 | 24–36 | 36–48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | $550/300 = 1.8333$ | $647/550 = 1.1764$ | $700/647 = 1.0819$ |
| 2014 | $979/500 = 1.9580$ | $x/979$ | |
| 2015 | $825/400 = 2.0625$ | | |
| **All-year simple average** | **1.9513** | $\dfrac{1.1764 + x/979}{2}$ | **1.0819** |

$$\text{Ultimate}_p = 450 \times 1.9513 \times \frac{1.1764 + x/979}{2} \times 1.0819 = 950.0 \times \frac{1.1764 + x/979}{2}$$

The paid technique's IBNR is still measured against reported claims, so

$$\text{IBNR}_p = \text{Ultimate}_p - 700$$

**Step 3 — impose the \$50,000 relationship and solve for $x$.** The reported IBNR is \$50 (thousand) *lower* than the paid IBNR:

$$\text{IBNR}_p = \text{IBNR}_r + 50 = 332.8 + 50 = 382.8 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{Ultimate}_p = 1{,}082.8$$

$$950.0 \times \frac{1.1764 + x/979}{2} = 1{,}082.8$$

$$\frac{1.1764 + x/979}{2} = 1.1397 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{x}{979} = 2.2795 - 1.1764 = 1.1031$$

$$x = 979 \times 1.1031 = 1{,}080$$

**Step 4 — total calendar year 2016 payments.** Difference the two diagonals:

| AY | Paid at 12/31/2016 | Paid at 12/31/2015 | CY 2016 paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 700 (48 mo.) | 647 (36 mo.) | 53 |
| 2014 | 1,080 (36 mo.) | 979 (24 mo.) | 101 |
| 2015 | 825 (24 mo.) | 400 (12 mo.) | 425 |
| 2016 | 450 (12 mo.) | — | 450 |
| **Total** | | | **1,029** |

$$\text{CY 2016 paid claims} = 53 + 101 + 425 + 450 = 1{,}029 \ (\$000) = \mathbf{\$1{,}029{,}000}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to apply both the paid and the reported claim development techniques, to know that IBNR is ultimate claims less *reported* claims, to solve for the missing paid amount using the \$50,000 IBNR difference, and then to convert the completed paid triangle into calendar year 2016 payments by differencing the two latest diagonals.

Common errors included computing IBNR as ultimate less *paid* claims, and treating the \$50,000 difference as applying to all accident years combined rather than to accident year 2016 alone.
