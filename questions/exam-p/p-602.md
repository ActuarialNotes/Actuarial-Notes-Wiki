A construction worker experiences up to five accidents this year.

i) The probability of no accidents is three times the probability of exactly one accident.
ii) The probability of exactly one accident is four times the probability of exactly two accidents.
iii) The probability of exactly two accidents is five times the probability of exactly three accidents.

The probability of three or fewer accidents is $0.95$.

Calculate the probability of two or fewer accidents.

- A) $0.71$
- B) $0.84$
- C) $0.91$
- D) $0.94$
- E) $0.96$

## Explanation

Let $p$ be the probability of exactly three accidents. Work the chain of ratios upward from there:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[2] &= 5p \\
P[1] &= 4(5p) = 20p \\
P[0] &= 3(20p) = 60p
\end{align*}
$$

The given cumulative probability fixes $p$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.95 &= P[0] + P[1] + P[2] + P[3] \\
     &= 60p + 20p + 5p + p = 86p \\
   p &= \frac{0.95}{86}
\end{align*}
$$

Dropping the $P[3]$ term leaves

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{2 or fewer}] &= 60p + 20p + 5p = 85p \\
                     &= 85\left(\frac{0.95}{86}\right) \\
                     &= \mathbf{0.9390}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 0.96, can be eliminated before any arithmetic: two or fewer accidents is a *subset* of three or fewer, so the answer cannot exceed the given 0.95. Equivalently, the answer is $0.95 - p = 0.95 - 0.01105$, which sits just below 0.95 because $P[3]$ is the smallest of the four probabilities.
