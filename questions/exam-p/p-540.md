An insurance company pays a benefit for a duration of one year, two years, or five years, depending upon the severity of the claim. Information about the distribution of claims in the past year by sex and duration of benefit is given in the following table:

| | 1 year | 2 years | 5 years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Male | 20% | 40% | 40% |
| Female | 10% | 70% | 20% |

Males accounted for 60% of all claims.

Calculate the probability that a claimant was female, given that this claimant received a five-year benefit.

- A) $1/5$
- B) $1/4$
- C) $1/3$
- D) $1/2$
- E) $3/4$

## Explanation

The table rows are **conditional on sex** — each row sums to 100%. The priors come from the separate statement that males are 60% of claims, so females are 40%.

$$
\begin{align*}
P[5 \mid M] = 0.4, \qquad &P[M] = 0.6 \\
P[5 \mid F] = 0.2, \qquad &P[F] = 0.4
\end{align*}
$$

Apply Bayes' theorem:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[F \mid 5] &= \frac{P[5 \mid F]P[F]}{P[5 \mid F]P[F] + P[5 \mid M]P[M]} \\
            &= \frac{(0.2)(0.4)}{(0.2)(0.4) + (0.4)(0.6)} \\
            &= \frac{0.08}{0.08 + 0.24} \\
            &= \frac{0.08}{0.32} \\
            &= \mathbf{\frac{1}{4}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 1/5, is $P[5 \mid F] = 0.2$ — the table entry, with the conditioning never reversed. The answer falls below the female prior of 0.40 because females receive five-year benefits only half as often as males do, so among five-year claimants they are underrepresented.
