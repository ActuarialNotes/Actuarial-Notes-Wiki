In a particular contract, there are two options available for each of two sections A and B. If $X$ is the number of options selected for section A and $Y$ is the number of options selected for section B, then the joint probability function of $X$ and $Y$ is

$$
p(x,y) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{x+y+2}{36}, & \text{for } x = 0, 1, 2 \text{ and } y = 0, 1, 2 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the variance of $X$.

- A) $0.56$
- B) $0.64$
- C) $0.83$
- D) $2.00$
- E) $3.36$

## Explanation

The variance of $X$ alone needs only the **marginal** distribution of $X$, obtained by summing the joint function over $y$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X=0] &= \frac{2+3+4}{36} = \frac{9}{36} = \frac{3}{12} \\
P[X=1] &= \frac{3+4+5}{36} = \frac{12}{36} = \frac{4}{12} \\
P[X=2] &= \frac{4+5+6}{36} = \frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}
\end{align*}
$$

These sum to 1, a useful check that the joint function was read correctly. Now take the two moments:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X] &= 0\left(\frac{3}{12}\right) + 1\left(\frac{4}{12}\right) + 2\left(\frac{5}{12}\right) \\
     &= \frac{14}{12}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= 0^2\left(\frac{3}{12}\right) + 1^2\left(\frac{4}{12}\right) + 2^2\left(\frac{5}{12}\right) \\
       &= \frac{24}{12} = 2
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X) &= E[X^2] - \bigl(E[X]\bigr)^2 \\
              &= 2 - \frac{196}{144} \\
              &= \frac{23}{36} \\
              &= \mathbf{0.639}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D) is $E[X^2] = 2$ with the $\bigl(E[X]\bigr)^2$ term never subtracted. Note that nothing about $Y$, or about whether $X$ and $Y$ are independent, enters the calculation — marginalizing first is what makes this a one-variable problem.
