$X$ and $Y$ are **not** independent. Which statement about $L = X + Y$ is still guaranteed to hold?

- A) $E[L] = E[X] + E[Y]$
- B) $\text{Var}(L) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y)$
- C) $\text{SD}(L) = \text{SD}(X) + \text{SD}(Y)$
- D) $L$ belongs to the same distribution family as $X$ and $Y$

<!-- rationale: 1: this drops the $2\,\text{Cov}(X,Y)$ term, which is nonzero under dependence · 2: standard deviations never add, even under independence · 3: only special families, such as independent normals, are closed under addition -->
