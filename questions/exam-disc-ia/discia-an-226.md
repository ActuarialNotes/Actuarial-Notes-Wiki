In a bar, a patron swings a bottle at another customer, who ducks, so the bottle touches no one. The bouncer then locks the patron in a storeroom for three hours, with no legal authority, until he agrees to pay his tab. Which intentional torts have been committed?

- A) Battery by the patron, and false imprisonment by the bouncer
- B) Assault by the patron, and false imprisonment by the bouncer
- C) Assault by the patron, and conversion by the bouncer
- D) Battery by the patron, and trespass to chattels by the bouncer

## Explanation

**Assault** is an act that intentionally puts someone in reasonable apprehension of an imminent battery, and no contact is needed: the customer ducked a swinging bottle. **False imprisonment** is intentionally confining someone within fixed boundaries without consent or legal authority, as the bouncer did.

- **A** and **D** call the swing a battery, which requires a harmful or offensive contact. The bottle touched no one.
- **C** and **D** treat locking up a person as a tort against property. Conversion and trespass to chattels are interferences with personal property, not with a person's freedom.
