A business manufactures light bulbs and sells them in boxes of 50. Let $p$ denote the probability that a light bulb is defective. The events that different light bulbs are defective are mutually independent.

Let $X$ denote the number of non-defective light bulbs in a box of 50. In addition, let $n$ be an integer such that $P[X \geq n] \geq 0.95$.

Determine which one of the following statements must be true.

- A) $\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{50}\left[\frac{50!}{k!(50-k)!}\right](1-p)^k p^{50-k} \geq 0.95$
- B) $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[\frac{50!}{k!(50-k)!}\right](1-p)^k p^{50-k} \geq 0.95$
- C) $\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+1}^{50}\left[\frac{50!}{k!(50-k)!}\right]p^k (1-p)^{50-k} \geq 0.95$
- D) $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[\frac{50!}{k!(50-k)!}\right]p^k (1-p)^{50-k} \geq 0.95$
- E) $\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{50}\left[\frac{50!}{k!(50-k)!}\right]p^k (1-p)^{50-k} \geq 0.95$

## Explanation

Each bulb is non-defective with probability $1-p$, independently of the others, so the number of non-defective bulbs in the box is binomial:

$$X \sim \text{Binomial}(50,\ 1-p)$$

The probability of at least $t^*$ non-defective bulbs in the box is therefore

$$P\left(X \geq t^*\right) = \sum_{t=t^*}^{50}\binom{50}{t}(1-p)^t\, p^{50-t}$$

and we want this probability to be at least $0.95$ for $t^* = n$. With $\binom{50}{k} = \dfrac{50!}{k!(50-k)!}$, that is exactly statement **A**.

The other statements are not implied: B sums the lower tail of $X$ rather than the upper, and C, D and E use $p^k(1-p)^{50-k}$, the probabilities for the number of *defective* bulbs, $50 - X$.
