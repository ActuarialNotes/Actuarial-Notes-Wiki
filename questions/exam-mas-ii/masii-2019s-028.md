You have two random variables $\phi$ and $\theta$ where

$$
\begin{align*}
\phi \mid \theta &\sim \text{Binomial}(n, \theta) \\
\theta &\sim \text{Beta}(a, b)
\end{align*}
$$

and $n$ is a fixed positive integer. Thus, the prior and the conditional distributions are given by

$$
\begin{align*}
\pi(\theta) &= \frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1} \\
f(\phi \mid \theta) &= \binom{n}{\phi}\theta^{\phi}(1-\theta)^{n-\phi}
\end{align*}
$$

where $\Gamma(\cdot)$ denotes the Gamma function and $\int \theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1}d\theta = \frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}$.

An actuary wants to use a Gibbs sampler to sample from the joint distribution of $\theta$ and $\phi$. She sets an initial value for $\phi$ and $\theta$ denoted by $(\phi_0, \theta_0)$ and subsequently in the $i^{th}$ step samples $\phi_i \sim f(\phi \mid \theta_{i-1})$ and $\theta_i \sim f(\theta \mid \phi_i)$.

Determine which conditional distribution for $\theta \mid \phi_i$ correctly implements the Gibbs sampler.

- A) $\text{Beta}(\phi_i,\, n - \phi_i)$
- B) $\text{Beta}(b + \phi_i,\, a + n - \phi_i)$
- C) $\text{Beta}(a + b + \phi_i,\, n - \phi_i)$
- D) $\text{Beta}(a + \phi_i,\, b + n - \phi_i)$
- E) None of the above

## Explanation

The Gibbs step for $\theta$ is just the beta-binomial conjugate update. Keeping only the factors in $\theta$:

$$
\begin{align*}
f(\theta \mid \phi_i) &\propto f(\phi_i \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta) \\
                      &\propto \theta^{\phi_i}(1-\theta)^{n-\phi_i} \cdot \theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1} \\
                      &= \theta^{a + \phi_i - 1}(1-\theta)^{b + n - \phi_i - 1}
\end{align*}
$$

That is the kernel of

$$
\text{Beta}\left(a + \phi_i,\ b + n - \phi_i\right),
$$

the familiar "add the successes to $a$, add the failures to $b$" rule.

The answer is **D**.
