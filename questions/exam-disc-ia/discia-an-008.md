A state's insurance regulation requires every insurer domiciled in the state to admit a type of asset that the NAIC *Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual* would nonadmit. How is this practice classified, and what must a domestic insurer that follows it disclose?

- A) A permitted practice; no disclosure, because the state requires it
- B) A permitted practice; its effect on net income and statutory surplus
- C) A prescribed practice; no disclosure, because it applies to all domestic insurers
- D) A prescribed practice; its effect on net income and statutory surplus

## Explanation

A prescribed practice is incorporated directly or by reference in a state's laws, regulations and general administrative rules applicable to all insurers domiciled or licensed there. A permitted practice is one an individual insurer requests and its domiciliary regulator approves. Under SSAP No. 1, an insurer using a practice that departs from NAIC SAP and affects surplus or risk-based capital must describe it, state that it differs, and give its monetary effect on net income and statutory surplus. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** and **B** — a rule that applies to every domestic insurer by regulation is prescribed, not permitted.
- **C** — being prescribed does not remove the disclosure. The NAIC basis is the standard against which every departure is measured and disclosed.
