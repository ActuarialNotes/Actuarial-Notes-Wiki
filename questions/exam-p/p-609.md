Losses under a theft insurance policy have cumulative distribution function

$$
F(x) = \begin{cases}
0, & \text{for } x < 0 \\
c(x^2 + x), & \text{for } 0 \le x \le 5 \\
1, & \text{for } x > 5,
\end{cases}
$$

where $c$ is a constant.

The policy has a deductible of 3.2 for each loss.

Calculate the probability that the insurer will pay something to the policyholder on a given loss.

- A) $0.296$
- B) $0.360$
- C) $0.448$
- D) $0.552$
- E) $0.704$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the loss. Since losses never exceed 5, the cdf must reach 1 there, which fixes $c$:

$$
1 = P[X \le 5] = F(5) = c(5^2 + 5) = 30c \implies c = \frac{1}{30}
$$

The insurer pays something exactly when the loss exceeds the deductible:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 3.2] &= 1 - F(3.2) \\
           &= 1 - \frac{1}{30}\left[(3.2)^2 + 3.2\right] \\
           &= 1 - \frac{13.44}{30} \\
           &= 1 - 0.448 \\
           &= \mathbf{0.552}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 0.448, is $F(3.2)$ itself — the probability that the loss falls *below* the deductible and the insurer pays nothing. It is the complement of what the question asks for, and the pair 0.448/0.552 is on the list precisely to catch the slip.
