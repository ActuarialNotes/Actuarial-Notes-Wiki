Each of an insurer's business units stays inside its own risk limit, set as if the units' results were independent. Yet the enterprise's total risk exceeds what its capital can support. What most likely explains this?

- A) The units' risks are positively correlated, so they diversify less than assumed
- B) The units' risks are negatively correlated, so they offset one another
- C) Each unit holds more capital than its own standalone need
- D) The units report their risks quarterly rather than monthly

## Explanation

Portfolio risk is not the simple sum of its parts, and it depends on the correlations. Limits set unit by unit assumed independence; if the units' results move together, the combined risk is larger than that assumption implies, so the enterprise can be short even though every unit is within its limit. Treating risk holistically means setting limits and treatments with those interactions in view. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Negative correlation would make the total *smaller* than independence implies: a natural hedge.
- **C** — Units holding extra capital would make the enterprise safer, not short of capital.
- **D** — Reporting frequency affects how quickly a problem is seen, not how large the aggregate risk is.
