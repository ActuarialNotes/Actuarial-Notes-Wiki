The time, in years, until replacement for a new telephone pole has probability density function

$$
f(t) = \begin{cases}
kt, & \text{for } 0 < t < 50 \\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $k$ is a constant.

Calculate the probability that a new telephone pole will be replaced within 25 years given that it is not replaced within 20 years.

- A) $0.09$
- B) $0.11$
- C) $0.16$
- D) $0.17$
- E) $0.84$

## Explanation

Normalize the density first:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= \int_0^{50} kt\,dt = \frac{k(50)^2}{2} = 1250k \\
k &= \frac{1}{1250}
\end{align*}
$$

The conditional probability restricts to poles surviving 20 years:

$$
P[T \leq 25 \mid T > 20] = \frac{P[20 < T < 25]}{P[T > 20]}
$$

Both pieces are integrals of $t/1250$, and $\int t\,dt = t^2/2$, so the constants cancel and only squares remain:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[20 < T < 25] &= \frac{25^2 - 20^2}{2500} = \frac{225}{2500} \\
P[T > 20]      &= \frac{50^2 - 20^2}{2500} = \frac{2100}{2500}
\end{align*}
$$

Dividing:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[T \leq 25 \mid T > 20] &= \frac{225}{2100} \\
                         &= \mathbf{0.10714}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.09, is the unconditional $P[20 < T < 25] = 225/2500$. Because this density rises with $t$, replacement becomes *more* likely as a pole ages — the opposite of the exponential's memorylessness — so the conditional probability exceeds the unconditional one.
