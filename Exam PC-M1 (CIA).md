# PC Module 1
**PC Module 1 – Principles and practice of insurance and Canadian automobile insurance** is one of the two modules of the property and casualty track of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Fellowship (FCIA) pathway.

> [!question]- About the module
>
> The primary audience for the FCIA modules is individuals who desire to be admitted as Fellows of the CIA.
>
> Individuals seeking continuing professional development credit can also register for the FCIA modules.
>
> Each FCIA candidate through Pathways 1 and 2 is required to complete both FCIA modules, while Pathway 3 candidates will have to complete the second module. It is anticipated that FCIA module candidates will have six months from the date of module purchase to complete each FCIA module. As a general guideline, candidates should expect to spend approximately 100 hours completing all activities included in a module.

## Learning Objectives

### Part A – Principles and practice of insurance

> [!example]- Introduction to risk and insurance
>
> 1. Describe [[Risk|risk]], including [[Insurable Risk|insurable risk]].
> 2. Provide examples of types of [[Insurable Risk|insurable risks]] and classes of property and casualty (P&C) insurance that respond to [[Insurable Risk|insurable risk]].
> 3. Describe and provide examples of perils and [[Hazard|hazards]].
> 4. Describe and provide examples of [[Proximate Cause|proximate cause]].
> 5. Explain the objectives of [[Risk Management|risk management]], including pre-loss and post-loss.
> 6. Explain the conflicts that can arise in the objectives of [[Risk Management|risk management]].
> 7. Identify and describe the five steps of the [[Risk Management Process|risk management process]]:
>    - a. Step 1 – Identifying and analyzing [[Exposure Base|exposures]].
>    - b. Step 2 – [[Formulating Option|Formulating options]].
>    - c. Step 3 – Selecting the best technique.
>    - d. Step 4 – Implementing the [[Risk Management Plan|risk management plan]].
>    - e. Step 5 – Monitoring results and modifying the plan.

> [!example]- Insurance categories and functions
>
> 1. Describe the basic insurance relationship of [[Pooling Fund|pooling funds]] to [[Pay Loss|pay losses]].
> 2. Explain the major functions of insurance:
>    - a. Spread of [[Risk|risk]].
>    - b. Aid to security.
>    - c. Aid to [[Credit|credit]].
>    - d. [[Loss Prevention|Loss prevention]].
>    - e. Source of [[Capital|capital]].
>    - f. Source of employment.
> 3. Identify and describe categories of [[Insurance|insurance]].

> [!example]- Regulatory framework
>
> 1. Describe the different roles of government in the regulation of [[Insurer|insurance companies]] including federal control and provincial and territorial control.
> 2. Describe the role of the Property and [[Casualty Insurance Compensation Corporation]] ([[PACICC]])
> 3. Describe the major laws and [[Regulations Impacting Insurance|regulations impacting insurance]] in Canada including:
>    - a. The [[Insurance Companies Act]].
>    - b. Provincial and [[Territorial Insurance Act|territorial insurance acts]].
>    - c. The [[Highway Traffic Act]].
>    - d. Regulations.
> 4. Explain the purpose of [[Statutory Condition|statutory conditions]] and [[Québec General Condition|Québec general conditions]] related to P&C insurance
> 5. Describe the role of [[Privacy Law|privacy laws]] in P&C insurance, including the [[Personal Information Protection]] and [[Electronic Documents Act]]

> [!example]- Insurance as a contract: The insurance policy

> 1. Describe the requirements of contracts in common law and in [[Québec]].

>    - a. [[Insurable Interest|Insurable interest]].
>    - b. Indemnity, including the principle of indemnity.

>    - a. Salvage.
>    - b. Subrogation.

>    - d. [[Excess Insurance|Excess insurance]].

>    - a. The policy.

>    - c. [[Endorsement|Endorsements]].
>    - d. [[Binders-cover Note|Binders/cover notes]].
> 2. Describe the major sections of an [[Insurance Policy|insurance policy]], including:
>    - a. [[Coverage Summary|Coverage summary]].

>    - c. [[Statutory Condition|Statutory conditions]] and [[Québec General Condition|Québec general conditions]].
>    - d. [[Policy Condition|Policy conditions]].
>    - e. [[Signature Clause|Signature clause]].
> 3. Describe the process and requirements for assigning, terminating, and renewing an [[Insurance Contract|insurance contract]].

> [!example]- Insurance companies
>
> 1. Differentiate the types of [[Insurance Provider|insurance providers]], including:
>    - a. [[Stock Company|Stock companies]].

>    - c. [[Government Insurer|Government insurers]].

>    - d. Subrogation.

>    - f. [[Coinsurance]].

### Part B – Automobile Insurance

>    - c. [[Municipal Bylaw|Municipal bylaws]].
> 2. Describe the origins of [[Personal Auto Policy|automobile insurance]], including [[Car-Use Exposures Requiring Coverage|car-use exposures requiring coverage]].

>    - a. [[Motor Vehicle Liability Insurance Card|Motor vehicle liability insurance cards]].
>    - b. Proof of [[Financial Responsibility|financial responsibility]].

> [!example]- Third-party liability coverage; direct compensation-property damage coverage
>
> 1. Differentiate between tort and [[Direct Compensation System|direct compensation systems]].
> 2. Describe [[Third-Party Liability Coverage|third-party liability coverage]] and exclusions under [[Third-Party Liability Coverage|third-party liability coverage]].

>    - a. Amounts exceeding the [[Policy Limit|policy limit]].
>    - b. Rented or [[Leased Automobile|leased automobiles]].
> 4. Describe the responsibilities of [[Insured Person|insured persons]].
> 5. Describe non-economic and [[Economic Loss|economic loss]] in the context of lawsuits for [[Automobile-Injury Claim|automobile-injury claims]].
> 6. Describe [[Absolute Liability|absolute liability]].
> 7. Describe [[Direct Compensation Property Damage|direct compensation-property damage]] (DCPD) coverage, including:
>    - a. [[Deductible|Deductibles]] under DCPD.
>    - b. Determination of fault.

> [!example]- Accident benefits
>

> 2. Describe the benefits under [[Accident Benefits Coverage|accident benefits coverage]], including:
>    - a. [[Income-replacement Benefit|Income-replacement benefit]].
>    - b. [[Non-earner Benefit|Non-earner benefit]].

>    - e. [[Payment]] of other [[Expenses|expenses]].
>    - f. Death and [[Funeral Benefit|funeral benefits]].
>    - g. [[Optional Benefit|Optional benefits]].
> 3. Identify and explain exclusions to [[Accident Benefits Coverage|accident benefits coverage]].
> 4. Describe the interaction of [[Accident Benefits Coverage|accident benefits coverage]] with other systems, including:
>    - a. Accidents arising out of province.
>    - b. [[Social-assistance Payment|Social-assistance payments]].
>    - c. [[Collateral Benefits|Collateral benefits]].
>    - d. [[Workers' Compensation|Workers’ compensation]].
> 5. Explain the situation with respect to [[Accident Benefits Fraud|accident benefits fraud]], including:
>    - a. Operation of [[Fraud Ring|fraud rings]].
>    - b. [[Planned Accident|Planned accidents]].
>    - c. Rehabilitation and [[Medical-Service Provider|medical-service providers]].

> [!example]- Uninsured automobile coverage

> 1. Describe the coverage for damage caused by uninsured or [[Unidentified Motorist|unidentified motorists]], including:
>    - a. What and who are covered.

>    - c. [[Claim|Claims]] procedures.
>    - d. Limitations and exclusions.
> 2. Explain coverage for damage caused by inadequately [[Insured Motorist|insured motorists]], including the [[Family Protection Endorsement|family protection endorsement]].
> 3. Identify and explain the operations of [[Motor Vehicle Accident Claims Fund|motor vehicle accident claims funds]].

Describe the functions fulfilled by other professionals in the litigation area, such as [[Life-Care Planner|life-care planners]], [[Vocational Expert|vocational experts]], accountants, economists, engineers, [[Personal Financial Planner|personal financial planners]], [[Business Valuator|business valuators]], appraisers and [[Structured Settlement Broker|structured settlement brokers]], and how these [[Professionals Complement|professionals complement]] and compete with the function of [[Actuary|actuaries]] in actuarial evidence.
