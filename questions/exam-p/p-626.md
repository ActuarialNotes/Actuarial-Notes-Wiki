An insurance company sells two types of policies. The annual number of claims for Type 1 policies follows a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda_1$ and the annual number of claims for Type 2 policies follows a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda_2$. The probability that a Type 1 policy has no claims in one year is one-half of the probability that a Type 2 policy has no claims in one year.

Let $V_n$ be the variance in the annual number of claims for a Type $n$ policy.

Calculate $V_1 - V_2$.

- A) $0.693$
- B) $0.724$
- C) $0.766$
- D) $0.813$
- E) $0.832$

## Explanation

For a Poisson distribution the variance equals the mean, so $V_i = \lambda_i$ and the question is really asking for $\lambda_1 - \lambda_2$.

The condition on the "no claims" probabilities is

$$
\frac{e^{-\lambda_1}\lambda_1^0}{0!} = 0.5\,\frac{e^{-\lambda_2}\lambda_2^0}{0!}
$$

which simplifies to

$$
\begin{align*}
2e^{-\lambda_1} &= e^{-\lambda_2} \\
2 &= e^{-(\lambda_2 - \lambda_1)} \\
\lambda_1 - \lambda_2 &= \ln 2 = \mathbf{0.6931}
\end{align*}
$$

Note what the data do and do not pin down: the condition fixes only the *difference* $\lambda_1 - \lambda_2$, never the individual means. Any pair with $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 + \ln 2$ satisfies it. That is why the question asks for a difference of variances — a difference of standard deviations, $\sqrt{\lambda_1} - \sqrt{\lambda_2}$, would not be determined at all.
