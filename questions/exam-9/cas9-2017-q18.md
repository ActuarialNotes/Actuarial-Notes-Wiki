## Part a (0.25 points)

Briefly discuss the meaning of the term "risk capital."

### Explanation
Risk capital is the capital provided by equity holder to the company to cover the risk that liability may exceed the funds provided by policyholder in forms of loss reserves and UPR.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand risk capital, allocation methodologies, and implications of capital allocation.

Candidates were expected to know the definition of risk capital.

A common error was giving the definition of other types of capital, such as economic capital

## Part b (2.25 points)

Determine which of the following results in a larger allocation of capital to Line B:

- i. Allocating the market value of equity using the Proportional Allocation based on 99.5% VaR Method, or
- ii. Allocating the risk capital using the Co-Measures Approach based on 99.5% Co-CTE.

### Explanation
99.5% VaR -> 6th worst observation of 1000

- Reserve VaR 99.5% = 850
- Line A VaR 99.5% = 1500
- Line B VaR 99.5% = 900

$$\text{Line B allocation} = [(900) / (850+1500+900)] \times 2900 = 803.077$$

99.5% Co-CTE -> average 5 worst observations where worst based on overall distribution:

$$\text{Reserve Co-CTE} = (800+500+500+100+300)/5 = 480 \quad \text{(as printed; the second-worst reserve value is 700)}$$

$$\text{Line A Co-CTE} = (1600+1500+1300+1400+1300)/5 = 1420$$

$$\text{Line B Co-CTE} = (600+700+900+1100+900)/5 = 840$$

Line B allocation = 840 <- since using 99.5% CTE as risk capital (instead of allocation market value of equity)

Line B allocation higher under Co-CTE in ii

Alternatively:

i.) capital for B = (900)/(900+1500+850) = 27.7%

ii.) capital for B = [(600+700+900+1100+900)/(5)] / [(3000+2900+...+2500)/(5)] = 30%

Co-Measures approach allocates more to B

Alternatively:

Var(99.5) is the 995 scenario

Var(99.5) = 850 reserve, 1500 A, 900 B = 3250 total

i) Line B = (900/3250)x(2900) = \$803.077

Co-CTE (99.5):

- = (800+500+500+100+300)/5 = 480 reserve (as printed; the second-worst reserve value is 700)
- = (1600+1500+1300+1400+1300)/5 = 1420 A
- = (600+700+900+1100+900)/5 = 840 B
- 2740

ii) Line B = (840/2740)x(2900) = \$889.051

So ii) allocates more to B

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know how to use the VaR and Co-CTE methods to find the correct allocated capital.

Common errors include:

- Using the wrong capital bases
- Selecting the wrong observations used for the methods

## Part c (0.5 points)

Explain one implication of allocating more capital to a line of business.

### Explanation
When measuring profitability with measures like RAROC, the line with more capital must generate more income to meet the target return.

Alternatively:

You will depress its RAROC which could influence decision-making (downsize, exit LOB), if it is below the cost of capital.

Alternatively:

If allocate more capital -> reduce the return on capital (RAROC) -> required to generate more income to be equally attractive as another line with the same cost of capital, but for which we allocate less capital.

Alternatively:

Allocating more capital means it is riskier and needs to produce higher returns in order to have a RAROC over the hurdle rate and justify its business.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know implications of allocating more capital to a given line. There were no common errors to this part as the question was straight from the text and several approaches were accepted.
