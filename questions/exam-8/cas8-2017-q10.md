An actuary is evaluating a risk to determine the appropriateness of applying any schedule credits or debits. In order to reduce expenses the insured removed a safety program 2 years ago and in the next month will be reducing its staff.

| Risk Characteristic | Description | Range of Modifications: Credit | Range of Modifications: Debit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooperation | Safety Program | 10% | 10% |
| Employees | Selection, training, supervision, experience | 5% | 5% |

Recommend and defend an appropriate schedule modification.

### Explanation
**Cooperation (safety program).** The experience used skips the prior year (the second year of no safety program) and uses the 3 years prior to that. So the safety program was in place for 2 of the 3 years in the experience, but not going forward. Apply a debit of $\tfrac{2}{3} \times 10\%$ (2/3 of the maximum) to account for the increase in future losses over historical.

**Employees.** The insured will be reducing staff next month, so this won't be in the experience at all. A reduction in staff does not mean the remaining staff is any less experienced, needs more training or more supervision. Hence there is no need (or ability) to schedule rate based solely on fewer employees: 0%.

Alternatively, assuming the risk is also experience rated using the standard 3 years of data with a lag:

- **Cooperation** — the safety program elimination should partially show in the claims experience, but not fully. Recommend a +5% debit for the piece not yet in the data.
- **Employees** — an upcoming change, so not in the data. This could lead to understaffing or improper supervision. Recommend the full 5% debit in this category.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of experience rating and its interaction with schedule rating.  To receive full credit, candidates needed to be able to recommend a specific debit/credit based upon the information given.
Candidates were expected to understand that the safety program removal was only partially reflected in experience and choose a specific debit for the modification.  They were also expected to realize that the mix of employees might change and this was not reflected in past experience, explain how that might occur, and give an appropriate debit or credit amount to go with the explanation.

Note that some candidates assumed this was an ISO G/L policy.  Credit was given accordingly for reasonable responses using this assumption.
Common mistakes included:

- Assuming that the experience period fully held experience of no safety program
- Not specifically mentioning that the experience period was the reason for the debit on the safety program
- Assuming that the employer received a credit in the employees characteristic due to exposure
- Confusing the credit/debit label or not giving a specific credit/debit at all
