OSFI's Minimum Capital Test (MCT) guideline sets out the capital framework for federally regulated P&C insurers that are not mortgage insurers. It outlines a risk-based formula for target and minimum capital required, defines the capital available to meet the minimum standard, and sets the Branch Adequacy of Assets Test (BAAT) for foreign companies operating in Canada on a branch basis. Its standards are the framework within which the Superintendent assesses whether an insurer maintains adequate capital under the Insurance Companies Act. This is the 2024 guideline (Guideline A), effective January 1, 2024.

## 1 Overview and General Requirements
- 1.1 Overview
    - 1.1.1 Minimum and target capital requirements under the MCT
        - Capital requirements are set directly at a target confidence level of 99% of the expected shortfall (CTE 99%) over a one-year horizon, including a terminal provision; the requirements are then divided by 1.5 to derive the minimum capital required, and the MCT ratio is capital available over minimum capital required.
        > $$\text{MCT ratio} = \frac{\text{Capital available}}{\text{Minimum capital required}}$$
    - 1.1.2 Risk-based capital adequacy
        - Minimum capital required is calculated on a consolidated basis as the sum of the target-level requirements for [[Insurance Risk Margin|insurance]], [[Market Risk Margin|market]], [[Credit Risk Margin|credit]] and [[Operational Risk Margin|operational]] risk, less the [[Diversification Credit|diversification credit]], divided by 1.5.
    - 1.1.3 Scope of consolidation
    - 1.1.4 Foreign companies
    - 1.1.5 Interpretation of results
        - The [[MCT]] is a standardized measure of capital adequacy, one of several indicators OSFI uses to assess an insurer's financial condition, and should not be used in isolation for ranking and rating insurers.
- 1.2 General Requirements
    - 1.2.1 MCT supervisory capital ratio for federally regulated insurers
        - Insurers must maintain an MCT ratio of at least 100%; OSFI's industry-wide [[Supervisory Target Capital Ratio|supervisory target]] of 150% provides a cushion above the minimum and facilitates early intervention.
        - Each insurer sets an [[Internal Target Capital Ratio|internal target]] under Guideline A-4 and maintains capital above it, informing OSFI immediately if it anticipates falling below; the Superintendent may set an alternative supervisory target for an individual insurer.
    - 1.2.2 Audit requirement

    - 1.2.4 Intra-Group Pooling Arrangements

## 2 Definition of Capital Available
- 2.1 Summary of Capital Components
    - The four primary considerations for defining [[Capital Available|capital available]] are availability, permanence, absence of encumbrances and mandatory servicing costs, and subordination; capital available is the sum of category A (common equity), category B and category C capital.
    - 2.1.1 Category A capital (i.e. common equity)
        - Includes qualifying common shares and contributed surplus, retained earnings, earthquake, nuclear and general contingency reserves, accumulated other comprehensive income, and the residual interest of owner-policyholders of mutual entities.
    - 2.1.2 Category B capital
    - 2.1.3 Category C capital
    - 2.1.4 Non-controlling interests
- 2.2 Capital Composition Limits
    - Category B and C instruments together may not exceed 40% of total capital available, and category C 7%, both excluding accumulated other comprehensive income; excess amounts are excluded from capital available.
- 2.3 Regulatory Adjustments to Capital Available
    - 2.3.1 Deductions
        - Deductions include interests in, and loans reported as equity to, non-qualifying subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures in which the company holds more than 10%; unregistered reinsurance receivables and recoverables not covered by payables or acceptable collateral; uncollateralized self-insured retentions where OSFI requires collateral; the earthquake premium reserve not used as a financial resource; and assets for [[Insurance Acquisition Cash Flows|insurance acquisition cash flows]].
    - 2.3.2 Additions
        - The net-of-reinsurance [[Contractual Service Margin|contractual service margin]] for title insurance contracts is added to capital available.
    - 2.3.3 Adjustments
- 2.4 Capital Treatment of Interests in and Loans to Subsidiaries, Associates and Joint Ventures
    - 2.4.1 Consolidated subsidiaries (e.g. property and casualty insurance and ancillary businesses such as agencies, brokerages and mutual funds)
    - 2.4.2 Non-qualifying subsidiaries
    - 2.4.3 Associates
    - 2.4.4 Joint ventures in which a company holds less than or equal to 10% ownership interest

## 4 Insurance Risk
- [[Insurance Risk|Insurance risk]] is divided into four parts: the [[Liability for Incurred Claims|liability for incurred claims]] (reserving risk), unexpired coverage (underwriting risk, including catastrophe risk other than earthquakes and nuclear), unregistered reinsurance, and earthquake and nuclear catastrophes.
- 4.1 Diversification Credit within Insurance Risk
    - The risk factors for each class of insurance contain an implicit diversification credit, assuming a well-diversified portfolio.
- 4.2 Margins for Liability for Incurred Claims and Unexpired Coverage
    - 4.2.1 Margin for liability for incurred claims
        - Calculated by class of insurance from the liability for incurred claims on insurance contracts issued less the asset for incurred claims on reinsurance contracts held, both excluding the [[Risk Adjustment for Non-Financial Risk|risk adjustment for non-financial risk]].
    - 4.2.2 Margin for unexpired coverage
        - Calculated by class of insurance by applying the risk factors to the greater of net unexpired coverage and 30% of net premiums received in the past 12 months; unexpired coverage for insurance contracts issued is determined by one of two methods, for groups measured under the [[General Measurement Model|general measurement model]] or the [[Premium Allocation Approach|premium allocation approach]].
    - 4.2.3 Risk factors for warranty business
- 4.3 Risk Mitigation and Risk Transfer - Reinsurance
    - 4.3.1 General
        - Credit for reinsurance with a [[Registered Reinsurance|registered]] or [[Unregistered Reinsurance|unregistered]] reinsurer requires the arrangement to comply with Guideline B-3 Sound Reinsurance Practices and Procedures.
    - 4.3.2 Registered reinsurers
    - 4.3.3 Unregistered reinsurers
        - Amounts receivable and recoverable from an unregistered reinsurer are deducted from capital available to the extent they are not covered by premiums payable to it or acceptable collateral (non-owned deposits under a reinsurance security agreement, other acceptable non-owned deposits, funds held and letters of credit), and a margin is required for the reinsurance ceded.
- 4.4 Self-Insured Retention
    - OSFI must be satisfied that [[Self-Insured Retention|self-insured retentions]] are collectable before they are admitted, and may require collateral.
- 4.5 Earthquake and Nuclear Catastrophes
    - 4.5.1 Earthquake risk exposure
        - Earthquake reserves, the earthquake premium reserve (EPR) plus the earthquake reserve component (ERC), are added to total capital required; OSFI expects a test of financial preparedness for a 500-year return period country-wide event, measured by the [[Probable Maximum Loss|PML]].
        > $$\text{Earthquake reserves} = (\text{EPR} + \text{ERC}) \times 1.25$$
        > $$\text{ERC} = \text{Country-wide PML}_{500} - \text{capital and surplus} - \text{reinsurance coverage} - \text{capital market financing} - \text{EPR} \geq 0$$
        > $$\text{Country-wide PML}_{500} = \left(\text{East Canada PML}_{500}^{\,1.5} + \text{West Canada PML}_{500}^{\,1.5}\right)^{1/1.5}$$
        - The financial resources are capital and surplus (up to 10%), the earthquake premium reserve, reinsurance coverage and capital market financing (with OSFI's prior approval); Guideline B-9 sets the expectations for earthquake exposure risk management.
    - 4.5.2 Nuclear reserve
        - Insurers issuing nuclear risk policies hold an additional provision of premiums received less premiums paid, net of commissions, multiplied by 1.25, for twenty years.

## 7 Operational Risk
- [[Operational Risk|Operational risk]] is the risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems or from external events; it includes legal risk but excludes strategic and reputation risk.
- 7.1 Operational Risk Formula
    - The two risk drivers are capital required and premiums, subject to a cap; $CR_0$ is total capital required before the operational risk margin and diversification credit, and $P_\Delta$ is premium growth above a 20% threshold.
    > $$\text{Operational risk margin} = \min\left\{30\%\,CR_0,\ \left(8.50\%\,CR_0 + 2.50\%\,P_d + 1.75\%\,P_a + 2.50\%\,P_r + 2.50\%\,P_\Delta\right) + \max\left(0.75\%\,P_{aig},\ 0.75\%\,P_{rig}\right)\right\}$$
- 7.2 Components of Operational Risk Margin
    - 7.2.1 Capital required
    - 7.2.2 Premium volume
    - 7.2.3 Year-over-year premium growth beyond a threshold
    - 7.2.4 Cap on operational risk margin

## 8 Diversification Credit
- Because losses across risk categories are not perfectly correlated, an explicit credit for diversification is allowed between the sum of the credit and market risk requirements ($A$) and the insurance risk requirement ($I$), with a [[Correlation|correlation]] factor $R$ of 50%.
- 8.1 Risk Aggregation and Diversification Credit
    > $$\text{Diversification credit} = A + I - \sqrt{A^2 + I^2 + 2 \times R \times A \times I}$$
