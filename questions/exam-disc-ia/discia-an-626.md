A fire caused by a party's negligence damages a landlord's building, and the landlord's insurer pays the claim under a commercial property coverage part. In which situation has the landlord **not** impaired its insurer's right of recovery?

- A) After the fire, it signs a written waiver releasing its tenant, whose employee caused the fire
- B) After the fire, it signs a written waiver releasing an outside contractor who caused the fire
- C) After the fire, it orally agrees not to pursue a neighboring business that caused the fire
- D) After the fire, it sells its claim against the supplier that caused the fire to a third party

## Explanation

The transfer of rights of recovery condition passes the insured's rights against others to the insurer to the extent of its payment and requires the insured to do nothing after the loss to impair them. The insured may waive its rights in writing before a loss against anyone, but after a loss only against someone insured by the policy, a business it owns or controls or that owns or controls it, or **its tenant**. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — An outside contractor is none of the parties against whom a post-loss waiver is permitted.
- **C** — The neighbor is not a permitted party either, and the permission is for a written waiver.
- **D** — Selling the claim transfers away rights that, after payment, belong to the insurer, which impairs them.
