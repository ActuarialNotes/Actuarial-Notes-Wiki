Under an insurance policy, no benefit is paid on 75% of the claims filed. The benefits paid on the remaining claims are exponentially distributed with mean 8.

Calculate the variance of the benefit for a randomly selected claim under this policy.

- A) $2$
- B) $14$
- C) $16$
- D) $28$
- E) $32$

## Explanation

The benefit $X$ is a **mixture**: with probability 0.75 it is exactly 0, and with probability 0.25 it is exponential with mean 8. Let $N$ be the indicator of a paying claim and use the law of total variance:

$$
\text{Var}(X) = \text{Var}\bigl(E[X \mid N]\bigr) + E\bigl[\text{Var}(X \mid N)\bigr]
$$

The two conditional summaries are

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X \mid N=0] &= 0, & E[X \mid N=1] &= 8 \\
\text{Var}(X \mid N=0) &= 0, & \text{Var}(X \mid N=1) &= 8^2 = 64
\end{align*}
$$

using that an exponential distribution has variance equal to the square of its mean.

**Between-group term.** $E[X \mid N]$ takes the value 8 with probability 0.25 and 0 otherwise, so it is 8 times a Bernoulli variable. A Bernoulli variable has variance $p(1-p)$, and scaling by 8 multiplies the variance by $8^2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}\bigl(E[X \mid N]\bigr) &= 8^2(0.25)(0.75) \\
                                  &= 12
\end{align*}
$$

**Within-group term.**

$$
\begin{align*}
E\bigl[\text{Var}(X \mid N)\bigr] &= 0(0.75) + 64(0.25) \\
                                  &= 16
\end{align*}
$$

Adding them:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X) &= 12 + 16 \\
              &= \mathbf{28}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 16, is the within-group term alone — the answer you get by averaging the conditional variances and forgetting that the *mean* benefit also varies between paying and non-paying claims. Both halves are needed. As a check, $E[X] = 2$ and $E[X^2] = 0.25(2)(64) = 32$, so $\text{Var}(X) = 32 - 4 = 28$ directly.
