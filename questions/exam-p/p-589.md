In a one-year period, the number of fires that occur in buildings insured by ABC insurance is Poisson distributed with mean 12. The resulting losses are independent random variables that are also independent of the number of losses, each having density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{8-x}{32}, & \text{for } 0 < x < 8 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

The payment for any loss is equal to the amount of the loss in excess of a deductible of 2.

Calculate the expected total payment that ABC must make for losses due to fire that occur in a one-year period.

- A) $8.0$
- B) $13.5$
- C) $16.5$
- D) $30.0$
- E) $32.0$

## Explanation

The total payment is a random sum: a Poisson number of fires, each producing an independent payment. Its expectation factors into the expected count times the expected payment per loss:

$$
E[\text{total}] = E[N]\,E[(X-2)_+]
$$

**Expected payment per loss.** Losses below 2 pay nothing, so integrate only over $[2,8]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[(X-2)_+] &= \int_2^8 (x-2)\frac{8-x}{32}\,dx \\
           &= \frac{1}{32}\int_2^8 \left(-x^2 + 10x - 16\right)dx \\
           &= \frac{1}{32}\left(-\frac{x^3}{3} + 5x^2 - 16x\right)\Bigg|_2^8 \\
           &= 1.125
\end{align*}
$$

**Multiply by the expected number of fires:**

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\text{total}] &= 12(1.125) \\
                &= \mathbf{13.5}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 32.0, is $12E[X]$ with the deductible ignored — the unlimited loss has mean $8/3$, giving 32. The deductible of 2 removes more than half the expected loss here, because the density is largest near zero where most losses fall below the deductible entirely.
