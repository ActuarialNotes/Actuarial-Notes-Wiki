Given the following information:

- Cumulative reported losses at 24 months for accident year 2015 are \$9,000
- The following reported development factors were derived:

| Age | Loss Development Factor | Cumulative Development Factor | % Reported |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 2.000 | 3.000 | 33.3% |
| 24 | 1.364 | 1.500 | 66.7% |
| 36 | 1.073 | 1.100 | 90.9% |
| 48 | 1.025 | 1.025 | 97.6% |
| 60 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 100.0% |

- Incremental losses, $C_{ij}$, follow an over-dispersed Poisson distribution with mean $x_i y_i$ and variance $\varphi x_i y_i$
- The variable $x_i$ represents the expected ultimate losses for accident year $i$.
- The variable $y_j$ represents the proportion of ultimate losses that emerge in development year $j$.
- The prior distribution for $x_i$ is gamma with mean $\alpha_i / \beta_i$ and variance $\alpha_i / \beta_i^2$
- The dispersion parameter, $\varphi$, for the over-dispersed Poisson distribution is 9.125
- The accident year 2015 estimates for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are 100 and 0.01234 respectively.
- The mean of $C_{ij}$ for this Bayesian model is:

$$Z_{ij}(\lambda_j - 1)D_{i,j-1} + (1 - Z_{ij})(\lambda_j - 1)M_i \frac{1}{\lambda_j \lambda_{j+1} \cdots \lambda_n}, \quad \text{where } Z_{ij} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{j-1} y_k}{\beta_i \varphi + \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} y_k}$$

- $\lambda_j$ is the incremental chain ladder loss development factor for development year $j$.
- $D_{ij}$ is the cumulative losses for accident year $i$ as of development year $j$.

## Part a (2 points)

Calculate the incremental losses for accident year 2015 expected to emerge between 24 and 48 months of development using the model.

### Explanation
$M = \alpha/\beta = 8104$.

**24 to 36 months:** $\lambda_j = 1.364$, $D_{i,j-1} = 9000$, % emerged $= 0.909 - 0.667 = 0.242$.

$$Z_{ij} = \frac{0.667}{0.01234 \times 9.125 + 0.667} = 0.856$$

$$E[C_{ij}] = 0.856 \times 9000 \times 0.364 + (1 - 0.856) \times 8104 \times 0.242 = 3087$$

**36 to 48 months:** $\lambda_j = 1.073$, $D_{i,j-1} = 12087$, % emerged $= 0.067$.

$$Z_{ij} = \frac{0.909}{0.01234 \times 9.125 + 0.909} = 0.890$$

$$E[C_{ij}] = 0.89 \times 12087 \times 0.073 + (1 - 0.89) \times 8104 \times 0.067 = 845$$

Total 24–48 months: $3087 + 845 = 3932$.

Alternatively:

Credibility-weight the chain ladder and prior estimates of the 24–48 month emergence directly:

$$E(\text{Incr Loss CL}) = 9000 \times 1.5 \times (0.976 - 0.667) = 4171.5$$

$$E(\text{Incr Loss prior}) = \frac{100}{0.01234} \times (0.976 - 0.667) = 2504$$

$$Z_{ij} = \frac{\%\text{ reported}}{\varphi\beta + \%\text{ reported}} = \frac{0.667}{9.125 \times 0.01234 + 0.667} = 0.856$$

$$\text{Expected incremental} = 0.856 \times 4171.5 + (1 - 0.856) \times 2504 = 3931.4$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to obtain estimates for IBNR in 2017 and 2018
Common errors include:

- Omitting the 36 to 48 month development
- Using chain ladder estimate for C2015,36  in place of credibility weighted IBNR from the model
- Applying incorrect Loss Development factors (Chain Ladder or B-F)
- Applying an incorrect percent reported (in the credibility calculation and in the B-F estimate)

## Part b (0.5 points)

Identify and briefly describe what parameter in the model would have to change in order to produce IBNR estimates closer to chain ladder indications.

### Explanation
β would have to change. β controls the variance for the prior distribution. If we want an estimate closer to chain ladder, we want a larger variance in the prior distribution so we want a smaller β.

Alternatively:

If β is reduced, it means Z gets larger and more weight is put on the CL indications in the Bayesian model formula.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to show understanding of the model parameters which changed the weights between the chain ladder method and the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method of estimating
IBNR.

Common errors include:

- Indicating the wrong direction for β
- Providing direction only for dispersion parameter (ϕ) - text provides examples of actuary selecting β and refers to ϕ as a "nuisance parameter" that is solved using maximum likelihood estimates.
