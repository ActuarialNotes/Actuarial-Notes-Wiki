OSFI's March 2023 guideline on **[[Climate Risk|climate risk management]]** for federally regulated financial institutions. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objectives C4 and C5.

## How climate risk becomes financial risk

- **Physical risks** — the financial risks from a changing climate, acute and chronic, including morbidity and mortality impacts.
- **Transition risks** — the financial risks from the adjustment toward a low-greenhouse-gas economy: government policy, legislation and regulation limiting emissions, technological change, and shifts in market and customer sentiment.
- **Liability risks** follow from both — climate-related claims under liability policies, and litigation or direct action against institutions for failing to manage their own climate risk.

Climate risks **drive** financial risks — credit, market, insurance and liquidity — and also strategic, operational and reputational risk. In severe cases they can threaten the **long-term viability of the institution's business model**. That framing is the point: climate is not a separate risk category on the balance sheet, it is a driver of the categories already there.

## The three expected outcomes (A3)

1. The institution **understands and mitigates** the potential impacts of climate-related risks on its business model and strategy.
2. It has **appropriate governance and risk management practices** to manage the risks it has identified.
3. It remains **financially resilient through severe yet plausible climate risk scenarios**, and **operationally resilient** through disruption caused by climate-related disasters.

Note the wording of the third: *severe yet plausible* is the same standard [[CIA FCT 1|FCT]] applies to adverse scenarios, and *operational* resilience is a separate test from financial resilience — a flooded claims centre during a flood event.

## Scope and proportionality

Applies to all FRFIs except foreign bank branches. There is **no one-size-fits-all approach**: the guideline is to be read and implemented from a risk-based perspective proportionate to the institution's size, nature, scope, complexity and risk profile. Chapters 1 and 2 are mutually reinforcing — disclosure (Chapter 2) is expected to improve the governance and risk management it reports on (Chapter 1).
