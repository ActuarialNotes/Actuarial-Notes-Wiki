An automobile insurance company specializes in insuring high-risk drivers.

The number of accidents for a randomly selected high-risk driver in year 1 is modeled by a random variable $X$. The number of accidents for the same driver in year 2 is modeled by a random variable $Y$.

The probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$ is

$$
p(x,y) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{(4-x)(3-y)}{60}, & \text{for } x = 0,1,2,3 \text{ and } y = 0,1,2 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate $\text{Var}(Y)$.

- A) $0.56$
- B) $0.67$
- C) $0.75$
- D) $1.00$
- E) $1.44$

## Explanation

Sum the joint function over $x$ to get the marginal distribution of $Y$. The $x$ factors contribute $4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10$ regardless of $y$:

$$
\begin{align*}
p_Y(0) &= \frac{(12 + 9 + 6 + 3)}{60} = \frac{30}{60} \\
p_Y(1) &= \frac{(8 + 6 + 4 + 2)}{60} = \frac{20}{60} \\
p_Y(2) &= \frac{(4 + 3 + 2 + 1)}{60} = \frac{10}{60}
\end{align*}
$$

These sum to 1, as required. Now the moments:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y] &= 0\left(\tfrac{3}{6}\right) + 1\left(\tfrac{2}{6}\right) + 2\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right) \\
     &= \frac{4}{6}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y^2] &= 0^2\left(\tfrac{3}{6}\right) + 1^2\left(\tfrac{2}{6}\right) + 2^2\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right) \\
       &= \frac{6}{6} = 1
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(Y) &= 1 - \left(\frac{4}{6}\right)^2 \\
              &= \frac{36 - 16}{36} = \frac{20}{36} \\
              &= \mathbf{0.56}
\end{align*}
$$

Because the joint function **factors** as a function of $x$ times a function of $y$, $X$ and $Y$ are independent here — so the marginal of $Y$ is just $(3-y)$ renormalized, and the $x$ sum of 10 divides out. Choice (D), 1.00, is $E[Y^2]$ with the $\bigl(E[Y]\bigr)^2$ term never subtracted; choice (B) is $E[Y] = 2/3$.
