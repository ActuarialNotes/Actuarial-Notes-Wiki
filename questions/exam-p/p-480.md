In a group of 144 car insurance policyholders, each policyholder has no accidents this year with probability 0.80, one accident with probability 0.16, and two accidents with probability 0.04.

The numbers of accidents this year for different policyholders are mutually independent.

Calculate the variance of the total number of accidents this year for this group of policyholders.

- A) $3.15$
- B) $34.56$
- C) $37.79$
- D) $46.08$
- E) $54.37$

## Explanation

Find one policyholder's variance first, then scale.

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X]   &= 0(0.80) + 1(0.16) + 2(0.04) = 0.24 \\
E[X^2] &= 0(0.80) + 1(0.16) + 4(0.04) = 0.32
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X) &= 0.32 - (0.24)^2 \\
              &= 0.32 - 0.0576 \\
              &= 0.2624
\end{align*}
$$

The policyholders are independent, so the variance of the total is the sum of 144 identical variances:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var(total)} &= 144(0.2624) \\
                  &= \mathbf{37.79}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 46.08, is $144(0.32) = 144E[X^2]$ with the squared mean never subtracted. Choice (B), 34.56, is $144(0.24)$, the *mean* of the total mistaken for its variance — a plausible slip here only because the per-policy mean and variance happen to be close.
