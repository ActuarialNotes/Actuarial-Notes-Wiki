For a randomly selected claim, let $Y$ be the loss and $X$ the insurer's payment, both in millions. The insurer never pays more than the loss, and $X$ and $Y$ have joint density function

$$
f(x, y) = \begin{cases}
6x, & 0 < x < y < 1 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the probability that the payment exceeds 0.3, given that the loss is 0.6. Give your answer as a decimal to two decimal places.

## Explanation

Conditioning on $Y = 0.6$ needs the conditional density of $X$, which is the joint density divided by the marginal density of $Y$. The marginal integrates $x$ out over the interval from 0 to $y$:

$$
\begin{align*}
f_Y(y) &= \int_0^{y} 6x\,dx \\
       &= 3y^2, \qquad 0 < y < 1
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
f_{X \mid Y}(x \mid 0.6) &= \frac{6x}{3(0.6)^2} \\
                         &= \frac{2x}{0.36}, \qquad 0 < x < 0.6
\end{align*}
$$

Given $Y = 0.6$, the payment lies in $(0, 0.6)$, so the upper limit is 0.6, not 1:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(X > 0.3 \mid Y = 0.6) &= \int_{0.3}^{0.6} \frac{2x}{0.36}\,dx \\
                        &= \frac{0.36 - 0.09}{0.36} \\
                        &= \mathbf{0.75}
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **0.75**.

**Common wrong entries**

- **0.25** is the complement, $P(X \le 0.3 \mid Y = 0.6)$.
- **0.50** treats the conditional distribution of $X$ as uniform on $(0, 0.6)$. The joint density $f(x, y) = 6x$ grows with $x$, so larger payments are more likely.
- **0.78** is the unconditional $P(X > 0.3) = \int_{0.3}^{1} 6x(1 - x)\,dx = 0.784$, from the marginal of $X$; it ignores the information that the loss is 0.6.
- **0.81** integrates the joint density $f(x, y) = 6x$ from 0.3 to 0.6 without dividing by $f_Y(0.6) = 1.08$, so the "conditional" density doesn't integrate to 1.
