A team of data scientists is analyzing renewal rates on personal auto policies for insurance company XYZ. Using historical renewal data and a binomial distribution, they have calculated the posterior distribution displayed below:

![Posterior density of the renewal rate over 0 to 1. The curve is a single smooth hump, near zero below 0.3, rising to a peak at about 0.67, and falling back to near zero by 1.0](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q17-renewal-posterior.png)

Identify which of the following ranges is most likely the 30% highest posterior density interval for renewal rate.

- A) [0.00, 0.30]
- B) [0.15, 0.85]
- C) [0.40, 0.80]
- D) [0.60, 0.70]
- E) [0.70, 1.00]

## Explanation

A highest posterior density interval is the **narrowest** interval carrying the stated probability, and for a single-peaked density it is the band straddling the mode. Two properties decide the question: the interval must be centred near the peak, and it must be narrow, because it carries only 30% of the mass.

Reading the plot, the density peaks at roughly 0.67.

- **(A)** lies almost entirely in the left tail where the density is near zero — it holds nowhere near 30% of the mass.
- **(E)** starts at the peak and runs to 1.0, so it is off-centre and drags in the thin right tail.
- **(B)** spans 0.15 to 0.85 and **(C)** spans 0.40 to 0.80; both are far too wide, capturing well over 30% of the mass. (B) in particular covers nearly the whole distribution.
- **(D)** is a narrow band of width 0.10 sitting right under the peak — the region of highest density, and about the right width to accumulate 30%.

The answer is **D**.
