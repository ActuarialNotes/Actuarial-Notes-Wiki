A company has filed for bankruptcy and cannot indemnify its former directors. Creditors sue the directors personally, alleging they misled lenders about the company's finances, and \$800,000 of defense costs are incurred before any finding of fraud. The company's D&O policy has a \$10,000,000 limit, a \$500,000 Side B retention and a \$1,000,000 Side C retention. Under which part of the policy are the directors' defense costs paid, and how much does the insurer pay toward them?

- A) Side A, paying the full \$800,000 with no retention
- B) Side B, paying \$300,000 after the \$500,000 retention
- C) Side C, paying nothing because the costs are below its retention
- D) None, because fraud is alleged and the conduct exclusion applies

## Explanation

Side A pays directors and officers directly when the organization cannot indemnify them, as here because it is insolvent. Side A normally has no retention, so the insurer pays the full **\$800,000**.

- **B** is Side B, which reimburses the organization for indemnifying its directors. The bankrupt company is not indemnifying them, and its retention would fall on the company.
- **C** is entity coverage for the company's own securities loss, not the directors' personal defense.
- **D** misreads the conduct exclusions, which usually apply only once a final adjudication establishes the fraud. Defense costs are advanced until then.
