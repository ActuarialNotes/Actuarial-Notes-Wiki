A machine's lifetime $X$, in years, is modeled by an exponential distribution. The probability that the machine still functions after one year is 0.80.

$F$ is the cumulative distribution function for $X$.

Determine $F(x)$ for $x \geq 0$.

- A) $F(x) = 0.8^x$
- B) $F(x) = 0.8^x e^{-0.8x}$
- C) $F(x) = 1 - 0.8^x$
- D) $F(x) = 1 - e^{-0.8x}$
- E) $F(x) = e^{-0.8x}$

## Explanation

An exponential lifetime has survival function $P[X > x] = e^{-\lambda x}$. The given information fixes $\lambda$ at $x = 1$:

$$
0.80 = P[X > 1] = e^{-\lambda}
$$

That single equation is enough — rather than solving for $\lambda = -\ln(0.8) = 0.2231$, substitute it back directly:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > x] &= e^{-\lambda x} \\
         &= \left(e^{-\lambda}\right)^x \\
         &= 0.8^x
\end{align*}
$$

The cumulative distribution function is the complement:

$$
F(x) = 1 - P[X > x] = \mathbf{1 - 0.8^x}
$$

Choice (A), $0.8^x$, is the survival function $P[X > x]$ rather than the cumulative one. Choice (E), $e^{-0.8x}$, makes that error and also reads 0.80 as the rate: at $x = 1$ it gives $e^{-0.8} = 0.449$, not 0.80. Choice (D) is the trap of reading 0.80 as the *rate* $\lambda$ rather than as a probability; the true rate is 0.2231, so $1 - e^{-0.8x}$ describes a machine that fails nearly four times as fast. Checking $F(1) = 1 - 0.8 = 0.2$ against the given 0.80 survival probability confirms the right choice at once.
