Which description fits a modular insurance policy rather than a self-contained one?

- A) One preprinted document holds every insuring agreement, tailored by endorsement
- B) The insurer drafts a unique form for one large insured
- C) There is no declarations page, because every fact is printed in the form
- D) Common declarations and conditions join coverage parts built from separate forms

## Explanation

A modular policy is assembled from separate standardized forms: in the ISO commercial package, common policy declarations and the Common Policy Conditions apply to all coverage parts, and each coverage part is built from its own declarations, coverage form, conditions and endorsements. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — describes a self-contained policy, a single document with all the insuring agreements, often preprinted and tailored through endorsements.
- **B** — describes a manuscript policy, which is a different distinction (who drafted the wording).
- **C** — every policy has declarations, the page or pages with the facts that vary from insured to insured.
