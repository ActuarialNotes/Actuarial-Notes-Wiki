A statistician models the size of unemployment claims that range from 0 to 1 using a probability density function proportional to the $n$th root of the size of the claim, for some positive integer $n$.

Determine the ratio of the 30th percentile to the 20th percentile of the size of an unemployment claim.

- A) $\sqrt[n]{1.5}$
- B) $(1.5)^n$
- C) $\sqrt[n]{(1.5)^{n+1}}$
- D) $\sqrt[n+1]{(1.5)^n}$
- E) $(1.5)^{n+1}$

## Explanation

"Proportional to the $n$th root" means $f(x) = cx^{1/n}$ on $(0,1)$. Find $c$ from the requirement that the density integrates to 1:

$$
\begin{align*}
1 &= \int_0^1 cx^{1/n}\,dx \\
  &= \frac{cx^{1/n + 1}}{(1/n)+1}\Bigg|_0^1 \\
  &= \frac{cn}{1+n} \\
c &= \frac{1+n}{n}
\end{align*}
$$

Now integrate to the $p$th percentile $x_p$:

$$
\begin{align*}
p &= F(x_p) = \int_0^{x_p} \frac{(1+n)x^{1/n}}{n}\,dx \\
  &= x^{1 + 1/n}\Big|_0^{x_p} \\
  &= (x_p)^{(n+1)/n}
\end{align*}
$$

Inverting gives the percentile itself:

$$
x_p = p^{\,n/(n+1)}
$$

The ratio asked for is therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{x_{0.3}}{x_{0.2}} &= \frac{0.3^{\,n/(n+1)}}{0.2^{\,n/(n+1)}} \\
                        &= \left(\frac{0.3}{0.2}\right)^{n/(n+1)} \\
                        &= (1.5)^{n/(n+1)} \\
                        &= \mathbf{\sqrt[n+1]{(1.5)^n}}
\end{align*}
$$

The normalizing constant $c$ fixes the percentiles themselves but not their ratio: with any constant in front of $x^{1/n}$, the exponent $(n+1)/n$ comes from the power rule, each percentile is a constant multiple of $p^{\,n/(n+1)}$, and the multiple cancels. Note the answer is close to but not equal to 1.5: as $n$ grows the exponent $n/(n+1)$ approaches 1, so the ratio approaches 1.5 from below. Choice (C), $\sqrt[n]{(1.5)^{n+1}} = (1.5)^{(n+1)/n}$, inverts the exponent; choice (B), $(1.5)^n$, comes from taking the distribution function, rather than the density, to be $x^{1/n}$, so that $x_p = p^n$.
