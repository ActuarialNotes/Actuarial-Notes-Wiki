An insurance company surcharges a driver, based on the year of the driver's last accident, using the following table with the current year denoted by $t$:

| Year of Last Accident | $t-1$ | $t-2$ | $t-3$ | $t-4$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surcharge | 20% | 15% | 10% | 5% |

The probability that a driver has at least one accident in any given year is 0.10, independent of the number of accidents in all other years.

Calculate the expected surcharge in year $t$ for a driver who has been driving since the beginning of year $t-4$.

- A) $4.5\%$
- B) $5.0\%$
- C) $8.6\%$
- D) $10.0\%$
- E) $19.4\%$

## Explanation

The surcharge depends on the **most recent** accident year, so year $t-1$ is checked first and the search stops at the first accident found going backwards. Each year independently has an accident with probability 0.1 and none with probability 0.9.

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{last accident in } t-1] &= 0.1 \\
P[\text{last accident in } t-2] &= (0.9)(0.1) \\
P[\text{last accident in } t-3] &= (0.9)^2(0.1) \\
P[\text{last accident in } t-4] &= (0.9)^3(0.1)
\end{align*}
$$

Each is the probability of no accident in every later year, times an accident in that year. The driver has only been on the road since $t-4$, so those four cases are the only ones carrying a surcharge; with probability $(0.9)^4 = 0.6561$ there is no accident at all and the surcharge is zero.

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\text{surcharge}] &= 0.20(0.1) + 0.15(0.1)(0.9) + 0.10(0.1)(0.9)^2 + 0.05(0.1)(0.9)^3 \\
                    &= 0.020000 + 0.013500 + 0.008100 + 0.003645 \\
                    &= 0.045245 \\
                    &= \mathbf{4.5\%}
\end{align*}
$$

The geometric weights are what keep the answer small. Averaging the four surcharge rates directly gives $12.5\%$, and weighting them by a flat 0.1 each without the $(0.9)^k$ "no later accident" factors gives $5.0\%$, which is choice (B). Neither accounts for the two-thirds of drivers who have no accident in the four years and pay no surcharge at all.
