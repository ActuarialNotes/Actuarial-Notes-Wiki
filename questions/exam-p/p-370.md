Small businesses in a particular city are categorized as retail, service, transportation, or other.

In a study of the yearly bankruptcies of small businesses in this city, the following information from the past year was observed:

i) 60% of the small businesses were retail, and of those, 12% went bankrupt.
ii) 25% of the small businesses were service, and of those, 8% went bankrupt.
iii) 10% of the small businesses were transportation, and of those, 6% went bankrupt.
iv) 5% of the small businesses were other, and none of those went bankrupt.

An auditor randomly selected a small business that went bankrupt last year.

Calculate the probability that it was a service business.

- A) $0.020$
- B) $0.080$
- C) $0.204$
- D) $0.250$
- E) $0.308$

## Explanation

Let $R$, $S$, $T$ and $O$ be the four categories and $B$ the event of bankruptcy. The four categories partition the businesses, so the total probability of bankruptcy is the weighted sum over them:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[B] &= P[B \mid R]P[R] + P[B \mid S]P[S] + P[B \mid T]P[T] + P[B \mid O]P[O] \\
     &= (0.12)(0.60) + (0.08)(0.25) + (0.06)(0.10) + (0)(0.05) \\
     &= 0.072 + 0.020 + 0.006 + 0 \\
     &= 0.098
\end{align*}
$$

Bayes' theorem then picks out the service share of that total:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S \mid B] &= \frac{P[B \mid S]P[S]}{P[B]} \\
            &= \frac{0.020}{0.098} \\
            &= \mathbf{0.20408}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A) is the numerator $P[S \cap B] = 0.020$, the joint probability rather than the conditional one. Choice (D) is the prior $P[S] = 0.25$ — the answer if bankruptcy told you nothing about category. It should pull the probability *down*, because service businesses fail less often than the retail businesses that dominate the city.
