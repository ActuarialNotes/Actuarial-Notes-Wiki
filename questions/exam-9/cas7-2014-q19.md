A U.S.-domiciled insurance company is holding the following bonds:

- Bonds issued by a U.S.-based bank with credit rating of AA valued at \$15 million. The mean time to maturity is five years.
- Bonds issued by a technology company located in the U.S. with credit rating of BBB valued at \$20 million. The mean time to maturity is 10 years.

There are no other bonds held by the company.

Identify and briefly describe three key drivers of credit risk for the insurance company and briefly comment on how they relate to the insurer's bond portfolio.

### Explanation
Any three of the following drivers, each described and applied to the portfolio:

- **Credit quality** (also accepted: counterparty risk, default risk, credit rating) — the probability that the issuer will meet all its contractual obligations, assessed at the initial investment and at each renewal point, commonly measured by the rating the agencies assign. *Portfolio:* credit risk from credit quality is higher for the technology company's bonds (BBB) than the bank's (AA).
- **Maturity** (also accepted: time horizon, duration) — the longer the term to maturity, the longer even a high-quality issuer has to deteriorate. *Portfolio:* "the credit risk from maturity is higher for the bank bonds since they have a longer maturity" (as printed in the examiner's report — the booklet gives the bank bonds a 5-year and the technology bonds a 10-year mean maturity).
- **Concentration by industry** (also accepted: concentration by type) — conditions that trigger credit events tend to hit the whole economy, but their impact varies between sectors, while entities within a sector tend to move together. *Portfolio:* this risk is reduced by holding bonds from companies in two unrelated industries (banking and technology).
- **Concentration by geography** (also accepted: by region or country) — credit risk is contagious, and economically depressed regions produce higher default experience than prosperous ones. *Portfolio:* there is some of this risk since all the bonds are from U.S. companies; or, it is reduced by holding a national rather than a regional bank's bonds.
- **Size of expected loss** — a credit event can cost some or all of the return, or some or all of the principal; a delayed payment loses return and reinvestment income over the deferral and may force the investor to borrow or sell assets to meet its own obligations.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to identify, briefly describe, and comment on the application of three key drivers of credit risk to the bond portfolio.
- Most candidates were successful in identifying, describing, and commenting on at least 2 key drivers. Many candidates successfully identified and commented on 3 key drivers, with the descriptions of the drivers being absent or insufficient.
- Additional common responses and remarks:
o Downgrade risk was a very common incorrect response. Partial credit was given when the response included an appropriate discussion of maturity.
o While not explicitly identified in the reading, credit was granted for responses describing lack of diversification with respect to the number of issuers.
o Some candidates listed sovereign risk as a key driver, and this was considered an incorrect response. However, partial credit was given if this response included an appropriate discussion relating to being concentrated in the US.
o Settlement risk was another incorrect response given by some candidates. Partial credit was given when the response included an appropriate discussion of maturity. Payment lag was not acceptable. Discussions of interest rate or liquidity risk were not accepted since this is a separate issue from credit risk.
o Descriptions and comments for "size of expected loss" pertaining to the size of the bond values were not accepted, as this pertains more to portfolio concentration risk with respect to lack of diversification.
