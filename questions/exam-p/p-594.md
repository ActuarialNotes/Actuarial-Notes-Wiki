From a standard deck of 52 playing cards, five cards are chosen without replacement.

Calculate the probability of obtaining at least two kings, but no aces or jacks.

- A) $0.0174$
- B) $0.0228$
- C) $0.0240$
- D) $0.0298$
- E) $0.0417$

## Explanation

Split the deck into three groups: 4 kings, 8 forbidden cards (4 aces and 4 jacks), and the remaining 40 cards. A hand qualifies when it holds 2, 3 or 4 kings, no forbidden cards, and the rest from the 40. This is a hypergeometric count:

$$
P = \frac{\dbinom{4}{2}\dbinom{8}{0}\dbinom{40}{3} + \dbinom{4}{3}\dbinom{8}{0}\dbinom{40}{2} + \dbinom{4}{4}\dbinom{8}{0}\dbinom{40}{1}}{\dbinom{52}{5}}
$$

Evaluating the numerator:

$$
\begin{align*}
6(9880) + 4(780) + 1(40) &= 59280 + 3120 + 40 \\
                         &= 62440
\end{align*}
$$

and $\binom{52}{5} = 2{,}598{,}960$, so

$$
P = \frac{62440}{2598960} = \mathbf{0.0240}
$$

Choice (E), 0.0417, is the probability of at least two kings with the aces and jacks left in play — it drops the "no aces or jacks" restriction. Choice (B), 0.0228, keeps the restriction but counts only hands with *exactly* two kings.
