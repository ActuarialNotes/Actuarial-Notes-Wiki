A homeowner purchases flood insurance that pays a benefit based on the amount of rain that falls. No benefit is paid for rainfall amounts less than twelve inches. For every full two inches greater than twelve, the insurer pays the homeowner 5000, with a maximum payment of 18,000.

The following table displays probabilities for the rainfall amounts.

| Inches of Rain ($x$) | Probability of being in interval |
| --- | --- |
| $0 \leq x < 2$ | 0.04 |
| $2 \leq x < 4$ | 0.06 |
| $4 \leq x < 6$ | 0.07 |
| $6 \leq x < 8$ | 0.09 |
| $8 \leq x < 10$ | 0.12 |
| $10 \leq x < 12$ | 0.14 |
| $12 \leq x < 14$ | 0.18 |
| $14 \leq x < 16$ | 0.11 |
| $16 \leq x < 18$ | 0.08 |
| $18 \leq x < 20$ | 0.07 |
| $20 \leq x$ | 0.04 |

Calculate the standard deviation of the benefit paid under the policy.

- A) $2201$
- B) $3120$
- C) $3200$
- D) $5452$
- E) $5680$

## Explanation

First convert rainfall to benefit. The payment counts only **full** two-inch steps above twelve, so rain in $[12, 14)$ has not yet completed a step and still pays nothing; $[14,16)$ pays 5000, $[16,18)$ pays 10,000, and $[18,20)$ pays 15,000. Rain of 20 inches or more would earn 20,000, but the policy maximum caps it at 18,000.

Collecting the intervals that map to each payment gives the distribution of the benefit $X$:

| Amount paid | Probability |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | $0.04+0.06+0.07+0.09+0.12+0.14+0.18 = 0.70$ |
| 5,000 | 0.11 |
| 10,000 | 0.08 |
| 15,000 | 0.07 |
| 18,000 | 0.04 |

Now take the first two moments:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X] &= 5000(0.11) + 10{,}000(0.08) + 15{,}000(0.07) + 18{,}000(0.04) \\
     &= 550 + 800 + 1050 + 720 \\
     &= 3120
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= 5000^2(0.11) + 10{,}000^2(0.08) + 15{,}000^2(0.07) + 18{,}000^2(0.04) \\
       &= 2{,}750{,}000 + 8{,}000{,}000 + 15{,}750{,}000 + 12{,}960{,}000 \\
       &= 39{,}460{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X) &= E[X^2] - \bigl(E[X]\bigr)^2 \\
              &= 39{,}460{,}000 - 3120^2 \\
              &= 29{,}725{,}600
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{SD}(X) &= \sqrt{29{,}725{,}600} \\
             &= \mathbf{5452}
\end{align*}
$$

Two traps sit in the setup rather than the arithmetic. Choice (B) is $E[X]$, not the standard deviation. And treating $[12,14)$ as a paying interval — or forgetting the 18,000 cap on the top interval — shifts 0.18 or 0.04 of the mass onto the wrong payment and changes both moments.
