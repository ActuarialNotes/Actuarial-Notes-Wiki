A pedestrian suffers \$250,000 of damages in an accident with the insured's car, and the jury finds the pedestrian 40% at fault. The case is tried in Virginia, which applies contributory negligence. How much can the pedestrian recover?

- A) \$0
- B) \$100,000
- C) \$150,000
- D) \$250,000

## Explanation

Under contributory negligence a plaintiff who contributed in any way to the harm recovers nothing; Alabama, Maryland, North Carolina, Virginia and the District of Columbia still follow it. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — \$100,000 is the pedestrian's own share of fault applied to the damages, the wrong direction.
- **C** — \$150,000 is the pure or modified comparative negligence result, $(1 - 0.40) \times 250{,}000$, which Virginia does not use.
- **D** — \$250,000 ignores the pedestrian's fault altogether.
