A charity's CGL (CG 00 01 04 13) has Coverage C. At its fundraising run, an unpaid volunteer who was directing traffic at the charity's request trips over a cable and is hurt; she also, while directing traffic, accidentally knocks over and injures a spectator. Which statement is correct?

- A) She is not an insured, so the spectator's claim against her is not covered, and her own medical expenses are payable under Coverage C
- B) She is an insured, so the spectator's claim against her is covered, and her own medical expenses are excluded under Coverage C because she is an insured
- C) She is not an insured, and her own medical expenses are excluded because she was performing work for the charity
- D) She is an insured for the spectator's claim, and her own medical expenses are still payable under Coverage C

## Explanation

A "volunteer worker" (not an employee, donating work at the named insured's direction within duties it sets, and unpaid) is an insured while performing duties related to the conduct of the business. So she is an insured for the spectator's injury. Coverage C excludes bodily injury to any insured **except "volunteer workers"**, so her own medical expenses remain payable. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** misses that volunteer workers are insureds while performing duties for the organization.
- **B** forgets the volunteer worker exception to Coverage C's "any insured" exclusion.
- **C** applies the hired person exclusion; it removes a person *hired* to do work, and an unpaid volunteer is not hired.
