A tenant installed improvements and betterments costing \$120,000. Its lease runs 2,190 days from the installation, with a renewal option for a further 1,095 days. A fire destroys the improvements 730 days before the lease's original expiration, and the tenant decides not to repair them. How does the BPP value them?

- A) \$40,000
- B) \$66,666.67
- C) \$80,000
- D) \$120,000

## Explanation

If the tenant does not repair promptly, improvements and betterments are valued at a proportion of original cost: the days from the loss to the lease's expiration divided by the days from installation to expiration. When the lease has a renewal option, the expiration of the renewal period replaces the expiration of the lease:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Remaining} &= 730 + 1{,}095 \\
&= 1{,}825 \\
\text{Total} &= 2{,}190 + 1{,}095 \\
&= 3{,}285 \\
\text{TIB} &= 120{,}000 \times \frac{1{,}825}{3{,}285} \\
&= 66{,}666.67
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Ignores the renewal option: $\text{TIB} = 120{,}000 \times 730/2{,}190 = 40{,}000$.
- **C** — Uses the elapsed part of the lease instead of the remaining part.
- **D** — Values at original cost, as if the improvements had been repaired at replacement cost.
