An individual buys an automobile policy and a homeowners policy for one year. The probability of an automobile claim is 0.10 and the probability of a homeowners claim is 0.05. Neither policy can have more than one claim. The correlation between the numbers of claims on these policies is 0.30.

Calculate the probability that both policies will have a claim.

- A) $0.005$
- B) $0.006$
- C) $0.025$
- D) $0.033$
- E) $0.045$

## Explanation

Let $X$ and $Y$ indicate a claim on each policy; both take only the values 0 and 1, so they are Bernoulli.

The key observation is that for two 0/1 variables the product $XY$ is itself 0/1, equal to 1 exactly when both claims occur. So

$$
E[XY] = 1(1)P[X=1, Y=1] = P[\text{both claim}]
$$

and finding $E[XY]$ *is* the answer.

Each standard deviation follows from $\sqrt{p(1-p)}$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\sigma_X &= \sqrt{0.1(0.9)} = 0.30 \\
\sigma_Y &= \sqrt{0.05(0.95)} = 0.218
\end{align*}
$$

Now use the definition of correlation:

$$
\begin{align*}
\rho &= \frac{E[XY] - E[X]E[Y]}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y} \\
0.30 &= \frac{E[XY] - (0.1)(0.05)}{(0.30)(0.218)}
\end{align*}
$$

Solving:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[XY] &= (0.30)(0.30)(0.218) + 0.005 \\
      &= 0.0196 + 0.005 \\
      &= \mathbf{0.025}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), 0.005, is $P[X=1]P[Y=1]$ — the answer if the two claims were independent. The positive correlation makes both-claims five times likelier than independence would suggest, which is the whole content of the question.
