The time $T$, in years, a fuse lasts is a random variable with density function

$$
g(t) = \begin{cases}
te^{-t}, & \text{for } t > 0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate the probability that a fuse lasts at most two years, given that it lasts at least one year.

- A) $0.061$
- B) $0.330$
- C) $0.448$
- D) $0.736$
- E) $0.807$

## Explanation

This is a gamma density with shape 2 and scale 1. Integration by parts gives a closed-form survival function:

$$
P[T > t] = \int_t^\infty ue^{-u}du = (1+t)e^{-t}
$$

Evaluate at the two boundaries:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[T > 1] &= 2e^{-1} = 0.73576 \\
P[T > 2] &= 3e^{-2} = 0.40601
\end{align*}
$$

Since $\{T \leq 2\} \cap \{T \geq 1\}$ is the interval $[1,2]$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[T \leq 2 \mid T \geq 1] &= \frac{P[T > 1] - P[T > 2]}{P[T > 1]} \\
                          &= \frac{0.73576 - 0.40601}{0.73576} \\
                          &= \mathbf{0.4482}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 0.736, is $P[T > 1]$ — the denominator reported on its own. Unlike an exponential, this gamma density is *not* memoryless: its hazard rate rises with age, so a fuse that has survived a year is more likely to fail in the next year than a new one was in its first.
