An actuary is using hierarchical clustering to group the following observations:

| |
|---|
| 13 |
| 40 |
| 60 |
| 71 |

The actuary recalculates the clustering using two linkage methods: complete and average.

- $h_{comp}$ is the height of the final fuse using complete linkage.
- $h_{avg}$ is the height of the final fuse using average linkage.

Calculate $|h_{comp} - h_{avg}|$.

- A) Less than 10
- B) At least 10, but less than 15
- C) At least 15, but less than 20
- D) At least 20, but less than 25
- E) At least 25

## Explanation

The pairwise distances are

| | 13 | 40 | 60 | 71 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | — | 27 | 47 | 58 |
| 40 | | — | 20 | 31 |
| 60 | | | — | 11 |

**First fuse (both methods).** The smallest distance is 11, so 60 and 71 join at height 11.

**Complete linkage** — inter-cluster distance is the *maximum* pairwise distance:

$$
\begin{align*}
d(\{13\}, \{40\}) &= 27 \\
d(\{40\}, \{60,71\}) &= \max(20, 31) = 31 \\
d(\{13\}, \{60,71\}) &= \max(47, 58) = 58
\end{align*}
$$

The smallest is 27, so 13 and 40 fuse at height 27. The final fuse joins $\{13,40\}$ to $\{60,71\}$ at

$$
h_{comp} = \max(47, 58, 20, 31) = 58.
$$

**Average linkage** — inter-cluster distance is the *mean* pairwise distance:

$$
\begin{align*}
d(\{13\}, \{40\}) &= 27 \\
d(\{40\}, \{60,71\}) &= \frac{20 + 31}{2} = 25.5 \\
d(\{13\}, \{60,71\}) &= \frac{47 + 58}{2} = 52.5
\end{align*}
$$

Now the smallest is 25.5, so **40 joins $\{60,71\}$** instead — the two linkages produce different trees. The final fuse joins 13 to $\{40,60,71\}$ at

$$
\begin{align*}
h_{avg} &= \frac{27 + 47 + 58}{3} \\
        &= 44
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
|h_{comp} - h_{avg}| &= |58 - 44| \\
                     &= 14
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[10,\, 15)$, so the answer is **B**.
