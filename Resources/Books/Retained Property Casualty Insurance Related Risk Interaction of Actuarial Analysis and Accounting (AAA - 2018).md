A public policy practice note (April 2018) developed by the Casualty Practice Council's Committee on Property and Liability Financial Reporting of the American Academy of Actuaries. It describes the ways entities retain property/casualty risk, the types of entities and exposures involved, the relevant actuarial concepts, standards of practice and accounting guidance, and the roles of actuary, accountants and risk managers. The note is not an actuarial standard of practice and is not binding upon any actuary.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objectives C4 and C7; Chapter 1, pages 7–10 ending with the section on Trusts, and Chapter 4, pp. 17–23 (the content outline cites it as an online publication).

## Overview of the Practice Note
- The note defines the ways entities retain risk, often described as methods of financing the entity's exposure to risk, and describes the types of entity and the common exposures they may retain.
- It describes the accounting guidance that applies to the various entities and exposures, how it interacts with the relevant actuarial concepts, and the roles and interactions of the actuary, accountants, third party administrators and risk managers.
- It closes by summarizing the actuarial standards of practice most applicable to the work.

## 1 Method of Retaining Risk and Associated Treatments
- Introduction
    - The chapter addresses types of risk retention and risk transfer from the point of view of the entity that initially bears the risk, then illustrates what has been transferred through the effect of a bankruptcy.
- Types of Risk Transfer
    - Guaranteed Cost Policies
        - The entity transfers all of the liability, subject to any limit in the contract, to an insurer for a fixed premium, so its costs are not influenced by actual loss experience.
    - Retrospectively Rated Policies
        - The entity transfers all of the liability to the insurer for a premium that is a function of actual loss experience, and the final premium may not be determined for several years after the policy expires.
    - Large Deductible Policies
        - The entity transfers all of the liability to the insurer, then takes back a substantial deductible by endorsement; until the deductible claims are paid the entity has a liability to the insurer.
    - Self-Insurance
    - Claims Made Coverage
        - Liability for claims reported after the policy expires remains with the entity, so even guaranteed cost coverage can leave unreported claim liability uninsured at an accounting date.
    - Captives
        - Direct Policies
        - Fronting Arrangements
        - Deductible Reimbursement
    - Trusts
- Risk Transfer Illustrated by What Happens in Bankruptcy
    - When the Insured Entity Goes Bankrupt
    - When the Insurer Goes Bankrupt
    - Bankruptcy of Insured vs. Insurer – Impact by Type of Insurance

## 4 Relevant Actuarial Concepts and Considerations
- Introduction
    - Considerations for unpaid claim estimates are covered in detail in ASOP No. 43; the chapter addresses unusual considerations in the context of retained risk.
- Intended Purpose of the Actuarial Analysis
    - A retained risk analysis is typically used in one of three contexts: adequacy of accruals for financial reporting, internal financial reporting and cost allocation, or a regulatory filing for a qualified self-insurance designation.
    - Adequacy of Accruals for Financial Reporting
        - Net or Gross of Insurance Recoverables
            - U.S. GAAP requires the separate presentation of a gross liability accrual for expected future loss payments and an asset for the related expected insurance recoveries.
        - Discounting for the Time Value of Money
        - Combined Accruals that Include Other Insurance-Related Balances
        - Prepaid Balances or Amounts Due From or To TPAs and/or Excess Insurers
    - Internal Financial Reporting and Cost Allocation
    - Regulatory Filing for a Qualified Self-Insurance Designation
- Coverage or Policy Period
- Key Dates and Interactions
    - ASOP No. 43 defines three key dates: the accounting date, the valuation date and the review date; the valuation date may precede the accounting date, in which case a roll forward procedure is needed.
- Loss Adjustment or Claim Adjustment Expenses
