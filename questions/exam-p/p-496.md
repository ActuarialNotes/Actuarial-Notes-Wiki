Three identical machines are purchased simultaneously and are still operating after one year. The manufacturer then buys an insurance policy that pays 1000 if any of the three machines fails during the second year of use. The three machines operate independently.

The age at failure, in years, for each of these machines has cumulative distribution function

$$
F(x) = \begin{cases}
1 - \left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^{1/4}, & \text{for } x \geq 1 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Note that $x \geq 1$ because each machine has already successfully completed one year of operation.

Calculate the expected claim payment for this policy.

- A) $4$
- B) $159$
- C) $405$
- D) $595$
- E) $996$

## Explanation

**One machine.** Failing during the second year means failing by age 2, and since the distribution already starts at $x = 1$ this is just $F(2)$:

$$
\begin{align*}
F(2) &= 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{1/4} \\
     &= 1 - 0.84090 \\
     &= 0.15910
\end{align*}
$$

**At least one of three.** The policy pays if *any* machine fails, so take the complement of all three surviving. Independence lets the survival probabilities multiply:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{at least one fails}] &= 1 - (1 - 0.15910)^3 \\
                             &= 1 - (0.84090)^3 \\
                             &= 1 - 0.59460 \\
                             &= 0.40540
\end{align*}
$$

**Expected payment.** The policy pays a flat 1000 whenever that event occurs — not 1000 per machine:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\text{payment}] &= 1000(0.40540) \\
                  &= \mathbf{405}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 159, is $1000F(2)$ — the payment for a single machine, ignoring that there are three. Choice (D), 595, is $1000(0.8409)^3$, the probability that *none* fails. Note $3(0.1591) = 0.477$ overstates the answer, because it double counts the cases where more than one machine fails.
