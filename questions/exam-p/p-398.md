Ten percent of homeowners in a certain city are classified as high-risk, and ninety percent are classified as low-risk. Each homeowner's classification remains unchanged over the next four years.

In any given year, each high-risk homeowner has probability 0.80 of experiencing no fires, and each low-risk homeowner has probability 0.99 of experiencing no fires. For each homeowner, the numbers of fires in different years are mutually independent.

A randomly chosen homeowner experiences no fires in the first and second years.

Calculate the probability that this homeowner will experience no fires in the third and fourth years.

- A) $0.9055$
- B) $0.9324$
- C) $0.9461$
- D) $0.9548$
- E) $0.9571$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the event of no fires in years 1 and 2, $Y$ the event of no fires in years 3 and 4, and $H$, $L$ the two risk classes. Fires are independent *given the class*, but not unconditionally — the two clean years are evidence about which class the homeowner is in.

Within each class the years multiply:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \mid H] &= 0.8^2, & P[X \mid L] &= 0.99^2 \\
P[X \cap Y \mid H] &= 0.8^4, & P[X \cap Y \mid L] &= 0.99^4
\end{align*}
$$

Now form the conditional probability, expanding both numerator and denominator by the law of total probability over the two classes:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[Y \mid X] &= \frac{P[X \cap Y]}{P[X]} \\
            &= \frac{P[X \cap Y \mid H]P[H] + P[X \cap Y \mid L]P[L]}{P[X \mid H]P[H] + P[X \mid L]P[L]} \\
            &= \frac{0.8^4(0.1) + 0.99^4(0.9)}{0.8^2(0.1) + 0.99^2(0.9)} \\
            &= \frac{0.04096 + 0.86471}{0.064 + 0.88209} \\
            &= \mathbf{0.95709}
\end{align*}
$$

The unconditional probability of two clean years is $0.8^2(0.1) + 0.99^2(0.9) = 0.946$, which is choice (C). The answer is *higher* than that because two fire-free years are weak evidence that the homeowner is low-risk, and low-risk homeowners are the safer bet going forward. That updating is the whole content of the question — assuming independence across all four years would give 0.946 and miss it.
