A Bayesian logistic regression model that predicts if a prospective customer will purchase an insurance policy is fit to a dataset with two cluster variables. The first cluster variable is the *channel* through which the insurance policy was sold. The *channel* variable can only take values from one of the following categories:

- Agency
- Broker
- Internet

The second cluster variable is the *type* of insurance policy. The *type* variable can only take values from one of the following categories:

- Personal Automobile
- Homeowners
- Workers Compensation

The model also includes two binary categorical variables that are represented as slopes. The first indicates if the offered insurance policy included a discount to the price. The second indicates if the prospective customer had a prior insurance policy.

In the model, let $y_i$ indicate if the $i^{th}$ prospective customer purchased the insurance policy. Let the *channel* and *type* of policy from the $i^{th}$ prospective customer be represented by the $channel[i]$ and $type[i]$ subscripts respectively. Let $D_i$ indicate if the offered insurance policy to the $i^{th}$ customer included a discount to the price, and let $P_i$ indicate if the prospective customer had a prior insurance policy.

The systematic portion of the model is specified below.

$$
\begin{align*}
y_i &\sim \text{Bernoulli}(p_i) \\
\text{logit}(p_i) &= \gamma_i + \delta_i D_i + \Lambda_i P_i \\
\gamma_i &= \alpha + \alpha_{channel[i]} + \alpha_{type[i]} \\
\delta_i &= \beta_D + \beta_{D,channel[i]} + \beta_{D,type[i]} \\
\Lambda_i &= \beta_P + \beta_{P,channel[i]} + \beta_{P,type[i]}
\end{align*}
$$

Multivariate Gaussian prior distributions are employed to model the covariance between the intercept and slope parameters for each cluster. In particular, an LKJ prior distribution for the correlation parameters is selected for this model.

Determine the number of correlation parameters that need to be estimated in this model.

- A) Less than or equal to 2
- B) Greater than 2 but less than or equal to 5
- C) Greater than 5 but less than or equal to 8
- D) Greater than 8 but less than or equal to 11
- E) Greater than 11

## Explanation

The LKJ prior is placed on the correlation matrix of the *varying effects within a cluster variable* — not on the number of categories.

Each cluster variable carries three varying effects: one varying intercept and two varying slopes.

- **channel**: $\alpha_{channel}$, $\beta_{D,channel}$, $\beta_{P,channel}$
- **type**: $\alpha_{type}$, $\beta_{D,type}$, $\beta_{P,type}$

A $3 \times 3$ correlation matrix has

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{k(k-1)}{2} &= \frac{3(2)}{2} \\
                 &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

free off-diagonal correlations. Note that the number of categories (3 channels, 3 types) is irrelevant — those determine how many *draws* come from each multivariate normal, not the dimension of its covariance matrix.

With one such matrix per cluster variable:

$$
2 \times 3 = 6 \text{ correlation parameters.}
$$

Six falls in $(5,\, 8]$, so the answer is **C**.
