Given the following information about 10 risks for a manufacturing class:

| Risk | Type of Manufacturing | Manual Premium | Losses | Current Modification | Proposed Modification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Uses Robots | 1,000 | 500 | 0.65 | 0.50 |
| 2 | Uses Robots | 1,000 | 600 | 0.75 | 0.55 |
| 3 | Made by hand | 1,000 | 700 | 0.70 | 0.70 |
| 4 | Uses Robots | 1,000 | 800 | 1.00 | 0.75 |
| 5 | Uses Robots | 1,000 | 900 | 0.90 | 0.95 |
| 6 | Made by hand | 1,000 | 1,100 | 1.15 | 1.05 |
| 7 | Uses Robots | 1,000 | 1,200 | 1.10 | 1.25 |
| 8 | Made by hand | 1,000 | 1,500 | 1.25 | 1.50 |
| 9 | Made by hand | 1,000 | 1,600 | 1.20 | 1.75 |
| 10 | Made by hand | 1,000 | 1,800 | 1.30 | 2.00 |

## Part a (3 points)

Evaluate the proposed experience rating plan compared to the current plan.

### Explanation
Group the risks into quintiles, ordered by modification:

| Quintile | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1, 3 | 1, 2 |
| 2 | 2, 5 | 3, 4 |
| 3 | 4, 7 | 5, 6 |
| 4 | 6, 9 | 7, 8 |
| 5 | 8, 10 | 9, 10 |

Calculate the manual and standard loss ratios by group:

| Quintile | Manual LR, Current | Manual LR, Proposed | Standard LR, Current | Standard LR, Proposed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.60 | 0.55 | 0.89 | 1.05 |
| 2 | 0.75 | 0.75 | 0.91 | 1.03 |
| 3 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 1.00 |
| 4 | 1.35 | 1.35 | 1.15 | 0.98 |
| 5 | 1.65 | 1.70 | 1.29 | 0.91 |

The manual loss ratios under the proposed plan are more dispersed than under the current plan. Therefore, the proposed plan is better at identifying risk differences.

The standard loss ratios under the proposed plan are closer to 1 (or show less variance) than the current plan. Therefore, the proposed plan is better when comparing standard loss ratios. Overall, the proposed plan is better. However, the proposed plan does show a decreasing trend in standard loss ratio, which suggests that the proposed plan puts too much credibility on experience.

Alternatively, use the efficiency test and choose the plan with the lowest test statistic:

$$\text{Test Statistic} = \frac{\text{Variance(Modified Loss Ratios)}}{\text{Variance(Manual Loss Ratios)}}$$

Ranking the risks by each plan's mod, with standard LR = manual LR / mod:

| Current: Risk | Mod | Man LR | Std LR | Proposed: Risk | Mod | Man LR | Std LR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.65 | 50% | 76.9% | 1 | 0.50 | 50% | 100.0% |
| 3 | 0.70 | 70% | 100.0% | 2 | 0.55 | 60% | 109.1% |
| 2 | 0.75 | 60% | 80.0% | 3 | 0.70 | 70% | 100.0% |
| 5 | 0.90 | 90% | 100.0% | 4 | 0.75 | 80% | 106.7% |
| 4 | 1.00 | 80% | 80.0% | 5 | 0.95 | 90% | 94.7% |
| 7 | 1.10 | 120% | 109.1% | 6 | 1.05 | 110% | 104.8% |
| 6 | 1.15 | 110% | 95.7% | 7 | 1.25 | 120% | 96.0% |
| 9 | 1.20 | 160% | 133.3% | 8 | 1.50 | 150% | 100.0% |
| 8 | 1.25 | 150% | 120.0% | 9 | 1.75 | 160% | 91.4% |
| 10 | 1.30 | 180% | 138.5% | 10 | 2.00 | 180% | 90.0% |

| | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Variance (Std LRs) | 0.0431 | 0.0036 |
| Variance (Man LRs) | 0.1801 | 0.1801 |
| Test Statistic | 0.2395 | 0.0201 |

By the efficiency test, the proposed plan has the lower test statistic, therefore this plan is preferred over the current plan. (The denominators are identical, so the variances of the modified loss ratios alone would do.) Ranked by the mods, the current plan's standard loss ratios show a clear increasing trend, so it is not assigning enough credibility to the actual risk experience; the proposed plan shows a small decreasing trend, so it is assigning too much credibility.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate the ability to compare two experience rating plans.
A number of approaches were allowed for full credit including the efficiency test and quintiles test.
Common mistakes include:
- Not assigning risks to the correct quintile.
- Not addressing the trend in standard loss ratios that suggests too much/little credibility in the proposed and current plans respectively.
- Grouping by manufacturing type.  Syllabus readings recommend separating tests by premium size but otherwise never mention separating within a single class. The question specifically stated that all risks were part of a single manufacturing class, therefore, candidates lost some credit for separating the 10 risks by manufacturing type in efficiency and quintiles tests.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Evaluate the classification plan for this class.

### Explanation
- Robots average loss = 800, Made by hand average loss = 1340.  It is apparent that the average loss for made by hand is higher than robot. The plan is not doing a good job in differentiating the loss potential between the two types of manufacturing. The made by hand class should be charged a higher manual rate.
- Risks that use robots consistently have lower mods than made by hand risks. Class may not be granular enough. Should consider splitting into two classes by manufacturing type if there is enough credibility to have two smaller classes.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to evaluate the appropriateness of these ten risks being grouped together in a single class.
Full credit answers recognized the class is not homogenous and demonstrated this by calculating the manual loss ratios, average loss, or experience mods for Robots and Made by hand manufacturing types.
Candidates also received credit for addressing credibility concerns with further refining the class.
Candidates who included a response in Part b that pertained to Part a, such as discussing the trend in proposed standard loss ratios and the implication of too much credibility, were given the appropriate credit in Part a.
Common mistakes include:

- Not discussing class fit.
- Not recognizing that the risks are currently part of the same class.
- Not fully justifying the decision to separate Robots and Made by hand into two classes.
