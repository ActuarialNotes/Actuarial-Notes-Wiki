An article in Product Matters!, the Society of Actuaries' product development newsletter (February 2015, Issue 91), on how companies can manage the post-level term (PLT) period of level premium term life. It examines four approaches that companies are weighing, with the potential benefits and drawbacks of each, taking the original approach as a starting point.

## What Limited Experience Tells Us
- There is currently limited credibility of mortality data; in the few years since the first generation of level premium term policies reached their PLT, the author has seen lower lapses in the early durations than assumed.
- If a carrier could retain even a small portion of the lives it expected to lapse, the effects on pool mortality may be highly accretive, but this remains a theory until sufficient claims experience is collected.

## The Pricing Approach
- Early level premium term carriers reported minimal reserves under the unitary reserve method, which values the level period and the yearly-renewable term period together and takes no account of lapses.
- Regulation XXX came into effect in 2000 to curb this practice, requiring segmented reserves that value the level period separately from the increasing period and that account for lapses.

## Varying Approaches, Uncertain Outcomes: The Original Approach
- The PLT yearly-renewable term rates go back to pre-level term days; setting the ceiling high gave the company leeway to alter rates to reflect emerging mortality.
- The shock rate led to the shock lapse, in which all but the worst risks are almost guaranteed to lapse, leaving the remaining lives amongst the worst mortality-wise.

## Simplified Re-Underwriting
- The company offers the insured the option to answer a simplified issue underwriting questionnaire as the PLT approaches, uses the answers to determine the insured's PLT risk class, and defaults those who decline to the traditional guaranteed rate.
- Offering the questionnaire may alert the insured to the pending premium jump, which could cause the best risks to lapse sooner or impaired risks to exercise a conversion option.

## The Graded Approach
- PLT rates increase at much smaller increments until a future anniversary, after which rates jump to the original yearly-renewable term schedule.
- Of the alternatives to the traditional approach, the graded approach seems to have the most actual supportable experience, and that experience appears positive from both a mortality and a lapse perspective.

## The Class-Continuation Approach
- The rate increase is modified by the insured's select risk class, with rates converging to an ultimate rate in later durations, so class structures continue into the PLT period rather than aggregating to a single rate.
- It seems the fairest approach, but its structure lends itself to the highest selective lapsation risk among the approaches.
