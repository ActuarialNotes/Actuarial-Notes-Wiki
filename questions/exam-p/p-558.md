The probability is 0.45 that a mouse exposed to a certain disease will develop it.

The events of individual mice developing the disease are all mutually independent.

Calculate the probability that the tenth mouse exposed to the disease will be the third to catch it.

- A) $0.0014$
- B) $0.0499$
- C) $0.1110$
- D) $0.1165$
- E) $0.1665$

## Explanation

Split the requirement in two. The tenth mouse must catch the disease, and exactly two of the first nine must have caught it.

**First nine mice** — a binomial probability:

$$
\binom{9}{2}(0.45)^2(0.55)^7
$$

**Tenth mouse** — fixed by the stopping condition, probability 0.45.

Multiplying and collecting the powers gives the negative binomial form:

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \binom{9}{2}(0.45)^3(0.55)^7 \\
  &= 36(0.091125)(0.015224) \\
  &= \mathbf{0.049943}
\end{align*}
$$

The binomial coefficient counts arrangements among the **first nine** trials only — the tenth mouse's outcome is not free to move. Using $\binom{10}{3} = 120$ instead would give 0.1665, which is choice (E): that is the probability of exactly three cases *somewhere* in ten mice, a different event that does not require the run to end on the tenth.
