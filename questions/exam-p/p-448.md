A policyholder sustains one loss covered by policy A and a second loss covered by policy B. The two losses are independent and uniformly distributed on the interval $[0,10]$. Each policy has a deductible of 5.

Calculate the probability that the larger of the two claim payments does not exceed $t$, for $0 \leq t \leq 5$.

- A) $\left(\dfrac{t}{5}\right)^2$
- B) $\left(\dfrac{t}{10}\right)^2$
- C) $\dfrac{5+t}{10}$
- D) $\left(\dfrac{5+t}{10}\right)^2$
- E) $1 - \left(\dfrac{5-t}{10}\right)^2$

## Explanation

Let $X$ and $Y$ be the two losses and $U$, $V$ the corresponding payments. With a deductible of 5, a payment is $\max(X-5, 0)$ — zero whenever the loss is at most 5.

The maximum of two values is at most $t$ exactly when *both* are, and independence lets the probabilities multiply:

$$
P[\max(U,V) \leq t] = P[U \leq t]P[V \leq t] = \bigl(P[U \leq t]\bigr)^2
$$

Now find $P[U \leq t]$ for a single policy. Because $t \geq 0$, this event covers both the losses that pay nothing and those that pay at most $t$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[U \leq t] &= P[X - 5 \leq t] \\
            &= P[X \leq t + 5] \\
            &= \frac{t+5}{10}
\end{align*}
$$

Squaring:

$$
P[\max(U,V) \leq t] = \mathbf{\left(\frac{5+t}{10}\right)^2}
$$

Choice (C) is the single-policy probability without squaring. Choices (A) and (B) treat the payment as if it ranged over $[0,10]$ or $[0,5]$ with no mass at zero — but the deductible puts a **point mass** of $P[X \leq 5] = 0.5$ at a payment of zero, which is why the answer equals $0.25$ rather than $0$ at $t = 0$. Checking $t = 0$ and $t = 5$ against the endpoints eliminates every wrong choice.
