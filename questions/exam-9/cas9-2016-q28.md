Given the following information:

- Assume a reinsurance contract covering two years with a single loss payment at the end of the two years.
- Assume no expenses.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean of loss distribution | 200,000 |
| Standard deviation of loss distribution | 300,000 |
| Dollar safety level associated with loss distribution | 2,000,000 |
| Annual expected yield rate of target investment | 7% |
| Standard deviation of investment yield over one year | 20% |
| Standard deviation of investment yield over two years | 30% |
| Annual risk free rate | 3% |

## Part a (1.5 points)

Use the swap technique to calculate the time-0 asset that needs to be allocated by the reinsurer for the reinsurance contract.

### Explanation
Under the swap technique the allocated asset must satisfy both constraints; use the higher.

Variance constraint, with the two-year standard deviation of the investment yield:

$$A_{\text{variance}} = \frac{300{,}000}{0.3} = 1{,}000{,}000$$

Safety constraint, with the target investment's yield compounded over two years:

$$A_{\text{safety}} = \frac{2{,}000{,}000 - 200{,}000}{1.07^2} = 1{,}572{,}190$$

Use the higher, 1,572,190. The sample's closing line gives the needed asset as 1,157,190 (as printed).

The same result follows from writing the safety constraint with the premium in it: $1.03^2\,(A + P) \geq 2M$, so $A \geq 1.885192M - P$; substituting $P = 1.885192M - A$ into $A\,(1.07)^2 = (1.885192M - A + A)(1.03)^2 - 200{,}000$ gives $A = 1{,}572{,}190$, larger than the investment variance constraint's 1,000,000, so the safety constraint is dominant.

Alternatively, for candidates assuming the given loss mean and standard deviation were one-year statistics:

- Safety constraint: $R = \dfrac{0.1449 - 0.0609}{1.0609} \times \dfrac{2{,}000{,}000 - 200{,}000}{1.1449} \times 2 = 110{,}651.5$
- Variance constraint: $A = \dfrac{VL}{VY}$, with $\sqrt{2} \times 300{,}000 = 424{,}269$, so $A = \dfrac{424{,}269}{0.3} = 1{,}414{,}213.5$ and $R = \dfrac{0.1449 - 0.0609}{1.0609} \times 1{,}414{,}213.5 = 111{,}975$

Since the risk load is greater under the variance constraint, it is dominant: R = 11975 (as printed) and A = 1,414,213.5.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the assets allocated under both the safety and variance constraints and select the higher of the two as the final allocated asset. Candidates also needed to account for the two-year contract by calculating the investment yield and risk free rate over a two-year period and using the standard deviation of investment yield over two years.
Common errors:

- Not adjusting or incorrectly adjusting the investment yield for the two-year contract in the safety constraint formula
- Using the standard deviation of investment yield over one year in the variance constraint formula
- Using the risk free rate instead of the investment yield in the safety constraint formula

## Part b (1 point)

Calculate the reinsurance premium.

### Explanation
The risk load on the allocated asset, over the two years:

$$R = 1{,}572{,}190 \times \frac{1.07^2 - 1.03^2}{1.03^2} = 124{,}483$$

$$P = R + \frac{UL}{1.03^2} = 313{,}002$$

Equivalently, from the safety constraint, $(P + A)(1 + RF)^2 = S$:

$$(P + 1{,}572{,}189.71)(1.03)^2 = 200{,}000 \text{ (as printed)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad P = 313{,}002.11$$

Alternatively, for candidates assuming the loss statistics were one-year figures (part a's alternative):

$$P = R + \frac{UL}{1 + RF} = 111{,}975 + 400{,}000 \div 1.0609 = 489{,}013$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the reinsurance premium for the contract given the allocated asset calculated in part a. There were several equivalent formulas that candidates used to arrive at the correct reinsurance premium (see sample answers). As in part a, candidates needed to account for the two-year contract by calculating the investment yield and risk free rate over a two-year period.
Common errors:

- Not adjusting or incorrectly adjusting the investment yield and risk free rate for the twoyear contract
- Calculating the risk load but not the reinsurance premium
- Calculation errors
