A liability insurer receives a suit against its insured whose allegations include conduct that may fall under an exclusion. It wants to defend the insured while it investigates, without giving up its right to deny coverage later. Which statement about its options is correct?

- A) A reservation of rights is a denial that ends the insurer's duty to defend
- B) A reservation of rights is generally a one-sided letter, while a non-waiver agreement may be signed by both parties
- C) A non-waiver agreement is a denial of coverage, sent once the investigation is complete
- D) Neither is needed, because defending a suit can never waive a coverage defense

## Explanation

A reservation of rights notifies the insured that coverage may not apply, so the insurer can investigate, or even defend, without waiving its right to deny later. It is generally a unilateral letter from the insurer. A non-waiver agreement serves the same purpose but, in one common usage, is a bilateral agreement signed by both insurer and insured. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — A reservation of rights is not a denial; the insurer is still defending and investigating.
- **C** — A non-waiver agreement, like a reservation of rights, preserves the insurer's right to deny coverage later; it is not itself a denial.
- **D** — An insurer that defends or investigates without reserving its rights risks being held to have waived, or being estopped from asserting, its coverage defenses.
