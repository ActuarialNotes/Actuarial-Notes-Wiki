A heating contractor's own crew installed a furnace in a customer's home last year. This year, during the contractor's occurrence CGL policy period, the furnace overheats and starts a fire that does \$300,000 of damage to the house and \$20,000 of damage to the furnace itself. The customer sues; the insurer spends \$40,000 defending. The policy's each occurrence limit is \$1,000,000 and its aggregates are unused. How much does the insurer pay toward the damages, excluding defense?

- A) \$0
- B) \$300,000
- C) \$320,000
- D) \$340,000

## Explanation

Working through the analysis: the fire is an occurrence during the policy period causing property damage, so the Coverage A insuring agreement is met; it is within the products-completed operations hazard (away from the contractor's premises, from completed work). Exclusion l removes property damage to "your work" arising out of it and within that hazard — the furnace installation, done by the contractor's own crew, so no subcontractor exception applies. The house is not the contractor's work, so it is covered. Defense is a supplementary payment, outside the limit.

$$
\text{Damages paid} = 300{,}000
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats the whole claim as excluded because it arose from the insured's work; the exclusion removes only damage to the work itself.
- **C** ignores the "your work" exclusion for the furnace.
- **D** also adds the \$40,000 of defense to the damages; defense is paid in addition to, not as part of, the damages and the limit.
