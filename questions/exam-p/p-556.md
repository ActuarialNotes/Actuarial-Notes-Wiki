The profit on an individual insurance policy is normally distributed. The 60th percentile of the profit is 1000. The 80th percentile of the profit is 2000.

Calculate the 95th percentile, to the nearest 100, of the profit.

- A) $2300$
- B) $2400$
- C) $2700$
- D) $3400$
- E) $3900$

## Explanation

Two percentiles determine both parameters. Writing each as $\mu + z\sigma$ with $z_{0.60} = 0.2533$ and $z_{0.80} = 0.8416$:

$$
\begin{align*}
1000 &= \mu + 0.2533\sigma \\
2000 &= \mu + 0.8416\sigma
\end{align*}
$$

Subtracting eliminates $\mu$:

$$
\begin{align*}
1000 &= (0.8416 - 0.2533)\sigma = 0.5883\sigma \\
\sigma &= 1699.9
\end{align*}
$$

Back-substituting gives $\mu = 1000 - 0.2533(1699.9) = 569.3$.

Now the 95th percentile, with $z_{0.95} = 1.6449$:

$$
\begin{align*}
x_{0.95} &= 569.3 + 1.6449(1699.9) \\
         &= 3365 \\
         &\approx \mathbf{3400}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 2700, is roughly $2000 + 1000\cdot(15/20)$ — extrapolating linearly in the *percentile* rather than in the $z$-score. Percentiles are linear in $z$, not in probability, and the standard normal's tail stretches out sharply past the 80th percentile, which is what carries the answer well beyond 2700.
