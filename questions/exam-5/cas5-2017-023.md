Given the following data as of December 31, 2016:

| Accident Year | Earned Premium | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 2,000 | 390 | 875 | 1,135 | 1,265 |
| 2014 | 2,260 | 425 | 1,065 | 1,355 | |
| 2015 | 2,730 | 564 | 1,267 | | |
| 2016 | 3,215 | 619 | | | |

The 12 / 24 / 36 / 48 columns are cumulative paid claims as of that many months.

- A court decision on December 31, 2014 led to a 20% increase in severity for all payments occurring after the decision.
- The company took a rate change of +20% effective on January 1, 2014.
- Policies are annual and are written evenly throughout the year.
- There is no development beyond 48 months.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate accident year 2016 ultimate claims using the paid claim development technique, incorporating the impact of the court decision.

### Answer
1,915

### Explanation
The court decision raises severity by 20% for every payment made **after** 12/31/2014 — that is, every payment made in calendar year 2015 or later. Payments made in CY 2013 and CY 2014 are therefore at a *lower* severity level than the environment the 2016 accident year will be paid in, and must be restated upward by 20% before the triangle can be developed.

Cumulative dollars cannot be adjusted directly, because a cumulative cell mixes pre- and post-decision payments. The adjustment has to be made on the **incremental** diagonal, since each incremental cell is exactly one calendar year of payments.

**Step 1 — de-cumulate the triangle into incremental payments.** The cell at accident year $a$ and age $m$ was paid in calendar year $a + m/12 - 1$:

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 390 | 485 | 260 | 130 |
| 2014 | 425 | 640 | 290 | |
| 2015 | 564 | 703 | | |
| 2016 | 619 | | | |

The two oldest diagonals are the pre-decision payments: CY 2013 is AY 2013 at 12 months (390), and CY 2014 is AY 2013 at 24 months (485) together with AY 2014 at 12 months (425). Everything else was paid in 2015 or 2016 and is already at the post-decision level.

**Step 2 — restate the pre-decision payments to the current severity level** by multiplying those three cells by 1.20:

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | $390(1.2) = 468$ | $485(1.2) = 582$ | 260 | 130 |
| 2014 | $425(1.2) = 510$ | 640 | 290 | |
| 2015 | 564 | 703 | | |
| 2016 | 619 | | | |

**Step 3 — re-cumulate:**

| AY | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 468 | 1,050 | 1,310 | 1,440 |
| 2014 | 510 | 1,150 | 1,440 | |
| 2015 | 564 | 1,267 | | |
| 2016 | 619 | | | |

**Step 4 — age-to-age factors on the adjusted triangle:**

| AY | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | $1{,}050/468 = 2.24$ | $1{,}310/1{,}050 = 1.25$ | $1{,}440/1{,}310 = 1.10$ |
| 2014 | $1{,}150/510 = 2.25$ | $1{,}440/1{,}150 = 1.25$ | |
| 2015 | $1{,}267/564 = 2.25$ | | |
| **Selected** | **2.25** | **1.25** | **1.10** |

There is no development beyond 48 months, so the tail factor is 1.00.

**Step 5 — develop accident year 2016.** Its 12-month paid claims of 619 were paid in CY 2016 and need no adjustment:

$$\text{Ultimate}_{2016} = 619 \times 2.25 \times 1.25 \times 1.10 = 619 \times 3.09375 = \mathbf{1{,}915}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the mechanics and assumptions associated with the paid loss development method and Cape Cod technique. Candidates were expected to adjust the loss triangle for a legal change and to on-level premium for a rate change.

Candidates were expected to know the mechanics and assumptions associated with the paid loss development method.

Common errors included:

- Not adjusting the triangle for the law change.
- Adjusting the triangle for payments occurring after December 31, 2014. These payments were made after the law change and already at the higher severity level.
- Adjusting the triangle to reflect the higher severity in the 2013 and 2014 diagonals, but not carrying this adjustment to the 2015 and 2016 diagonals for accident years 2013 and 2014. For example, the incremental portion of the cumulative payments for accident year 2015 at 36 months (as printed) that were made in 2013 and 2014 needed to be increased to account for the higher severity.
- Ignoring the 2013-to-2014 and 2014-to-2015 diagonals when selecting LDFs to only use data post-law change. The question specifically asks for the impact of the court decision to be included, and one diagonal of data is not sufficient to incorporate the impact.

## Part b (1.5 points)

Calculate the accident year 2016 ultimate claims using the Cape Cod technique, incorporating the impact of the court decision.

### Answer
1,972

### Explanation
Cape Cod estimates one loss ratio from the whole triangle, using **on-level premium scaled down to the portion already "used up"** — the fraction of ultimate claims each year has paid to date — as the denominator. Both sides of that ratio must be on a common basis: premium on level for the +20% rate change, claims on level for the court decision.

**Step 1 — on-level factors.** The +20% change is effective 1/1/2014 on annual policies written evenly, so under the parallelogram method:

| AY | Portion earned at new rate | Average rate level | On-level factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 0% | 1.000 | $1.2/1.000 = 1.200$ |
| 2014 | 50% | $0.5(1.0) + 0.5(1.2) = 1.100$ | $1.2/1.100 = 1.091$ |
| 2015 | 100% | 1.200 | $1.2/1.200 = 1.000$ |
| 2016 | 100% | 1.200 | 1.000 |

The 2014 factor is where candidates lose points: 1.10 is the *average rate level* for the year, not the factor. The on-level factor is the ratio of the current level to that average, $1.2 / 1.1 = 1.091$.

**Step 2 — cumulative development factors** from part a, used to measure how much premium each year has used up:

$$\text{CDF}_{2013} = 1.000, \quad \text{CDF}_{2014} = 1.10, \quad \text{CDF}_{2015} = 1.25(1.10) = 1.375, \quad \text{CDF}_{2016} = 2.25(1.25)(1.10) = 3.09375$$

**Step 3 — used-up on-level earned premium**, $\text{OLEP} \times (1/\text{CDF})$, against the **court-decision-adjusted** cumulative paid claims from part a:

| AY | EP | OLF | On-level EP | $1/\text{CDF}$ | Used-up OLEP | Adjusted paid claims |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 2,000 | 1.200 | 2,400 | 1.000 | 2,400 | 1,440 |
| 2014 | 2,260 | 1.091 | 2,465 | 0.909 | 2,242 | 1,440 |
| 2015 | 2,730 | 1.000 | 2,730 | 0.727 | 1,985 | 1,267 |
| 2016 | 3,215 | 1.000 | 3,215 | 0.323 | 1,039 | 619 |
| **Total** | | | | | **7,666** | **4,766** |

**Step 4 — the Cape Cod loss ratio:**

$$\text{ELR} = \frac{4{,}766}{7{,}666} = 62.2\%$$

**Step 5 — accident year 2016 ultimate.** Add expected unpaid claims to what has actually been paid. AY 2016 is already at current rate level, so its on-level premium is 3,215:

$$\text{Ultimate}_{2016} = 619 + 3{,}215 \times 0.622 \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{3.09375}\right) = 619 + 3{,}215 \times 0.622 \times 0.6768 = \mathbf{1{,}972}$$

*Alternatively:* a second published sample developed each year's adjusted paid claims to ultimate ($1{,}440$, $1{,}584$, $1{,}742$, $1{,}915$; total $6{,}681$) and divided by total on-level earned premium of $10{,}810$ for a loss ratio of $61.8\%$, giving $619 + 3{,}215(0.618)(1 - 1/3.09375) = 1{,}964$. That is a chain-ladder-weighted loss ratio rather than the used-up-premium ratio Cape Cod is defined on; the two agree closely here, but the used-up-premium form in step 3 is the method being tested.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the mechanics and assumptions associated with Cape Cod method.

Common errors included:

- Incorrectly calculating on-level factor. For example, calculating the correct average rate factor (1.1) for AY 2014 but not adjusting the rate to the current level. The correct on level factor is 1.2/1.1 = 1.09.
- Not using court decision adjusted paid losses for the loss ratio calculation.
- Selecting an expected loss ratio rather than using aggregated losses and used up premium for the Cape Cod loss ratio calculation.
- Using the incorrect formula for the ultimate loss calculation.
