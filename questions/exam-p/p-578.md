An insurance company offers two kinds of insurance: automobile insurance and homeowners insurance. This year, the company's profit from automobile insurance is normally distributed with mean 200 and standard deviation 400. The company's profit from homeowners insurance is normally distributed with mean 400 and standard deviation 300. The two profits are independent.

Let $Z$ represent the standard normal random variable.

Determine the probability that the company's overall profit this year is positive.

- A) $P[Z > -1.83]$
- B) $P[Z > -1.20]$
- C) $P[Z > -0.86]$
- D) $P[Z > -0.83]$
- E) $P[Z > -0.50]$

## Explanation

The overall profit is a sum of independent normal variables, hence normal:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[S] &= 200 + 400 = 600 \\
\text{Var}(S) &= 400^2 + 300^2 = 250{,}000 \\
\text{SD}(S) &= 500
\end{align*}
$$

Another 3-4-5 triple: the combined standard deviation is 500, not the 700 that adding would give.

Standardizing the break-even point:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S > 0] &= P\left[Z > \frac{0 - 600}{500}\right] \\
         &= \mathbf{P[Z > -1.20]}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), $-0.86$, is $600/700$ — the answer from adding the standard deviations, which would apply only to perfectly correlated lines. Independence is what buys the improvement from $-0.86$ to $-1.20$, and hence a materially higher probability of an overall profit.
