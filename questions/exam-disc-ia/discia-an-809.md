A trade association's CGL policy (CG 00 01 04 13) runs from January 1 to December 31, 2026. In November 2025 the association posted an article on its website stating, without knowing it was untrue, that a member firm had been fined for fraud. It reposted the article in its March 2026 newsletter. The firm sues in May 2026 for libel arising from both publications. How does Coverage B respond?

- A) It does not cover the claim, because the material was first published before the policy period began
- B) It covers the March 2026 publication, because that offense was committed during the policy period
- C) It covers both publications, because the claim was made during the policy period
- D) It does not cover the claim, because libel is an intentional act that Coverage B excludes

## Explanation

Coverage B exclusion c removes personal and advertising injury arising out of oral or written publication of material whose *first* publication took place before the beginning of the policy period. The article was first published in November 2025, so republishing it in 2026 does not bring it within the policy. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** treats the republication as a new offense; the exclusion looks to the first publication.
- **C** applies a claims-made trigger. Coverage B is triggered by an offense committed during the policy period, not by when the claim is made.
- **D** — libel is a listed offense. Coverage B excludes publication *with knowledge of falsity*, and the association did not know the statement was false.
