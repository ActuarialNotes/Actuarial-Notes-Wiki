The following table summarizes the results of various claim projection techniques as of December 31, 2017 in total for all accident years:

| Projection Technique | Ultimate Claims Estimate |
|---|---|
| Reported Development | \$6,500 |
| Paid Development | \$4,800 |
| Expected Claims | \$4,900 |
| Reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson | \$6,100 |
| Paid Bornhuetter-Ferguson | \$4,850 |
| Reported Berquist-Sherman | \$4,900 |
| Paid Berquist-Sherman | \$4,850 |

- There are no large losses.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Describe how an operational change could be causing the variation in the ultimate claims estimates.

### Explanation
An **increase in case reserve adequacy** explains the pattern. The techniques that rely on reported claims — reported development (6,500) and reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson (6,100) — are well above the others, because the strengthened case reserves on the latest diagonal are projected with historical factors built from weaker reserves. The paid techniques (4,800 and 4,850) are unaffected by case reserves, and the reported Berquist-Sherman technique, which restates case reserves to a consistent adequacy, gives 4,900 — in line with the paid techniques and the expected claims technique. Payment patterns have not changed, since the paid development, paid Bornhuetter-Ferguson and paid Berquist-Sherman results agree.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to analyze various claim projection results in order to identify operational changes. Candidates were expected to provide diagnostics which could support operational changes.

Candidates were expected to discuss how increasing case reserve adequacy would cause the increases seen in the reported development and reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson (BF) techniques that was corrected in the reported Berquist-Sherman (BS) technique.

Common errors included:

- Naming an operational change but not providing any support from the technique projections provided in the question.
- Identifying a change in payment pattern. A change in payment pattern is not supported since the paid development, paid BF, and paid BS techniques all have similar results.

## Part b (1 point)

Identify and briefly describe how two diagnostics could indicate the presence of the operational change identified in part a. above.

### Explanation
- **Average case outstanding** (case outstanding ÷ open claim counts): if case reserves have been strengthened, the average case at each maturity will rise along the latest diagonals faster than the expected severity trend.
- **Ratio of paid to reported claims:** with case reserves strengthened but payments unchanged, reported claims rise relative to paid, so the paid-to-reported ratios decline along the latest diagonals compared with earlier years at the same maturity.

(The ratio of reported claims to reported claim counts, rising faster than severity trend, is another diagnostic of the same change.)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify two diagnostics that could be used to demonstrate the presence of the operational change identified in part a.

Common errors included:

- Stating a diagnostic but failing to describe how the diagnostic shows evidence for the operational change.
- Identifying diagnostics that did not relate to the identified operational change or incorrectly relating the results to the operational change.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Identify and briefly describe a diagnostic that highlights the similarity between the paid development technique and paid Berquist-Sherman technique estimates.

### Explanation
The **ratio of closed claim counts to ultimate claim counts** (the disposal rate) at each maturity. If it is stable down each column, the rate of claim settlement has not changed. The paid Berquist-Sherman technique adjusts only for changes in settlement rate, so when the disposal rates are stable it makes almost no adjustment and its estimate matches the paid development technique — as it does here (4,850 versus 4,800).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the relationship between the paid claims development and paid Berquist-Sherman techniques.

Common errors included:

- Incorrectly defining disposal rate as closed claim counts / reported claim counts
- Some candidates cited the paid-to-reported triangle, but in this case looking at this diagnostic would not show consistency, as there are changes in case reserve adequacy.
