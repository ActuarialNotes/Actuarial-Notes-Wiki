In State X, an injured driver collects medical expenses and lost wages from her own insurer whether or not she caused the accident. She may also sue the other driver for pain and suffering for any injury, however minor. How is State X's auto injury system classified?

- A) A no-fault state with a verbal threshold
- B) A no-fault state with a monetary threshold
- C) A traditional tort state
- D) An add-on state

## Explanation

The two features to check are whether the driver's own insurer pays first-party benefits regardless of fault, and whether the right to sue is restricted. State X pays first-party benefits but does not restrict lawsuits, which is an **add-on** system: first-party benefits added on to traditional tort liability. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — A verbal threshold restricts suits for pain and suffering to injuries meeting a statutory description of serious injury; State X has no restriction.
- **B** — A monetary threshold allows a suit only once medical expenses reach a stated amount; State X has no such threshold.
- **C** — A traditional tort state has no first-party benefits paid regardless of fault.
