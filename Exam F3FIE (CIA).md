# Exam F3FIE
**F3FIE – Risk Management** is an exam of the finance, investments and ERM track of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries' Fellowship (FCIA) pathway.

> [!question]- About the exam
>
> The aim of this exam is for candidates to develop the following skills:
>
> 1. Understand the main principles and techniques of enterprise risk management (ERM) and economic capital modelling that are relevant to life insurance.
>
> 2. Apply these principles and techniques within the context of investments.
>
> 3. Understand how ERM and economic capital modelling links to wider business processes (e.g., business planning, pricing, and capital setting).
>
> 4. Create and evaluate hypothetical scenarios, including using judgement to assess the implications of possible actions and to develop appropriate investment proposals or recommendations.
>
> Exams are administered as an open-book, four-hour exam requiring analysis in the context of a problem and written responses to specific questions. Candidates will complete the exam through the CIA learning management system.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- Principal terms {0%}
>
> 1. Define [[Principal|principal]] terms used in [[Enterprise Risk Management|enterprise risk management]], [[Economic Capital Modelling|economic capital modelling]], and stress and scenario testing. (A1)

> [!example]- ERM concept, framework, and process {10%}
>
> 1. Apply the concept of [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]]. (D3)
> 2. Apply the framework for [[Risk Management|risk management]] and control within an [[Insurer|insurer]]. (D3)
> 3. Describe regulatory requirements related to [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]] including [[ORSA|own-risk and solvency assessment]]. (A2)
> 4. Describe the perspectives of [[Rating Agency|credit-rating agencies]]. (B2)
> 5. Reflect [[Data Issues|data issues]] in general and special considerations with respect to [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]]. (C3)
> 6. Demonstrate how to determine and articulate: (C3)
>    - a. [[Risk Appetite|Risk appetite]].
>    - b. [[Risk Identification|Risk identification]].
>    - c. [[Risk Assessment|Risk assessment]].
>    - d. [[Risk Monitoring|Risk monitoring]].
>    - e. [[Risk Appetite|Risk limits]].
>    - f. [[Risk Mitigation|Risk mitigation]].
>    - g. [[Risk Capacity|Risk capacity]].
>    - h. [[Risk Tolerance|Risk tolerances]].
>    - i. [[Desired Risk Profile|Desired risk profile]].
>    - j. [[Risk]] objectives.
>    - k. [[Risk Reporting|Risk reporting]].
> 7. Assess the implications of financial and other [[Risk|risks]] and opportunities for strategic planning. (C4)
> 8. Describe the [[Risk Management|risk management]] control cycle, including the relevance of [[External Influence|external influences]] and [[Emerging Risk|emerging risks]]. (C2)
> 9. Utilize methods to identify [[Risk|risks]] and their causes and implications. (D4)

> [!example]- Risk categories and risk identification {10%}
>
> 1. Explain what is meant by [[Risk|risk]] and uncertainty, including different definitions and concepts of [[Risk|risk]]. (B2)
> 2. Describe [[Risk Taxonomy|risk taxonomy]], including an awareness of how [[Individual Risk Model|individual risks]] might be categorized in different ways. (B1)
> 3. Describe and assess common [[Risk|risks]] faced by [[Life Insurer|life insurers]], including: (B2)
>    - a. [[Market Risk|market risk]],
>    - b. [[Currency Risk|currency risk]],
>    - c. [[Credit Risk|credit risk]],
>    - d. [[Spread Risk|spread risk]],
>    - e. [[Liquidity Risk|liquidity risk]],
>    - f. [[Interest Rate Risk|interest rate risk]],
>    - g. [[Prepayment Risk|prepayment risk]],
>    - h. [[Reinvestment Risk|reinvestment risk]],
>    - i. [[Equity Risk|equity risk]],
>    - j. [[Hazard-insurance Risk|hazard/insurance risk]],
>    - k. [[Inflationary Risk|inflationary risk]],
>    - l. ESG and [[Climate-Related Risk|climate-related risk]],
>    - m. [[Pricing Risk|pricing risk]],
>    - n. [[Foreign Exchange Risk|foreign exchange risk]], and
>    - o. country and [[Sovereign Credit Risk|sovereign credit risk]].
> 4. Analyze the relationship between [[Systemic Risk|systemic risk]] vs. non-systemic and specific [[Risk|risk]] vs. concentration of [[Risk|risk]]. (B4)

> [!example]- Economic capital modelling and aggregation of risks {25%}
>
> 1. Describe the extent to which each of the [[Risk|risks]] in 3.3 can be amenable to quantitative analysis. (B2)
> 2. Demonstrate an understanding of the use of [[Correlation|correlation]] measures.
>    - a. Describe [[Enterprise-Wide Risk Aggregation|enterprise-wide risk aggregation]] techniques that incorporate the use of [[Correlation|correlation]]. (C2)
>    - b. Apply different [[Correlation|correlation]] measures based on their [[Relative Merit|relative merits]] and implications. (C4)
> 3. Apply copulas as part of the process of [[Modelling Multivariate Risk|modelling multivariate risks]] and evaluate different types of copulas for a given situation. (C5)
> 4. Reflect the importance of the tails of [[Probability Distributions|distributions]] and [[Tail Correlation|tail correlations]]. (C4)
> 5. Describe how [[Extreme-Value Theory|extreme-value theory]] can be used to help [[Model Risk|model risks]] that have a [[Low Probability|low probability]]. (C2)
> 6. Demonstrate how model and [[Parameter Risk|parameter risk]] can be incorporated into an [[Economic Capital|economic capital]] model. (C4)
> 7. Use [[Economic Capital|economic capital]] models in the overall [[ERM Decision-Making Process|ERM decision-making process]]. (C4)
>    - a. Describe the development and use of models for [[Decision-Making|decision-making]] purposes in [[Enterprise Risk Management|ERM]]. (B2)
>    - b. Demonstrate how the [[Decision-Making Process Consider|decision-making process considers]] the [[Organization's Risk Appetite|organization’s risk appetite]] and [[Corporate Governance|corporate governance]], and builds on the results of [[Stochastic Modelling|stochastic modelling]], scenario analysis, [[Stress Testing|stress testing]], and analysis of model and [[Parameter Risk|parameter risk]]. (D4)
>    - c. Evaluate different types of models for a given purpose. (D5)

> [!example]- Risk measurement and assessment {25%}
>
> 1. Using common [[Risk Measure|risk measures]] (such as VAR, TVAR, and [[Probability|probability]] of ruin), determine [[Risk Exposure|risk exposures]] and tolerances using these measures. (C3)
> 2. Understand the key considerations in deriving and applying [[Economic Capital-Modelling|economic capital-modelling]] techniques using deterministic and stochastic models. (D2)
> 3. Recommend a specific choice of model based on the results of both quantitative and qualitative analysis of financial and [[Insurance Data|insurance data]]. (D5)
> 4. Describe approaches, including parameterization and [[Model Validation|validation]], for the assessment of [[Regulatory Capital|capital requirements]] for the following [[Risk|risk]] types: (C2)
>    - a. Market.
>    - b. [[Credit]].
>    - c. Liquidity.
>    - d. Regulatory.
>    - e. Operational.
>    - f. Climate related .
> 5. Explain the advantages and limitations of different [[Risk Metrics|risk metrics]], including value at [[Risk|risk]], sensitivities, Greeks, [[Minor Greek|minor Greeks]], etc. (C4)
> 6. Describe the practical considerations including [[Data Availability|data availability]], parameterization, and [[Model Validation|validation]] procedures) that should be borne in mind when [[Undertaking Capital Modelling|undertaking capital modelling]]. (D2)
> 7. Calculate [[Regulatory Capital|regulatory capital]] requirement. (C3)

> [!example]- Stress and scenario testing {15%}
>
> 1. Describe the use of scenario analysis and [[Stress Testing|stress testing]] in the [[Risk-Measurement Process|risk-measurement process]], including the advantages and disadvantages of each. (B2)
> 2. Describe the [[Appointed Actuary's Responsibility|Appointed Actuary’s responsibilities]] related to [[FCT|financial condition testing]] ([[FCT]]). (A2)
> 3. Explain the primary categories of [[Risk|risk]] associated with [[Climate Change|climate change]] from an investment perspective. (A2)
> 4. Describe the actuary’s role with respect to [[Climate-Change Risk|climate-change risk]]. (B2)

> [!example]- Economic capital and ORSA {15%}
>
> 1. Describe the concept of economic measures of value (e.g., market-consistent embedded value) and demonstrate their uses in the [[Risk Management|risk management]] and [[Corporate Decision-Making Process|corporate decision-making processes]]. (B6)
> 2. Determine and evaluate the key elements of [[ORSA|own-risk and solvency assessment]] ([[ORSA]]) and [[FCT]], specifically [[Risk Identification|risk identification]] and assessment, quantification of [[Risk|risk]] to [[Capital|capital]], [[Board Oversight|board oversight]] and [[Senior Management Responsibility|senior management responsibility]], monitoring, reporting, and [[Internal Control|internal controls]]. (C5)
> 3. Demonstrate an understanding of [[Capital|capital]] calculations:
>    - a. Utilize the concept of economic measures of value and [[Capital|capital]] and their uses in [[Corporate Decision-Making Process|corporate decision-making processes]]. (D5)
>    - b. Evaluate different [[Risk Measure|risk measures]] and [[Economic Capital|capital-assessment]] approaches. (D5)
>    - c. Demonstrate the ability to develop a [[Capital|capital]] model for a [[Representative Financial Firm|representative financial firm]]. (D6)
> 4. Compare techniques for [[Allocating Capital|allocating capital]] across an organization. (C4)
