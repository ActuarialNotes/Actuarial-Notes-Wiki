An insurance company has experienced a large claim in accident year 2016. Given the following information for accident year 2016:

| Estimated Ultimate ALAE Without Adjustment for Large Claim | |
|---|---|
| Paid ALAE Development Technique | Paid ALAE to Paid Claims Only Ratio Technique |
| \$11,000 | \$12,000 |

| | Paid Claims Only | Paid ALAE | Ultimate Claims Only | Ultimate ALAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All Claims | \$7,000 | \$2,000 | \$30,000 | Not Provided |
| Large Claim | \$0 | \$1,500 | \$6,000 | \$2,000 |

## Part a (0.75 points)

Estimate ultimate ALAE for accident year 2016 using the paid ALAE development technique, including an adjustment for the large claim.

### Answer
\$4,750

### Explanation
The large claim has already consumed \$1,500 of the \$2,000 paid ALAE while paying nothing on the claim itself. Developing that \$2,000 with an all-claims factor treats \$1,500 of highly atypical, front-loaded expense as if it were ordinary immature ALAE, which is why the unadjusted estimate of \$11,000 is distorted. The fix is to **exclude the large claim, develop what is left, then add the large claim's ultimate ALAE back in** — and that ultimate, \$2,000, is given.

**Step 1 — recover the implied development factor.** The unadjusted estimate was built by developing all paid ALAE to ultimate, so:

$$\text{CDF} = \frac{\text{Unadjusted ultimate ALAE}}{\text{Paid ALAE}} = \frac{11{,}000}{2{,}000} = 5.5$$

**Step 2 — develop the ALAE excluding the large claim:**

$$\text{Paid ALAE excl. large} = 2{,}000 - 1{,}500 = 500$$

$$\text{Ultimate ALAE excl. large} = 500 \times 5.5 = 2{,}750$$

**Step 3 — add back the large claim's given ultimate ALAE:**

$$\text{Ultimate ALAE} = 2{,}750 + 2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$4{,}750}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to back out the paid ALAE development factor implied by the unadjusted estimate, apply it to paid ALAE excluding the large claim, and add the large claim's given ultimate ALAE.

Common errors included deriving a large-claim adjustment factor and applying it to the all-claims-excluding-large ultimate, which throws away the large claim ultimate the question supplies; omitting the large claim ultimate from the final total; and calculating the development factor incorrectly.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Estimate ultimate ALAE for accident year 2016 using the paid ALAE to paid claims ratio technique, including an adjustment for the large claim.

### Answer
\$4,400

### Explanation
This technique projects the **ratio** of ALAE to claims, not ALAE itself, so the development factor is the ratio of the ultimate ALAE-to-claims ratio to the paid ALAE-to-claims ratio. The large claim must be stripped out of the ratio being developed and its ultimate ALAE added at the end.

**Step 1 — recover the implied ratio development factor** from the unadjusted figures:

$$\text{CDF} = \frac{\text{Ultimate ALAE} / \text{Ultimate claims}}{\text{Paid ALAE} / \text{Paid claims}} = \frac{12{,}000/30{,}000}{2{,}000/7{,}000} = \frac{0.400}{0.2857} = 1.4$$

**Step 2 — develop the paid ratio excluding the large claim.** The large claim has paid \$1,500 of ALAE and \$0 of claims, so removing it changes only the numerator:

$$\text{Paid ratio excl. large} = \frac{2{,}000 - 1{,}500}{7{,}000 - 0} = \frac{500}{7{,}000} = 0.0714$$

$$\text{Ultimate ratio excl. large} = 1.4 \times 0.0714 = 0.10$$

**Step 3 — apply the ratio to ultimate claims excluding the large claim, then add the large claim's ultimate ALAE:**

$$\text{Ultimate claims excl. large} = 30{,}000 - 6{,}000 = 24{,}000$$

$$\text{Ultimate ALAE} = 0.10 \times 24{,}000 + 2{,}000 = 2{,}400 + 2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$4{,}400}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to compute the development factor appropriate to the *ratio* method, apply it to the paid ALAE-to-paid-claims ratio excluding the large claim, multiply the developed ratio by ultimate claims excluding the large claim, and then add the large claim's ultimate ALAE.

Common errors included computing the factor as a straight paid ALAE development factor rather than a ratio-to-ratio factor; applying no development to the paid ratio at all; deriving a large-claim adjustment factor and applying it to the excluding-large ultimate, which ignores the large claim ultimate given in the question; and failing to add the large claim ultimate back in.
