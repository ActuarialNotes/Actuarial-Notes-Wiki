## Part a (0.5 points)

As a result of the decision made by the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council on the 1910 Federal Insurance Act, describe the two ways in which a domestic Canadian insurance company can operate in more than one province.

### Explanation
- Federally incorporated insurer which has both status and capacity to operate in any province
- Incorporate in one province and obtain permission from each other province it wants to operate in

Alternatively:

- Be federally incorporated
- Be provincially incorporated and have permission (license) from the other provinces it wishes to operate in

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand the ways in which a domestic Canadian insurance company can operate in more than one province.
Common errors included:

- Stating that a company could use different sales channels
- Stating that a company could be incorporated in more than one province

## Part b (1 point)

Briefly describe four aspects considered by Canada's Minister of Finance in determining whether letters patents should be issued for the incorporation of an insurance company.

### Explanation
- Contribution to the financial system
- Soundness of the business plan
- Level of capital to support the company
- Skill, competence and integrity of the person that will operate the business

Alternatively:

- Nature and sufficiency of capital
- Soundness of business plan
- Whether the company would be in the best interest of Canadians
- In the case of a foreign company, how a Canadian company would be treated in their jurisdiction

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the aspects Canada's Minister of Finance considers when incorporating an insurance company.

Common errors included:

- Not providing enough information, such as stating only "business plan" or "capital"
- Stating "must appoint an actuary and auditor", which is not a consideration of Canada's Minister of Finance
- Stating "company must meet the minimum capital required." A company must have more than the minimum required capital.
