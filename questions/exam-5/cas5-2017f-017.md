The following information is available for a private passenger automobile insurer:

- The insurer started writing business five years ago.
- External data from other private passenger automobile insurers is used to supplement the insurer's data in estimation of unpaid claims.
- Unpaid claims have been estimated historically on a combined bodily injury and property damage basis.
- Internal data shows that the bodily injury claims take longer to reach ultimate than the property damage claims.

Internal management has asked the reserving actuary to begin estimating reserves separately for bodily injury and property damage claims using only internal data.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Fully discuss an argument to support this proposed change.

### Explanation
The proposal has **two** distinct pieces — splitting bodily injury (BI) from property damage (PD), and dropping the external data — and a full argument has to address both.

**Splitting BI from PD raises homogeneity.** Development techniques assume the historical pattern in the data applies to the immature years. The insurer's own data already shows BI reaching ultimate more slowly than PD, so a combined triangle blends two genuinely different patterns into one average. That average only projects correctly if the BI/PD mix is stable; as soon as the mix drifts — a shift in coverage purchased, a change in the book, a legislative change touching only injury claims — the combined pattern becomes wrong for both segments and the combined estimate is biased. Separating the two produces homogeneous groupings whose selected factors actually describe the claims they are applied to, and therefore more accurate unpaid claim estimates.

**Separate segments also improve diagnostics.** With BI and PD analyzed apart, a change in frequency, severity, settlement rate or case adequacy can be attributed to the segment where it is occurring rather than being masked or diluted by the other. Emerging trends are visible earlier, which supports both reserving and pricing decisions and helps the insurer's competitive position.

**Using internal data only improves the relevance of the pattern.** Industry or peer data reflects other companies' mix of business, underwriting, claim handling philosophy, case reserve adequacy and settlement practice. If the insurer's operations differ from that composite in any of those respects, the external pattern is systematically wrong for this book. Five years of data is enough to have produced a meaningful history for the shorter-tailed PD coverage, and the insurer's own experience is by construction the most relevant to its own claims.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate general knowledge with respect to: homogeneity and credibility of data, fundamentals of different types of insurance, types of data and their sources, understanding of 'development patterns', etc.

Candidates were expected to demonstrate advantages and disadvantages for BOTH separating Auto Claims by claim type with respect to a reserve analysis, and moving from supplementing internal with external data to analyzing with internal data only.

Candidates were expected to demonstrate an understanding of the advantages/benefits that come from: separately analyzing PD vs BI claims with respect to a reserve analysis, AND those that come from reliance only on the company's internal data.

Common errors included:

- Only addressing the claim type split, or the internal vs external data component, but not both.
- Only responding with an acknowledgment that BI vs PD claims develop differently, as the question states, but fail to appropriately tie that to accuracy of the reserve estimate or other diagnostics/conclusions that may be drawn from evaluating claim types separately.
- Broadly assuming that insurers writing business for 5 years results in fully credible internal data, even by claim type. Candidates who responded with this, generally contradicted this statement in part b (appropriately), acknowledging 5 years is too immature, especially for BI claims to be fully reliant on internal data.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Fully discuss an argument against this proposed change.

### Explanation
The counter-argument runs on the same two pieces, and turns on **credibility and volume**.

**Five years of internal history is thin, especially for BI.** Bodily injury is a long-tailed coverage; with only five accident years the insurer has not yet observed a single cohort run to ultimate, so the tail of its own pattern is unsupported and would have to be extrapolated. The very external data the actuary would be discarding is what currently supplies that tail and stabilizes the early-maturity factors. Removing it means the estimate rests entirely on immature, unproven emergence.

**Splitting the data compounds the credibility problem.** Whatever volume exists is roughly halved when the triangle is cut by claim type, so each segment's link ratios become noisier. Combining "internal only" with "split by coverage" therefore attacks credibility from both directions at once, and the result can easily be more volatile — and less accurate — than the combined, supplemented estimate it replaced. The insurer has been supplementing with external data precisely because its own data was not credible on its own.

**There is also a cost and a communication issue.** Two analyses instead of one means more data preparation, more selections and more review for every valuation, and the incremental accuracy may not justify that. And if the split, thinner estimates swing materially from period to period, the reserve movements flow through to the financial results and to the key metrics investors, rating agencies and regulators watch, which can be misleading about the underlying performance of the book.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate an understanding of the disadvantages/drawbacks that come from: separately analyzing PD vs BI claims with respect to a reserve analysis, AND those that come from reliance only on the company's internal data.

Common errors included:

- Only addressing the claim type split, or the internal vs external data component, but not both.
- Generalization of 'lack of credibility;' there were opportunities to mention both lack of credibility for internal data only due to immaturity of company data, as well as credibility lost by further splitting down to claim type level, which would have received additional credit.
- Mentioning the possibility of claims not being able to be broken out, but the question has already stated that internal data is down to this grain since differences in development patterns have been recognized.
