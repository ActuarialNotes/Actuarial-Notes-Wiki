An office's BPP has a 90% coinsurance percentage. A fire spreads from a trash bin and destroys five ornamental shrubs worth \$300 each and a rooftop satellite dish worth \$700. The shrubs and the dish are not stock. What does the outdoor property coverage extension pay?

- A) \$1,000
- B) \$1,250
- C) \$1,950
- D) \$2,200

## Explanation

Outdoor shrubs and antennas, including satellite dishes, are property not covered except under the outdoor property extension, which applies because coinsurance is at least 80%. Fire is one of its five causes of loss. It pays at most \$250 for any one shrub and \$1,000 for the occurrence:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Shrubs} &= 5 \times \min(300,\ 250) \\
&= 1{,}250 \\
\text{Paid} &= \min(1{,}250 + 700,\ 1{,}000) \\
&= 1{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — \$1,250 applies the per-plant cap but leaves out the dish and ignores the \$1,000 occurrence limit.
- **C** — \$1,950 applies the per-plant cap but not the \$1,000 limit for the occurrence.
- **D** — \$2,200 is the full value, with neither cap applied.
