## Overview of Bundled Payments
- Under a bundled payment the payor makes a single payment for all services related to a treatment or condition, possibly spanning multiple providers in multiple settings; in practice the services in the bundle are defined by a payor-provider contract.
- Bundled payments appear in contracts when they bring advantages to both providers and payors, for example to attract more business, including self-pay patients, medical tourism and payor contracts, and to engage physicians.

## Recent Developments with Bundled Payments
- Bundled Payments for Care Improvement Initiative: four bundled payment options that vary in services included, time frames covered and whether payments are prospective or retrospective.
- Medicare ACE demonstration: from 2009, bundled payments for acute care episodes for select orthopedic and cardiovascular inpatient procedures, awarded by competitive bid.

## A Simple Bundled Payment Model: Hip or Knee Replacement
- Figure 1: Bundled Payment Model
    - An example splits a $30,000 bundled payment among providers delivering services in the preoperative, operative and post-discharge periods.
- Bundled payments change the risk: providers bear the risk for costs greater than the fixed fee, including outliers and readmissions.

## Illustration
- A claim data analysis of hip- and knee-replacement surgery uses the 2008 Thomson Reuters MarketScan database, summarizing claims by 30 days preoperative, operative inpatient stay and 60 days post-discharge.
- Figures 2 to 8 show allowed dollars and resources (relative value units) by treatment phase, by region, between physician and facility during the inpatient stay, and average allowed costs per case.

## Considerations in Contracting for Bundled Payments
- Defining the episode
- Evaluating catastrophic risk
- Financial stability for low case loads
- Determining provider allocation of funds
- Distinguishing case severity
- Quality outcome requirements
- Administrative complexity
- Risk-sharing alternatives
- Potential for increased utilization

## Conclusion
- Bundled payment arrangements have the potential to reduce claim costs for payors and create market and financial advantages for providers, but they change the nature of risk.
