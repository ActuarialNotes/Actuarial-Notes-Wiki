Given the following information as of December 31, 2018:

| Accident Year | Incurred Losses (\$000) | Chain Ladder Ultimate (\$000) | Bornhuetter-Ferguson Ultimate (\$000) | Bayesian Ultimate (\$000) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 4,400 | 4,400 | 4,400 | 4,400 |
| 2014 | 5,000 | 6,000 | 5,833 | 5,840 |
| 2015 | 2,800 | 4,000 | 4,300 | 4,278 |
| 2016 | 3,000 | 5,400 | 5,222 | 5,301 |
| 2017 | 4,000 | 10,000 | 7,000 | 7,750 |
| 2018 | 750 | 3,000 | 4,500 | |

- $\alpha_{2018} = 12{,}500$
- $\beta_{2018} = 2.5$
- $\varphi_{2018} = 1.1$

## Part a (0.5 points)

Compare the variance of the prior distributions for accident years 2016 and 2017.

### Explanation
- AY 2016: the Bayesian estimate is only slightly closer to the BF method than to the chain ladder, so the prior's variance is moderately small, but not too small.
- AY 2017: the Bayesian estimate is very close to the BF method — a strong prior opinion, so a small variance.
- The variance of the prior for AY 2016 is larger than for AY 2017.

Alternatively, back out the credibility given to the chain ladder:

$$2016{:}\; 5{,}301 = 5{,}400\,Z + 5{,}222\,(1 - Z) \;\Rightarrow\; Z = 44.4\%$$

$$2017{:}\; 7{,}750 = 10{,}000\,Z + 7{,}000\,(1 - Z) \;\Rightarrow\; Z = 25\%$$

Since $Z$ is smaller for 2017, $\beta$ is bigger, so the variance ($\alpha/\beta^2$) is smaller in 2017.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know that when the distribution of initial expected ultimate has a high variance, the Bayesian ultimate will be closer to the chain ladder ultimate and then apply that to compare the variances of the distributions of initial expected ultimate losses between accident years. Alternatively, candidates could calculate the variances of the prior distributions and compare.
Common mistakes included:

- Stating that when the distribution of initial expected ultimate losses has a high variance, then the Bayesian ultimate will be closer to the Bornhuetter-Ferguson (B-F) ultimate.
- Defining closeness based on subtracting Bayesian from B-F and not as a ratio to the range of Chain ladder to B-F

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the Bayesian ultimate loss estimate for accident year 2018.

### Explanation
$$p_k = \frac{750}{3{,}000} = 0.25$$

$$Z = \frac{0.25}{0.25 + 1.1 \times 2.5} = 0.083$$

$$0.083 \times 3{,}000 + (1 - 0.083) \times 4{,}500 = 4{,}376$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to be able to calculate the Bayesian ultimate loss estimate.
Common mistakes included:

- Using an incorrect formula for calculating credibility
- Using expected ultimate claims as the complement of credibility instead of the B-F estimate

## Part c (0.5 points)

Calculate the accident year 2014 correlated chain ladder log mean ultimate loss given the following parameters:

- $\alpha_{2013} = 8.5172$
- $\alpha_{2014} = 8.5172$
- $\beta_6 = 0$
- $\rho = 0.2$

### Explanation
$$\mu_{2013} = 8.5172 + 0 = 8.5172$$

$$\mu_{2014} = 8.5172 + 0 + 0.2\left(\ln(4{,}400) - 8.5172\right) = 8.4916$$

(ult $= e^{8.4916} = 4{,}874$)

Alternatively, assuming a different scale was used for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (losses in dollars rather than \$000):

$$\mu_{2014} = 8.5172 + 0 + 0.2\left(\ln(4{,}400{,}000) - 8.5172\right) = 9.873$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to be able to calculate the logged mean ultimate of the Correlated Chain Ladder model using given parameters.
Common mistakes included:

- Using an incorrect equation

## Part d (0.5 points)

Briefly describe one advantage of each of the following methods:

- Correlated chain ladder method
- Bayesian method

### Explanation
Correlated chain ladder — any of:

- Incorporates correlation between accident years.
- Shown by Meyers to accurately describe the distribution of reserves.

Bayesian method — any of:

- Can incorporate expert opinion into the simulation.
- Can produce a full predictive distribution in addition to a point estimate.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know advantages of the Correlated Chain Ladder and Bayesian methods.
Common mistakes for CCL included:

- Correlated Calendar years
- Incorporate calendar year effects (trend, claim settlement rate – see CIT and CSR models)
- Passes the K-S test

Common mistakes for Bayesian included:

- It credibility weights chain ladder and Bornhuetter-Ferguson
- Minimizes MSE
- You can assume an a priori distribution
- Adds stability
