Claims arrive according to a Poisson process at a rate of 2 per year. Let $T$ be the time in years until the third claim, so that $T$ follows a gamma distribution with shape $\alpha = 3$ and scale $\theta = 0.5$.

Calculate $P(T > 1)$.

- A) $0.135$
- B) $0.271$
- C) $0.406$
- D) $0.677$
- E) $0.857$

## Explanation

For **integer** shape $\alpha$ the gamma survival function has a closed form through its Poisson link:

$$P(T > t) = \sum_{k=0}^{\alpha-1} \frac{e^{-t/\theta}\,(t/\theta)^k}{k!}$$

With $\alpha = 3$ and $t/\theta = 1/0.5 = 2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(T > 1) &= \sum_{k=0}^{2} \frac{e^{-2}\,2^k}{k!} \\
         &= e^{-2}\left(1 + 2 + 2\right) \\
         &= 5e^{-2} \\
         &= 5(0.135335) \\
         &= \mathbf{0.677}
\end{align*}
$$

The identity is a restatement, not a formula to memorize: "the 3rd claim has not arrived by time 1" is the same event as "at most 2 claims occurred in $[0,1]$", and that count is $\text{Poisson}(2)$. Recognizing this avoids integrating $x^2e^{-2x}$ by parts twice.

Choice (A) is $P(N = 0) = e^{-2}$ alone; choice (C) is $3e^{-2}$, which stops the sum one term early.
