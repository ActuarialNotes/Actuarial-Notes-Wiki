You assume that the number of claims for a single policy, $Y$, follows a Poisson distribution with mean parameter $\lambda$.

$$
f(Y \mid \lambda) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{Y}}{Y!} \quad \text{for } Y \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\}
$$

We initially choose an exponential prior for $\lambda$ with mean 7. You find that the mean number of claims per policy in your dataset is 2, and the mode of the posterior distribution of $\lambda$ is 2.1.

You are considering 3 possible alternative prior distributions.

I. Gamma with a mean of 7 and variance of 7
II. Exponential with a mean of 5
III. Uniform between 0 and 2

Determine which of the above alternative prior distributions will increase the mode (relative to the current mode) of the posterior distribution of $\lambda$.

- A) None
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

The data pull toward 2 and the prior pulls toward 7, which is why the current posterior mode of 2.1 sits just above the sample mean. A change to the prior moves the mode up only if it pulls *harder* toward the high values.

**I increases the mode.** A gamma with mean 7 and variance 7 has $\alpha\theta = 7$ and $\alpha\theta^2 = 7$, so $\theta = 1$ and $\alpha = 7$. Its mean is the same as the current prior's, but its variance is 7 against the exponential's $7^2 = 49$ — it is a far tighter prior concentrated around 7. Note also that $\alpha = 7 > 1$ contributes an extra $\lambda^{6}$ to the posterior kernel, whereas the exponential ($\alpha = 1$) contributes none. Both effects push the mode up.

**II decreases the mode.** Keeping the exponential form but lowering the mean from 7 to 5 weakens the upward pull, moving the posterior closer to the data value of 2.

**III decreases the mode.** A uniform prior on $[0, 2]$ assigns zero density above 2, so the posterior is truncated there and its mode cannot exceed 2 — below the current 2.1.

Only alternative I increases the mode. "I only" is not among options (A) through (D), so the answer is **E**.
