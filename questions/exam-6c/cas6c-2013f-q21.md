Given the following information regarding an insurance company's paid losses and discounted claim liabilities:

Paid Losses During Calendar Year (CY) (\$000s):

| As of CY | Accident Year 2010 | Accident Year 2011 | Accident Year 2012 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | \$52,000 | | | \$52,000 |
| 2011 | \$21,000 | \$51,000 | | \$72,000 |
| 2012 | \$18,000 | \$33,000 | \$67,000 | \$118,000 |

Discounted Claim Liabilities (\$000s):

| As of CY | Accident Year 2010 | Accident Year 2011 | Accident Year 2012 | Total | Annual Yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | \$59,000 | | | \$59,000 | 3.50% |
| 2011 | \$35,000 | \$63,000 | | \$98,000 | 4.50% |
| 2012 | \$27,000 | \$45,000 | \$74,000 | \$146,000 | 4.00% |

Excess (Deficiency) During CY 3 – By Accident Year (\$000s):

| Accident Year | Paid Losses During 2012 (1) | Discounted Claim Liabilities 2012 (2) | Discounted Claim Liabilities 2011 (3) | Investment Income in 2012 on unpaid claims (4) | Excess (Deficiency) During 2012 (5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | \$18,000 | \$27,000 | \$35,000 | A | B |
| 2011 | \$33,000 | \$45,000 | \$63,000 | C | D |
| 2012 | \$67,000 | \$74,000 | - | E | - |

## Part a (1.25 points)

Calculate the cumulative excess/deficiency ratio for accident year 2010 as of December 31, 2012.

### Explanation
Investment income in 2012 for AY 2010:

$$\frac{27{,}000 + 35{,}000}{2} \times 4.00\% = 1{,}240$$

Investment income in 2011 for AY 2010:

$$\frac{59{,}000 + 35{,}000}{2} \times 4.5\% = 2{,}115$$

Excess in 2012 for AY 2010:

$$(35{,}000 + 1{,}240) - (27{,}000 + 18{,}000) = -8{,}760$$

Excess in 2011 for AY 2010:

$$(59{,}000 + 2{,}115) - (35{,}000 + 21{,}000) = 5{,}115$$

Cumulative excess (deficiency) ratio:

$$-(5{,}115 - 8{,}760) / 59{,}000 = -6.2\% \text{ (as printed)}$$

Alternatively:

$$\text{Inv income 2011} = 0.045 \times (59{,}000 + 35{,}000)/2 = 2{,}115$$

$$\text{Inv income 2012} = 0.04 \times (27{,}000 + 35{,}000)/2 = 1{,}240$$

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Cumulative excess} &= \text{Beg reserves} - \text{Paid} + \text{Inv income} - \text{End reserves} \\ &= 59{,}000 - (21{,}000 + 18{,}000) + (2{,}115 + 1{,}240) - 27{,}000 \\ &= -3{,}645 \end{aligned}$$

$$\text{Excess/deficiency ratio} = -3{,}645 / 59{,}000 = -0.062$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates scored well. Some errors included adding investment income from CY 2010, or forgetting to calculate the ratio itself once the excess/deficiency amount had been calculated.

## Part b (0.25 points)

One way to account for the time value of money is to explicitly calculate an amount of investment income as in the table above. Briefly describe another way to account for the time value of money when calculating excess/deficiency amounts.

### Explanation
Discount the amounts in subsequent terms to time 0. It was enough for full marks to say to discount the claims liabilities back to time t-1 instead of incorporating investment income.

Alternatively, calculate the present value of the cash flows from the calendar year.

### Examiner Report
Candidates did not score well at all. Although many answers spoke about discounting, they did not include enough detail to indicate that the candidate meant that the discounting should be applied to the paid/outstanding at time t to bring it back to time t-1.
