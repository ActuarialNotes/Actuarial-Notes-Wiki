**Cumulative Paid Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 1,000 | 2,000 | 3,100 | 3,410 |
| 2014 | 1,500 | 3,300 | 4,785 | |
| 2015 | 2,000 | 3,600 | | |
| 2016 | 2,500 | | | |

**Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 3,000 | 3,600 | 3,960 | 4,000 |
| 2014 | 4,200 | 5,250 | 5,775 | |
| 2015 | 5,100 | 6,630 | | |
| 2016 | 7,500 | | | |

**Case Outstanding (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 2,000 | 1,600 | 860 | 590 |
| 2014 | 2,700 | 1,950 | 990 | |
| 2015 | 3,100 | 3,030 | | |
| 2016 | 5,000 | | | |

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the expected incremental reported claims for accident year 2016 in calendar year 2019 using the reported claim development technique.

### Answer
Approximately \$104,000

### Explanation
Accident year 2016 is at 12 months as of December 31, 2016, so it reaches 24 months during CY 2017, 36 months during CY 2018, and **48 months during CY 2019**. The quantity asked for is therefore the *incremental* reported amount between 36 and 48 months, not the ultimate.

**Step 1 — reported age-to-age factors.**

| AY | 12–24 | 24–36 | 36–48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | $3{,}600/3{,}000 = 1.200$ | $3{,}960/3{,}600 = 1.100$ | $4{,}000/3{,}960 = 1.010$ |
| 2014 | $5{,}250/4{,}200 = 1.250$ | $5{,}775/5{,}250 = 1.100$ | |
| 2015 | $6{,}630/5{,}100 = 1.300$ | | |
| **Selected (all-year average)** | **1.250** | **1.100** | **1.010** |

**Step 2 — develop accident year 2016 forward one age at a time.**

$$\text{Reported}_{24} = 7{,}500 \times 1.250 = 9{,}375$$
$$\text{Reported}_{36} = 9{,}375 \times 1.100 = 10{,}312.5$$
$$\text{Reported}_{48} = 10{,}312.5 \times 1.0101 = 10{,}416.7$$

**Step 3 — take the increment that emerges in calendar year 2019.**

$$\text{Incremental reported in CY 2019} = 10{,}416.7 - 10{,}312.5 = 104.2 \ (\$000) \approx \mathbf{\$104{,}000}$$

Alternatively, work with cumulative factors. The 12-to-ultimate CDF is $1.250 \times 1.100 \times 1.0101 = 1.3889$ and the 12-to-36 CDF is $1.250 \times 1.100 = 1.375$, so the increment is $7{,}500 \times (1.3889 - 1.375) = 104$. A third route is to note that $1/1.0101 = 99\%$ of ultimate is reported at 36 months, so the increment is $7{,}500 \times 1.3889 \times (1 - 0.99) \approx 104$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to build age-to-age factors from the cumulative reported claim triangle and use them to project accident year 2016 not only to ultimate but to the intermediate ages, then isolate the piece emerging in calendar year 2019.

Common errors included calculating age-to-age factors but failing to convert them to cumulative development factors when the chosen approach required them, and developing 2016 all the way to ultimate without extracting the incremental portion reported in calendar year 2019.

## Part b (2 points)

Calculate the expected incremental reported claims for accident year 2016 in calendar year 2019 using the incremental paid to previous case outstanding technique.

### Answer
Approximately \$100,000

### Explanation
The case outstanding technique projects two things off the case reserve at the *previous* maturity: how much gets paid in the next period, and how much case reserve survives into the next period. Reported claims are paid claims plus case outstanding, so the incremental reported amount over a period is

$$\Delta\text{Reported} = \text{Incremental paid} + \Delta\text{Case outstanding}$$

**Step 1 — case outstanding development ratios** (case at the end of the period divided by case at the start):

| AY | 12–24 | 24–36 | 36–48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | $1{,}600/2{,}000 = 0.800$ | $860/1{,}600 = 0.538$ | $590/860 = 0.686$ |
| 2014 | $1{,}950/2{,}700 = 0.722$ | $990/1{,}950 = 0.508$ | |
| 2015 | $3{,}030/3{,}100 = 0.977$ | | |
| **Selected** | **0.8332** | **0.5226** | **0.6860** |

**Step 2 — incremental paid triangle**, from successive differences of the cumulative paid claims:

| AY | 0–12 | 12–24 | 24–36 | 36–48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,100 | 310 |
| 2014 | 1,500 | 1,800 | 1,485 | |
| 2015 | 2,000 | 1,600 | | |
| 2016 | 2,500 | | | |

**Step 3 — incremental paid to previous case outstanding ratios.** Each incremental payment is divided by the case reserve standing at the *beginning* of that period:

| AY | 12–24 | 24–36 | 36–48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | $1{,}000/2{,}000 = 0.500$ | $1{,}100/1{,}600 = 0.688$ | $310/860 = 0.360$ |
| 2014 | $1{,}800/2{,}700 = 0.667$ | $1{,}485/1{,}950 = 0.762$ | |
| 2015 | $1{,}600/3{,}100 = 0.516$ | | |
| **Selected** | **0.5609** | **0.7245** | **0.3605** |

**Step 4 — roll accident year 2016 forward from its \$5,000 case reserve at 12 months.**

$$\text{Case}_{24} = 5{,}000 \times 0.8332 = 4{,}166 \qquad \text{Case}_{36} = 4{,}166 \times 0.5226 = 2{,}177 \qquad \text{Case}_{48} = 2{,}177 \times 0.6860 = 1{,}494$$

**Step 5 — the calendar year 2019 period (36 to 48 months).** The payment made in that period is driven by the case reserve at 36 months:

$$\text{Incremental paid}_{36\text{--}48} = 2{,}177 \times 0.3605 = 785$$

and the case reserve *falls* over the period:

$$\Delta\text{Case} = 1{,}494 - 2{,}177 = -683$$

$$\text{Incremental reported in CY 2019} = 785 - 683 = 101 \ (\$000)$$

The published samples round each selection to three decimals before projecting ($0.832$, $0.523$, $0.686$ and $0.561$, $0.725$, $0.360$), which gives $783 - 683 = 100$. Carrying the selections unrounded gives $101$ instead. The gap is rounding in the *selections*, not a difference in method, and either is acceptable — the exhibit supports no more precision than that.

Alternatively, build the full cumulative triangles for accident year 2016 and difference them. The incremental payments are $2{,}500$, $5{,}000 \times 0.5609 = 2{,}805$, $4{,}166 \times 0.7245 = 3{,}018$ and $785$, so cumulative paid runs $2{,}500 \to 5{,}305 \to 8{,}323 \to 9{,}108$. Adding case outstanding at each age gives cumulative reported of $7{,}500 \to 9{,}471 \to 10{,}500 \to 10{,}601$, and $10{,}601 - 10{,}500 \approx 101$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the mechanics of the incremental paid to previous case outstanding technique: construct the case outstanding development ratios and the incremental paid to prior case ratios, make selections, project accident year 2016's case reserves and payments forward, and then convert those outputs into an *incremental reported* figure.

The most common error was stopping at the incremental paid claims and reporting those instead of the incremental reported claims — the change in case outstanding must be added to the payment to get the reported movement.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly describe whether the case outstanding technique is appropriate to project ultimate claims when performing an analysis on each of the following bases:

i. Report year
ii. Accident year

### Explanation
The technique projects future payments and future case reserves from the case reserve already standing on *known* claims. It therefore captures IBNER — development on reported claims — but has no mechanism at all for pure IBNR, claims that have occurred but have not yet been reported.

**i. Report year — appropriate.** A report year contains only claims already reported, and every one of them is known by the end of the first maturity, so a report year has no pure IBNR. All remaining development is IBNER on claims that already carry a case reserve, which is precisely what the technique projects.

**ii. Accident year — generally not appropriate.** An accident year at immature maturities still contains claims that have occurred but not been reported, and those claims carry no case reserve for the technique to work from, so ultimate claims will be understated. It is only reasonable for a short-tailed line in which essentially all claims are reported by the first maturity.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to link the mechanics of the technique — that it works off existing case reserves and so recognizes only IBNER — to the composition of each data aggregation, and to explain the consequence for each.

Common errors included describing accident year and report year aggregations in general without connecting them to how the technique works, and giving a bare verdict such as "appropriate" or "not appropriate" with no supporting explanation.
