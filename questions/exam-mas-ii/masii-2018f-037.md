You are given a data set consisting of 150 data points. There are three possible classifications for each data point, as shown in the leftmost graph, with 50 data points falling into each classification.

Based on this data you train a K-Nearest Neighbors model using 95 of the data points. You evaluate k ranging from 1 to 95. For each k, you calculate the test error rate on the remaining 55 data points with results shown in the rightmost graph.

![Two plots side by side. Left: scatter plot of Feature 2 (about 2.0 to 4.4) against Feature 1 (about 4.3 to 7.9) with three marker types — circles clustered at Feature 1 below about 5.8 and Feature 2 above about 2.9, while triangles and plus signs overlap heavily in the region Feature 1 from 5 to 7.9, Feature 2 from 2.2 to 3.8. Right: test error rate against k from 1 to 95; the error stays between roughly 0.15 and 0.24 up to k of about 72, then climbs steeply to about 0.6 to 0.65 by k of about 78 and ends near 0.71 at k = 95](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2018f-q37-knn-data-and-error.png)

Determine the cause of the rapid increase in error rate between k equals 70 and k equals 95.

- A) As k increases the K-Nearest Neighbors algorithm performs better.
- B) As k increases the K-Nearest Neighbors algorithm performs worse.
- C) As k approaches 95, all data points are predicted to have the same classification.
- D) As k approaches 95, all data points are incorrectly classified.
- E) There is no clear relationship between the value of k and the error rate.

## Explanation

As $k$ grows, the KNN neighbourhood grows, and the fit becomes smoother and less flexible. When $k$ reaches the size of the training set, 95 here, every neighbourhood is the *entire* training set. Every test point then gets the same prediction: the most common class among the 95 training points.

The three classes are equally represented in the full data (50 each), so the training set holds roughly a third of each class. A constant prediction is then right for only about a third of the 55 test points, and the error rate approaches roughly $2/3$. That is the plateau of about 0.6 to 0.7 on the right of the graph, and the steep climb after $k \approx 70$ is the classifier collapsing towards that single class.

The other options don't fit the graph:

- A and B are not true in general. The error is roughly flat, at about 0.15 to 0.24, from $k = 1$ to about 70.
- D is too strong. An error of about 0.65 means about a third of the test points are still classified correctly.
- E is contradicted by the clear jump at large $k$.

The answer is **C**.
