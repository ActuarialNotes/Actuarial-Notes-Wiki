Each person in a certain large population independently has probability 0.0625 of having a certain disease. People in this population are tested for the disease, until somebody is found to have the disease.

Calculate the mode of the number of people tested.

- A) $1$
- B) $6$
- C) $11$
- D) $15$
- E) $16$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the number of people tested up to and including the first positive. Testing stops at the first success, so $X$ is geometric:

$$
P[X = n] = (1 - 0.0625)^{n-1}(0.0625), \qquad n \geq 1
$$

The mode is the value of $n$ maximizing this. Since $0 < 1 - 0.0625 < 1$, the factor $(0.9375)^{n-1}$ **decreases** with every additional test, and the constant 0.0625 does not depend on $n$. So the probability is largest when the exponent $n - 1$ is smallest:

$$
\text{mode} = \mathbf{1}
$$

A geometric distribution's mode is always 1, whatever the success probability — each successive value is strictly less likely than the one before.

Choice (E), 16, is the *mean* $1/p = 1/0.0625 = 16$. The gap between a mode of 1 and a mean of 16 is a striking illustration of how right-skewed this distribution is: the single most likely outcome is finding the disease on the first test, even though the average wait is sixteen tests.
