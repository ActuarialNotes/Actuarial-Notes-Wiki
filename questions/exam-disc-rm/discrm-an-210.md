An underwriter is reviewing a commercial property account with twelve warehouses, eight of them within a few miles of each other on the Gulf Coast. Which cause of loss does that concentration of value most affect in the underwriter's evaluation?

- A) Windstorm, a catastrophe peril evaluated by location and concentration
- B) Theft, because its exposure depends on how many buildings are insured
- C) Fire, because twelve buildings give twelve chances of a fire starting
- D) Collapse, because older warehouses are more likely to collapse

## Explanation

Windstorm, hail, flood and earthquake are catastrophe perils, and an underwriter evaluates them by location and by how much value is concentrated in one area. One hurricane could damage all eight Gulf Coast warehouses at once, so the concentration matters most for windstorm. The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — Theft depends mainly on the occupancy, the property's condition and its security, not on how close the buildings are to each other.
- **C** — More buildings mean more chances of a fire, but one fire doesn't reach buildings miles apart, so the concentration adds little to the fire exposure.
- **D** — Collapse depends on the property's condition. Nothing in the concentration of locations changes it.
