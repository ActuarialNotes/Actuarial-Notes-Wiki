Statement of Statutory Accounting Principles No. 55 of the NAIC's Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual. It establishes statutory accounting principles for recording liabilities for unpaid claims and claim adjustment expenses on life and accident and health contracts and unpaid losses and loss adjustment expenses on property and casualty contracts, for direct and reinsurance-assumed obligations alike.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objectives C1–C2; NAIC Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual, 2025, SSAP No. 55, paragraphs 1-6 and 11-19, available in Appendix IV of the COPLFR P&C Practice Note (as the content outline states it)

## Scope of Statement
- The statement applies to all insurance contracts as defined in SSAP No. 50 and to direct and reinsurance-assumed obligations equally.
- It does not address policy reserves for life and accident and health policies, or liabilities for punitive damages, which follow SSAP No. 5R.

## Summary Conclusion
- Claims, losses and loss/claim adjustment expenses are recognized as expenses when a covered or insured event occurs, and a liability is established for unpaid amounts with a corresponding charge to income; for claims-made policies the covered event is the reporting of the incident.
- Property/Casualty
    - The future costs considered in the liability are reported losses, [[Loss Reserving|incurred but not reported]] (IBNR) losses, which may include bulk provisions for deficiencies in known case reserves, and loss adjustment expenses.
    - Loss adjustment expenses are classified as Defense and Cost Containment (DCC) and Adjusting and Other (AO), each with a list of included items.
    - Payments to third-party administrators and management companies are evaluated against the prepaid asset criteria of SSAP No. 29 and do not offset the liability until they are paid to the policyholder or claimant.
- Life, Accident and Health
- Managed Care
- Managed Care and Accident and Health
- General
    - The liability is based on the estimated ultimate cost of settling the claims, including the effects of inflation and other societal and economic factors, and is not discounted unless authorized for specific types of claims by specific SSAPs.
    - No single projection method is inherently better than any other in all circumstances, and the results of more than one method should be considered.
    - Management records its best estimate for each line of business and in aggregate; where it develops a range, the best estimate within the range is recorded, the range is realistic rather than the set of all possible outcomes, and there is no specific requirement to include a provision for adverse deviation.
    - When no point in the range is a better estimate than any other, the midpoint of management's range is accrued.
    - Estimated [[Salvage and Subrogation|salvage and subrogation]] recoverables, if anticipated, are deducted from the liability; changes in estimates are recorded in the period the change becomes known under SSAP No. 3.
- Disclosures
    - Disclosures include the beginning and ending balances of the liabilities, incurred and paid amounts for current and prior years, the reasons for prior-year changes, significant changes in methodologies and assumptions, and amounts paid and reserved for asbestos and environmental claims.
    - The dollar amount and number of claims paid on extra contractual obligation or bad faith lawsuits are disclosed.
