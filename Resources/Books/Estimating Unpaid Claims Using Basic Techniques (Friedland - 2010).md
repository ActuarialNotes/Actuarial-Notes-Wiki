The primary [[Loss Reserving|reserving]] text on the [[Exam 5 (CAS)|Exam 5]] syllabus, covering the estimation of [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]] for both insurers and non-insurance entities that retain risk.

## Part 1 — Introduction

### 1 Overview

- Why accurate [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claim estimates]] matter: solvency, financial reporting, [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]], and management decisions
- Users of the estimates: management, investors, regulators, and rating agencies

### 2 The Claims Process

- Claim lifecycle from occurrence through reporting, [[Case Reserves|case reserving]], and settlement
- [[Occurrence Coverage|Occurrence]] vs. [[Claims Made Coverage|claims-made]] triggers and their effect on reporting lag
- Components of unpaid claims: case outstanding, [[IBNR]] (pure IBNR and IBNER)

### 3 Understanding the Types of Data Used

- [[Reserving Data Organization|Data organization]]: [[Calendar Year]], [[Accident Year]], [[Policy Year]], [[Underwriting Year]], [[Report Year]]
- [[Paid Losses|Paid]] vs. [[Incurred Losses|reported]] data; claim counts, [[Allocated Loss Adjustment Expense|ALAE]], and recoveries
- [[Homogeneity]] and [[Credibility]] in segmenting the data

## Part 2 — Information Gathering

### 4 Meeting with Management

- Operational changes to probe: [[Claims Coding Changes|claims coding]], [[Claims Processing Changes|claims processing]], [[Underwriting Changes|underwriting]], [[Policy Provision Changes|policy provisions]], marketing, and reinsurance

### 5 The Development Triangle

- Constructing paid, reported, and count [[Development Triangle|triangles]]; [[Claim Count Triangle|claim count triangles]]
- Reading a triangle by accident period, maturity, and diagonal

### 6 The Development Triangle as a Diagnostic Tool

- Diagnostics: [[Frequency]], [[Severity]], [[Loss Ratio|loss ratios]], average case outstanding, paid-to-reported ratios, and closure rates
- Detecting changes in [[Case Adequacy|case adequacy]] and [[Settlement Rate|settlement rates]] before selecting a method

## Part 3 — Estimation Methods

### 7 Development Technique

- The [[Chain Ladder Method|chain ladder (development) method]] on paid and reported data
- Selecting [[Age to Age Factor|age-to-age factors]], chaining to a [[Cumulative Development Factor]], and applying a [[Tail Factor]]

### 8 Expected Claims Technique

- The [[Expected Loss Method|expected claims method]] and its insensitivity to actual emergence

### 9 Bornhuetter-Ferguson Technique

- The [[Bornhuetter-Ferguson Method|BF method]] as a credibility blend of development and expected claims

### 10 Cape Cod Technique

- The [[Cape Cod Method|Cape Cod (Stanard-Bühlmann)]] technique: deriving the a priori loss ratio from the data itself using used-up premium

### 11 Frequency-Severity Techniques

- [[Frequency-Severity Method|Frequency-severity]] approaches, including disposal-rate and trended-severity variants

### 12 Case Outstanding Development Technique

- The [[Case Outstanding Development Method|case outstanding development]] technique for books with stable case reserving

### 13 Berquist-Sherman Techniques

- [[Berquist-Sherman Method|Berquist-Sherman]] adjustments for changing [[Case Adequacy|case adequacy]] (restated reported triangle) and changing [[Settlement Rate|settlement rates]] (restated paid triangle)

## Part 4 — Adjustments and Special Topics

### 14 Recoveries: Salvage and Subrogation and Reinsurance

- Estimating [[Salvage and Subrogation|salvage and subrogation]] recoveries
- [[Reinsurance]]: calculating [[Gross Losses|gross]], [[Ceded Losses|ceded]], and [[Net Losses|net]] unpaid claims

### 15 Evaluation of Techniques

- Comparing method results and selecting an estimate; reconciling divergence between paid and reported indications
- Using [[External Information in Reserving|external benchmarks]] where internal data is thin

### 16 Estimating Unpaid ALAE

- [[Allocated Loss Adjustment Expense|ALAE]] triangles, ratio-to-loss approaches, and additive methods

### 17 Estimating Unpaid ULAE

- [[Unallocated Loss Adjustment Expenses ULAE|ULAE]]: the classical (dollar-based) ratio method, Kittel refinement, and the Mango-Allen and Conger-Nolibos generalized approaches

### 18 Reserve Variability

- Sources of uncertainty: process, parameter, and model risk; ranges vs. point estimates

## Part 5 — Monitoring and Communication

### 19 Evaluating Estimates of Unpaid Claims

- [[Reserve Adequacy|Testing adequacy and reasonableness]] using loss ratios, severities, pure premiums, frequencies, and implied development

### 20 Monitoring Results

- [[Actual vs Expected Analysis|Actual vs. expected]] emergence testing and [[Roll Forward Analysis|roll-forward]] between valuation dates

### 21 Communicating Results

- [[Reserve Communication|Communicating]] estimates and drivers of change to [[Stakeholder Reporting|internal management, investors, and regulators]]
- Documentation and disclosure requirements under [[ASOP 43 - Property Casualty Unpaid Claim Estimates (ASB - 2007)|ASOP 43]]
