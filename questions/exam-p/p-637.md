The daily numbers of claims processed by an insurance company can be regarded as independent observations from a distribution having mean 3300 and standard deviation 130.

Calculate the approximate probability that the insurance company processes more than one million claims over a 303-day period.

- A) $0.22$
- B) $0.48$
- C) $0.50$
- D) $0.52$
- E) $0.78$

## Explanation

Let $S$ be the number of claims processed over the 303 days. The daily counts are independent, so the Central Limit Theorem makes $S$ approximately normal with

$$
\begin{align*}
E(S) &= 3300(303) = 999{,}900 \\
\text{SD}(S) &= 130\sqrt{303} = 2262.90
\end{align*}
$$

The standard deviation scales with $\sqrt{n}$, not $n$ — that is the whole point of the approximation. Then

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S > 1{,}000{,}000] &= P\!\left[Z > \frac{1{,}000{,}000 - 999{,}900}{2262.90}\right] \\
                     &= P[Z > 0.0442] \\
                     &= \mathbf{0.482}
\end{align*}
$$

The target of one million sits only 100 claims above the mean of 999,900 — a small fraction of one standard deviation — so the answer has to be just *below* 0.50, which rules out (C), (D) and (E) at a glance.

Choice (C), 0.50, is what you get by scaling the standard deviation by 303 instead of $\sqrt{303}$: $130(303) = 39{,}390$ gives $P[Z > 0.0025] = 0.499$, indistinguishable from a coin flip.
