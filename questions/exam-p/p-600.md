Hospitalization losses under a certain policy are mutually independent and each has cumulative distribution function

$$
F(x) = \begin{cases}
0, & \text{for } x < 0 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{x + 15x^2}{16}, & \text{for } 0 \le x \le 1 \\[4pt]
1, & \text{for } x > 1
\end{cases}
$$

For each hospitalization loss, the insurer reimburses up to a maximum of 0.80.

Calculate the probability that a policyholder's first partially unreimbursed hospitalization loss occurs on the third hospitalization.

- A) $0.027$
- B) $0.080$
- C) $0.128$
- D) $0.148$
- E) $0.350$

## Explanation

A loss is fully reimbursed exactly when it falls below the 0.80 maximum. Let $p$ be that probability:

$$
\begin{align*}
p &= P[\text{loss} < 0.8] = F(0.8) \\
  &= \frac{0.8 + 15(0.8)^2}{16} \\
  &= \frac{0.8 + 9.6}{16} \\
  &= 0.65
\end{align*}
$$

The losses are independent, so the number of hospitalizations up to and including the first partially unreimbursed one is geometric. The first two must be fully reimbursed and the third must not:

$$
\begin{align*}
p^2(1 - p) &= (0.65)^2(0.35) \\
           &= \mathbf{0.1479}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (E), 0.350, is $1 - p$ — the probability that a *single* loss is partially unreimbursed, with no requirement that it be the third. That number is the last factor of the answer, not the answer.
