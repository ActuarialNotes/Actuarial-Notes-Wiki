A contractor's van, insured by Insurer X in Ontario, is struck by a car insured by Insurer Y. Under the fault determination rules, the car's driver is 75% at fault and the van's driver is 25% at fault. The contents of the van suffer \$32,000 of damage, for which Insurer X compensates its insured under direct compensation.

Ontario Regulation 664, section 8, gives the insurer of an automobile whose contents suffer damage greater than \$20,000 a right of indemnification from the insurer of the other automobile involved.

Calculate the amount Insurer X can recover from Insurer Y.

- A) \$3,000
- B) \$9,000
- C) \$12,000
- D) \$15,000
- E) \$24,000

## Explanation

Under direct compensation, Insurer X compensates its own insured, to the extent the van's driver was not at fault, and ordinarily recovers nothing from the other insurer. Section 8 of Regulation 664 makes one exception for large contents losses: the indemnity is "limited to that proportion of the loss **over \$20,000** that is attributable to the fault, as determined under the fault determination rules, of the driver of the other automobile."

$$
\text{Indemnity} = 0.75 \times (\$32{,}000 - \$20{,}000) = 0.75 \times \$12{,}000 = \$9{,}000
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — \$3,000 applies the *van* driver's 25% to the excess. The indemnity follows the fault of the driver of the *other* automobile.
- **C** — \$12,000 recovers the whole excess and ignores the fault apportionment the regulation applies.
- **D** — \$15,000 applies 75% to the first \$20,000, the part of the loss the regulation leaves with Insurer X under direct compensation.
- **E** — \$24,000 applies 75% to the whole loss. That is roughly what direct compensation pays the insured, not what section 8 lets Insurer X recover, which is limited to the loss over \$20,000.
