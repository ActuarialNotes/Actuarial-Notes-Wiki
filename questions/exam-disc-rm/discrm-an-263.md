Investment yields are high, and an insurer accepts combined ratios above 100% because investment income on its reserves more than covers the underwriting loss. What is this practice called, and what does it tend to do to the underwriting cycle?

- A) Risk-based pricing, which shortens the hard phase
- B) Cash flow underwriting, which prolongs a soft market
- C) Surplus relief, which hardens the market sooner
- D) Underwriting discipline, which ends the soft phase

## Explanation

When investment yields are high, insurers accept higher combined ratios because investment income covers the underwriting loss. This is cash flow underwriting, and it prolongs a soft market by keeping prices low longer. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Risk-based pricing charges each risk for its expected cost; it isn't the practice described.
- **C** — Surplus relief is a function of reinsurance, not a pricing practice.
- **D** — Underwriting discipline is the opposite: holding rates and standards even at the cost of volume.
