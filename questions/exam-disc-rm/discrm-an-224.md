A builder is building three houses on separate lots under one contract with a developer. The first house is finished and sold, and its buyer has moved in; the other two are still under construction. The buyer's guest is injured when a stair rail in the finished house gives way. How is the builder's work on the first house treated under the CGL?

- A) As ongoing operations, because the contract as a whole is not finished
- B) As ongoing operations, because the builder is still working on that job
- C) As completed work, because that part of the work has been put to its intended use
- D) As outside the CGL, because injury from defective work is a business risk

## Explanation

Work is deemed completed at the earliest of three times: when all the work under the contract is done, when all the work at one job site is done if the contract covers several sites, or when that part of the work has been put to its intended use by anyone other than another contractor on the project. The first house is a separate site whose work is done, and it is being lived in, so the injury falls within the products-completed operations hazard. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Waiting for the whole contract ignores the other two tests, either of which is met here.
- **B** — The builder is still working on the development, but not at the finished house.
- **D** — The business risk exclusions remove damage to the insured's own work, not bodily injury to others caused by it.
