A policyholder is insured against both fire and theft claims. The probability of a fire claim is 10%. The probability of a theft claim is 30%.

Let $p$ be the probability of having neither a fire nor a theft claim, assuming fire and theft claims are independent.

Let $r$ be the probability of having neither a fire nor a theft claim, assuming fire and theft claims are mutually exclusive.

Calculate $p - r$.

- A) $-0.06$
- B) $-0.03$
- C) $0.00$
- D) $0.03$
- E) $0.06$

## Explanation

**Independent case.** The complements multiply:

$$
\begin{align*}
p &= (1 - 0.10)(1 - 0.30) \\
  &= (0.90)(0.70) = 0.63
\end{align*}
$$

**Mutually exclusive case.** The two claim types never overlap, so their probabilities simply add and

$$
\begin{align*}
r &= 1 - (0.10 + 0.30) \\
  &= 0.60
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
p - r &= 0.63 - 0.60 \\
      &= \mathbf{0.03}
\end{align*}
$$

The difference is exactly $P[\text{fire}]P[\text{theft}] = 0.03$ — the overlap that independence allows and mutual exclusivity forbids. Since the independent assumption puts positive probability on *both* claims occurring, it leaves more room for neither occurring, so $p > r$ and the answer is positive; choices (A) and (B) have the comparison backwards.
