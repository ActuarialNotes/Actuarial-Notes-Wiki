Given the following information for a medical association:

**Cumulative Reported Claim Counts Evaluated as of Month:**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 | 60 | 72 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 26 | 52 | 71 | 80 | 85 | 89 |
| 2011 | 37 | 54 | 70 | 82 | 84 | 85 |
| 2012 | 44 | 81 | 85 | 100 | 110 | 112 |
| 2013 | 19 | 44 | 59 | 67 | 70 |  |
| 2014 | 15 | 44 | 72 | 83 |  |  |
| 2015 | 38 | 59 | 77 |  |  |  |
| 2016 | 33 | 65 |  |  |  |  |
| 2017 | 30 |  |  |  |  |  |

| Claims-Made Year | Step Factor |
|---|---|
| First | 30% |
| Second | 60% |
| Third | 80% |
| Fourth | 90% |
| Fifth | 95% |
| Six and more | 100% |

- The medical association had claims-made policy coverages up until December 31, 2014.
- The claims-made policies have a retroactive date of January 1, 2010.
- The medical association switches to occurrence policies on January 1, 2015.
- All policies are annual.
- All policies incept January 1.

## Part a (0.25 points)

Calculate the total reported claim counts covered under claims-made policies.

### Explanation
A claims-made policy covers claims reported during the policy period that occurred on or after the retroactive date. The claims-made policies ran through December 31, 2014 with a January 1, 2010 retroactive date, so they cover accident years 2010–2014 reported by December 31, 2014. The triangle is cumulative, so read each accident year at its December 31, 2014 evaluation:

| Accident Year | Age at 12/31/2014 | Reported |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 60 | 85 |
| 2011 | 48 | 82 |
| 2012 | 36 | 85 |
| 2013 | 24 | 44 |
| 2014 | 12 | 15 |
| **Total** | | **311** |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand both claims-made and occurrence coverage, how to apply those terms to claim data, and how each coverage responds when there is a change in coverage type. Candidates were expected to recognize a cumulative triangle and develop the immature years to ultimate.

Candidates were expected to know which claims were covered under a claims-made policy given a specific retroactive date.

Common errors included:

- Not realizing that the data was in a cumulative format.
- Incorrect application of the retroactive date.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Calculate the total reported claim counts covered under occurrence policies.

### Explanation
Occurrence policies from January 1, 2015 cover claims that occur in 2015 or later, whenever they are reported. Using each accident year's latest cumulative count:

$$77 + 65 + 30 = 172$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know which claims were covered under an occurrence policy given a specific effective date.

Common errors included:

- Not realizing that the data was in a cumulative format
- Not properly reflecting what is covered under the Occurrence policies.

## Part c (1 point)

Briefly describe the coverage gap and calculate the estimated number of ultimate claims falling in the gap for the medical association.

### Explanation
**The gap.** Claims that occurred while the claims-made policies were in force (2010–2014) but are reported after December 31, 2014 are covered by neither policy: the claims-made policies have expired, and the occurrence policies cover only occurrences from 2015 on.

**Size of the gap.** Develop accident years 2010–2014 to ultimate from their latest reported counts. The step factors are the cumulative share of an accident year's claims reported by the end of each year (first year 30%, ..., six and more 100%), so ultimate = latest reported ÷ step factor:

| Accident Year | Latest age | Latest reported | Step factor | Ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 72 | 89 | 1.00 | 89.0 |
| 2011 | 72 | 85 | 1.00 | 85.0 |
| 2012 | 72 | 112 | 1.00 | 112.0 |
| 2013 | 60 | 70 | 0.95 | 73.7 |
| 2014 | 48 | 83 | 0.90 | 92.2 |
| **Total** | | | | **451.9** |

$$\text{Claims in the gap} = 451.9 - 311 = 140.9$$

About 141 ultimate claims fall in the coverage gap.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to develop claims count to ultimate using either the given step factors or by deriving LDFs. Candidates were also expected to explain why there was a gap in coverage from a switch of policy type and which claims would be affected.

Common errors included:

- Not being precise enough in their explanation of the coverage gap. The explanation was expected to include that the claims had to occur during the claims-made policy and that they were reported during the occurrence policy.
- Not realizing that the triangle was in a cumulative format.
- Not developing the claim count triangle to ultimate.
- Not properly using the step factor provided.
- Not using the most recent reported to date claim count to develop to ultimate.
- Applying incremental LDF to develop claim count to ultimate.

## Part d (0.25 points)

Briefly describe a solution for the medical association to address the coverage gap in part c. above.

### Explanation
Buy **tail coverage** — an extended reporting period endorsement on the last claims-made policy — which covers claims that occurred before the claims-made coverage ended but are reported after it.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know which additional coverage could be purchased to cover the gap between the claims-made policy and the occurrence policy.

Common errors included:

- Simply stating purchasing "gap coverage", "an extension", or "an endorsement" as these responses are too vague. For example, not all endorsements to a policy would be a solution to the coverage gap.
