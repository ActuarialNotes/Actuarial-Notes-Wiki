An insurer wrote an annual homeowners policy on July 1 for \$1,800. A covered fire occurred on December 10; by December 31 the insurer has paid \$4,000 of a loss it estimates at \$10,000. What statutory liabilities does the insurer carry for this policy at December 31?

- A) Unearned premium of \$900, less the \$6,000 still unpaid on the fire
- B) No unearned premium, because the loss used it up, and \$6,000 of loss reserve
- C) Unearned premium of \$900 and a loss reserve of \$10,000
- D) Unearned premium of \$900 and a loss reserve of \$6,000

## Explanation

The unearned premium reserve is for the unexpired coverage, six of twelve months: $1{,}800 \times \tfrac{6}{12} = 900$. The fire is a covered event that has occurred, so under SSAP No. 55 it is recognized as a loss, and the unpaid part, $10{,}000 - 4{,}000 = 6{,}000$, is a loss reserve. The two liabilities cover different things: losses that have occurred, and coverage still to be provided. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — losses that have occurred are not deducted from the unearned premium; they get their own reserve.
- **B** — premium earns with the passage of time, whether or not claims occur.
- **C** — the \$4,000 already paid is no longer a liability; the reserve is the unpaid amount.
