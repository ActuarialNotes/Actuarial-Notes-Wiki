An insurer sells coverage with an attachment point of 5,000 and a layer limit of 5,000. The following table represents the expected cumulative severity distribution and limited expected severity for the current year at various limits:

| $l$ | $F(l)$ | $E[X;l]$ |
|---|---|---|
| 4,545 | 0.842 | 1,807 |
| 5,000 | 0.859 | 1,875 |
| 5,500 | 0.875 | 1,941 |
| 9,091 | 0.938 | 2,256 |
| 10,000 | 0.947 | 2,308 |
| 11,000 | 0.954 | 2,357 |

The actuary expects a 10% severity increase for all claim sizes next year.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the percent change in frequency of claims in the layer.

### Explanation
Reading "claims in the layer" as the claims that fall in the layer from 5,000 to 10,000. Next year's claims deflated by 1.1 give the equivalent current-year limits:

$$\text{Current layer frequency} = F(10{,}000) - F(5{,}000) = 0.947 - 0.958 = 0.088$$

(0.958 as printed; the table's $F(5{,}000)$ is 0.859.)

$$\text{Future layer frequency} = F(10{,}000/1.1) - F(5{,}000/1.1) = 0.938 - 0.842 = 0.096$$

$$\%\ \text{change in frequency} = 9.09\%$$

Alternatively, reading "claims in the layer" as all claims above the 5,000 attachment point (the report accepted both readings):

$$t_n = \frac{S(a/t)}{S(a)} = \frac{S(4545)}{S(5000)} = \frac{1 - 0.842}{1 - 0.859} = 1.12 \;\Rightarrow\; 12.1\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the effect of inflation on the frequency.
Most candidates were able to calculate this correctly and received full marks. A common mistake was multiplying by the trend factor (1.1).

## Part b (1 point)

Calculate the percent change in pure premiums in the layer.

### Explanation
Layer severity is the layer's expected loss divided by its frequency:

$$\text{Current layer severity} = \frac{E[X;10000] - E[X;5000]}{F(10000) - F(5000)} = \frac{2308 - 1875}{0.088} = 4920$$

$$\text{Future layer severity} = \frac{1.1\left(E[X;9091] - E[X;4545]\right)}{F(9091) - F(4545)} = \frac{1.1(2256 - 1807)}{0.096} = 1.1 \times 4677 = 5145$$

$$\%\ \text{change in severity} = \frac{5145}{4920} - 1 = 4.57\%$$

$$\%\ \text{change in pure premium} = (1 + 0.099)(1 + 0.0457) - 1 = 14.06\%$$

(0.099 as printed; the frequency change from part a is 9.09%.) The same figure follows directly from the layer's expected losses:

$$t_s = \frac{t\left(E[X;10000/1.1] - E[X;5000/1.1]\right)}{E[X;10000] - E[X;5000]} - 1 = \frac{1.1(2256 - 1807)}{2308 - 1875} - 1 = 14.06\%$$

Alternatively, with "claims in the layer" read as all claims above 5,000:

$$\text{Current layer severity} = \frac{E[X;10000] - E[X;5000]}{1 - F(5000)} = \frac{2308 - 1875}{1 - 0.859} = 3070.9$$

$$\text{Future layer severity} = \frac{1.1\left(E[X;9091] - E[X;4545]\right)}{1 - F(4545)} = \frac{1.1(2256 - 1807)}{1 - 0.842} = 3125.9$$

$$\%\ \text{change in severity} = \frac{3125.9}{3070.9} - 1 = 1.8\% \qquad \%\ \text{change in pure premium} = (1.121)(1.018) - 1 = 14.1\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate the effect of inflation on the severity and pure premium.
Common mistakes included:

- Multiplying the pure premium trend by the frequency trend
- Forgetting to multiply the severity trend by the frequency trend
- Forgetting to multiply the future layer severity by the trend factor (1.1)
