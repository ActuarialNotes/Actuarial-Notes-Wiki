An insurance company's medical claims for individual policyholders are normally distributed with a mean of 1000 and a standard deviation of 625.

The insurance company sells the medical insurance to a group of 25 individuals whose claims are mutually independent.

The insurance company will lose money if the total claims for the 25 individuals exceeds 27,500.

Calculate the probability that the insurance company will lose money.

- A) $0.07$
- B) $0.10$
- C) $0.14$
- D) $0.21$
- E) $0.44$

## Explanation

A sum of independent normal variables is normal, so the group total $S$ needs only its mean and standard deviation. Means add directly; **variances** add, so the standard deviation grows by $\sqrt{25} = 5$, not by 25:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[S] &= 25(1000) = 25{,}000 \\
\text{SD}(S) &= 625\sqrt{25} = 3125
\end{align*}
$$

Standardize the loss threshold:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S > 27{,}500] &= P\left[Z > \frac{27{,}500 - 25{,}000}{3125}\right] \\
                &= P[Z > 0.80] \\
                &= \mathbf{0.2119}
\end{align*}
$$

No continuity correction is needed — claims are continuous here, unlike a count variable.

Choice (E), 0.44, comes from scaling the standard deviation by 25 instead of $\sqrt{25}$, giving $\text{SD}(S) = 15{,}625$ and a $z$ of only 0.16. Pooling 25 policies makes the total's *relative* volatility smaller than an individual's, which is exactly why a 10% margin above expected claims is worth more than it first looks.
