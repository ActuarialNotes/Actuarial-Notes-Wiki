Non-binding guidance from the International Actuarial Association to aid its member associations in developing and maintaining professionalism standards. It defines professionalism for the actuarial profession as the application of specialist actuarial knowledge and expertise, the demonstration of ethical behaviour, and the actuary's accountability to a professional actuarial association on the basis of a code of conduct, and discusses the three principles that definition is derived from: knowledge and expertise, values and behaviour, and professional accountability.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6I (CAS)|Exam 6I]] — objectives E3–E4; the whole document.

## 1 Introduction
- The distinguishing feature of a profession that sets it apart from a trade, a craft, a guild or a syndicate is the overriding interest of the individual professional in the public well-being.
- The definition of professionalism is derived from three high-level principles: A. Knowledge and expertise; B. Values and behaviour; C. Professional accountability.

## 2 Principle A: Knowledge and Expertise
- "An actuary shall perform professional services only if competent and appropriately experienced to do so" (IAA Internal Regulations 2.2.2 (a) (iv)).
- 2.1 Specialist knowledge
- 2.2 Professional communication
- 2.3 Required education
- 2.4 Continuing Professional Development

## 3 Principle B: Values and Behaviour
- "An actuary shall perform professional services with integrity, skill and care. An actuary shall act in a manner that fulfils the profession's responsibility to the public. An actuary shall act in a manner that upholds the reputation of the actuarial profession." (IAA Internal Regulations 2.2.2 (a)(i) and (ii)).
- 3.1 Ethical behaviour
- 3.2 Integrity
- 3.3 Independent advice
- 3.4 Trust and reputation
- 3.5 Responsibility to the public

## 4 Principle C: Professional Accountability
- 4.1 Entry and qualification standards
- 4.2 Code of Conduct / Standards of practice
- 4.3 Disciplinary process

## 5 Adoption
- The document is the result of a formal review of the "Principles of Professionalism" by the IAA's Professionalism Committee; the original version was formally adopted by Council on 23 January 2012, after consultation with the IAA's Full Member Associations.
