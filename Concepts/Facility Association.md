The **Facility Association** (FA) is the non-profit entity through which Canada's automobile insurers jointly provide coverage to drivers who cannot obtain it in the voluntary market. Every insurer licensed to write auto in a participating jurisdiction is a member, so all insurers bear the residual market whether or not they service it.

Each member shares FA's results in proportion to its voluntary market share:

> $$\text{Member share of FA result} = \frac{\text{Member's voluntary premium}}{\text{Total voluntary premium}}$$

A driver unable to obtain coverage applies through a broker to a **servicing carrier**. A servicing carrier is an insurer that administers FA business under contract, issuing policies and handling claims for a fee, but not bearing the risk. FA's rates are filed with each provincial regulator like any insurer's and are typically well above voluntary market rates.

FA's programs are the **Facility Association Residual Market** (FARM), for private passenger and commercial auto that no insurer will write, the [[Risk Sharing Pool|risk sharing pools]] in several provinces, and an Uninsured Automobile Fund in some jurisdictions.

## FARM and the risk sharing pool

The two residual mechanisms differ in who issues the policy and what the driver sees:

| | FARM | [[Risk Sharing Pool]] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Policy issued by | FA, through a servicing carrier | an ordinary insurer |
| Rate | FA's higher rate | the insurer's ordinary filed rate |
| Driver knows | yes | no: the insurer transfers the risk to the pool |
| Risks handled | risks nobody will write | risks everybody must write |

Drivers who end up in FARM are those with serious conviction or claim records, unusual commercial vehicles, drivers with no insurance history who fail every filed underwriting rule, and, in provinces where rates are suppressed, ordinary risks that no longer fit anyone's rules.

> [!example]- Which residual mechanism is likely to apply to each of these drivers, and what does the driver experience? {Example}
> 1. A driver with two impaired-driving convictions in three years.
> 2. A newly licensed $19$-year-old whom the insurer would rather not write but who meets its filed rules.
> 3. An owner-operator with a specialty heavy vehicle no insurer's rating plan contemplates.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Driver 1 goes to **FARM**. No insurer's filed underwriting rules accept this record, so the driver applies through a broker to a servicing carrier, pays FA's rates and knows they are in the residual market.
> > - Driver 2 goes to the **risk sharing pool**. The insurer must write the driver under the [[Take-All-Comers Rule|take-all-comers rule]] at its filed rate because the driver meets its rules.
> > - Believing that rate inadequate for this risk, the insurer writes the policy and cedes it to the pool. The driver pays the normal rate and never learns of the transfer.
> > - Driver 3 most likely goes to **FARM**, since the vehicle falls outside every voluntary rating plan and FA maintains commercial and specialty programs for this case.

## Reading FA volume

Growth in FA volume signals rate inadequacy or classification restriction in the voluntary market, not a change in driver behaviour. FA publishes volume by province, which makes it a public indicator of each province's rate regime.

> [!trap] Confusing FARM with the risk sharing pool
> A driver in a risk sharing pool holds an ordinary policy at the insurer's ordinary rate and is not in FARM. Only FARM issues FA's own policy at FA's rates.
