A fair die is rolled repeatedly. Let $X$ be the number of rolls needed to obtain a 5 and $Y$ the number of rolls needed to obtain a 6.

Calculate $E(X \mid Y = 1)$.

- A) $5.0$
- B) $5.5$
- C) $6.0$
- D) $6.5$
- E) $7.0$

## Explanation

Unconditionally, $X$ is geometric: each roll is a success with probability $p = 1/6$, so

$$
E[X] = \frac{1}{p} = 6
$$

Conditioning on $Y = 1$ says the **first roll was a 6**. That roll therefore was not a 5, so it contributes to the count without ending the wait. Because the rolls are independent, what happens from the second roll onward is an unchanged geometric search for a 5, needing $1/p = 6$ more rolls on average.

Counting the first roll plus that fresh wait:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X \mid Y = 1] &= 1 + \frac{1}{p} \\
                &= 1 + 6 \\
                &= \mathbf{7}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C) is the unconditional $E[X] = 6$, which is what you get by treating $X$ and $Y$ as independent and ignoring the conditioning entirely. They are not independent: knowing the first roll was a 6 rules out the one outcome that would have stopped $X$ immediately, which is exactly why the expectation goes *up* rather than staying put.
