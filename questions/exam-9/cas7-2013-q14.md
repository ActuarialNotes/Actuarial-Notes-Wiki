Given the following bond portfolio for an insurance company:

| | Years to Maturity | Bond Rating | Credit Risk Solvency Capital Requirement (\$000) | Bond Issuer's Current Annual Default Probability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bond 1 | 1 | BBB | 45 | 0.2% |
| Bond 2 | 3 | BBB | 157 | 0.2% |

Assume that the best diversification can be approximated by correlation coefficients of 0.5.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Identify and briefly describe three sources of credit risk that may be associated with this bond portfolio.

### Explanation
- **Direct default:** Both bonds have a non-zero possibility of going into default and not paying the coupon/principal.
- **Downgrade risk:** The risk that the bonds will be downgraded and lose value in the market, and possibly increase the probability of default.
- **Concentration risk:** Only invest in BBB-rated bonds and no higher grade bonds. Also, all bonds seem to be of a lower rating type (no mention of industry/geography, but there may still be concentration risk).

Alternatively:

- **Default risk:** Risk that the bond issuer will default on the interest and/or principal payment of the bond.
- **Downgrade:** Risk that the bond will get a rating downgrade, which will lower the value of the bond.
- **Settlement risk:** Risk related to the lag between the end date of the bond and the payment of capital.

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to identify and describe 3 credit risks.

Many candidates did not sufficiently describe the risk (Default risk is the risk of default) or described a different risk than identified. Some did not identify the risk.  The question specifically asked for credit risk and financial risks were not allowed (interest rate risk, assetliability mismatch, reinvestment risk, etc.)

Credit was given if the list included Key driver of credit risk (Credit quality, Maturity, Size of expected loss)

## Part b (0.75 points)

Calculate the capital requirement due to credit risk for this bond portfolio assuming that both bonds are from the same issuer.

### Explanation
Because they are from the same issuer, assume the credit risk correlation is 1.00. In this case the total capital required is

$$\text{Cap Req}_1 + \text{Cap Req}_2 = 45 + 157 = 202$$

The two values are simply added because they are 100% correlated.

### Examiner Report
Bonds from the same issue should have an α= 0 and the standard formula will simplify to the sum of the individual risks. And the answer should include 45+157=202

Some credit was given for the formula, but candidates must show the final answer and how it was derived.
