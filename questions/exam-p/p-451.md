A system has three mutually independent components. Each component has a lifetime that is modeled by a random variable with density function

$$
f(y) = \begin{cases}
e^{-(y-5)}, & \text{for } y > 5 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

The system will fail when any of the three components fail.

Calculate the expected lifetime of the system.

- A) $5.20$
- B) $5.33$
- C) $5.67$
- D) $6.00$
- E) $6.33$

## Explanation

The system fails as soon as the *first* component does, so its lifetime is the **minimum** of the three — the first order statistic.

Each component's distribution function is

$$
F(y) = \int_5^y e^{-(t-5)}\,dt = 1 - e^{-(y-5)}
$$

The density of the minimum of three independent copies is

$$
\begin{align*}
g_1(y) &= 3f(y)\bigl[1 - F(y)\bigr]^2 \\
       &= 3e^{-(y-5)}\left[e^{-(y-5)}\right]^2 \\
       &= 3e^{-3(y-5)}
\end{align*}
$$

The factor of 3 counts which component fails first, and the squared survival term keeps the other two alive.

That is a shifted exponential: the minimum is $5$ plus an exponential variable with **rate 3**, hence mean $1/3$. So

$$
\begin{align*}
E[\text{system lifetime}] &= 5 + \frac{1}{3} \\
                          &= \mathbf{5.33}
\end{align*}
$$

Each component alone has mean $5 + 1 = 6$, which is choice (D). Taking the minimum of three compresses the *excess* over 5 by a factor of three, not the whole lifetime — the floor of 5 is common to all three components and cannot be reduced by adding more of them.
