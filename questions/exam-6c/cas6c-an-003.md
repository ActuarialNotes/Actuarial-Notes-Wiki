An Ontario driver insured under a standard Ontario auto policy is in a collision in Toronto with another driver whose insurer is licensed in Ontario. Under the fault determination rules, the insured driver is 30% at fault.

Determine which of the following statements about Direct Compensation – Property Damage (DC-PD) coverage are true.

- I. DC-PD pays for the 30% of the vehicle damage for which the insured driver was at fault.
- II. The insured collects for the damage to the vehicle and its contents from the insured's own insurer, even though the other driver caused part of it.
- III. DC-PD would not respond in the same way if the accident had occurred outside Ontario.

- A) I only
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

FSCO's *What do the coverages mean?* defines DC-PD as covering damage to your vehicle or its contents **if another person was at fault**. It is called direct compensation because, even though someone else caused the damage, you collect directly from your own insurer. The accident must occur in Ontario, and both drivers must be insured by insurers licensed in the province. The Marshall report's table of Ontario coverages says the same: DC-PD covers damage to the insured vehicle **to the extent the insured driver was not at fault**.

- **I is false.** DC-PD pays the 70% for which the *other* driver was at fault. The 30% for which the insured was at fault falls to collision coverage, if purchased. This is the misconception that "direct compensation" means the insured's own fault is covered.
- **II is true.** That is what "direct" means: the claim against the at-fault driver is replaced by a claim against the insured's own insurer.
- **III is true.** FSCO states that the accident must occur in Ontario, with both drivers insured by an insurer licensed in Ontario.

The true set is II and III, so the answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A**, **B** and **C** each include statement I, which confuses DC-PD with collision coverage.
- **E** would require all three statements to be true, and statement I is false.
