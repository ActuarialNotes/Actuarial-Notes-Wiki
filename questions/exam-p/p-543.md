A renter's insurance policy pays a percentage $p$ of the amount by which each loss exceeds a deductible $d$. Each policyholder reports a loss if and only if the loss exceeds the deductible.

The variance of a reported loss is $v$.

Determine the variance of the claim payment resulting from a reported loss.

- A) $pv$
- B) $p^2v$
- C) $p(v-d)$
- D) $p^2(v-d)$
- E) $(1-p)^2v$

## Explanation

Let $X$ be a reported loss, so $\text{Var}(X) = v$. The claim payment is

$$
Y = p(X - d)
$$

Because losses are reported *only* when they exceed the deductible, every reported loss produces a payment by this formula — there is no point mass at zero to complicate the distribution.

Now apply the two basic variance rules. Subtracting the constant $d$ shifts the distribution without changing its spread:

$$
\text{Var}(X - d) = \text{Var}(X) = v
$$

Multiplying by the constant $p$ scales the variance by $p^2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(Y) &= p^2\text{Var}(X - d) \\
              &= \mathbf{p^2v}
\end{align*}
$$

Choices (C) and (D) subtract $d$ from the variance, which confuses a shift in location with a change in spread — the deductible moves every payment down by the same amount and so cannot affect the variance at all. Choice (A) forgets to square the coinsurance factor.
