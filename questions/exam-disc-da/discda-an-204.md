An actuary is preparing two exhibits for a rate filing. Exhibit 1 must let a regulator look up the exact base rate and factor for each of 30 rating classes. Exhibit 2 must show that average claim severity has risen steadily over the last 40 quarters. Which pairing of displays is best?

- A) A line graph for Exhibit 1 and a table for Exhibit 2
- B) A table for Exhibit 1 and a line graph for Exhibit 2
- C) A table for each exhibit, so that every value is precise
- D) A line graph for each exhibit, so that both show a trend

## Explanation

Tables work best when the reader needs to look up individual values or needs them precisely; graphs work best when the message is in the shape of the data, such as a trend (Few, *Effectively Communicating Numbers*). Exhibit 1 is a lookup of exact factors, so a table. Exhibit 2 is a trend over time, so a line graph with time on the horizontal axis. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — swaps the two: a line through 30 unordered classes shows no meaningful shape, and 40 quarters in a table hide the trend.
- **C** — precision is the right goal for Exhibit 1 only. A table of 40 quarterly values makes the reader find the trend.
- **D** — the regulator cannot read exact factors from a graph, and rating classes are not a sequence.
