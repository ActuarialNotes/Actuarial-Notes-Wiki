**Federal-Provincial Jurisdiction** over insurance is the constitutional division that makes Canadian insurance regulation two-headed: Parliament regulates the **institution** (incorporation, solvency, capital) under its power over banking, trade and commerce and federal works, while the provinces regulate the **contract and the market** under "property and civil rights in the province" (*Constitution Act, 1867*, s. 92(13)).

> $$\text{Federal: the insurer}$$
>
> $$\text{Provincial: the policy}$$

- The line was drawn by a series of early cases holding that the *business of insurance within a province* is a provincial matter — Parliament may charter and supervise an insurer, but it may not dictate the terms on which the policy is sold in Ontario.
- Consequences that recur on the exam: a federally incorporated insurer still needs a **licence in every province**; [[Rate Regulation|rate regulation]] is provincial and therefore inconsistent across the country; [[Automobile Insurance Reform|auto reform]] happens province by province; and there is **no national insurance regulator**.
- Some lines are effectively federal by another route — marine insurance falls under federal navigation and shipping power, and federal works and undertakings bring their own wrinkles — but P&C insurance as studied on 6C follows the split above.
- The **[[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators]]** exists precisely because the division makes voluntary harmonisation the only available substitute for a single regulator.
- Compare the United States, where insurance is regulated almost entirely at the **state** level under the McCarran-Ferguson Act with no federal solvency regulator at all — Canada's federal prudential layer via [[OSFI]] is the structural difference from the 6U syllabus.

> [!example]- Which Level of Government Can Do This? {Example}
> For each proposal, say whether the federal or provincial government has the authority.
>
> 1. Ban the use of postal code in auto rating.
> 2. Raise the minimum capital an insurer must hold.
> 3. Require insurers to offer overland [[Flood Insurance|flood]] coverage in every home policy.
> 4. Create a national flood insurance backstop funded by the federal treasury.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > 1. **Provincial.** [[Territorial Rating|Territory]] is a rating variable, and risk classification in the contract is provincial — which is why the debate over postal code has played out in Ontario and not in Ottawa.
> > 2. **Federal**, for federally regulated insurers, through the [[MCT]] guideline; **provincial** for provincially incorporated ones, which typically adopt the same guideline.
> > 3. **Provincial.** Mandating a coverage term is regulating the contract.
> > 4. **Federal.** Spending its own money is within federal power even where regulating the contract is not — which is exactly how [[Disaster Financial Assistance Arrangements|DFAA]] and the proposed national flood program are structured.
> >
> > Item 4 is the pattern worth remembering: Ottawa's practical lever over insurance markets it cannot regulate is the **spending power**.
