The loss due to an injury in a certain sport is uniformly distributed on an interval.

The interquartile range of a random variable is defined as the difference between its 75th and 25th percentiles.

Determine the correct statement about the ratio of the standard deviation to the interquartile range of the loss due to a given injury in that sport.

- A) The ratio is $1 : \sqrt{3}$, regardless of the endpoints of the interval.
- B) The ratio is $1 : 1$, regardless of the endpoints of the interval.
- C) The ratio is $2 : \sqrt{3}$, regardless of the endpoints of the interval.
- D) The ratio depends on the length of the interval.
- E) The ratio depends on the location of the center of the interval.

## Explanation

Write $L$ for the interval length. Both quantities are proportional to $L$, which is what makes the ratio universal.

**Standard deviation.** A uniform distribution has variance $L^2/12$, so

$$
\sigma = \frac{L}{\sqrt{12}}
$$

**Interquartile range.** For a uniform distribution, percentiles sit at proportional positions along the interval. The 75th and 25th percentiles are three-quarters and one-quarter of the way along, so they are separated by 50% of the interval:

$$
\text{IQR} = \frac{L}{2}
$$

Taking the ratio, $L$ cancels:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{\sigma}{\text{IQR}} &= \frac{L/\sqrt{12}}{L/2} \\
                          &= \frac{2}{\sqrt{12}} \\
                          &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
\end{align*}
$$

so the ratio is $\mathbf{1 : \sqrt{3}}$, independent of both endpoints.

Choices (D) and (E) fail because a shift changes neither quantity and a stretch scales both equally — the ratio is invariant under every affine change of the interval. This is a property of the uniform *family*, not of any particular interval; other distributions have their own fixed ratio (about $1:1.35$ for the normal).
