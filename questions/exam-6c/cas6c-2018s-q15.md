The annual yield was 3.0% for calendar year 2017.

## Part a (1.25 points)

Calculate the discounted excess or deficiency of unpaid claims during calendar year 2017 for accident years 2016 and prior.

### Explanation
- APV CY 2016 $= 122{,}456 + 88{,}789 + 55{,}680 = 266{,}925$
- APV CY 2017 for prior AY $= 89{,}510 + 67{,}557 + 45{,}700 = 202{,}767$
- Paid in CY 2017 for prior AY $= 28{,}930 + 19{,}980 + 11{,}400 = 60{,}310$
- Investment income CY 2017 for prior AY:

$$\left(\frac{55{,}680 + 45{,}700}{2} + \frac{88{,}789 + 67{,}557}{2} + \frac{122{,}456 + 89{,}510}{2}\right) \times 3\% = 7{,}045$$

$$\text{Discounted excess/deficiency} = 266{,}925 - 202{,}767 - 60{,}310 + 7{,}045 = 10{,}893$$

Alternatively:

- Unpaid claims a/o 2016 $= 55{,}680 + 88{,}789 + 122{,}456 = 266{,}925$
- Unpaid claims a/o 2017 $= 45{,}700 + 67{,}557 + 89{,}510 = 202{,}767$
- Paid claims in 2017 $= 11{,}400 + 19{,}980 + 28{,}930 = 60{,}310$
- Investment income in 2017 $=$ Annual yield $\times$ (reserves @ 2016 $+$ reserves @ 2017) / 2 $= 3\% \times (266{,}925 + 202{,}767) / 2 = 7{,}045$
- Discounted excess (deficiency) $= 266{,}925 - 60{,}310 + 7{,}045 - 202{,}767 = 10{,}893$

### Examiner Report
Candidates needed to calculate the excess/deficiency of unpaid claims, including each component of the formula, during calendar year 2017 for accident years 2016 and prior.
A common error was calculating the excess/deficiency on an undiscounted basis by:

- Calculating the investment income incorrectly
- Pulling incorrect figures from the triangles to enter into the equation

## Part b (2 points)

Calculate the **undiscounted** loss ratio and **discounted** loss ratio as at December 31, 2017 in the Unpaid Claims and Loss Ratio Analysis Exhibit for accident year 2014.

### Explanation
- Undiscounted Ultimate $= 39{,}412 + 29{,}980 + 29{,}750 + 20{,}800 + 11{,}400 = 131{,}342$
- Undiscounted Loss Ratio $= 131{,}342 / (210{,}000 + 120{,}000 - 130{,}000) = 65.7\%$

- Investment income from unpaid CY 2017 for AY 2014 $= (55{,}680 + 45{,}700)/2 \times 3\% = 1{,}520.7$
- Discounted ultimate $= 131{,}342 - 39{,}412 + 45{,}700 = 137{,}630$
- Investment income from UPR $= (120{,}000 + 130{,}000)/2 \times 3\% = 3{,}750$

$$\text{Discounted LR} = \frac{137{,}630 + (1{,}855 + 2{,}997 + 2{,}036 + 1{,}520.7)}{210{,}000 + 120{,}000 - 130{,}000 + 3{,}750} = 63.4\%$$

(as printed: the investment income on unpaid claims is deducted from the discounted ultimate, as in the alternative below, to give 63.4%.)

Alternatively:

- Earned premium (AY 2014) $= 210{,}000 + 120{,}000 - 130{,}000 = 200{,}000$
- Claims incurred $= 29{,}980 + 29{,}750 + 20{,}800 + 11{,}400 + 39{,}412 = 131{,}342$
- Undiscounted Loss Ratio $= 131{,}342 / 200{,}000 = 65.7\%$
- Investment income (UPR) $= (120{,}000 + 130{,}000) / 2 \times 3\% = 3{,}750$
- Investment income (Loss reserves) $= 1{,}855 + 2{,}997 + 2{,}036 + 3\% \times (55{,}680 + 45{,}700)/2 = 8{,}409$

$$\text{Discounted LR} = \frac{(29{,}980 + 29{,}750 + 20{,}800 + 11{,}400) + 45{,}700 - 8{,}409}{200{,}000 + 3{,}750} = 63.4\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates needed to calculate both the undiscounted and the discounted loss ratios.
Common errors included:

- Incorrectly calculating earned premiums
- Omitting to include either investment income on unpaid claims or investment income on UPR in the discounted loss ratio
