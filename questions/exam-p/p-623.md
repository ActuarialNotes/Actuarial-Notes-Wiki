The scores on a math exam are modeled by a normal distribution. The mode of the exam scores is 56.00, and the 40th percentile is 52.20.

Calculate the percentile corresponding to an exam score of 65.50.

- A) 71st
- B) 74th
- C) 78th
- D) 81st
- E) 85th

## Explanation

Let $X$ be the score. The normal density is symmetric and single-peaked, so its mode, median and mean all coincide — the given mode makes $\mu = 56$.

The 40th percentile then fixes $\sigma$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X < 52.20] &= 0.40 \\
\frac{52.20 - 56}{\sigma} &= -0.253347 \\
\sigma &= \frac{-3.80}{-0.253347} = 14.999
\end{align*}
$$

Now standardize 65.50:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X < 65.50] &= P\!\left[Z < \frac{65.50 - 56}{14.999}\right] \\
             &= P[Z < 0.6334] \\
             &= 0.7368
\end{align*}
$$

so 65.50 sits at the **74th percentile**.

Two sign checks keep this on the rails. The 40th percentile must fall *below* the mean, so the $z$ attached to it is negative and $\sigma = (52.20 - 56)/z$ comes out positive; flipping that sign yields a negative $\sigma$, which is the signal that the tail was read backwards. And 65.50 lies above the mean, so the answer must exceed the 50th percentile — as 74th does.
