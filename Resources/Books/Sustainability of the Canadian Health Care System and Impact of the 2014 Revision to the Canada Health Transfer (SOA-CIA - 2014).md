A research report, dated September 2013 and prepared by Stéphane Levert for the Canadian Institute of Actuaries and the Society of Actuaries, that estimates the future cost of the Canadian health care system over a 25-year horizon. It assesses the system's sustainability and analyzes the implications of the changes to the Canada Health Transfer (CHT) that the federal government proposed on December 19, 2011.

## Executive Summary
- Provincial and territorial health care expenditures over the last five years outpaced the annual rate of inflation by nearly 4 percent; the federal government supports provincial and territorial delivery through the Canada Health Transfer (CHT).
- Without significant government intervention, the Canadian health care system in its current form is not sustainable.
- With no governmental steps to curb expenditures, provincial and territorial spending on health care grows at 5.1 percent real growth per year, from 44 percent of total provincial and territorial revenues today to 103 percent by 2037.
- With some governmental action limiting real growth to 3.5 percent (the base scenario, Appendix 4), health care still absorbs 69 percent of total revenues available to provinces and territories by 2037, and 86 percent of own-source revenues.
- The proposed CHT changes reduce the federal government's portion of provincial and territorial health care expenditures from 21.0 percent to 14.3 percent by 2037.
- The supply of physicians needs to increase by at least 46 percent over the next 25 years to keep up with demand from aging and population growth.
- Two key causes drive the growth in the share of budgets used for health care: real growth in expenditures, much of it due to the aging population, and reduced GDP growth as the ratio of working Canadians falls.

## Chapter 11—Public Accounts and the Canada Health Transfer
- Introduction
    - Historically, provincial and territorial health care expenditures have grown at an annual real rate of 4.2 percent, 1.0 percent from aging and population growth and 3.2 percent from real price increases.
    - The report assumes unspecified public policies limit future cost increases, for an expected growth rate of 3.5 percent on top of inflation (Table 11.1).
    - Under that base scenario expenditures grow from $135.0 billion in 2012 to $315.2 billion in 2037, in constant 2012 dollars.
- Implications on Public Accounts
    - Future health care expenditures are compared with the revenues available to provinces and territories, split into own-source revenues and federal cash transfers.
    - Own-source revenues are assumed to grow at the same rate as GDP; all federal cash transfers other than the CHT are projected to grow at the rate of GDP.
    - Total available revenues grow at 1.9 percent a year in real terms (1.5 percent own-source revenues, 2.1 percent federal cash transfers), against 3.5 percent for health care expenditures.
    - Health care expenditures would use about 65 percent of available revenues in 2037, up from 44 percent in 2012, and 86 percent of own-source revenues.
- Canada Health Transfer
    - The CHT currently includes tax points and cash transfers.
    - CHT cash transfers are allocated so that each province or territory receives a total CHT entitlement proportionate to its population.

## Appendix 4—Projections Using Different Scenarios
