You are reviewing a dataset with 100 observations in four variables: $X_1$, $X_2$, $X_3$, and $X_4$. You analyze this data using two principal components:

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_1 &= \varphi_{11}X_1 + \varphi_{21}X_2 + \varphi_{31}X_3 + \varphi_{41}X_4 \\
Z_2 &= \varphi_{12}X_1 + \varphi_{22}X_2 + \varphi_{32}X_3 + \varphi_{42}X_4
\end{align*}
$$

You are given the following statements:

I. $\sum_{i=1}^{100}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{4}\varphi_{j1}x_{ij}\right)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{100}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{4}\varphi_{j2}x_{ij}\right)^2$
II. $\sum_{j=1}^{4}\varphi_{j1}\varphi_{j2} = 0$
III. $\sum_{j=1}^{4}\varphi_{j1}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{4}\varphi_{j2}^2 = 1$

Determine which of the preceding statements are always true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

Translate each statement out of summation notation.

**I is false.** The two sides are the sums of squared scores on the first and second principal components — that is, (up to the constant $1/n$) the sample variances of $Z_1$ and $Z_2$. Principal components are constructed in order of decreasing variance, so $\operatorname{Var}(Z_1) \geq \operatorname{Var}(Z_2)$, with equality only in the degenerate case of tied eigenvalues. Requiring them to be equal is exactly backwards.

**II is true.** This is the dot product of the two loading vectors. Loading vectors are the eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix and are constructed to be **orthogonal**, so the dot product is 0 for every dataset.

**III is false.** Each loading vector is normalized to *unit length* individually:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{4}\varphi_{j1}^2 = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{j=1}^{4}\varphi_{j2}^2 = 1
$$

so their sum is 2, not 1.

Only statement II is always true, so the answer is **B**.
