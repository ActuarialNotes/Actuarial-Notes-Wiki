In the supplemental material, you have been given a case study, "Warranty Payments Case Study". You have also been asked to evaluate three statements regarding the residuals results of the study.

*The relevant output from the case study is reproduced below. The log of individual warranty payments for one product is modelled with linear mixed-effects models in R's `nlme` package; each plot shows a model's normalized residuals, against its predicted log payment and by payment lag (months since sale, 1 to 12).*

**Model II** — payment lag and month sold as fixed effects, store as a random effect, variance assumed to be constant:

![Two plots of Model II normalized residuals. Top: residuals against predicted log payment, which runs from about 0.5 to 4.9; the spread widens steadily from about plus or minus 1.5 at the left to about -4.5 to +5 at the right, a fan shape. Bottom: box plots of the residuals by payment lag 1 to 12; the boxes and whiskers widen steadily with lag, from a box of about -0.3 to +0.3 at lag 1 to about -1.0 to +0.9 at lag 12](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q02-model2-residuals.png)

**Model III** — the same fixed and random effects, with variance a function of payment lag using the exponential variance function, `varExp(form = ~Pay_Lag/2)`:

![Model III normalized residuals against predicted log payment, which runs from about 0.5 to 4.9: a band of roughly constant width, mostly between -2.5 and +2.5 with scattered points out to about plus or minus 4, at every predicted value](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q02-model3-resid-vs-predicted.png)

![Box plots of Model III normalized residuals by payment lag 1 to 12: every box runs from about -0.7 to +0.7 with a median near 0 and whiskers near plus or minus 2.7, with no widening or narrowing across lags](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q02-model3-resid-by-lag.png)

**Model IV** — the same fixed and random effects, with variance a function of payment lag using the power variance function, `varPower(form =~Pay_Lag)`:

![Model IV normalized residuals against predicted log payment, which runs from about 0.5 to 4.9: a band of roughly constant width, mostly between -2.7 and +2.7 with scattered points out to about plus or minus 4, at every predicted value](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q02-model4-resid-vs-predicted.png)

![Box plots of Model IV normalized residuals by payment lag 1 to 12: boxes from about -0.7 to +0.7 with medians near 0 and similar whiskers at every lag, lags 1 and 12 only marginally wider than the rest and no trend across lags](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/masii-2019f-q02-model4-resid-by-lag.png)

- I. Model II does not show any indication of heteroscedasticity.
- II. Model III does show indications of heteroscedasticity.
- III. Model IV does show indications of heteroscedasticity.

Determine which of the above statements are true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

Normalized residuals divide out the variance the model assumes. If a model's variance assumption is right, their spread should be the same across the predicted values and across payment lags, and a spread that grows or shrinks is a sign of heteroscedasticity.

**I is false.** Model II assumes constant variance, and its residuals fan out. Against the predicted values the band widens from about $\pm 1.5$ at the left to about $-4.5$ to $+5$ at the right. By payment lag, the boxes and whiskers grow steadily from lag 1 to lag 12, with the interquartile range about three times as wide at lag 12 (roughly $-1.0$ to $+0.9$) as at lag 1 (roughly $-0.3$ to $+0.3$). This is clear heteroscedasticity, with the variance of log payments rising with payment lag, and it is what Models III and IV are built to fix.

**II is false.** Model III lets the variance change exponentially with payment lag. Its normalized residuals form a band of roughly constant width against the predicted values, and the box plots by lag are nearly identical, each box running from about $-0.7$ to $+0.7$ with whiskers near $\pm 2.7$. The variance function has absorbed the pattern, and no heteroscedasticity remains.

**III is false.** Model IV lets the variance follow a power of payment lag, and its plots tell the same story as Model III's: a constant-width band against the predicted values and boxes of about the same size at every lag. Lags 1 and 12 are marginally wider, but there is no trend.

None of the three statements is true, which is not one of options (A) through (D), so the answer is **E**.
