You are given the following statements are true regarding the variance-covariance $\mathbf{D}$ matrix relating the random effects in a Linear Mixed Model:

I. The matrix is always symmetrical.
II. All elements of the matrix must be non-negative.
III. Under the compound symmetry structure, all the elements along the diagonal of the matrix are equal.

Determine which of the above statements are true.

- A) None of I, II, or III is true
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I is true.** $\mathbf{D}$ is a variance-covariance matrix, and $\operatorname{Cov}(u_j, u_k) = \operatorname{Cov}(u_k, u_j)$ for every pair, so it is symmetric by construction.

**II is false.** The *diagonal* elements are variances and must be non-negative, but the off-diagonal elements are covariances and may be negative. A random-intercept-and-slope model where groups starting high tend to grow more slowly has a negative intercept-slope covariance — a perfectly ordinary fit.

**III is true.** Compound symmetry is the structure

$$
\mathbf{D} = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma^2 + \sigma_1^2 & \sigma_1^2 & \sigma_1^2 \\ \sigma_1^2 & \sigma^2 + \sigma_1^2 & \sigma_1^2 \\ \sigma_1^2 & \sigma_1^2 & \sigma^2 + \sigma_1^2 \end{bmatrix},
$$

a constant variance down the diagonal and a single common covariance everywhere off it.

Statements I and III only, so the answer is **C**.
