## Part a (0.75 points)

List three components of an unpaid claims estimate.

### Explanation
An unpaid claims estimate decomposes into three distinct pieces:

- **Case outstanding** — the unpaid amounts held on claims that have been reported to and recorded by the insurer, as set by the claims adjusters.
- **IBNER** (incurred but not enough reported) — the expected *future development on reported claims*, i.e. the amount by which the case reserves on known claims will prove inadequate or redundant as those claims mature.
- **IBNYR** (incurred but not yet reported), or pure IBNR — the estimate for claims that have already occurred but have not yet been reported to the insurer.

$$\text{Unpaid Claims} = \text{Case Outstanding} + \text{IBNER} + \text{IBNYR}$$

Reopened claims and claims in transit (reported but not yet recorded) are also acceptable components.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to list three distinct parts of an unpaid claims estimate. Common mistakes included naming claims-related expenses such as ULAE, ALAE or LAE, or naming recoveries, without describing them as *unpaid*; and describing the methods or the considerations used in selecting an unpaid claims estimate rather than the components of the estimate itself.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Briefly describe how an inadequate unpaid claims estimate can impact the decision-making of each of the following parties:

i. Internal management

ii. Investors

iii. Regulators

### Explanation
An inadequate estimate makes the company's results look better than they are, so each stakeholder acts on an overly optimistic picture.

**i. Internal management.** Believing recent accident years to be more profitable than they really are, management may set rates too low or approve rate decreases, loosen underwriting guidelines, or expand into a segment that is in fact unprofitable. It may also commit capital it does not have — funding large projects or paying dividends — because the reported surplus is overstated.

**ii. Investors.** The understated liability overstates both earnings and surplus, so the company appears more profitable and better capitalized than it is. Investors overvalue the company and put in capital they would not commit on accurate figures; when the deficiency emerges and reserves must be strengthened, earnings and share value fall.

**iii. Regulators.** Solvency monitoring depends on the reported liabilities. An inadequate estimate makes the company's capital position look adequate, so the regulator does not intervene — or does not intervene in time — in a company drifting toward insolvency. It may also decline a rate increase the insurer genuinely needs, because the filed indication rests on understated losses.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to explain how an inadequate unpaid claims estimate affects each stakeholder's decisions. The common mistake was listing general considerations such as pricing or underwriting without analyzing how the inadequacy would actually change the decision that stakeholder makes.
