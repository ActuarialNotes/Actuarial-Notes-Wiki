The following information is available for an insurance company:

| Month | Paid Development Factors to Ultimate | Reported Development Factors to Ultimate |
|---|---|---|
| 12-Ult | 2.22 | 1.54 |
| 15-Ult | 1.82 | 1.33 |
| 18-Ult | 1.50 | 1.25 |
| 21-Ult | 1.35 | 1.18 |
| 24-Ult | 1.25 | 1.11 |

Accident year 2013 as of March 31, 2014:

- Reported claims: $2,200
- Paid claims: $1,650
- Selected ultimate claims: $3,000

Accident year 2013 as of May 31, 2014:

- Reported claims: $2,500
- Paid claims: $1,875

## Part a (1.25 points)

Considering the data through March 31, 2014, compare the cumulative expected reported claims to the actual reported claims as of May 31, 2014 for accident year 2013.

### Explanation
Convert the reported development factors to cumulative percentages reported:

| Age | Reported CDF | Reported % |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | 1.54 | 64.94% |
| 15 | 1.33 | 75.19% |
| 18 | 1.25 | 80% |
| 21 | 1.18 | 84.75% |
| 24 | 1.11 | 90.09% |

March 31, 2014 is 15 months and May 31, 2014 is 17 months.

$$\text{IBNR as of 3/31/14} = 3{,}000 - 2{,}200 = 800$$

Linear interpolated reported % at month 17: $75.19\% + (80\% - 75.19\%) \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 78.49\%$, so the CDF at 17 months is 1.276.

$$\text{Expected reported 3/31 to 5/31} = 800 \times \frac{78.4\% - 75.19\%}{1 - 75.19\%} = 103.51$$

$$\text{Cumulative expected as of 5/31} = 2{,}200 + 103.51 = 2{,}303.51$$

Actual as of 5/31 is 2,500, so the difference is $2{,}303.51 - 2{,}500 = -196.49$ — significantly more was reported than expected.

Alternatively, apply the emergence pattern to the selected ultimate instead of to the unreported amount:

$$\text{Expected emergence} = 3{,}000 \times \left(1.25^{-1} - 1.33^{-1}\right) \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 96.24$$

Actual reported between March 31 and May 31 was $2{,}500 - 2{,}200 = 300$, a difference of 203.76.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Considering the data through March 31, 2014, compare the cumulative expected paid claims to the actual paid claims as of May 31, 2014 for accident year 2013.

### Explanation
Convert the paid development factors to cumulative percentages paid:

| Age | Paid CDF | Paid % |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | 2.22 | 45.05% |
| 15 | 1.82 | 54.95% |
| 18 | 1.5 | 66.67% |

$$\text{Unpaid as of 3/31/14} = 3{,}000 - 1{,}650 = 1{,}350$$

Linear interpolated paid % at month 17: $54.95\% + (66.67\% - 54.95\%) \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 62.76\%$.

$$\text{Expected paid 3/31 to 5/31} = 1{,}350 \times \frac{62.76\% - 54.95\%}{1 - 54.95\%} = 234.04$$

$$\text{Cumulative expected as of 5/31} = 1{,}650 + 234.04 = 1{,}884.04$$

Actual as of 5/31 is 1,875, so the difference is $1{,}884.04 - 1{,}875 = 9.04$ — actual paid is slightly lower than expected but broadly in line.

Alternatively, applying the pattern to the selected ultimate:

$$\text{Expected emergence} = 3{,}000 \times \left(1.5^{-1} - 1.82^{-1}\right) \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 234.43$$

Actual paid between March 31 and May 31 was $1{,}875 - 1{,}650 = 225$, a difference of 9.43.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Given the results calculated in parts a. and b. above, describe a situation in which the actuary would revise the March 31, 2014 estimate of ultimate claims.

### Explanation
Answers receiving full credit include:

- If the higher than expected reported claims was due to an actual change in the underlying loss ratio that had not yet shown up in the data at 15 months, the actuary should change the estimate of 2013 ultimate claims.
- If there was an influx of claims explaining the increase (an unusually stormy season, say), the ultimate would have to be adjusted to reflect the expected increase in ultimate claims.
- It could be a large loss reported but not yet paid. Since this is not anticipated, increase the estimate.
- If a material change has caused reported claims to come in higher than expected — a change in law increasing minimum limits, which shows in reported before paid — the ultimate may need to be increased to reflect the higher ultimate implied by actual reported emergence.

## Part d (0.5 points)

Given the results calculated in parts a. and b. above, describe a situation in which the actuary would not revise the March 31, 2014 estimate of ultimate claims.

### Explanation
Answers receiving full credit include:

- The actuary would not revise the estimate if there was a change in case reserve philosophy (strengthening). Actual paid losses were in line with expected, and the reported increase is due to case strengthening with no expected impact on the ultimate settlement value.
- A change in the claims reporting pattern with no impact on ultimate settlement (more claims reported earlier).
- If the claims department changed to get claims into the system more quickly (claims in transit were previously higher), there would be no reason to change the ultimate.
