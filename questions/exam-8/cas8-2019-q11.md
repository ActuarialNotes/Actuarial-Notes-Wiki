Consider the following sample of five insurance risks which have been experience rated:

| Risk | Mod | Manual Loss Ratio | Standard Loss Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.60 | 0.60 | 1.00 |
| 2 | 0.79 | 0.80 | 1.01 |
| 3 | X | 0.96 | Y |
| 4 | 1.10 | 1.10 | 1.00 |
| 5 | 1.14 | 1.15 | 1.01 |

The actual claims for Risk 3 in the experience period are as follows:

| Claim # | Incurred Loss Amount |
|---|---|
| 001 | 3,200 |
| 002 | 3,000 |
| 003 | 2,800 |
| 004 | 2,700 |
| 005 | 3,300 |
| 006 | 2,900 |
| 007 | 10,000 |
| 008 | 3,100 |
| 009 | 3,000 |
| 010 | 3,100 |
| Total | 37,100 |

- Expected Losses for Risk 3 in the experience period = 32,000
- Credibility factor = 0.75

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the modification factor, X, for Risk 3.

### Explanation
$$X = 1 + Z\,\frac{A - E}{E} = 1 + 0.75 \times \frac{37{,}100 - 32{,}000}{32{,}000} = 1.1195$$

Equivalently, $X = \dfrac{37{,}100(0.75) + 32{,}000(0.25)}{32{,}000} = 1.12$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide the formula used to calculate the modification factor for Risk 3, and to provide the final modification factor in numeric form.
A common mistake included:

- Making a simple calculation error

## Part b (0.5 points)

Provide a recommendation to improve the effectiveness of experience rating for Risk 3 and briefly justify.

### Explanation
Cap claims at 4,000. This includes all of the smaller claims but prevents the one large claim (10,000) from unduly affecting the mod. (The report accepted various capping thresholds and claim removal proposals.)

Alternatively, reduce the credibility. The standard loss ratio for Risk 3 is $0.96/1.1195 = 0.8575$, which is not close to the other risks' standard loss ratios (about 1.00). The mod should be lower — closer to the manual loss ratio of 0.96 — so the experience should be given less credibility: more weight on expected experience and less on actual.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide a reasonable recommendation to improve the effectiveness of experience rating for Risk 3, and briefly justify the recommendation.
Common mistakes included:

- Providing a correct recommendation but not justifying it
- Providing a recommendation that did not improve the effectiveness of experience rating for Risk 3.

## Part c (2.75 points)

Show, quantitatively, that the recommendation in part b. above improves the plan. Assume no other risks are impacted by the recommendation.

### Explanation
**Capping claims (at 5,000 here).** The efficiency test compares the variance of the standard loss ratios with that of the manual loss ratios; the lower the statistic, the better the plan.

Without the change, $Y = 0.96/1.1195 = 0.8575$.

- Average manual LR $= (0.6 + 0.8 + \dots + 1.15)/5 = 0.922$; using the sample variance, $\text{Var(Manual LR)} = \left[(0.6 - 0.922)^2 + (0.8 - 0.922)^2 + \dots + (1.15 - 0.922)^2\right]/(5 - 1) = 0.05092$
- Average standard LR $= (1 + 1.01 + 0.8575 + 1 + 1.01)/5 = 0.9755$; $\text{Var(Std LR)} = \left[(1 - 0.9755)^2 + (1.01 - 0.9755)^2 + \dots + (1.01 - 0.9755)^2\right]/(5 - 1) = 0.004376$
- Efficiency statistic $= 0.004376/0.05092 = 0.0859$

With actual losses limited to 5,000 per claim (37,100 becomes 32,100):

$$\text{New Mod} = \frac{0.75(32{,}100) + 0.25(32{,}000)}{32{,}000} = 1.0023 \qquad Y = \frac{0.96}{1.0023} = 0.9578$$

- $\text{Var(Manual LR)} = 0.05092$, unchanged
- Average standard LR $= (1 + 1.01 + 0.9578 + 1 + 1.01)/5 = 0.99556$; $\text{Var(Std LR)} = \left[(1 - 0.99556)^2 + (1.01 - 0.99556)^2 + \dots + (1.01 - 0.99556)^2\right]/(5 - 1) = 0.0004706$
- Efficiency statistic $= 0.0004706/0.05092 = 0.009242$

Since $0.009242 < 0.0859$, the loss cap improves the plan.

Alternatively, **decreasing the credibility** (to 0.25):

$$\text{New Mod} = \frac{0.25(37{,}100) + 0.75(32{,}000)}{32{,}000} = 1.04 \qquad Y = \frac{32{,}000}{33{,}333 \times 1.04} = 92.3\%$$

| Old Manual | Old Standard | New Manual | New Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.60 | 1.00 | 0.60 | 1.00 |
| 0.80 | 1.01 | 0.80 | 1.01 |
| 0.96 | 0.86 | 0.96 | 0.92 |
| 1.10 | 1.00 | 1.10 | 1.00 |
| 1.15 | 1.01 | 1.15 | 1.01 |

$$\text{Eff test}_{\text{Old}} = \frac{0.00384}{0.040736} = 0.0715 \qquad \text{Eff test}_{\text{New}} = \frac{0.001176}{0.040736} = 0.029$$

The lower test statistic implies an improvement.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate a new mod and standard loss ratio (X & Y) based on the recommendation in part b, perform an efficiency test on both the original and recommended experience rating plans, and explain the result of the test. Full credit was given for various forms of variance calculation (e.g., sample or population variance or standard deviation). Partial credit was awarded for a qualitative response based on the quintiles test. Candidates should note, though, that Fisher et. al. (p. 10) specifies that the quintiles test is a qualitative test (observing general trends) , and this question specifically asked for a quantitative demonstration (calculating and comparing two quantities to support a conclusion).
Common mistakes included:

- Calculating a test statistic for the recommended plan but failing to calculate one for the original plan
- Not drawing a conclusion on the results of the test
- Multiplying SLR = MLR * Mod rather than dividing SLR = MLR / Mod.
