Losses under a policy are uniformly distributed on the interval $[0, 480]$. For each loss, the claim payment is a constant percentage of the amount in excess of a deductible of 240.

The insurer wants the variance of the claim payment for a single loss to equal 2000.

Calculate the percentage the insurer should choose.

- A) $11.1\%$
- B) $33.3\%$
- C) $57.7\%$
- D) $64.5\%$
- E) $91.3\%$

## Explanation

Let $Y$ be the loss, uniform on $[0,480]$, and $X$ the payment. Then $X = 0$ when $Y < 240$ and $X = p(Y - 240)$ otherwise, so $X$ has a **mixed** distribution: a point mass at zero plus a continuous piece.

$$
P[X = 0] = P[Y < 240] = 0.5
$$

For $0 < x < 240p$, the continuous piece inherits the uniform density:

$$
F_X(x) = P\left[Y \leq 240 + \frac{x}{p}\right] = 0.5 + \frac{x}{480p}, \qquad f_X(x) = \frac{1}{480p}
$$

Take the two moments, remembering the point mass contributes nothing to either:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X] &= 0(0.5) + \int_0^{240p} \frac{x}{480p}\,dx \\
     &= \frac{(240p)^2}{960p} = 60p
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= 0^2(0.5) + \int_0^{240p} \frac{x^2}{480p}\,dx \\
       &= \frac{(240p)^3}{1440p} = 9600p^2
\end{align*}
$$

Setting the variance to 2000:

$$
\begin{align*}
2000 &= 9600p^2 - (60p)^2 \\
     &= 6000p^2 \\
p    &= \sqrt{\frac{2000}{6000}} \\
     &= \mathbf{0.577}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 33.3%, is $p^2 = 1/3$ reported without taking the square root. The point mass at zero is what makes $E[X^2]$ and $E[X]^2$ differ by only $6000p^2$ rather than the $9600p^2$ a purely continuous payment would give.
