A customer uses an ATM. Let $Y$ be the time that the customer spends waiting in line before reaching the ATM. Let $Z$ be the time that the customer spends at the ATM performing the transaction. $Y$ and $Z$ are independent random variables. Let $X = Y + Z$.

Assume the following:

i) $E(X) = 2.00$
ii) $\text{Var}(X) = 2.25$
iii) $E(Y) = 1.20$
iv) $\text{Var}(Y) = 0.90$

Calculate the coefficient of variation for $Z$.

- A) $0.59$
- B) $0.69$
- C) $1.16$
- D) $1.35$
- E) $1.45$

## Explanation

Means add, and — because $Y$ and $Z$ are independent — variances add too. Both relations invert directly:

$$
\begin{align*}
2 &= 1.2 + E(Z) &\implies&& E(Z) &= 0.8 \\
2.25 &= 0.9 + \text{Var}(Z) &\implies&& \text{Var}(Z) &= 1.35
\end{align*}
$$

The coefficient of variation is the standard deviation divided by the mean:

$$
\text{CV}(Z) = \frac{\sqrt{1.35}}{0.8} = \frac{1.16190}{0.8} = \mathbf{1.4524}
$$

Choice (D), 1.35, is $\text{Var}(Z)$ handed back before either the square root or the division. Choice (C), 1.16, stops one step later, at $\text{SD}(Z) = 1.1619$. Only the third step — dividing by the mean — produces the coefficient of variation, and here the mean is below 1, so that division *increases* the figure rather than shrinking it.
