A personal lines pricing actuary who is working for a large insurance company in the province of Ontario, is asked by management to review three opportunities presented below.

**Opportunity A:** A broker has a contract with the company and believes there is an opportunity to target young drivers in the more affluent areas of Toronto. The broker wants to offer these drivers a quick and superior quoting service and policy issuance process based on the average income in their postal code.

**Opportunity B:** The CEO of the insurance company wants to target customers who have personal property policies with the company. To do so, the CEO suggests offering a multi-line discount of 10% on the auto policy. The historical experience of these customers is reviewed and they are found to indeed have better auto loss experience.

**Opportunity C:** A combination of competitive analysis and loss experience shows that the company is underpriced in the city of Hamilton but overpriced in the London area. The company would like to increase rates in Hamilton by 10%, and reduce rates in London by 5%. The overall impact of these changes is 0%.

For each opportunity, answer the following:

## Part a (1 point)

Explain whether the opportunity is permissible according to Ontario regulations.

### Explanation
**Opportunity 1:** This is not permissible. According to Regulation 7/00 (UDAP), credit information is defined to include income. UDAP prohibits using credit information to treat customers differently.

**Opportunity 2:** Yes, this is allowed, there is no barrier to offering a discount for combined policies and it is justified by experience

**Opportunity 3:** Yes. There is no barrier to changing territorial differentials

Alternatively:

- A. Not permissible. Changes to the ON regulation regarding credit information meant that insurers couldn't provide different treatment for quotes and renewal applications based on credit information.
- B. Permissible. A discount can be offered if the target group has lower costs due to favourable experience.
- C. Permissible. The insurer is allowed to provide different rates for territories based on experience and an approved methodology.

## Part b (1.5 points)

If the opportunity is permissible, identify three types of actuarial information relevant to this specific change that must be provided to the Financial Services Commission of Ontario (FSCO) to obtain approval.

### Explanation
**Opportunity 1:** Not applicable due to 1

**Opportunity 2:** Information in **section 5** is required (three required for full points):

- Approach in costing and narrative of process
- Justification based on lower expenses, costs, or loss costs
- Any external data used, source and applicability
- Written premium distribution for discounts
- Exposure distribution for discounts
- Current and proposed distribution of insurer's book to determine average premium change
- All assumptions and detailed calculations

**Opportunity 3:** Information in **section 4.k** is required (three required for full points):

- Territorial indications using insurers own data
- Any external data used, source and applicability
- Current indicated and proposed territory differentials by coverage
- Written premium distribution by coverage and territory
- Exposure distribution by coverage and territory
- Credibility procedure if used
- Changes to approach or underlying data from prior filing
- Offbalancing – all data used and calculation of offbalance
- Judgements used in offbalancing process

Alternatively:

- B. The number of exposures affected, the impact of the discount on premium. The written premium for the groups receiving and not receiving the discount.
- C. The methodology used for risk classification for territories e.g. GLM and the results. The written / earned premium for each territory. The overall and individual impacts of the risk classification changes.

### Examiner Report
This is a very important question for any candidate practicing in Canada.  The candidates need to know what is legal for auto insurance in Ontario.
For part A. it is important they understand what is allowable for quoting practices, as well as pricing.  We also wanted the candidates to mention that income is considered part of the definition of credit information, for full marks.
For part B. there are many possible answers, and many outside the key were acceptable.  The question did specify that the answers should be specific to the particular change discussed, not just filings in general.
