A CAS study note (January 2020) giving a foundational discussion of the functions and financial implications of reinsurance. It works through nine roles reinsurance plays in the insurance marketplace, each with a business issue and a reinsurance solution, and gives examples of the accounting treatment of the reinsurance transactions on an array of financial statements.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objectives D1 and D4; the whole study note, including errata.

## Introduction
- The paper opens with the 1842 fire in Hamburg, from whose ashes emerged the first dedicated reinsurance company, Cologne Re, established to protect against catastrophic risk.
- It discusses catastrophic risk and eight other roles of reinsurance, works through real-world business issues and the corresponding reinsurance solutions, and highlights the accounting impact of each solution on financial statements and metrics.

## Nine Functions of Reinsurance
- 1. Large Line Capacity
    - Large line capacity is an insurer's maximum appetite for assuming risk on a single insurance policy or location; the example compares the statutory statement of earnings of a company that declines large risks with one that writes them and buys per-risk reinsurance.
- 2. Catastrophic Risk Protection
- 3. Surplus Relief / Capital Efficiencies
- 4. Stabilization of Results
    - The paper stresses defining what is meant by stabilization: stability of what metric (net combined ratio, earnings), over what time horizon.
- 5. Market Entrance / Underwriting Guidance
- 6. Withdrawal from a Market Segment
    - Management may remove liabilities from the balance sheet by retroactive reinsurance, which comes with complex accounting requirements; the paper cites SSAP 62R on agreements that transfer both components of insurance risk and cover liabilities that occurred prior to the effective date.
- 7. Mandatory and Voluntary Pools
- 8. Internal Reinsurance Transactions
- 9. Fronting Arrangements
    - A fronting company licensed where the risk is located issues the original policy, then cedes the financial results to the unlicensed company.

## Conclusion
- The paper is an introductory-level description of the motivations for buying reinsurance and the financial impacts of such decisions; a holistic approach includes the accounting, legal, regulatory, economic, tax and financial facets of the transaction.
