The standard governing how actuaries design, review, and change **risk classification systems**. On the [[Exam 5 (CAS)|Exam 5]] syllabus for objectives A4–A5 (evaluating ratemaking data and grouping it to balance [[Homogeneity]] and [[Credibility]]). Adopted December 2005; a revision was subsequently adopted with a December 2023 effective date.

## Section 1 — Purpose, Scope, and Effective Date

- Applies whenever an actuary designs, reviews, or changes a [[Classification Ratemaking|risk classification system]] used in financial or personal security programs
- Does not apply where classes are prescribed by law or where the actuary is only implementing an existing system

## Section 2 — Definitions

- **Risk characteristic** — a measurable or observable attribute used to assign a risk to a class
- **Risk class** — a set of risks grouped together under a classification system
- **Adverse selection** — disproportionate purchasing or persistency by risks whose expected cost exceeds the price charged
- **Credibility** — a measure of the predictive value of the data (see [[Credibility]])
- **Homogeneity** — the degree to which risks within a class have similar expected outcomes (see [[Homogeneity]])

## Section 3 — Analysis of Issues and Recommended Practices

### 3.2 Considerations in Selecting Risk Characteristics

- **Relationship to expected outcomes** — demonstrated by a rational explanation or by relevant data; a purely statistical correlation without a causal or plausible rationale is weaker support
- **Causality** is desirable but not required
- **Objectivity** — the characteristic should be measurable and not readily manipulable by the risk
- **Practicality** — cost and administrative burden of collecting and verifying the characteristic
- **Applicable law** — the system must comply with statutory and regulatory constraints (see [[Ratemaking Constraints]])
- **Industry practices** and **business practices** as legitimate secondary considerations

### 3.3 Considerations in Establishing Risk Classes

- **Intended use** — a classification system built for rating may not suit reserving or underwriting
- **Actuarial considerations** — the trade-off between [[Homogeneity|homogeneity]] (narrower classes) and [[Credibility|credibility]] (wider classes); this trade-off is the core of syllabus objective A5
- **Other considerations** — reasonableness of results, and the potential for [[Classification Ratemaking|adverse selection]] if classes are too broad

### 3.4 Testing the Risk Classification System

- Test periodically for effectiveness, including whether experience by class supports the differentials
- Where the system produces classes that no longer differentiate, the actuary should consider revision

### 3.5 Quantitative Analysis

- Rates within a system should reflect **differences in expected outcomes**, but need not be strictly proportional
- Cross-subsidies should be identified and, where unintended, addressed

### 3.6 Reliance on Data or Other Information Supplied by Others

- The actuary may rely on others but should disclose the reliance

## Section 4 — Communications and Disclosures

- Disclose the intended use, the risk characteristics used, and any material changes to the system
- Disclose reliance on others and any deviation from the standard
