Once each morning and once each afternoon, the driver of a delivery truck is assigned to a route with a length that depends upon the items being delivered. The morning route may be 5, 10 or 40 miles. The afternoon route may be 0, 5 or 10 miles. The probabilities with which the pairs of routes have been assigned are:

| Morning route (miles) | Afternoon 0 | Afternoon 5 | Afternoon 10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 0 | $2p$ | $3p$ |
| 10 | 0 | $2p$ | 0 |
| 40 | $q$ | 0 | 0 |

The lengths of the assigned afternoon routes have a mean of 6 miles.

Calculate the variance of the length of an afternoon route.

- A) $11.0$
- B) $12.0$
- C) $16.5$
- D) $26.5$
- E) $42.0$

## Explanation

Sum each column to get the marginal distribution of the afternoon length $A$:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[A = 0] &= q \\
P[A = 5] &= 2p + 2p = 4p \\
P[A = 10] &= 3p
\end{align*}
$$

Two equations pin down $p$ and $q$. The probabilities must total 1:

$$
q + 7p = 1
$$

and the given mean supplies the other:

$$
\begin{align*}
6 &= 0(q) + 5(4p) + 10(3p) = 50p \\
p &= 0.12
\end{align*}
$$

so $q = 1 - 7(0.12) = 0.16$, giving $P[A=0] = 0.16$, $P[A=5] = 0.48$, $P[A=10] = 0.36$.

Compute the variance directly from the definition:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(A) &= (0-6)^2(0.16) + (5-6)^2(0.48) + (10-6)^2(0.36) \\
              &= 5.76 + 0.48 + 5.76 \\
              &= \mathbf{12.0}
\end{align*}
$$

The morning route is a distraction — only the column sums matter. Note the two extreme values contribute nearly all the variance: the 0-mile and 10-mile routes are each 6 and 4 miles from the mean, while the 5-mile route sits almost on it.
