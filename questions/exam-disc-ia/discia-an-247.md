A homeowners policy covers "water damage" without defining it and does not exclude earth movement. Heavy rain causes a mudslide that pushes mud and water into the house. The insurer says the term was meant only for water escaping from plumbing. How is a court likely to rule?

- A) The loss is excluded, because the insurer's intended meaning controls its own policy
- B) The loss is covered, because an ambiguous term is construed against the insurer that drafted it
- C) The policy is void, because an insurance contract is a contract of adhesion
- D) The court must send the question to the insurance commissioner, who approved the form

## Explanation

Under **contra proferentem**, an ambiguous contract term is construed against the party that drafted it. "Water damage" can reasonably be read to include a rain-driven mudslide, so the court reads it in the insured's favor and finds the loss covered. The rule matters most in insurance, where the insurer writes the contract.

- **A** reverses the rule. The drafter's private intent does not resolve an ambiguity in its favor.
- **C** misunderstands adhesion. Insurance policies are contracts of adhesion, and courts enforce them, interpreting ambiguities against the insurer.
- **D** confuses form approval with interpretation. Interpreting a disputed term in a lawsuit is the courts' role.
