Given the following information regarding a book of retrospectively rated policies:

| Retrospective Adjustment | First | Second | Third |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss Age | 18 | 30 | 42 |
| Premium Age | 27 | 39 | 51 |
| Loss Capping Ratio | 80% | 50% | 70% |
| LDF to Ultimate | 1.800 | 1.150 | 1.050 |

- Tax multiplier: 1.04
- Loss conversion factor: 1.15
- Total basic premium: \$12,456,000
- Total standard premium: \$54,309,000
- Expected loss ratio to standard premium: 75%

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the PDLD ratio as of the first retrospective adjustment.

### Explanation
$$E(L) = 75\% \times 54{,}309{,}000 = 40{,}731{,}750$$

$$L_{18} = 40{,}731{,}750 \times \frac{1}{1.8} = 22{,}628{,}750$$

$$CL_{18} = 22{,}628{,}750 \times 80\% = 18{,}103{,}000$$

$$\text{PDLD}_{18} = \frac{P_{18}}{L_{18}} = \frac{(BP + CL_{18} \times LCF) \times TM}{L_{18}} = \frac{12{,}456{,}000 \times 1.04 + 18{,}103{,}000 \times 1.15 \times 1.04}{22{,}628{,}750} = 1.53$$

Alternatively:

$$\text{PDLD}_1 = \frac{BP}{SP \times ELR \times \%L_1} \times TM + \frac{CL_1}{L_1} \times LCF \times TM = \frac{12{,}456{,}000}{54{,}309{,}000 \times 75\% \times (1/1.8)} \times 1.04 + 0.8 \times 1.15 \times 1.04 = 1.529268 \approx 1.53$$

### Examiner Report
Part A, this is a relatively straightforward application of the formula for PDLD ratio as provided
within Teng and Perkins which calculates the Initial Premium as of the first adjustment as compared to the expected losses to have emerged as of that time. Common errors include arithmetic errors and errors in memorizing formulas.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the PDLD ratio as of the second retrospective adjustment.

### Explanation
Treating the loss capping ratios as cumulative:

$$L_{30} = E(L)/1.15 = 35{,}418{,}913$$

$$CL_{30} = E(L)/1.15 \times 50\% = 17{,}709{,}457$$

$$\text{PDLD}_2 = \left(\frac{CL_{30} - CL_{18}}{L_{30} - L_{18}}\right) \times LCF \times TM = \left(\frac{17{,}709{,}457 - 18{,}103{,}000}{35{,}418{,}913 - 22{,}628{,}750}\right) \times 1.15 \times 1.04 = -0.0368$$

Alternatively:

Assuming the loss capping ratio given is the incremental loss capping ratio (an assumption that must be stated):

$$\text{PDLD}_2 = \left(\frac{CL_2 - CL_1}{L_2 - L_1}\right) \times LCF \times TM = 0.5 \times 1.15 \times 1.04 = 0.598$$

### Examiner Report
Part B, the graders were expecting the candidates to understand the difference between
cumulative and incremental activity. The true retrospective premium adjustments are based on the incremental activity from the prior adjustment to the subsequent adjustment. The loss capping ratios are cumulative unless they are labeled as incremental loss capping ratio. And candidates are expected to know how to use cumulative loss capping ratios to calculate the incremental ones. Common errors include confusing cumulative loss capping ratios to be incremental ones, forgetting TM and LCF, and arithmetic errors. For candidates who are using loss capping ratios as incremental ones, they must state their assumptions clearly; otherwise they will need to treat loss capping ratios as cumulative.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Calculate the PDLD ratio as of the third retrospective adjustment.

### Explanation
$$L_{42} = E(L)/1.05 = 38{,}792{,}143$$

$$CL_{42} = E(L)/1.05 \times 0.7 = 27{,}154{,}500$$

$$\text{PDLD}_3 = \left(\frac{CL_{42} - CL_{30}}{L_{42} - L_{30}}\right) \times LCF \times TM = \frac{27{,}154{,}500 - 17{,}709{,}457}{38{,}792{,}143 - 35{,}418{,}913} \times 1.15 \times 1.04 = 3.35$$

Alternatively:

Assuming the loss capping ratio given is the incremental loss capping ratio:

$$\text{PDLD}_3 = \left(\frac{CL_3 - CL_2}{L_3 - L_2}\right) \times LCF \times TM = 0.7 \times 1.15 \times 1.04 = 0.8372$$

### Examiner Report
Part C, please see part B.

## Part d (0.75 points)

Based on the results of parts a., b. and c. above, discuss the reasonableness of the loss capping ratios shown above.

### Explanation
- Loss capping ratios should ideally decrease; here 70% at the third adjustment is higher than 50% at the second, so they are not reasonable.
- As losses mature, more policies hit the maximum premium and the per-occurrence limit, and more losses develop outside the caps, so capping ratios should decrease as losses mature.

Alternatively:

- Premium responsiveness is expected to decline with maturity: as more losses emerge, more are capped by the per-occurrence limit or the maximum premium, and only capped losses contribute additional premium.
- In parts a–c, premium responsiveness at the 3rd adjustment is higher than at the 2nd, contrary to that expectation, because the loss capping ratio is higher at the 3rd adjustment — implying fewer losses are being capped, which is not a reasonable assumption.

### Examiner Report
Part D, the candidates are expected to understand that the loss capping ratios given in the
question were not reasonable in normal circumstance and why. The candidates are also expected to answer the question by using the data given in the question. Common errors will be candidates just giving generic comments/conclusions without tying them back to the situation that was given in the question.
