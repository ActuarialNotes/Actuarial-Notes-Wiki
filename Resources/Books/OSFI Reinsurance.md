OSFI's January 2025 guideline on **sound [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] practices and procedures**. It applies to every federally regulated insurer party to cessions, retrocessions and, where applicable, assumption reinsurance. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective C5.

## Contents

- **I. Key principles** — the elements of the **Reinsurance Risk Management Policy (RRMP)**: managing risks *through* reinsurance; managing risks *arising from* the use of reinsurance; other information to be included; senior management oversight
- **II. Guideline administration**
- **III. Other OSFI guidance**

## The Reinsurance Risk Management Policy

The RRMP is expected to be **an integral component of the enterprise-wide risk management framework**, to reflect the scale, nature and complexity of the business, and to consider the insurer's [[Risk Appetite|risk appetite]]. It must document:

- the **purpose and objectives** for seeking reinsurance;
- **ceding limits**;
- **counterparty risk**; and
- **concentration limits**.

## Managing risks *through* reinsurance

Where reinsurance reduces insurance risk, OSFI's capital guidelines recognise the benefit **if certain conditions are met** — under the [[MCT]] (and LICAT) the insurer may reduce required capital for insurance risk ceded. An insurer using reinsurance to mitigate large and catastrophic losses must **regularly assess the adequacy and effectiveness** of the arrangements, which may involve **[[Stress Testing|stress testing]]** to confirm that losses are mitigated to levels consistent with its risk appetite.

Where reinsurance is used for purposes **not directly linked to mitigating insurance risk** — the [[Finite Reinsurance|finite]] and financial reinsurance problem — OSFI evaluates the arrangement, including the appropriateness of capital credit, on the **risk impact** to the insurer. OSFI will **generally not recognise or grant credit** for a foreign insurer's arrangements where risks insured in Canada are ceded back to the foreign insurer's home office through affiliated reinsurers.

## Managing risks *arising from* reinsurance

- **Ceding limits** must be set for the overall book, and may be set by line of business. **An insurer should not, in the normal course of business, cede 100% or substantially all of its insurance risks** — the rule that stops an insurer becoming a front for a reinsurer it does not control.
- **Counterparty risk** — monitor and manage all the factors affecting it: the reinsurer's financial strength, whether it is [[Registered Reinsurance|registered]] or [[Unregistered Reinsurance|unregistered]], collateral held, and concentration of cessions with one counterparty (see [[OSFI Concentration]]).
- **Concentration limits** on exposure to any one reinsurer.

## Why it is on the syllabus

Reinsurance is the largest single lever a Canadian P&C insurer has over its capital position, and it works only if the counterparty pays. The guideline is the prudential complement to the accounting in [[CIA IFRS 1]] and to the [[Risk Transfer|risk transfer]] question in [[Freihaut and Vendetti]] — a contract that transfers no risk gets no capital credit no matter how it is documented.
