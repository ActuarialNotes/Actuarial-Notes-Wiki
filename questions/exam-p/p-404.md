Two losses have joint density

$$f(x,y) = 8xy, \qquad 0 < x < y < 1$$

Calculate $E[X \mid Y = 0.5]$.

- A) $1/4$
- B) $1/3$
- C) $3/8$
- D) $1/2$
- E) $2/3$

## Explanation

The marginal of $Y$ is $f_Y(y) = \int_0^y 8xy\,dx = 4y^3$ on $(0,1)$. Divide to get the conditional density:

$$
\begin{align*}
f_{X \mid Y}(x \mid y) &= \frac{8xy}{4y^3} \\
                       &= \frac{2x}{y^2}, \qquad 0 < x < y
\end{align*}
$$

At $y = 0.5$ this is $f_{X \mid Y}(x \mid 0.5) = 8x$ on $0 < x < 0.5$. Then:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X \mid Y = 0.5] &= \int_0^{0.5} x \cdot 8x\,dx \\
                  &= 8\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^{0.5} \\
                  &= \frac{8(0.125)}{3} \\
                  &= \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}
\end{align*}
$$

Answer (A) is the trap: given $Y = 0.5$ the variable $X$ lies in $(0, 0.5)$, but it is **not** uniform there — the density $8x$ puts more weight near the upper end, pulling the mean above the midpoint $0.25$.
