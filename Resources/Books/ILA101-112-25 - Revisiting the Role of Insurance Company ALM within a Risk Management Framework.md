A Goldman Sachs Asset Management white paper from its Insurance Asset Management group, dated October 2010, that takes a fresh look at the role of insurance company asset liability management (ALM) and investment strategy. It looks back at the impact of the financial crisis on US insurers and how the industry responded, then presents a case study applicable to all insurers to demonstrate "ALM at work", and proposes a revised ALM, risk management and investment strategy framework.

## Executive Summary
- The authors propose a revised ALM, risk management and investment strategy framework as a catalyst to improve the decision-making process and the operating performance of the insurance organization.
- The paper explores the complexities and stress within the US life insurance industry during the financial crisis and then presents a case study of an integrated ALM framework.

## Complex Landscape for Insurers
- Insurers answer to policyholders, shareholders, rating agencies, regulators and tax agencies, and many of these stakeholders use different metrics to measure assets, liabilities and capital.

## History of the Financial Crisis from US Life Insurers' Perspective
- Unrealized Losses via AOCI
- Severe Drop in Market Capitalization
- Falling RBC Ratios
- Changing Portfolio Allocations
- Raising Capital

## Conclusion
- Insurers should periodically review and reset their ALM and strategic asset allocation (SAA) in the context of changing markets, business mixes and regulatory regimes, and should stress-test their assumptions.
- Setting risk budgets at the aggregate level enables a holistic SAA to benefit from diversification of market risks.
