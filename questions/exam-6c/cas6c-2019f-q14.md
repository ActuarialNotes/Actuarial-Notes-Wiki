## Part a (1.25 points)

Calculate the total net commissions as they appear on the Annual Return page 80.10.

### Explanation
Back out the missing commission figures from the page 80.10 identities (all \$000s).

Ceded commission income is the change in unearned commissions plus ceded commissions:

$$N = B - H + E \quad\Rightarrow\quad 600 = 2{,}200 - 2{,}500 + E \quad\Rightarrow\quad E = 900$$

Net commissions attributable to the period:

$$I = (A - G) + C + D - (B - H + E)$$
$$17{,}100 = (A - G) + 18{,}000 + 0 - (2{,}200 - 2{,}500 + 900) \quad\Rightarrow\quad A - G = -300$$

Gross commission expense $= (A - G) + C + D = -300 + 18{,}000 + 0 = 17{,}700$.

- Total gross commissions $= 17{,}700 + J + L = 17{,}700 + 500 + 300 = 18{,}500$
- Total ceded commissions $= N + K + M = 600 + 250 + 100 = 950$
- **Total net commissions** $= 18{,}500 - 950 = 17{,}550$

Equivalently, $I + (J - K) + (L - M) = 17{,}100 + 250 + 200 = 17{,}550$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how to derive the total net commissions using the components from page 80.10 of the P&C Return.
Common mistakes included:

- Not knowing the formula for gross commission expense

## Part b (3.5 points)

Calculate the premium deficiency, if any.

### Explanation
ULAE, taken as a percentage of premium: $T \times O = 5.00\% \times 120{,}000 = 6{,}000$.

Net undiscounted losses and ULAE on the unexpired risk, after the future reinsurance cost:

$$[(O - Q) - R] \times S + \text{ULAE} = [(120{,}000 - 6{,}000) - 5{,}000] \times 88\% + 6{,}000 = 101{,}920$$

Present-value factors from the payment pattern, paid at mid-year:

| $t$ | PV @ 3.5% | PV @ 2.75% |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | $60\% \times 1.035^{-0.5}$ | $60\% \times 1.0275^{-0.5}$ |
| 1.5 | $30\% \times 1.035^{-1.5}$ | $30\% \times 1.0275^{-1.5}$ |
| 2.5 | $10\% \times 1.035^{-2.5}$ | $10\% \times 1.0275^{-2.5}$ |
| | Sum = 0.966 | Sum = 0.973 |

Adjusted for the average accident date of the unexpired risk:

- Adjusted PV @ 3.5% $= 0.966 \times 1.035^{(1/2 - 1/3)} = 0.972$
- Adjusted PV @ 2.75% $= 0.973 \times 1.0275^{(1/2 - 1/3)} = 0.978$

Then:

- Net PV $= 101{,}920 \times 0.972 = 99{,}066$
- Gross PV $= (120{,}000 \times 88\% + 6{,}000) \times 0.972 = 108{,}475$
- Ceded PV $= 108{,}475 - 99{,}066 = 9{,}409$

Net actuarial present value, with the investment-return MfAD in the lower discount rate and the claims-development and reinsurance-recovery MfADs added:

$$\text{Net APV} = 101{,}920 \times 0.978 + 99{,}066 \times 7.00\% + 9{,}409 \times 2.00\% = 106{,}781$$

Maintenance expense $= O \times Y = 120{,}000 \times 3.5\% = 4{,}200$.

Premium liabilities $=$ net APV $+$ expected reinsurance premium $+$ maintenance expense $+$ contingent commission $= 106{,}781 + 5{,}000 + 4{,}200 + 0 = 115{,}981$.

Equity in unearned premium $=$ net UPR $-$ premium liabilities $+$ gross unearned commissions $= 114{,}000 - 115{,}981 + 2{,}500 = 519$.

Since the maximum DPAE is positive, the **premium deficiency is 0**.

Alternatively, taking the ULAE ratio as a percentage of losses rather than premium:

- Net unearned premium $= O + P - Q = 120{,}000 + 0 - 6{,}000 = 114{,}000$; gross unearned premium $= O + P = 120{,}000$
- ULAE $= 5.00\% \times 120{,}000 \times 88\% = 5{,}280$ (the sample also prints "ULAE $= T \times O \times S = 6{,}000$", as printed)
- Net undiscounted losses & LAE $= (114{,}000 - 5{,}000) \times 88\% + 5{,}280 = 101{,}200$
- Gross undiscounted losses & LAE $= 120{,}000 \times 88\% + 5{,}280 = 110{,}880$

$$\text{PV factor @ 3.5\%} = \left[\frac{0.60}{1.035^{0.5}} + \frac{0.30}{1.035^{1.5}} + \frac{0.10}{1.035^{2.5}}\right] \times 1.035^{(0.5 - 1/3)} = 0.9720$$

$$\text{PV factor @ 2.75\%} = \left[\frac{0.60}{1.0275^{0.5}} + \frac{0.30}{1.0275^{1.5}} + \frac{0.10}{1.0275^{2.5}}\right] \times 1.0275^{(0.5 - 1/3)} = 0.9778$$

- Net PV @ 3.5% $= 101{,}200 \times 0.9720 = 98{,}366$
- Net PV @ 2.75% $= 101{,}200 \times 0.9778 = 98{,}954$
- Gross PV @ 3.5% $= 110{,}880 \times 0.972 = 107{,}775$
- Ceded PV @ 3.5% $= 107{,}775 - 98{,}366 = 9{,}409$

PfADs:

- Claims development $= 98{,}366 \times 7.00\% = 6{,}886$
- Interest rate $= 98{,}954 - 98{,}366 = 588$
- Reinsurance $= 9{,}409 \times 2.00\% = 188$

Net APV $= 98{,}366 + 6{,}886 + 588 + 188 = 106{,}028$, so premium liabilities $= 106{,}028 + 5{,}000 + 4{,}200 + 0 = 115{,}228$ and equity in unearned premium $= 114{,}000 - 115{,}228 + 2{,}500 = 1{,}272$. Again the maximum DPAE is positive, so the premium deficiency is 0.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected determine the premium deficiency, or the maximum allowable DPAE, given all the components and assumptions that would normally be available to the actuary when performing the calculation.
Credit was given to candidates when a mistake in calculation led to a negative equity in unearned premium if they indicated this would be a premium deficiency.
Common errors included:

- Assuming the ULAE ratio is applied to both gross and net losses or gross and net premiums, which meant a ceded ULAE > 0.  Since this is very uncommon in practice, credit was only given if it was clearly stated that ULAE is assumed to be ceded as part of the reinsurance contract.
- Excluding the expected reinsurance premium when determining ceded losses, either by deducting it from gross unearned premiums, or by calculating ceded losses directly from ceded unearned premiums without adding the expected reinsurance premium.
- Determining the reinsurance PfAD as a percent of the expected reinsurance premium.
- Using gross unearned premiums to calculate equity in unearned premiums, instead of net unearned premiums.
- Not adding gross unearned commissions to the equity in unearned premium.
- Using a commission other than gross unearned commissions in the equity in unearned premium calculation.
