Given the following for an insurance company:

- The exposure base is number of occupants in the home per year.
- The company writes a one-year homeowners policy effective April 1, 2017 for a home with four occupants.
- On October 1, 2017, two occupants leave the home, and the home has only two occupants for the remainder of the policy term.

## Part a (0.25 points)

Calculate the total written exposures for calendar year 2017.

### Explanation
Written exposures are booked when the policy is written, and a mid-term change is booked when it takes effect. Both transactions happen in calendar year 2017:

- April 1, 2017: the one-year policy is written for 4 occupants, so 4 occupant-years are written.
- October 1, 2017: two occupants leave with 6 months of the term remaining, so -2 × 6/12 = -1 occupant-year is written.

$$\text{CY 2017 written exposures} = 4 - 1 = 3$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate how to calculate written and earned exposures on calendar year and policy year bases. Candidates were also expected to understand the three criteria of an exposure base.

Candidates were expected to calculate written exposures in the calendar year the policies were effective.

Common errors included:

- Not properly accounting for mid-term cancellation of policies.

## Part b (0.25 points)

Calculate the total earned exposures for calendar year 2017.

### Explanation
Earned exposures follow the time the exposure was actually covered within calendar year 2017:

- April 1 to September 30, 2017: 4 occupants for 6 months, 4 × 6/12 = 2.0
- October 1 to December 31, 2017: 2 occupants for 3 months, 2 × 3/12 = 0.5

$$\text{CY 2017 earned exposures} = 2.0 + 0.5 = 2.5$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate earned exposures in the calendar year the policies were effective.

Common errors included:

- Miscalculation of percent earned in calendar year.
- Not properly accounting for mid-term cancellation of policies.

## Part c (0.25 points)

Calculate the total policy year 2017 written exposures evaluated as of September 30, 2017.

### Explanation
Policy year 2017 contains every transaction on policies effective in 2017, but only those that have happened by the evaluation date. As of September 30, 2017 the policy has been written for 4 occupants and the October 1 change has not yet happened:

$$\text{PY 2017 written exposures as of 9/30/2017} = 4$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate written exposures on a policy year basis.

Common errors included:

- Failing to distinguish the difference between policy year and calendar year.

## Part d (0.75 points)

Briefly evaluate the number of occupants based on the three criteria of an exposure base.

### Explanation
- **Proportional to expected loss:** more occupants plausibly means more property in the home and more activity (cooking, heating, wear), so some losses rise with occupants; but the main homeowners exposure — the value of the dwelling — does not depend on how many people live there, so the relationship is weak. It is less proportional to loss than an amount-of-insurance or house-years base.
- **Practical:** the number of occupants is not easy to verify, changes during the term as people move in and out (as in this question), and is open to manipulation by an insured who under-reports it to lower the premium.
- **Historical precedence:** homeowners is rated on house-years and amount of insurance; changing to occupants would require system and rating-algorithm changes, cause premium swings unrelated to any change in risk, and leave the insurer without historical or industry data on the new base.

On all three criteria the number of occupants is a poor exposure base.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the three criteria of a good exposure base and evaluate a potential exposure base using each criterion: proportional to expected loss, practical, and historical precedence.

Candidates were expected to evaluate the proposed exposure base by explaining why they believed it met or did not meet each of the three criteria.

- Proportional to expected loss: candidates needed to demonstrate that there was or wasn't a relationship between number of occupants and expected loss.
- Practical: candidates needed to demonstrate it would or wouldn't be practical based on whether it was or wasn't objectively defined, easy/inexpensive to obtain, easy/inexpensive to verify, or subject to manipulation.
- Historical Precedence: candidates needed to demonstrate whether or not this was met by explaining the change of base based on either impact to systems (IT costs), impact to policyholders (premium swings) or data (current data would be difficult to work with, there would be a lack of industry benchmarks, etc.).

Common errors included:

- Simply listing the criteria, or only stating that the proposed exposure base met or did not meet the criteria without any further explanation.
- Proportional to Expected Loss: only saying that it is more/less proportional than house-years.
- Practical: saying it is practical or impractical without explanation.
- Historical Precedence: only stating that the current base is house-years, so number of occupants doesn't meet historical precedence, or just saying assuming number of occupants is current base, so it is met (and not making the connection that there would be a lack of industry benchmarks).
