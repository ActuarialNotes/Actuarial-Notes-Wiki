Given the following information as of December 31, 2016:

**Cumulative Reported Claims as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 1,000 | 1,350 | 1,450 | 1,480 |
| 2014 | 4,500 | 6,000 | 6,400 | |
| 2015 | 4,800 | 6,350 | | |
| 2016 | 4,100 | | | |

**Reported Claims Age-to-Age Factors**

| Accident Year | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 1.350 | 1.074 | 1.021 |
| 2014 | 1.333 | 1.067 | |
| 2015 | 1.323 | | |

**Earned Premium**

| Accident Year | Earned Premium |
|---|---|
| 2013 | 1,500 |
| 2014 | 6,800 |
| 2015 | 7,200 |
| 2016 | 7,500 |

- Accident year 2016 paid claims as of December 31, 2016 = 2,775.
- Expected claim ratio for all years = 75%.
- There is no reported claims development after 48 months.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the IBNR and the total unpaid claims for accident year 2016 as of December 31, 2016 using the expected claims technique.

### Answer
IBNR = 1,525; total unpaid claims = 2,850.

### Explanation
The expected claims technique ignores the year's own emerged experience entirely: the ultimate is the a priori estimate, premium times the expected claim ratio.

$$\text{Ultimate}_{2016} = 7{,}500 \times 0.75 = 5{,}625$$

The two reserve quantities differ only in what is subtracted from that ultimate:

- **IBNR** is everything not yet *reported*, so subtract reported claims:

$$\text{IBNR} = 5{,}625 - 4{,}100 = \mathbf{1{,}525}$$

- **Total unpaid claims** is everything not yet *paid* — case reserves plus IBNR — so subtract paid claims:

$$\text{Total unpaid} = 5{,}625 - 2{,}775 = \mathbf{2{,}850}$$

As a check, the two reconcile through case reserves: reported less paid is $4{,}100 - 2{,}775 = 1{,}325$ of case, and $1{,}525 + 1{,}325 = 2{,}850$.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand difference between IBNR and total unpaid claims, and how to calculate these values given various claims development techniques.

Candidates were expected to understand and apply mechanics of the expected claims technique to calculate IBNR and total unpaid claims.

Common errors included:

- Calculating either IBNR or total unpaid claims but not both.
- Calculating an expected claims ratio based upon experience rather than utilizing the 75% provided in the question.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Calculate the IBNR and the total unpaid claims for accident year 2016 as of December 31, 2016 using the reported Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique.

### Answer
IBNR $\approx$ 1,768; total unpaid claims $\approx$ 3,093.

### Explanation
The Bornhuetter-Ferguson method splits the ultimate: the portion already reported is taken as actual, and only the *unreported* portion is estimated from the a priori.

$$\text{IBNR} = \text{Expected claims} \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{\text{CDF}}\right)$$

**Step 1 — select age-to-age factors** from the given triangle. The all-year straight averages are

| | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 1.350 | 1.074 | 1.021 |
| 2014 | 1.333 | 1.067 | |
| 2015 | 1.323 | | |
| **Selected** | **1.335** | **1.070** | **1.021** |

**Step 2 — cumulate to age-to-ultimate factors.** There is no development after 48 months, so the tail is 1.000 and the factors are built backwards from there:

$$\text{CDF}_{36} = 1.021, \qquad \text{CDF}_{24} = 1.070 \times 1.021 = 1.0925, \qquad \text{CDF}_{12} = 1.335 \times 1.0925 = 1.4585$$

Accident year 2016 is at 12 months, so the relevant factor is the full **12-to-ultimate** CDF of 1.4585 — not the 12-24 factor alone.

$$\% \text{ reported} = \frac{1}{1.4585} = 0.6857 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \% \text{ unreported} = 0.3143$$

**Step 3 — IBNR.** Expected claims are the same 5,625 from part a:

$$\text{IBNR} = 5{,}625 \times 0.3143 = \mathbf{1{,}768}$$

**Step 4 — total unpaid claims.** Add the case reserves the year has actually established:

$$\text{Total unpaid} = \text{IBNR} + (\text{reported} - \text{paid}) = 1{,}768 + (4{,}100 - 2{,}775) = \mathbf{3{,}093}$$

Equivalently, the Bornhuetter-Ferguson ultimate is $4{,}100 + 1{,}768 = 5{,}868$, and $5{,}868 - 2{,}775 = 3{,}093$.

Other defensible factor selections shift the answer only slightly. A two-year average of the latest diagonal, for instance, gives 1.328 and 1.0705, a 12-to-ultimate CDF of 1.4512 and 68.9% reported, for an IBNR of about 1,749 and total unpaid of about 3,074.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand and apply mechanics of the Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique to calculate IBNR and total unpaid claims.  Candidates were expected to make reasonable age-to-age factor selections and calculate age-to-ultimate cumulative development factors.

Common errors included:

- Calculating either IBNR or total unpaid claims but not both.
- Using the 12-24 age-to-age factor instead of the 12-Ultimate cumulative development factor to determine percent unreported.
