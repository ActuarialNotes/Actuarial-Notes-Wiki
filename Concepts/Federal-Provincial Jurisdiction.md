**Federal-Provincial Jurisdiction** over insurance is the constitutional division that gives Canadian insurance regulation two levels. Parliament regulates the insurer as an institution, and the provinces regulate the insurance contract and the market under "property and civil rights in the province" (*Constitution Act, 1867*, s. 92(13)).

The division is:

| | Federal | Provincial |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Regulates | the insurer: incorporation, solvency, capital | the policy: the contract and the market |
| Source of power | banking, trade and commerce, and federal works | property and civil rights in the province |
| Regulator | [[OSFI]], for federally regulated insurers | each province's regulator |

The line was drawn by a series of early cases holding that the business of insurance within a province is a provincial matter. Parliament may charter and supervise an insurer, but it may not dictate the terms on which the policy is sold in Ontario.

## Consequences of the division

1. A federally incorporated insurer still needs a licence in every province where it writes.
2. [[Rate Regulation|Rate regulation]] is provincial, and therefore inconsistent across the country.
3. [[Automobile Insurance Reform|Auto reform]] happens province by province.
4. There is no national insurance regulator.

The [[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators]] exists because the division makes voluntary harmonisation the only available substitute for a single regulator.

Some lines are effectively federal by another route. Marine insurance falls under the federal navigation and shipping power, and federal works and undertakings bring their own wrinkles. P&C insurance as studied on Exam 6C follows the split above.

> [!example]- Which level of government has the authority for each of these proposals? {Example}
> 1. Ban the use of postal code in auto rating.
> 2. Raise the minimum capital an insurer must hold.
> 3. Require insurers to offer overland [[Flood Insurance|flood]] coverage in every home policy.
> 4. Create a national flood insurance backstop funded by the federal treasury.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - Item 1 is **provincial**. [[Territorial Rating|Territory]] is a rating variable, and risk classification in the contract is provincial.
> > - Item 2 is **federal** for federally regulated insurers, through the [[MCT]] guideline. It is **provincial** for provincially incorporated insurers, which typically adopt the same guideline.
> > - Item 3 is **provincial**, because mandating a coverage term is regulating the contract.
> > - Item 4 is **federal**. Spending its own money is within federal power even where regulating the contract is not, which is how [[Disaster Financial Assistance Arrangements|DFAA]] and the proposed national flood program are structured.

## The spending power

Ottawa's practical lever over insurance markets it cannot regulate is the spending power. It can fund a program, such as a flood backstop, without regulating the policy.

## Comparison with the United States

In the United States, insurance is regulated almost entirely at the state level under the McCarran-Ferguson Act, with no federal solvency regulator. Canada's federal prudential layer through [[OSFI]] is the structural difference from the Exam 6U syllabus.

> [!trap] Treating a federal charter as a licence to sell
> Federal incorporation and OSFI supervision cover the insurer's solvency. Selling policies in a province still needs that province's licence, and the policy follows that province's rules.
