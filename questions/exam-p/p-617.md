A policyholder owns two properties and has hurricane insurance on both properties. One property is located in Texas. The other property is located in Florida.

Let $T$ be the number of hurricane claims in a 5-year period for the Texas property, and let $F$ be the number of hurricane claims in a 5-year period for the Florida property. The joint probability function for $T$ and $F$ is given in the following table.

| Texas \ Florida | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | 0.40 | 0.14 | 0.04 | 0.02 |
| **1** | 0.16 | 0.08 | 0.04 | 0.02 |
| **2** | 0.04 | 0.03 | 0.02 | 0.01 |

Calculate $\text{Var}(T \mid F = 1)$.

- A) $0.18$
- B) $0.24$
- C) $0.45$
- D) $0.49$
- E) $0.80$

## Explanation

Start from the conditional pmf. The $F = 1$ column has marginal

$$
P[F = 1] = 0.14 + 0.08 + 0.03 = 0.25
$$

so dividing each entry by 0.25 gives

$$
p(t \mid F = 1) = \begin{cases}
14/25 = 0.56, & t = 0 \\
8/25 = 0.32, & t = 1 \\
3/25 = 0.12, & t = 2
\end{cases}
$$

Then

$$
\begin{align*}
E(T \mid F = 1)   &= 0(0.56) + 1(0.32) + 2(0.12) = 0.56 \\
E(T^2 \mid F = 1) &= 0^2(0.56) + 1^2(0.32) + 2^2(0.12) = 0.80
\end{align*}
$$

and therefore

$$
\text{Var}(T \mid F = 1) = 0.80 - (0.56)^2 = \mathbf{0.4864}
$$

Choice (C), 0.45, is the *unconditional* $\text{Var}(T)$, computed from the row totals $0.60, 0.30, 0.10$. Choice (E), 0.80, is $E(T^2 \mid F = 1)$ with the final subtraction of $\left[E(T \mid F = 1)\right]^2$ left off.
