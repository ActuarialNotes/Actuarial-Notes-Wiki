At an annual effective interest rate of 5%, the present value of an increasing perpetuity-due with annual payments of 1, 2, 3, etc., is equal to $X$.

Calculate $X$.

- A) 400
- B) 420
- C) 440
- D) 441
- E) $\infty$

## Explanation

An increasing perpetuity-immediate paying $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ has present value $(Ia)_{\overline{\infty}|} = \dfrac{1}{i} + \dfrac{1}{i^{2}}$. The perpetuity-due makes each payment one year earlier, so its present value is $(1+i)$ times as large:

$$
\begin{align*}
X &= \left[\frac{1}{0.05} + \frac{1}{0.05^{2}}\right](1.05) \\
  &= (20 + 400)(1.05) \\
  &= 441
\end{align*}
$$
