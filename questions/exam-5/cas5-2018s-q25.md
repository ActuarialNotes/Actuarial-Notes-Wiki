- There is no development beyond 48 months.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Estimate ultimate ALAE for accident year 2017 using the paid ALAE development technique.

### Explanation
Volume-weighted paid ALAE development factors:

$$\text{12–24: } \frac{5{,}580}{930} = 6.000 \qquad \text{24–36: } \frac{7{,}200}{3{,}600} = 2.000 \qquad \text{36–48: } \frac{4{,}224}{3{,}840} = 1.100 \qquad \text{48–ult: } 1.000$$

$$\text{AY 2017 ultimate ALAE} = 340 \times 6.0 \times 2.0 \times 1.1 = 340 \times 13.2 = 4{,}488$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of the paid development technique on ALAE, the development technique using ratio of paid ALAE to paid claims with additive factors, and when each technique works and when it does not.

Candidates were expected to apply the paid development technique to estimate ultimate ALAE for the latest accident year.

A common error was calculating development factors based on paid claims or paid to paid triangles rather than the paid ALAE triangle

## Part b (1 point)

Estimate ultimate ALAE for all accident years using the paid ALAE-to-paid claims only development technique with additive factors.

### Explanation
With **additive** factors the development is the change in the ratio of paid ALAE to paid claims from one age to the next: 12–24 +0.04 (every year), 24–36 +0.02, 36–48 0.00, and nothing after 48 months. The ultimate ratio is applied to the selected ultimate claims:

| AY | Latest ratio | Additive development | Ultimate ratio | Selected ultimate claims | Ultimate ALAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 0.10 | 0.00 | 0.10 | 42,240 | 4,224.0 |
| 2015 | 0.10 | 0.00 | 0.10 | 36,960 | 3,696.0 |
| 2016 | 0.08 | 0.02 + 0.00 | 0.10 | 43,560 | 4,356.0 |
| 2017 | 0.08 | 0.04 + 0.02 + 0.00 | 0.14 | 66,440 | 9,301.6 |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use the development technique using a ratio of paid ALAE to paid claims with additive factors to estimate ultimate ALAE for all accident years.

Common errors included:

- Using multiplicative factors instead of additive factors.
- Using paid claims or paid ALAE for development instead of the paid to paid ratio.
- Calculating ultimate ALAE for only the latest accident year instead of all years.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Recommend and briefly justify an estimate of ultimate ALAE for accident year 2017 from the results of parts a. and b. above.

### Explanation
Select **4,488** (paid ALAE development). Accident year 2017's 12-month ratio of 0.08 is double every earlier year's 0.04 because its paid claims at 12 months (4,250) are about half the usual level while its paid ALAE (340) is normal — and the reported development estimate (35,000) is far below the selected 66,440, so the 2017 claims data is distorted. The ratio technique carries that distorted ratio forward and then applies it to a large selected ultimate, giving 9,301.6, or about 2.66 of ALAE per ultimate claim against 1.17–1.28 for 2014–2016. The paid ALAE technique gives 4,488, or 1.28 per claim, in line with the earlier years.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to select an estimate of ultimate ALAE and justify their selection by noting a weakness in the technique not selected or a strength of the technique selected. These can be general weaknesses/strengths or weakness/strengths applicable to the data for this specific scenario.

Common errors included incomplete or insufficient explanations such as:

- Simply selecting a number with no explanation.
- Ignoring the specific information in the question.
- Stating that a method is over or understated without describing why or providing an explanation that would have caused it to move in the opposite direction.
