A tutorial from thisMatter.com on how a bank makes a profit. It explains that a bank earns money from the fees it charges for its services and the interest it earns on its assets, that its major expense is interest paid on its liabilities, and that banks increase profits by using leverage. Profit is measured as a return on assets (ROA) and a return on equity (ROE), which with leverage is much larger than ROA.

## Profit Measures: Return on Assets and Return on Owners' Equity
- Assets = Liabilities + Bank Capital (Owners' Equity)
- Vault cash and loan loss reserves are assets that do not earn income
- Net interest income is interest received on assets minus interest paid on liabilities; the interest rate spread is the average rate earned on assets minus the average rate paid on liabilities
- Net interest margin is net interest income divided by average total assets
- ROA is net income divided by average total assets
- The leverage ratio is bank assets divided by bank capital, and ROE = ROA × leverage ratio = net income divided by bank capital
