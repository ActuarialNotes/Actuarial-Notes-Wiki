Given the following information:

| | Market Portfolio | Market's Zero-Beta Portfolio | Minimum-Variance Portfolio |
|---|---|---|---|
| E(r) | 8% | Not Given | 6% |
| σ | 14% | 14% | 10% |

- The risk-free rate is 3%.
- The covariance of Security A and the market portfolio is 0.0392.
- The minimum-variance frontier is symmetric around the minimum-variance portfolio.

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the expected return for Security A using the zero-beta extension of CAPM.

### Explanation
$$E(r_A) = E(r_Z) + \beta_A\left[E(r_M) - E(r_Z)\right]$$

$$\beta_A = \frac{\text{Cov}(r_A, r_M)}{\text{Var}(r_M)} = \frac{0.0392}{(0.14)^2} = 2$$

Given that the minimum-variance frontier is symmetric around the minimum-variance portfolio, the zero-beta portfolio is the same distance away from the minimum-variance portfolio as the market portfolio, on the inefficient side of the frontier:

$$E(r_Z) = E(r_{\text{minvar}}) - \left[E(r_M) - E(r_{\text{minvar}})\right] = 0.06 - (0.08 - 0.06) = 0.04$$

$$E(r_A) = 0.04 + 2(0.08 - 0.04) = 0.12$$

Only one numeric final answer was accepted (12%). Candidates could have solved for $E(r_Z) = 0.04$ in various ways:

- Numerically (as above).
- Visually, by drawing the efficient frontier and labelling the points corresponding to the market portfolio and the market's zero-beta portfolio (one sample sketched the frontier with 0.08, 0.06 and 0.04 marked on the expected-return axis, 0.02 apart).
- Verbally, by explaining how one can infer the vertical position of the zero-beta portfolio given the symmetry of the efficient frontier and the information given in the problem.

### Examiner Report
The candidate was expected to know the equation for the zero-beta extension of CAPM and to use the fact that the efficient frontier is symmetric to calculate E(rZ).  Most candidates were able to state the equation that they needed to solve for E(rA), and most were further able to calculate βA.
Common errors included:

- not using the information about the symmetry of the efficient frontier to calculate E(rA)
- incorrectly stating that the expected return for portfolio A was a function of actual returns on the zero-beta and market portfolios
- using the risk free rate instead of E[rz] in the zero-beta equation
- using the minimum-variance portfolio sigma in calculating βA instead of the market portfolio sigma

## Part b (0.25 points)

Identify the assumption that is relaxed under the zero-beta extension of CAPM.

### Explanation
Responses that received full credit for this subpart include:

- Investors can borrow/lend at the risk-free rate
- Investors cannot borrow/lend at the risk-free rate.

Some candidates elaborated that the ability to borrow/lend freely at the risk-free rate is unrealistic in practice and explained that the higher zero-beta return replaces $r_f$ in this extension. This additional detail did not impact grading for this subpart.

The question asked candidates to identify which CAPM assumption is relaxed in the zero-beta extension. Candidates who wrote that "investors cannot borrow and lend at the risk-free rate" were not penalized.

Sample answers:

- Investors cannot borrow at risk-free rate.
- CAPM assumes all investors can borrow & lend at the risk free rate. This assumption is relaxed under the zero-beta extension of CAPM.

### Examiner Report
Candidates generally performed well on this subpart and accurately noted that the ability to freely borrow and lend at the risk-free rate is relaxed in the zero-beta extension of CAPM.
Examples of responses that did not receive full credit:

- listing another CAPM assumption such as "All investors are mean-variance optimizers"
- stating that investors faced "restrictions on borrowing" under the zero-beta extension but not specifying the assumption asked for in the question
- stating that investors have to or do not have to borrow/lend at the risk-free rate. The CAPM assumption pertains to the ability to borrow/lend at rf.

## Part c (0.25 points)

State the correlation between the market portfolio and the market's zero-beta portfolio.

### Explanation
Responses that received full credit for this subpart include:

- $\rho = 0$
- 0
- The portfolios are uncorrelated.

No credit was given for describing that the two portfolios were on opposite sides of the efficient frontier, unless candidates further stated that the portfolios are also uncorrelated.

Sample answers:

- They are NOT correlated.
- $\text{Cov}(r_z, r_m) = \rho_{z,m}\sigma_z\sigma_m$; $\text{Cov}(r_z, r_m) = 0$, so $\rho_{z,m} = 0$. The market and zero-beta portfolios are uncorrelated.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know that portfolios on the efficient frontier have a companion portfolio on the inefficient half of the frontier with which they are uncorrelated (known as the zero-beta portfolio). The candidates should therefore note that the market portfolio and its zero-beta portfolio are uncorrelated.

Common errors included listing correlations other than 0, such as:

- 1 (perfectly positively correlated)
- -1 (perfectly negatively correlated)
- other numeric value, calculated by trying to back into $\beta_z$ using the original form of the CAPM equation: $E[r_z] = r_f + \beta_z(E[r_M] - r_f)$.  Note though that this was the very assumption relaxed in the zero-beta extension.

Other responses that did not receive credit:

- stating the relationship between the zero-beta portfolio and market portfolio – e.g. that they lie on opposite sides of the efficient frontier – without specifying the correlation
- leaving an answer in equation form.  For instance, $\rho = \dfrac{\text{Cov}(r_M, r_z)}{\sigma_M\sigma_z}$

Candidates generally performed well on this subpart.
