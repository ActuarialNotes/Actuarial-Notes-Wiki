We observe a random sample, $\{X_1, X_2, \ldots X_n\}$ from a continuous uniform distribution $U(0, \beta)$.

$\hat{\beta} = \max(X_1, X_2, \ldots X_n)$ is chosen as an estimator of $\beta$.

- I. $\hat{\beta}$ is both the maximum likelihood estimator and method of moments estimator of $\beta$
- II. $\hat{\beta}$ is sufficient statistic for $\beta$
- III. The minimum variance unbiased estimator of $\beta$ is a function of $\hat{\beta}$

Determine which of the above statements are true.

- A) None are true
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

The likelihood of a $U(0, \beta)$ sample is

$$
L(\beta) = \beta^{-n}, \qquad \beta \ge \max(X_1, \ldots, X_n),
$$

and zero otherwise. It is decreasing in $\beta$, so it is maximized at the smallest admissible value — the sample maximum.

**I is false.** $\hat{\beta} = \max(X_i)$ is the maximum likelihood estimator, but the method of moments sets $E[X] = \beta/2$ equal to $\bar{X}$, giving $2\bar{X}$, which is a different estimator.

**II is true.** The likelihood depends on the data only through $\max(X_i)$ (it is $\beta^{-n}$ times the indicator that $\beta \ge \max(X_i)$), so by the factorization theorem $\hat{\beta}$ is sufficient for $\beta$.

**III is true.** The maximum is a complete sufficient statistic with $E[\hat{\beta}] = \frac{n}{n+1}\beta$, so $\frac{n+1}{n}\hat{\beta}$ is unbiased. By the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem it is the minimum variance unbiased estimator, and it is a function of $\hat{\beta}$.

II and III are true, so the answer is **D**.
