An annuity provides the following payments:

- i) $X$ at the beginning of each year for 20 years, starting today
- ii) $4X$ at the beginning of each year for 30 years, starting 20 years from today

Calculate the Macaulay duration of this annuity using an annual effective interest rate of 2%.

- A) 27.32
- B) 27.87
- C) 28.30
- D) 33.53
- E) 35.41

## Explanation

The denominator of the duration is the present value of the annuity:

$$
\begin{align*}
X\ddot{a}_{\overline{20}|0.02} + 4Xv^{20}\ddot{a}_{\overline{30}|0.02} &= X(16.6785) + 4X(0.67297)(22.8444) \\
&= X(16.6785 + 61.4945) \\
&= 78.1729X
\end{align*}
$$

The numerator is the time-weighted present value of the annuity. In units of $X$ we need the present value of $0, 1, \ldots, 19, 80, 84, \ldots, 196$. One way to view this is as four times a 49-year increasing immediate annuity (so payments of $4, 8, \ldots, 76, 80, 84, \ldots, 196$) less three times a 19-year increasing immediate annuity (so payments of $3, 6, \ldots, 57$). The present value is:

$$
\begin{align*}
4X(Ia)_{\overline{49}|0.02} - 3X(Ia)_{\overline{19}|0.02} &= [4(655.2078) - 3(147.4923)]X \\
&= 2{,}178.3542X
\end{align*}
$$

The duration is the ratio, $2{,}178.3542/78.1729 = 27.87$.
