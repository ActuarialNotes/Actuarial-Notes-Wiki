A bank accepts a 20,000 deposit from a customer on which it guarantees to pay an annual effective interest rate of 10% for two years. The customer needs to withdraw half of the accumulated value at the end of the first year. The customer will withdraw the remaining value at the end of the second year.

The bank has the following investment options available, which may be purchased in any quantity:
- Bond H: A one-year zero-coupon bond yielding 10% annually
- Bond I: A two-year zero-coupon bond yielding 11% annually
- Bond J: A two-year bond that sells at par with 12% annual coupons

Any portion of the 20,000 deposit that is not needed to be invested in bonds is retained by the bank as profit.

Determine which of the following investment strategies produces the highest profit for the bank and is guaranteed to meet the customer's withdrawal needs.

- A) 9,091 in Bond H, 8,264 in Bond I, 2,145 in Bond J
- B) 10,000 in Bond H, 10,000 in Bond I
- C) 10,000 in Bond H, 9,821 in Bond I
- D) 8,910 in Bond H, 731 in Bond I, 10,000 in Bond J
- E) 8,821 in Bond H, 10,804 in Bond J

## Explanation

The deposit accumulates to $20{,}000(1.1) = 22{,}000$ at the end of year 1; the customer withdraws half, 11,000, and the remaining 11,000 accumulates to $11{,}000(1.1) = 12{,}100$ at the end of year 2. The correct answer is the lowest-cost portfolio that provides 11,000 at the end of year one and 12,100 at the end of year two.

Let $H$, $I$ and $J$ represent the face amount of each purchased bond. Bond J pays a coupon of $0.12J$ at time 1 and $1.12J$ at time 2, so

- the time-one payment is exactly matched with $H + 0.12J = 11{,}000$;
- the time-two payment is matched with $I + 1.12J = 12{,}100$.

The cost of the three bonds is $H/1.1 + I/1.2321 + J$ (Bond I yields 11%, and $1.11^2 = 1.2321$). This is to be minimized under the two constraints. Substituting for $H$ and $I$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Cost} &= \frac{11{,}000 - 0.12J}{1.1} + \frac{12{,}100 - 1.12J}{1.2321} + J \\
&= 19{,}820.63 - 0.0181J
\end{align*}
$$

This is minimized by purchasing the largest possible amount of $J$, which is $12{,}100/1.12 = 10{,}803.57$ (then $I = 0$). Then $H = 11{,}000 - 0.12(10{,}803.57) = 9{,}703.57$, and the cost of Bond H is $9{,}703.57/1.1 = 8{,}821.43$.

The bank invests 8,821 in Bond H and 10,804 in Bond J. The total cost is $8{,}821.43 + 10{,}803.57 = 19{,}625$, leaving a profit of $20{,}000 - 19{,}625 = 375$.
