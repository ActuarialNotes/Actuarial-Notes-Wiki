An HO-3 (HO 00 03 05 11) names a bank as mortgagee. The named insured deliberately sets fire to his house, and the insurer denies his claim. The bank has reported everything it knew about the property and submits a sworn statement of loss on time. What happens?

- A) Neither is paid, because intentional loss bars every party with an interest
- B) The bank is paid, and the insurer acquires the bank's rights against him
- C) The insured and the bank are paid jointly, because the bank is named in the policy
- D) The bank is paid only if the insured's spouse did not take part in the fire

## Explanation

Under the mortgage clause, a denial of the insured's claim does not apply to a valid claim of the mortgagee that meets its conditions: reporting changes in ownership, occupancy or risk it knows of, paying any premium the insured hasn't, and submitting a sworn statement of loss within 60 days of notice. Having paid the mortgagee and denied the insured, the insurer is **subrogated** to the mortgagee's rights under the mortgage, or may pay off the mortgage and take an assignment of it. The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — the intentional loss exclusion bars every *insured*; the mortgage clause protects the mortgagee independently.
- **C** — joint payment "as interests appear" applies to a covered loss; here the insured's claim is denied.
- **D** — the spouse's role is irrelevant to the mortgagee, and under the intentional loss exclusion no insured is covered even if innocent.
