Losses follow an exponential distribution with mean 2,000. A policy pays the full loss up to a maximum of 3,000 per claim, with no deductible.

Calculate the expected payment per loss.

- A) $1{,}447$
- B) $1{,}554$
- C) $1{,}723$
- D) $2{,}000$
- E) $2{,}231$

## Explanation

The payment is $Y = \min(X, u)$ with $u = 3{,}000$. Integrating the survival function up to the limit gives the general result for an exponential:

$$E[\min(X, u)] = \theta\left(1 - e^{-u/\theta}\right)$$

$$
\begin{align*}
E[Y] &= 2{,}000\left(1 - e^{-3000/2000}\right) \\
     &= 2{,}000\left(1 - e^{-1.5}\right) \\
     &= 2{,}000\left(1 - 0.223130\right) \\
     &= 2{,}000(0.776870) \\
     &= \mathbf{1{,}554}
\end{align*}
$$

The answer must fall below the uncapped mean of 2,000 (choice D) — a limit can only reduce the expected payment. Check it with the identity

$$E[\min(X,u)] + E[(X-u)_+] = E[X]$$

Here $E[(X-3000)_+] = 2{,}000\,e^{-1.5} = 446$, and $1{,}554 + 446 = 2{,}000$ as required.
