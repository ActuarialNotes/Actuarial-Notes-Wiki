An insurance company portfolio consists of the following:

- 1,000 two-year policies with an effective date of April 1, 2015.
- 1,000 one-year policies with an effective date of July 1, 2015.

## Part a (0.75 points)

Calculate the following for calendar year 2015:

i. Written exposures
ii. Earned exposures

### Answer
i. 3,000 written exposures; ii. 1,250 earned exposures

### Explanation
The whole question turns on one distinction: a **two-year policy carries two exposure units**, written all at once at inception but earned evenly over twenty-four months.

**i. Written exposures.** An exposure is written in full in the calendar year the policy is written, for the entire policy term:

$$\text{WE}_{2015} = \underbrace{1{,}000 \times 2}_{\text{two-year policies}} + \underbrace{1{,}000 \times 1}_{\text{one-year policies}} = 3{,}000$$

**ii. Earned exposures.** Each policy earns in proportion to the fraction of its term that falls inside calendar year 2015:

- The two-year policies are effective 4/1/2015, so 9 of their 24 months fall in 2015. Each policy earns $2 \times \tfrac{9}{24} = 0.75$ exposures.
- The one-year policies are effective 7/1/2015, so 6 of their 12 months fall in 2015. Each policy earns $1 \times \tfrac{6}{12} = 0.5$ exposures.

$$\text{EE}_{2015} = 1{,}000 \times \frac{9}{24} \times 2 + 1{,}000 \times \frac{6}{12} \times 1 = 750 + 500 = 1{,}250$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate written and earned exposures for a portfolio mixing one-year and two-year policies, in the calendar year the policies became effective.

Common errors included failing to account for the two-year policy term — treating each two-year policy as a single exposure unit rather than two — and miscalculating the fraction of the term earned within the calendar year.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the earned exposures for calendar year 2016.

### Answer
1,500 earned exposures

### Explanation
In 2016 the two-year policies are in force for all twelve months, and the one-year policies run out their remaining six months:

- Two-year policies: 12 of 24 months fall in 2016, so each earns $2 \times \tfrac{12}{24} = 1.0$ exposure.
- One-year policies (effective 7/1/2015) earn their final $\tfrac{6}{12} = 0.5$ exposure before expiring 6/30/2016.

$$\text{EE}_{2016} = 1{,}000 \times \frac{12}{24} \times 2 + 1{,}000 \times \frac{6}{12} \times 1 = 1{,}000 + 500 = 1{,}500$$

As a check, the portfolio's total exposures must equal the 3,000 written: $1{,}250$ earned in 2015, $1{,}500$ in 2016, leaving $250$ to be earned in 2017 by the two-year policies over 1/1/2017 – 3/31/2017 ($1{,}000 \times \tfrac{3}{24} \times 2 = 250$).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate earned exposures for the same portfolio in the second calendar year, where the two-year policies are in force for the full year and the one-year policies expire mid-year.

Common errors were the same as in part a: not properly accounting for the two-year policy term, and miscalculating the percentage earned within the calendar year.
