A pricing actuary finds that a new rating characteristic is strongly correlated with loss costs in several years of data, but no one can explain why it should cause losses. The characteristic is not prohibited by any applicable law. Under ASOP No. 12, *Risk Classification*, what is the actuary's position?

- A) It may be used if its relationship to expected outcomes is shown; causality is not required
- B) It may not be used until a cause-and-effect relationship with losses has been established
- C) It may be used only if the actuary obtains the regulator's prior written approval
- D) It may not be used because unexplained characteristics are unfairly discriminatory

## Explanation

ASOP 12 (§3.2.1) requires the actuary to select risk characteristics related to expected outcomes, a relationship shown when variation in actual or reasonably anticipated experience correlates with the characteristic. It adds (§3.2.2) that it is not necessary to establish a cause-and-effect relationship between the characteristic and the expected outcome in order to use it. The actuary should still consider whether applicable law limits the choice (§3.2.5). The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** — states the opposite of ASOP 12's causality provision.
- **C** — regulatory approval of rates is a matter of state rating law, not a condition the standard imposes on selecting a characteristic.
- **D** — unfair discrimination concerns rates that do not reflect differences in expected cost, or prohibited bases; a characteristic correlated with cost is not unfair merely because its cause is unknown.
