A varying-intercepts model is built to model the test scores of students in eight schools:

$$
\begin{align*}
score_i &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu_i, p_i) \\
\mu_i &= \alpha_{SCHOOL[i]} + \beta_1 x_i \\
\alpha_{SCHOOL} &\sim \text{Normal}(\alpha, \sigma) \\
\beta_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 2) \\
\alpha &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 1) \\
\sigma &\sim \text{HalfCauchy}(0, 1)
\end{align*}
$$

Three additional models (Model I, Model II, and Model III) are proposed:

**Model I**

$$
\begin{align*}
score_i &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu_i, p_i) \\
\mu_i &= \alpha + \alpha_{SCHOOL[i]} + \beta_1 x_i \\
\alpha &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 1) \\
\alpha_{SCHOOL} &\sim \text{Normal}(0, \sigma) \\
\beta_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 2) \\
\sigma &\sim \text{HalfCauchy}(0, 1)
\end{align*}
$$

**Model II**

$$
\begin{align*}
score_i &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu_i, p_i) \\
\mu_i &= \alpha_{SCHOOL[i]} + \beta_1 x_i \\
\alpha_{SCHOOL} &\sim \text{Normal}(\alpha, 2\sigma) \\
\beta_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 2) \\
\alpha &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 1) \\
\sigma &\sim \text{HalfCauchy}(0, 1)
\end{align*}
$$

**Model III**

$$
\begin{align*}
score_i &\sim \text{Normal}(\mu_i, p_i) \\
\mu_i &= \alpha_{SCHOOL[i]} + \beta_1 x_i \\
\alpha_{SCHOOL} &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 1) \\
\beta_1 &\sim \text{Normal}(0, 2)
\end{align*}
$$

Determine which of the three proposed models imposes additional model shrinkage:

- A) None
- B) Model I and Model II only
- C) Model I and Model III only
- D) Model II and Model III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

Shrinkage in a varying-intercepts model is controlled by how tightly the school intercepts are held around their common centre — a **smaller** prior scale means more shrinkage.

**Model I imposes the same shrinkage.** This is the non-centred reparameterization: instead of drawing $\alpha_{SCHOOL} \sim \text{Normal}(\alpha, \sigma)$ directly, it draws deviations $\alpha_{SCHOOL} \sim \text{Normal}(0, \sigma)$ and adds the global $\alpha$ in the linear predictor. The implied distribution of the school intercepts is identical, so the shrinkage is unchanged. (It is a sampling-efficiency device, not a modelling change.)

**Model II imposes *less* shrinkage.** Widening the scale from $\sigma$ to $2\sigma$ lets the school intercepts sit further from the common mean before the prior objects. That is the opposite of additional shrinkage.

**Model III does not impose additional shrinkage either.** Replacing the adaptively estimated $\sigma$ with a *fixed* scale of 1 abandons the hierarchy — the model no longer learns from the data how similar the schools are. A fixed scale of 1 could turn out tighter or looser than whatever $\sigma$ the data would have supported, so it cannot be said to add shrinkage; it removes the adaptive pooling that produced the shrinkage in the first place.

None of the three adds shrinkage, so the answer is **A**.
