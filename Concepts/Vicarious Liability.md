**Vicarious Liability** is liability imposed on one party for the negligence of another because of the relationship between them. The party held liable need not have been careless at all.

It arises in two relationships:

1. **Owner and driver.** Provincial *Highway Traffic Act* provisions make the owner of a motor vehicle liable for loss caused by its negligent operation by anyone in possession with the owner's consent.
2. **Employer and employee.** An employer is liable for an employee's tort committed in the course of employment.

The rationale is that the party with control over the activity and the deeper pocket should bear its cost, which gives it an incentive to select and supervise carefully. The injured party's ability to recover is the practical driver.

## Statutory vicarious liability in auto

The owner's statutory liability is why auto insurance follows the vehicle, and why an owner's policy responds when a friend borrows the car. The risk being insured is the vehicle and everyone permitted to drive it, not only the named insured. A household with an unlisted young driver is a real exposure whether or not that driver is rated, which is why misrepresentation about drivers is treated so seriously.

Some provinces cap an owner's vicarious liability for a permitted driver's negligence, so the driver's own policy responds above the cap. The cap changes which insurer pays.

> [!example]- Who is liable for this collision, and which policies respond? {Example}
> A car owned by A is lent to B, who lends it to C without A's knowledge. C causes a collision injuring D, who suffers $\$800{,}000$ of damages. A carries $\$1$ million liability, and C carries $\$500{,}000$ on their own vehicle.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - C is personally liable as the negligent driver. Many auto policies extend coverage to the insured while driving a non-owned vehicle, typically as excess coverage.
> > - A is vicariously liable if C was in possession with A's consent. Consent given to B usually extends to a sub-bailee unless A expressly prohibited it, so A is liable.
> > - A's policy responds first, because auto coverage follows the vehicle and A's policy insures permitted drivers. Its $\$1$ million limit covers the full $\$800{,}000$.
> > - C's policy is excess and contributes nothing here. It would contribute if damages exceeded $\$1$ million.
> > - If A had expressly forbidden lending the car on, C's possession would be without consent and A's vicarious liability would fail. D would then have C's $\$500{,}000$ and a $\$300{,}000$ shortfall, recoverable only from C personally or from D's own [[Uninsured Automobile Coverage|underinsured motorist]] coverage.

## Employer liability

The modern Canadian test for an employer's vicarious liability asks whether the employment materially increased the risk of the wrong occurring. This test has extended vicarious liability well beyond simple negligence.

For commercial insureds, vicarious liability drives the exposure base. An employer's general liability exposure grows with payroll and headcount, because each employee is a source of imputed liability.

> [!trap] Assuming consent always flows through
> The owner's liability, and so the owner's policy, turns on consent. A driver in possession against the owner's express prohibition leaves the owner not vicariously liable, and the victim limited to the driver's own coverage.
