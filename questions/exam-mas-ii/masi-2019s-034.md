A statistician has a dataset with n = 50 observations and p = 22 independent predictors. He is using 10-fold cross validation to select from a variety of available models.

Calculate the number of times that the first observation will be included in the training dataset as part of this procedure.

- A) 0
- B) At least 1, but less than 5
- C) At least 5, but less than 10
- D) At least 10, but less than 20
- E) At least 20

## Explanation

In $k$-fold cross-validation the data are split into $k$ folds of roughly equal size. The model is fit $k$ times: each fold is held out once as the validation set while the other $k - 1$ folds form the training set.

The first observation belongs to exactly one of the 10 folds. It is the validation data in the one fit where its fold is held out, and it is part of the training data in each of the other fits:

$$
k - 1 = 10 - 1 = 9.
$$

The number of observations ($n = 50$) and predictors ($p = 22$) do not affect this count.

This falls in $[5, 10)$, so the answer is **C**.
