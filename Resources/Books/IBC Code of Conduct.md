The [[Insurance Bureau of Canada|IBC]]'s voluntary code governing insurers' use of **[[Credit|credit information]]** in personal lines. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective A1 — industry self-regulation, alongside the [[Code of Consumer Rights and Responsibilities]].

## The ten principles

1. **Comply with provincial laws.** Several provinces prohibit or restrict the use of credit information outright — [[Ontario Reg. 664]] bars it for private passenger automobile — so the code operates only where provincial law permits.
2. **Ensure credit information used is current and accurate.**
3. **Obtain prior consent** to collect and use credit information, written or verbal.
4. **Keep customers' credit information confidential and private.**
5. **Do not use credit as a sole variable** — credit may inform a rating or underwriting decision but may not by itself determine it.
6. **Legitimate uses of credit information** are defined and bounded.
7. **Handle consumer disputes** about the information used.
8. **Taking adverse action** as a result of credit information — the customer must be told.
9. **Refusal of consent** must not by itself be treated as adverse information.
10. **Extraordinary life circumstances** — the insurer will consider events (illness, job loss, divorce) that damaged a credit record through no fault of the insured.

## Why it is on the syllabus

Credit information is highly predictive and highly contested — the textbook case of a variable that is actuarially sound and socially objectionable, and therefore of [[Unfair Discrimination|unfair discrimination]] argued as *indirect* discrimination (see [[CIA Bias]]). The code is also the syllabus's example of **industry self-regulation**: the industry constrains itself in the hope of forestalling a legislated ban, which raises the question the exam actually asks — whether a voluntary code without an enforcement mechanism is regulation at all.
