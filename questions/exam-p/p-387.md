When a computer crashes, each of the data files $1, 2, \ldots, d$ has the same probability of being corrupted, independently of the other files. Given that a crash causes exactly two of the $d$ files to be corrupted, the probability that neither of the two most recently created files are corrupted is $\dfrac{40}{51}$.

Calculate the probability that none of the three most recently created files are corrupted, given that a crash causes exactly two of the files to be corrupted.

- A) $0.523$
- B) $0.676$
- C) $0.686$
- D) $0.695$
- E) $0.710$

## Explanation

Given that exactly two files are corrupted, all $\binom{d}{2}$ pairs are equally likely, so the corrupted pair is a simple random sample of size 2 from $d$ files.

**Find $d$ from the given probability.** Neither of the two most recent files is corrupted when both corrupted files come from the other $d-2$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{\binom{2}{0}\binom{d-2}{2}}{\binom{d}{2}} &= \frac{(d-2)(d-3)}{d(d-1)} = \frac{40}{51}
\end{align*}
$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$
\begin{align*}
51(d-2)(d-3) &= 40d(d-1) \\
51d^2 - 255d + 306 &= 40d^2 - 40d \\
11d^2 - 215d + 306 &= 0 \\
(11d - 17)(d - 18) &= 0
\end{align*}
$$

The root $d = 17/11$ is not an integer, so $d = 18$.

**Answer the question asked.** With $d = 18$, the probability that all three most recent files escape is the probability that both corrupted files come from the other 15:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{\binom{3}{0}\binom{d-3}{2}}{\binom{d}{2}} &= \frac{(d-3)(d-4)}{d(d-1)} \\
                                                &= \frac{(15)(14)}{(18)(17)} \\
                                                &= \frac{35}{51} \\
                                                &= \mathbf{0.686}
\end{align*}
$$

The independence mentioned in the setup is a red herring once the conditioning is imposed: given *exactly two* corruptions, the identity of the corrupted pair is uniform over all pairs, which is what makes this a hypergeometric calculation rather than a binomial one. Choice (D), 0.695, is $(40/51)^{3/2}$ — treating the three-file event as the two-file event raised to a power, as though each additional file contributed an independent factor. Given the conditioning, it does not.
