Given the following information for an insurance company:

Loss distribution for earthquake (EQ)

| Losses ($x$, in \$000,000) | $f(x)$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0.91 |
| 200 | 0.07 |
| 400 | 0.02 |

Loss distribution for coastal property (CP)

| Losses ($y$, in \$000,000) | $g(y)$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0.80 |
| 250 | 0.20 |

- Losses for these two perils are independent.
- The Wang transform has the form $F^*(x) = \Phi\{\Phi^{-1}[F(x)] - \Phi^{-1}(\alpha)\}$, where $\Phi$ represents the cumulative standard normal distribution.
- The Wang transform risk measure for CP is \$197.5 million.
- The Wang transform risk measure for CP and EQ combined is \$323 million.

| $z$ | $\Phi(z)$ |
|---|---|
| 0.304 | 0.62 |
| 0.409 | 0.66 |
| 0.804 | 0.79 |
| 0.842 | 0.80 |
| 1.341 | 0.91 |
| 1.645 | 0.95 |
| 2.054 | 0.98 |

## Part a (1.75 points)

For EQ, calculate:

- i. Tail value-at-risk (TVaR) at the 95% level.
- ii. Wang transform at the 95% level.
- iii. Expected policyholder deficit (EPD) at the 5% level.

### Explanation
**i.** $\text{TVaR}_{0.95}$ is the conditional mean of losses greater than the 95th percentile:

$$\text{TVaR}_{0.95} = \frac{0.03 \times 200 + 0.02 \times 400}{0.03 + 0.02} = 280\text{M}$$

**ii.** Wang transform, $F^*(x) = \Phi\{\Phi^{-1}[F(x)] - \Phi^{-1}(\alpha)\}$ with $\Phi^{-1}(0.95) = 1.645$:

| Loss | $F(x)$ | $\Phi^{-1}[F(x)]$ | $\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)$ | $\Phi^{-1}[F(x)] - \Phi^{-1}(\alpha)$ | $F^*(x)$ | $f^*(x)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.91 | 1.341 | 1.645 | -0.304 | 0.38 | 0.38 |
| 200 | 0.98 | 2.054 | 1.645 | 0.409 | 0.66 | 0.28 |
| 400 | 1.00 | | | | 1.00 | 0.34 |

$$WT(0.95) = (0 \times 0.38) + (200 \times 0.28) + (400 \times 0.34) = 192\text{M}$$

**iii.**

$$\text{EPD}_\alpha = (1 - \alpha) \times (\text{TVaR}_\alpha - \text{VaR}_\alpha) = (1 - 0.95) \times (280 - 200) = 4\text{M}$$

Some candidates used 0.05 for $\alpha$, resulting in 76M; this was accepted as full credit.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Evaluate whether using TVaR as a risk measure for EQ would satisfy the company's requirement to have a pricing charge for any risk taken.

### Explanation
TVaR0.95 only includes tail risk and therefore does not meet the criteria to charge for any risk taken.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Evaluate whether using the Wang transform as a risk measure for EQ would satisfy the company's requirement to have a pricing charge for any risk taken.

### Explanation
The Wang transform uses the entire loss distribution to calculate the risk measure and therefore does meet the criteria to charge for all risks taken (not just tail risk).

## Part d (0.5 points)

Evaluate whether using TVaR as a risk measure for EQ would reflect the company's increasing risk aversion to larger losses.

### Explanation
No. By taking the average of losses above the threshold, TVaR is linear in the tail and fails to demonstrate that the company is more risk averse as the loss size increases.

Alternate solution: Yes. Since it uses tail losses, which would reflect the largest losses of the distribution, it does reflect an increasing risk aversion to larger losses.

## Part e (0.5 points)

Evaluate whether using the Wang transform as a risk measure for EQ would reflect the company's increasing risk aversion to larger losses.

### Explanation
Yes. The Wang transform modifies the probability distribution to put more weight on large losses.  That is, f*(x) > f(x) for large losses.  This reflects an aversion to larger loss sizes.

## Part f (2.5 points)

Calculate the TVaR and Wang transform risk measures at the 95% level for the combined CP and EQ book of business. Evaluate whether these measures exhibit the diversification benefits of writing both coverages.

### Explanation
Assuming independence, the combined distribution is:

| EQ+CP | $f(\text{EQ+CP})$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0.728 |
| 200 | 0.056 |
| 250 | 0.182 |
| 400 | 0.016 |
| 450 | 0.014 |
| 650 | 0.004 |

$$\text{TVaR}_{0.95}(\text{EQ+CP}) = (0.016 \times 250) + (0.016 \times 400) + (0.014 \times 450) + (0.004 \times 650) = 386\text{M}$$

(the division of this 5% tail by 0.05 is omitted, as printed in the examiner's report).

$$\text{TVaR}_{0.95}(\text{EQ+CP}) < \text{TVaR}_{0.95}(\text{EQ}) + \text{TVaR}_{0.95}(\text{CP}): \quad 386\text{M} < 280\text{M} + 250\text{M} = 530\text{M}$$

$\text{TVaR}_{0.95}$ is sub-additive and reflects the diversification benefits.

Wang transform: $WT(\text{EQ}) = 192\text{M}$, $WT(\text{CP}) = 197.5\text{M}$ and $WT(\text{EQ+CP}) = 323\text{M}$ (given in the problem; full credit was given for calculating it or for pointing out that it was given).

$$WT(\text{EQ+CP}) < WT(\text{EQ}) + WT(\text{CP}): \quad 323\text{M} < 192\text{M} + 197.5\text{M} = 389.5\text{M}$$

The Wang transform is sub-additive and reflects the diversification benefits.

A common place to lose points was to answer parts b) and c) with a solution that was more applicable to the question asked in parts d) and e).
