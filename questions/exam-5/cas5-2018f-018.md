Given the following data as of December 31, 2017:

**Cumulative Reported Claims (\$000) as of (months)**

| Accident Year | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 500 | 1,100 | 1,800 | 2,500 |
| 2015 | 900 | 1,700 | 2,300 | |
| 2016 | 1,000 | 1,900 | | |
| 2017 | 1,100 | | | |

| Calendar Year | Earned Premium (\$000) |
|---|---|
| 2014 | 5,300 |
| 2015 | 7,200 |
| 2016 | 7,800 |
| 2017 | 8,500 |

| | |
|---|---|
| 1.3 | 48 to ultimate reported claim development factor |

- In 2015 the company started writing a new class of insureds within this line of business.
- Both existing and new classes of insureds are priced accurately.

## Part a (1.25 points)

Estimate ultimate claims for accident year 2017 using the reported Cape Cod technique.

### Answer

Approximately \$5,408 thousand

### Explanation

**Step 1 — Development factors** (straight averages):

| | 12–24 | 24–36 | 36–48 | 48–ult |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 2.200 | 1.636 | 1.389 | |
| 2015 | 1.889 | 1.353 | | |
| 2016 | 1.900 | | | |
| Selected | 1.996 | 1.495 | 1.389 | 1.300 |
| CDF to ult | 5.388 | 2.699 | 1.806 | 1.300 |

**Step 2 — Used-up premium** = earned premium × percent reported ($1/\text{CDF}$):

| AY | Reported | Earned Premium | % Reported | Used-Up Premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 2,500 | 5,300 | 0.7692 | 4,077 |
| 2015 | 2,300 | 7,200 | 0.5538 | 3,988 |
| 2016 | 1,900 | 7,800 | 0.3705 | 2,890 |
| 2017 | 1,100 | 8,500 | 0.1856 | 1,578 |
| **Total** | **7,800** | | | **12,532** |

**Step 3 — Cape Cod expected claims ratio** — total reported claims over total used-up premium:

$$\text{ECR} = \frac{7{,}800}{12{,}532} = 0.6224$$

**Step 4 — AY 2017 ultimate** = reported + expected unreported:

$$1{,}100 + 8{,}500 \times 0.6224 \times (1 - 0.1856) = 1{,}100 + 4{,}308 = 5{,}408$$

Alternative development factor selections — weighted averages, or excluding the 2014 factors because of the new class of business — also received full credit.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to calculate the estimated ultimate loss using the Cape Cod technique, and explain how the change in the mix of business impacted the Cape Cod ultimate claims for Accident Year 2017.

Candidates were expected to select claim development factors from a reported claims triangle and use these development factors to calculate used up premium.  Candidates were expected to calculate the expected claims ratio as the ratio of reported losses to used up premium (as defined by the Cape Cod technique), and apply this expected claims ratio to get the estimated unreported claims, and subsequently, the ultimate claims.

Common mistakes include:

- Calculating the expected claims ratio as something other than total reported losses over total used up premium (e.g., straight average or total developed claims over total earned premium)
- Multiplying the expected claims ratio by the earned premium to get the ultimate claims

## Part b (0.5 point)

Discuss the effect the new class of insureds has on the reported Cape Cod technique for accident year 2017.

### Explanation

Both classes are priced accurately, so the new class should not change the underlying expected claims ratio. The problem is the **reporting pattern**: the development factors drop sharply once the new class enters. The 24–36 factor is 1.636 for accident year 2014 but only 1.353 for 2015, and the 12–24 factor falls from 2.200 to about 1.89–1.90 — reporting has sped up.

Selecting factors that average in the pre-2015 experience therefore produces CDFs that are **too high**. That inflates the percentage unreported, which understates used-up premium, which in turn **overstates the expected claims ratio**. The AY 2017 ultimate is hit twice — too high an ECR applied to too large an unreported percentage — so the Cape Cod estimate of AY 2017 ultimate claims (and its IBNR) is **overstated**.

### Examiner Report

Candidates were expected to recognize the change in the development factors as a result of the change in the mix of business, and to discuss the impact of this change in development factors on the estimated ultimate claims.

Common mistakes include:

- Stating that the Cape Cod ultimate claims would be impacted without detailing how.
- Stating that the new mix of business would have no impact on Cape Cod ultimate claims.
- Confusion regarding the loss ratio as the source of the effect to the Cape Cod technique rather than the change in reported development.
- Stating that the new mix of business would lower the estimated ultimate claims.
