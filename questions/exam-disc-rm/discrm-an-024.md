An insurer writes homeowners policies in many states. Determine which of the following risks the insurer can reduce, per policy, by writing more policies in more independent locations.

- I. Kitchen fires that strike individual homes independently of one another
- II. A nationwide rise in inflation that raises the cost of repairing every claim
- III. Burglaries of individual homes in different cities

- A) I only
- B) I and III only
- C) II and III only
- D) I, II and III

## Explanation

A risk is **diversifiable** when losses are independent across exposures, so a larger pool makes the average loss more predictable.

- **I. Diversifiable:** Kitchen fires strike homes independently, so adding homes in more places reduces the variability per policy.
- **II. Not diversifiable:** Inflation is systemic: it raises every claim at once, and adding more policies adds more of the same effect.
- **III. Diversifiable:** Burglaries in different cities are largely independent events particular to each home.

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Leaves out burglaries, which are as particular to each home as kitchen fires.
- **C** — Includes inflation, a systemic risk shared by every exposure, and leaves out fires.
- **D** — Treats inflation as diversifiable; writing more policies does not reduce a risk that affects all of them together.
