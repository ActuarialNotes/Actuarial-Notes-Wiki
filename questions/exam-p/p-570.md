A homeowner hires a moving company to transport ten pieces of electronic equipment to a new home. Each piece is equally likely to be damaged during the move, and the event of damage to any one piece is independent of the events of damage to the other pieces.

The homeowner fully insures two of these pieces, partially insures three of them, and leaves the other five pieces uninsured.

During the move, three pieces are damaged.

Calculate the probability that at least one damaged piece is fully insured and at least one damaged piece is partially insured.

- A) $0.242$
- B) $0.250$
- C) $0.325$
- D) $0.378$
- E) $0.400$

## Explanation

Because every piece is equally likely to be damaged, conditioning on exactly three damaged pieces makes those three a simple random sample of size 3 from the ten. All $\binom{10}{3} = 120$ triples are equally likely.

The event requires **both** conditions, so count its complement with inclusion–exclusion. Let $A$ = no fully insured piece damaged and $B$ = no partially insured piece damaged:

$$
\begin{align*}
|A| &= \binom{8}{3} = 56 \quad \text{(all three from the other 8)} \\
|B| &= \binom{7}{3} = 35 \quad \text{(all three from the other 7)} \\
|A \cap B| &= \binom{5}{3} = 10 \quad \text{(all three uninsured)}
\end{align*}
$$

The favourable count is the total minus the union of the two failure modes:

$$
\begin{align*}
120 - (56 + 35 - 10) &= 120 - 81 = 39
\end{align*}
$$

so

$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \frac{39}{120} \\
  &= \mathbf{0.325}
\end{align*}
$$

Adding back $|A \cap B|$ is essential — the ten all-uninsured triples fail both conditions and would otherwise be removed twice. Forgetting that term leaves 29/120 = 0.242, which is choice (A).
