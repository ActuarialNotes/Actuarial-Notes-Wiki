Claim amounts from accidents in an amusement park are uniformly distributed on an interval. The probability that a randomly selected claim amount is less than 12 is 0.50. The probability that a randomly selected claim amount exceeds 6 is 0.875.

Calculate the probability that a randomly selected claim amount is less than 10, given that it exceeds 6.

- A) $2/9$
- B) $1/4$
- C) $2/7$
- D) $3/8$
- E) $3/7$

## Explanation

Let the distribution be uniform on $[a,b]$.

**The first fact fixes the midpoint.** A uniform distribution is symmetric, so its median equals its midpoint:

$$
\frac{a+b}{2} = 12 \implies a + b = 24
$$

**The second fact fixes the endpoints.** Substituting $a = 24 - b$:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.875 &= \frac{b - 6}{b - a} = \frac{b-6}{2b - 24} \\
b - 6 &= 1.75b - 21 \\
0.75b &= 15 \implies b = 20, \quad a = 4
\end{align*}
$$

**Now the conditional probability.** Conditioning on $X > 6$ restricts to the interval $(6, 20)$ of length 14, within which $X < 10$ covers the first 4:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X < 10 \mid X > 6] &= \frac{10 - 6}{20 - 6} \\
                     &= \frac{4}{14} \\
                     &= \mathbf{\frac{2}{7}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (D), 3/8, is the unconditional $P[X < 10] = 6/16$. Once the conditioning is imposed the lower endpoint moves from 4 to 6, which shortens both the numerator and the denominator — and not by the same proportion.
