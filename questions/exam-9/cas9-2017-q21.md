Given the following:

- An insurance company writes only two lines of business.
- All policy terms are one year.
- Surplus is \$25 million.
- The after-tax investment yield is 5%.
- Regarding the company's surplus:
  - 100% of surplus is allocated by line
  - surplus is committed when premium is written and is released when the policy expires
  - surplus is committed in proportion to premium
- The company's pricing actuary is recommending to change the surplus allocation method to be:
  - committed when losses are incurred
  - released when losses are paid
  - committed in proportion to reserves
- The company sets rates for each line to achieve a 12% internal rate of return.

|  | Line A | Line B |
|---|---|---|
| Written Premium | \$30 million | \$20 million |
| Loss Ratio | 65% | 60% |
| Average time from loss to payment | 2.5 years | 0.5 years |

## Part a (0.25 points)

Calculate the allocated surplus for each line of business based on the current allocation method.

### Explanation
Surplus is committed in proportion to premium:

$$\text{Line A allocation} = 25M \times \frac{30M}{30M + 20M} = 15M$$

$$\text{Line B allocation} = 25M \times \frac{20M}{30M + 20M} = 10M$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to allocate capital based on premium.
There were no common errors to this part as it required a calculation straight from the text.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the allocated surplus for each line of business based on the pricing actuary's proposed allocation method.

### Explanation
Surplus is committed in proportion to reserves, weighted by how long the reserves are held:

$$\text{Reserve A} = 30M \times 0.65 = 19.5M \qquad \text{Reserve B} = 20M \times 0.60 = 12M$$

$$\text{Line A allocation} = \frac{19.5M \times 2.5}{19.5M \times 2.5 + 12M \times 0.5} = 22.26M$$

$$\text{Line B allocation} = \frac{12M \times 0.5}{19.5M \times 2.5 + 12M \times 0.5} = 2.74M$$

(as printed: each ratio is a share of the 25M of surplus, a multiplication the sample does not show)

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to allocate capital based on reserves.
Common errors include:

- Attempting to discount the surplus allocation using the after-tax investment yield
- Not correctly adjusting the allocation for the average time from loss to payment for each line.

## Part c (0.5 points)

For Line A, describe qualitatively whether the rate level should increase or decrease if the pricing actuary's recommendation is implemented.

### Explanation
Given that the investment yield is 5% and the IRR target is 12%, management will require the difference to be made up by earning underwriting income or investment income from policyholder-supplied funds. Since the surplus allocation increased for Line A, additional underwriting profit will be required to meet the 12% target return on equity. The rate for Line A needs to be increased.

Alternatively:

Based on the proposed surplus allocation, Line A has almost 10 times the surplus of Line B, but its premium is only 50% higher. Since there is a mismatch in the amount of risk reflected in the premium (as it was probably based on the initial allocation), the rate level for Line A should increase.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand of how capital allocation strategies would impact rate levels and describe why the rate level would decrease if the pricing strategy is implemented.
Common errors include:

- Support the decision quantitatively rather than qualitatively
- Falsely concluding that the allocation should decrease simply because the average time from loss to payment was longer for line A so there is more investment income to be earned
- Concluding that the rate level should increase without explaining why
