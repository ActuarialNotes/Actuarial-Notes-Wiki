A CGL policy with a \$2,000,000 general aggregate is issued for January 1 to December 31. In November the insurer agrees to extend it to March 31 of the next year. By then \$1,700,000 of general-aggregate claims have been paid. How much general aggregate is available for claims in the extension?

- A) \$0, because an extension added after issuance carries no limits of its own
- B) \$300,000, because the extension is treated as part of the last annual period
- C) \$2,000,000, because limits apply separately to any remaining period under 12 months
- D) \$500,000, because the aggregate is prorated to the three months of the extension

## Explanation

Limits apply separately to each consecutive annual period and to any remaining period of less than 12 months — but if the policy period is extended *after issuance* for less than 12 months, the extension is deemed part of the last preceding period. The extension shares the year's aggregate:

$$
\begin{align*}
G &= 2{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}700{,}000 \\
&= 300{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **B**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — the extension is covered; it just shares the last period's limits.
- **C** applies the rule for a policy *issued* for more than a year, whose short final period gets its own limits.
- **D** — the form does not prorate limits by time.
