A homeowner's HO-3 carries a personal property replacement cost endorsement. A fire destroys her television, which would cost \$2,000 to replace and had an actual cash value of \$900. She has not yet bought a new one. Which statement describes her settlement, before the deductible?

- A) She receives \$2,000 now, because the endorsement removes the deduction for depreciation
- B) She receives \$900, and the endorsement adds nothing because televisions are excluded from it
- C) She receives \$900 now, and can claim the remaining \$1,100 once she replaces the set
- D) She receives nothing until she replaces the set, and then \$2,000

## Explanation

The endorsement settles covered personal property at replacement cost, but when the replacement cost of the loss is more than \$500 the insurer pays no more than actual cash value until the property is actually repaired or replaced; the insured may claim on an ACV basis and then claim the difference within 180 days. So she gets \$900 now and \$1,100 more after replacing it. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** skips the condition that replacement must happen first for a loss over \$500.
- **B** — a television is ordinary personal property; the ineligible classes are items such as antiques, fine art, memorabilia and articles not kept in workable condition.
- **D** — the actual cash value is payable before replacement.
