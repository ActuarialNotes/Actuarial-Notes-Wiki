In order to predict individual candidates' test scores a regression was performed using one independent variable, Hours of Study, plus an intercept. Below is a partial table of data and model results:

| Candidate | Test Score | Hours of Study | Leverage | Standardized Residuals |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 2,041 | 538 | 0.6205 | -1.3477 |
| 2 | 2,502 | 548 | 0.2018 | -0.4171 |
| 3 | 2,920 | 528 | 0.6486 | -1.1121 |
| 4 | 2,284 | 608 | 0.2807 | 1.1472 |

Calculate the number of observations above that are influential using Cook's Distance with a unity threshold.

- A) 0
- B) 1
- C) 2
- D) 3
- E) 4

## Explanation

Cook's distance measures how far the whole set of fitted values moves when one observation is dropped. It combines the size of the observation's standardized residual $r_i$ with its leverage $h_i$:

$$
D_i = \frac{r_i^2}{p} \cdot \frac{h_i}{1 - h_i}
$$

where $p = 2$ is the number of parameters (the intercept and the Hours of Study slope). With a unity threshold, an observation is influential when $D_i > 1$.

| Candidate | $r_i^2$ | $h_i/(1-h_i)$ | $D_i$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 1.8163 | 1.6350 | 1.485 |
| 2 | 0.1740 | 0.2528 | 0.022 |
| 3 | 1.2368 | 1.8458 | 1.141 |
| 4 | 1.3161 | 0.3902 | 0.257 |

Candidates 1 and 3 exceed 1: both pair a leverage above 0.6 with a sizeable residual. Candidate 4 has as large a residual as candidate 3, but its low leverage keeps it well under the threshold.

Two observations are influential, so the answer is **C**.
