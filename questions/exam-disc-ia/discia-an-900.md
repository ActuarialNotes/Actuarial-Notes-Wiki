A Texas driver carries the state minimum liability limits of 30/60/25 and causes a crash. One injured person has \$50,000 of bodily injury damages and another \$20,000, and the other car, worth \$31,000, is destroyed. How much of the damages is the driver personally liable for after the policy pays?

- A) \$6,000
- B) \$16,000
- C) \$26,000
- D) \$46,000

## Explanation

In 30/60/25, \$30,000 is the most paid for one person's bodily injury, \$60,000 for all bodily injury in the accident, and \$25,000 for property damage.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid}_{\text{BI}} &= \min(30{,}000 + 20{,}000,\ 60{,}000) \\
&= 50{,}000 \\
\text{Paid}_{\text{PD}} &= \min(31{,}000,\ 25{,}000) \\
&= 25{,}000 \\
\text{Driver owes} &= 50{,}000 + 20{,}000 + 31{,}000 - 75{,}000 \\
&= 26{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** — Counts only the property damage above \$25,000 and forgets the first person's \$20,000 above the per-person limit.
- **B** — Ignores the per-person limit, paying \$60,000 of the \$70,000 bodily injury.
- **D** — Treats \$30,000 as the most paid for all bodily injury in the accident.
