The number of errors on any page of text produced at a particular office is modeled by a Poisson distribution with mean 0.10. The number of errors on any one page is independent of the number of errors on all other pages.

Calculate the probability of more than four errors in a 100-page document produced at the office.

- A) $0.01$
- B) $0.03$
- C) $0.97$
- D) $0.98$
- E) $0.99$

## Explanation

Independent Poisson counts add, so a 100-page document has mean

$$
\lambda = 100(0.10) = 10
$$

"More than four" is the upper tail, easiest as a complement:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X > 4] &= 1 - P[X \le 4] \\
         &= 1 - \left[\frac{e^{-10}10^0}{0!} + \frac{e^{-10}10^1}{1!} + \frac{e^{-10}10^2}{2!} + \frac{e^{-10}10^3}{3!} + \frac{e^{-10}10^4}{4!}\right] \\
         &= 1 - \left[0.000045 + 0.00045 + 0.00227 + 0.00757 + 0.01892\right] \\
         &= \mathbf{0.9707}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (B), 0.03, is that bracket — $P[X \le 4] = 0.0293$ — reported without subtracting from 1. The direction is easy to check without arithmetic: the document averages 10 errors, so seeing four or fewer would be unusual and "more than four" must be close to 1, not close to 0.
