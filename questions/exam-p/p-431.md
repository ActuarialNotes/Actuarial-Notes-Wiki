Each year, a car insurance company's four quarterly profits are mutually independent and normally distributed with common mean and variance. Each quarter, the probability that the company earns a positive profit is 0.80.

Calculate the probability that the company earns an overall positive profit in a given year.

- A) $0.410$
- B) $0.663$
- C) $0.800$
- D) $0.954$
- E) $0.998$

## Explanation

Neither $\mu$ nor $\sigma$ is given, but their **ratio** is recoverable and that is all the question needs.

For a single quarter,

$$
\begin{align*}
0.80 &= P[X_i > 0] = P\left[Z > \frac{0 - \mu}{\sigma}\right] \\
\frac{\mu}{\sigma} &= 0.84
\end{align*}
$$

The yearly total $S = X_1 + X_2 + X_3 + X_4$ is normal, with mean $4\mu$ and variance $4\sigma^2$ — variances add, so the standard deviation is $2\sigma$, not $4\sigma$.

$$
\begin{align*}
P[S > 0] &= P\left[Z > \frac{0 - 4\mu}{2\sigma}\right] \\
         &= P\left[Z > -2\frac{\mu}{\sigma}\right] \\
         &= P[Z > -1.68] \\
         &= \mathbf{0.9535}
\end{align*}
$$

The mean grows by a factor of 4 while the standard deviation grows only by $\sqrt{4} = 2$, so the $z$-score doubles — that asymmetry is the whole reason the yearly figure so far exceeds the quarterly 0.80. Choice (A), 0.410, is $(0.8)^4$, the probability that *all four* quarters are profitable, a much stronger requirement than a positive total.
