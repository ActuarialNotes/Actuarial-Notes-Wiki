You are given the following:

- $h$ is normally distributed with mean $\mu$ and variance 1.
- $\mu$ is normally distributed with mean 25 and variance 9.
- You observe a value of $h$ of 24.
- The posterior distribution of $h$ has a mean of 24.1 and a variance of 0.81.

Calculate the width of the 50% highest posterior density interval of $h$.

- A) Less than 1.10
- B) Greater than or equal to 1.10 and less than 1.15
- C) Greater than or equal to 1.15 and less than 1.20
- D) Greater than or equal to 1.20 and less than 1.25
- E) Greater than or equal to 1.25

## Explanation

The posterior is normal, and the normal density is symmetric and unimodal. For such a density the highest posterior density (HPD) interval is the interval centred on the mean — the shortest interval carrying the required probability.

$$
\begin{align*}
\sigma &= \sqrt{0.81} \\
       &= 0.9
\end{align*}
$$

A 50% central interval runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile, so its half-width is $z_{0.75}\sigma$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{width} &= 2 z_{0.75}\,\sigma \\
             &= 2(0.6745)(0.9) \\
             &= 1.214
\end{align*}
$$

Note the posterior mean of 24.1 never enters — the width of a normal interval depends only on its variance.

This falls in $[1.20,\, 1.25)$, so the answer is **D**.
