You are given the following three statements regarding parameter estimates of Linear Mixed Models using maximum likelihood (ML) estimation and residual maximum likelihood (REML) estimation.

- I. ML estimates of the covariance parameters are biased.
- II. The variance of the ML estimates of the fixed effects are biased.
- III. The variance of the REML estimates of the fixed effects are unbiased.

Determine which of the preceding statements are true.

- A) None are true
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

ML maximizes the full likelihood over $\beta$ and the covariance parameters $\theta$ together. REML maximizes a likelihood of residual contrasts that removes $\beta$ first.

**I is true.** ML estimates of the covariance parameters are biased (downward) because they take no account of the degrees of freedom used up in estimating the fixed effects. This is the familiar $\frac{1}{n}$ versus $\frac{1}{n-p}$ bias of the variance estimate in ordinary regression. REML corrects for it.

**II is true.** The estimated variance of $\hat{\beta}$ is $(X^\top \hat{V}^{-1} X)^{-1}$, which treats the estimated covariance matrix $\hat{V}$ as if it were known. Ignoring the uncertainty in estimating $\theta$ makes it biased downward.

**III is false.** The same formula is used under REML, so it too plugs in $\hat{\theta}$ as if it were the true value and understates the variability of $\hat{\beta}$. REML reduces the bias in the covariance parameters themselves, not in the variance of the fixed-effect estimates. That is why small-sample adjustments such as Kenward–Roger exist.

Only I and II are true, so the answer is **B**.
