![[CFAI - Cover.svg]]

Alberta Treasury Board and Finance's August 2025 paper setting out **Care-First Auto Insurance**, the province's replacement for its tort-based automobile compensation system. Read for [[Exam 6C (CAS)|Exam 6C]] objective A1 — the current state of [[Insurance Regulation|insurance regulation]] in Canada and the [[Automobile Insurance Reform|reform]] of the compulsory auto product. It supersedes [[Alberta Auto Reform]], the 2020 advisory-committee report that recommended the move.

## What the paper covers

- **Alberta auto insurance today** — why the province concluded reform was needed: premium growth, the cost and delay of tort litigation, and the gap between what a claimant recovers and what the system spends getting it to them
- **Care-First Auto Insurance** — a [[No-Fault Insurance|no-fault]] model in which benefits are paid regardless of fault and access to the courts is narrowed rather than removed
- **A comparison of accident benefits** — the Care-First benefit suite set against the [[Statutory Accident Benefits|accident benefits]] payable under the outgoing model
- **Claims process** and **injury management** — the administrative path a claimant follows, in place of a tort action
- **Medical and rehabilitation benefits**, **permanent impairment**, **income replacement**, **childcare reimbursement**, **other expenses**, **death benefits**
- **Fee schedules** — the prices paid to treating providers, which is how a care-first system controls cost without a [[Tort Threshold and Deductible|deductible or threshold]]
- **Dispute resolution** and **access to court** — the retained causes of action (notably against impaired and criminal drivers)
- **Implementation** — the transition timetable

## Why it is on the syllabus

Care-First is the live Canadian example of the trade-off the syllabus keeps returning to: a [[No-Fault Insurance|no-fault]] system delivers care faster and more predictably and removes most transaction cost, at the price of the injured party's right to sue for [[Tort Litigation|general damages]]. Compare with the [[Minor Injury Guideline|minor injury]] cap and [[Catastrophic Impairment|catastrophic impairment]] tiers Ontario uses to reach a similar end (see [[Marshall]]), and with Quebec's and Manitoba's [[Public Auto Insurance|public]] pure no-fault schemes.
