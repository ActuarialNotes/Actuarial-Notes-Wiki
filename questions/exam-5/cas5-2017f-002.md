Given the following information:

| Rate Change Effective Date | Overall Rate Change |
|---|---|
| July 1, 2013 | 5% |
| October 1, 2015 | 2% |
| October 1, 2016 | -4% |

| Calendar Year | Earned Premium (\$000) | Earned Premium (\$000) at Current Rate Level |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 15,000 | 14,775 |
| 2015 | 18,000 | 17,622 |

- 2016 Earned Premium = \$22,000,000.
- 2014 through 2016 combined projected ultimate loss and LAE = \$40,000,000.
- Selected annual premium trend = 2%.
- Fixed expense provision = 8%.
- Variable expense provision = 20%.
- Target underwriting profit provision = 5%.
- All policies are annual.
- Rates are to be in effect for one year.
- The rate revision is planned to be effective October 1, 2017.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Calculate the projected earned premium at current rate level for 2014 through 2016.

### Answer
\$57,098 thousand

### Explanation
Earned premium at current rate level is given for 2014 and 2015, so only 2016 needs on-leveling — but it needs it carefully, because **two** rate changes bear on calendar year 2016.

**Step 1 — CY2016 average rate level (parallelogram method).** With annual policies written uniformly, a rate change effective at fraction $p$ through a year leaves $p^2/2$ of the *following* year's earned premium at the old level, and puts $(1-p)^2/2$ of its *own* year at the new level. Both October 1 changes have $p = 0.75$:

| Region | Weight | Rate level |
|---|---|---|
| Still at the pre-2% level (from the 10/1/2015 change) | $0.75^2/2 = 0.28125$ | $1.05$ |
| Between the two changes | $1 - 0.28125 - 0.03125 = 0.68750$ | $1.05 \times 1.02 = 1.071$ |
| Already at the post-(-4%) level (from the 10/1/2016 change) | $0.25^2/2 = 0.03125$ | $1.05 \times 1.02 \times 0.96 = 1.02816$ |

$$ARL_{2016} = 0.28125(1.05) + 0.68750(1.071) + 0.03125(1.02816) = 1.063755$$

$$CRL = 1.05 \times 1.02 \times 0.96 = 1.02816$$

$$OLF_{2016} = \frac{1.02816}{1.063755} = 0.966538$$

$$EP_{2016} \text{ at CRL} = 22{,}000 \times 0.966538 = 21{,}264$$

The on-level factor is *below* 1.0 because the -4% change has barely earned into 2016, so the year's premium sits above the current rate level.

**Step 2 — trend each year to the projected period.** Rates are effective 10/1/2017 for one year on annual policies, so policies are written 10/1/2017 – 9/30/2018 (average written date 4/1/2018) and the average *earned* date is six months later: **10/1/2018**. Trending from each calendar year's average earned date of 7/1:

| CY | EP at CRL | Trend period | Trend factor | Projected EP at CRL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 14,775 | $7/1/2014 \to 10/1/2018 = 4.25$ yrs | $1.02^{4.25} = 1.087804$ | 16,072 |
| 2015 | 17,622 | $3.25$ yrs | $1.02^{3.25} = 1.066475$ | 18,793 |
| 2016 | 21,264 | $2.25$ yrs | $1.02^{2.25} = 1.045563$ | 22,233 |
| **Total** | | | | **57,098** |

$$\text{Projected earned premium at current rate level} = \$57{,}098 \text{ thousand}$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how to utilize each piece of the information provided to bring premiums to current rate level (via on-leveling) and apply trend to calculate projected premium. As a final step, the candidate is expected to determine the indicated rate change as a result of the projected premium.

Candidates were expected to understand the impacts of the historical rate changes on the calendar years and determine the average rate level for CY16. This required candidates to utilize the parallelogram method. Candidates needed to display an understanding of utilizing the average rate level and the current rate level to bring earned premium for CY16 to current rate level. Candidates could utilize a similar approach for CY14 and CY15, though the earned premium at current rate level for each was provided in the question instructions.

Candidates were then expected to understand how the annual premium trend would impact each of the calendar years and apply trend appropriately to project the earned premium at current rate level to the projection period where rates would be in effect.

Common errors included:

- Trending all earned premium grouped together across the CYs or not correctly understanding the starting and/or ending points in the one-step trend.  Sometimes a two-step trend was applied.  The most common mistake made by candidates was in the trend step.
- Failing to realize that there were two rate changes that impacted the average rate level in CY16.  The 10/1/15 rate change was often ignored in building the average rate level calculation.
- Calculating weights assigned to each of the different rate levels within CY16 incorrectly.
- Calculating the on level factors for CYs 2014 and 2015 incorrectly and carrying this forward through the solution, even though the EP @ CRL was given for these years.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Calculate the indicated rate change.

### Answer
+4.07%

### Explanation
With a fixed expense provision stated as a percentage of premium, the loss ratio method gives

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{\text{Loss \& LAE ratio} + \text{Fixed expense \%}}{1 - \text{Variable expense \%} - \text{Profit \%}} - 1$$

The projected loss and LAE ratio uses the projected premium from part a:

$$\text{Loss \& LAE ratio} = \frac{40{,}000}{57{,}098} = 0.70054$$

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{0.70054 + 0.08}{1 - 0.20 - 0.05} - 1 = \frac{0.78054}{0.75} - 1 = +4.07\%$$

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to utilize the projected premium from part (a) to calculate the indicated rate change using a loss ratio method.

Common errors included:

- Misreading question information and applying the fixed expense provision as an LAE load (LR * 1.08 instead of LR + 0.08) in the numerator
- Applying an incorrect trend within this part (for example, applying a factor of 1.021.25 or 1.022.25 to the 2014-2016 total EP or EP @ CRL)
