**A Superintendent of Insurance** is the provincial official who licenses insurers, agents and adjusters to operate in a province, supervises [[Market Conduct Regulation|market conduct]], and administers the provincial *Insurance Act*. The federal counterpart is the Superintendent of Financial Institutions ([[OSFI]]), who supervises **solvency** — the two roles are distinct and both apply to the same insurer.

- Every province has such an office, though the modern name varies: **[[Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario|FSRA]]** in Ontario, the **[[Autorité des marchés financiers|AMF]]** in Quebec, the Alberta Superintendent of Insurance, BCFSA in British Columbia.
- **Core powers:** issue and revoke the licence that lets an insurer write in the province; approve or refuse [[Rate Regulation|rate filings]] where the province regulates rates; investigate unfair practices; set the [[Statutory Accident Benefits|benefit]] and coverage terms prescribed by regulation; and enforce agent and adjuster licensing.
- Provincially incorporated insurers are also **prudentially** supervised by their province, so in that case the Superintendent does the job OSFI does federally, usually applying the [[MCT]] guideline by adoption rather than writing a separate capital rule.
- A licence to write in a province is required **per province**, so a national insurer holds a federal order to commence and carry on business plus a licence from every jurisdiction it operates in — the practical source of Canada's multi-jurisdiction compliance burden.
- Superintendents coordinate through the **[[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators]]**, which produces harmonised positions but has no power of its own; each Superintendent must adopt them separately.

> [!example]- One Insurer, Many Regulators {Example}
> A federally incorporated insurer wants to begin writing personal property insurance in three new provinces next year. What approvals does it need, and from whom?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - **[[OSFI]]**: no new approval to enter a province as such, but the expansion is a change in business plan that feeds capital adequacy and would be discussed with the supervisory team; the [[ORSA]] should reflect the new exposure.
> > - **Each provincial Superintendent**: a licence for that province, before a single policy is written.
> > - **Rate and form filings**: for personal property, most provinces do not pre-approve rates the way they do for auto, but the *forms* and endorsements may still need filing. The insurer must check each province separately — there is no national filing.
> >
> > Because the insurer now has catastrophe exposure in three new territories, the [[Earthquake Exposure Risk Margin|earthquake]] and [[Concentration Risk|concentration]] implications are a separate OSFI question, and the [[Appointed Actuary]] should reflect the new exposure in the next [[FCT]] report.
