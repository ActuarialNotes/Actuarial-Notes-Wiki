# Exam 5
The **Basic Techniques for Ratemaking and Estimating Claim Liabilities** is a 4-hour computer-based exam (a 4.5-hour Pearson VUE appointment, with a 15-minute scheduled break) covering **Ratemaking** and **Loss Reserving** as part of the ACAS credentialing pathway.

## Learning Objectives

> [!example]- A. Ratemaking {45–55%}
> Candidates should have a thorough understanding of basic [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]] so that they can analyze [[Ratemaking Data Organization|data]], select appropriate techniques, and develop solutions to problems.
> 
> 1. Define and describe [[Exposure Base|exposure bases]] as used in the [[Ratemaking|ratemaking process]].
> 2. Evaluate and select an [[Exposure Base|exposure base]] in a given scenario for use in the [[Ratemaking|ratemaking process]] (e.g., [[Line of Business|line of business]], use cases).
> 3. Aggregate and/or organize [[Ratemaking Data Organization|ratemaking data]] in the following ways: [[Calendar Year|calendar year]], [[Policy Year|policy year]], [[Accident Year|accident year]], [[Report Year|report year]], and [[In-Force|in-force]].
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Close Year]] · [[Net of Reinsurance]] · [[Written Premium]] · [[Earned Premium]] · [[Unearned Premium]] · [[Earned Exposure]]
> 4. Evaluate [[Ratemaking Data Organization|ratemaking data]] and analyses for [[Data Issues|errors]], [[Data Quality|completeness]], and [[Reasonableness Testing|reasonableness]].
> 5. Separate [[Ratemaking Data Organization|data]] into [[Data Segmentation|groups]] that balance [[Homogeneity|homogeneity]] and [[Credibility|credibility]] and summarize the considerations for determining such [[Data Segmentation|groups]].
> 6. Calculate [[Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense|loss and loss adjustment expenses]] to be used for [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]] (e.g., [[Basic Insurance Ratios|common ratios]], adjustments to [[Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense|losses]], [[Claims Made Coverage|claims-made]] vs [[Occurrence Coverage|occurrence coverage]]).
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Frequency]] · [[Severity]] · [[Pure Premium]] · [[Loss Ratio]] · [[Loss Development]] · [[Loss Trend]]
> 7. Calculate various adjustments to the [[Insurance Premium|premium]] used for [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]] (e.g., [[On-Leveling|on-leveling]], [[Premium Audit|premium audit]]).
>    - *Key concepts:* [[On Level Premium]] · [[Parallelogram Method]] · [[Extension of Exposures]]
> 8. Calculate and apply [[Loss Trend|trends]] (e.g., [[Exposure Trend|exposure]], [[Premium Trend|premium]], [[Loss Trend|losses]]) using different approaches (e.g., [[Exponential Trend|exponential]], and [[Linear Trend|linear analyses]]).
> 9. Calculate the [[Expense Provisions|underwriting provisions]] underlying the [[Overall Rate Level Indication|overall rate level indication]] (e.g., [[Fixed Expenses|fixed]] and [[Variable Expenses|variable expenses]], [[Profit and Contingency Provision|profit and contingency]], [[Reinsurance|reinsurance costs]]).
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Expense Ratio]] · [[Underwriting Profit]] · [[Rate Change]]
> 10. Demonstrate the reasonable handling of [[Extraordinary Losses|events]] for [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]] purposes (e.g., [[Large Loss|large losses]], [[Catastrophe Loss|catastrophes]], Covid-19).
> 11. Construct an [[Overall Rate Level Indication|overall rate level indication]] using the [[Pure Premium Method|pure premium]] and [[Loss Ratio Method|loss ratio methods]].
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Permissible Loss Ratio]]
> 12. Apply [[Credibility|credibility]] to [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]] analyses using different methods and justify choice of [[Complement of Credibility|complement]].
> 13. Select and justify a [[Rate Change|final rate change]] to implement beyond the calculated [[Overall Rate Level Indication|overall rate level indication]] (e.g., [[Ratemaking Constraints|Operational/Marketing/Regulatory Constraints]], [[Lifetime Value]]).
> 14. Perform calculations related to alternative [[Ratemaking|ratemaking]] procedures (e.g., [[Classification Ratemaking|classification]], [[Territory Ratemaking|territory]], [[Deductible Rating|deductibles]], [[Increased Limits|increased limits]], [[Coinsurance Rating|coinsurance]], [[Commercial Lines Rating|commercial lines rating mechanisms]], etc.).
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Loss Elimination Ratio]] · [[Limited Expected Value]] · [[Coinsurance Percentage]] · [[Experience Rating]] · [[Schedule Rating]] · [[Retrospective Rating]] · [[Self-Insured Retention]] · [[Large Deductible Policy]]
> 15. Analyze results of [[Predictive Analytics|predictive models]] (e.g., [[Generalized Linear Model|GLM]]).
> 16. Apply the [[Principles of Ratemaking|four principles of ratemaking]] to a scenario.
> 17. Understand the [[Considerations for Implementing Rates|considerations for implementing rates]] to achieve an organization’s goals (e.g., [[Non-Pricing Solutions|non-pricing solutions]], [[Minimum Premium|minimum premium]], [[Rating Algorithm|rating algorithms]]).
> 18. Understand qualitative considerations for developing and filing [[Considerations for Implementing Rates|rate proposals]].
> 
> **Readings:**
> - Werner & Modlin
> - CAS Ratemaking Principles
> - ASOP 12
> - ASOP 13
> - ASOP 43

> [!example]- B. Estimating Claim Liabilities (Reserving) {45–55%}
> Candidates should understand how to estimate [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]] for both [[Insurer|insurance entities]] and also for [[Self-Insured Retention|non-insurance entities]] that retain [[Risk|risk]].
> 
> 1. Organize [[Reserving Data Organization|reserving data]] in the following ways: [[Calendar Year|calendar year]], [[Accident Year|accident year]], [[Policy Year|policy year]], [[Underwriting Year|underwriting year]], [[Report Year|report year]], etc.
> 2. Evaluate [[Reserving Data Organization|reserving data]] and analyses for [[Data Issues|errors]] and [[Reasonableness Testing|reasonableness]].
> 3. Describe the role of [[Homogeneity|homogeneity]] and [[Credibility|credibility]] of [[Reserving Data Organization|data]] in the process of estimating [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]].
> 4. Describe the fundamentals of different [[Types of Insurance|types of insurance]] (e.g., [[Long Tail Lines|long tail]] versus [[Short Tail Insurance|short tail]] lines, low [[Frequency|frequency]] versus [[Frequency|high frequency]] lines, [[Occurrence Coverage|occurrence]] versus [[Claims Made Coverage|claims made]]).
> 5. Articulate the importance of [[Unpaid Claims|accurate estimates]] of [[Unpaid Claims|unpaid claims]].
> 6. Build and analyze [[Development Triangle|development triangles]] (e.g., [[Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense|loss]], [[Claim Count Triangle|count]], [[Allocated Loss Adjustment Expense|allocated loss adjustment expenses (ALAE)]]).
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Paid Losses]] · [[Incurred Losses]] · [[Case Reserves]] · [[Age to Age Factor]] · [[Cumulative Development Factor]]
> 7. Apply a [[Tail Factor|tail factor]].
> 8. Use [[Development Triangle|development triangles]] as [[Data Diagnostic Analysis|diagnostic tools]] to identify changes and [[Loss Development|trends]] (e.g., [[Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense|loss]] and [[Claim Count Triangle|claim count]], [[Loss Ratio|ratio of losses to premium]], [[Severity|severity]], ratios of [[Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense|loss]] and [[Claim Count Triangle|claim counts]]).
>    - *Key concepts:* [[Severity Analysis]]
> 9. Calculate and evaluate [[Loss Reserving|unpaid loss estimation techniques]] (e.g., [[Chain Ladder Method|development/chain ladder]], [[Case Outstanding Development Method|case outstanding development]], [[Expected Loss Method|expected losses]], [[Bornhuetter-Ferguson Method|Bornhuetter-Ferguson]], [[Cape Cod Method|Cape Cod]], [[Frequency-Severity Method|frequency-severity]], [[Berquist-Sherman Method|Berquist-Sherman]], [[Benktander Method|Benktander]]).
>    - *Key concepts:* [[IBNR]] · [[Ultimate Loss]]
> 10. Assess the influence of [[Changing Conditions|operating changes]] on the [[Loss Reserving|estimation of unpaid losses]] (e.g., [[Claims Coding Changes|claims coding]] and/or [[Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense|claim-related expenses]], [[Claims Processing Changes|claims processing]], [[Underwriting Changes|underwriting]] and [[Policy Provision Changes|policy provisions]], [[Changing Conditions|marketing]], [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]], treatment of [[Recoveries|recoveries]] such as [[Deductible Recovery|deductibles]] and [[Salvage and Subrogation|salvage and subrogation]]).
> 11. Adjust [[Reserving Data Organization|data]] and/or estimation techniques for changes in the [[Changing Conditions|internal and external environment]] (e.g., [[Claims Processing Changes|claims processes]] that result in shift in the [[Case Adequacy|adequacy of case outstanding]] or shift in [[Settlement Rate|settlement rates]], change in [[Mix of Business|mix of business]], change in [[Rate Level Change|rate level]], [[Inflation|inflationary]] or [[Tort Litigation|legal environment]]).
> 12. Consider the impact of and adjust for the presence of [[Large Loss|large losses]] in a reserving analysis.
> 13. Calculate and evaluate the estimation techniques for [[Recoveries|recoveries]] (e.g., [[Salvage and Subrogation|salvage and subrogation]], [[Reinsurance Recovery|reinsurance]]).
> 14. Calculate and evaluate the estimation techniques for [[Allocated Loss Adjustment Expense|allocated loss adjustment expenses]].
> 15. Calculate and evaluate the estimation techniques for [[Unallocated Loss Adjustment Expenses ULAE|unallocated loss adjustment expenses]].
> 16. Evaluate the results of a [[Claim Reserve|reserve]] analysis for [[Reserve Adequacy|adequacy and reasonableness]] using [[Loss Ratio|loss ratios]], [[Severity|severities]], [[Pure Premium Analysis|pure premiums]], [[Frequency|frequencies]], [[Unpaid Claims|indicated unpaid losses]], etc.
> 17. Monitor results for [[Reserve Adequacy|adequacy]] and [[Reasonableness Testing|reasonability]] including [[Roll Forward Analysis|interim valuations]] (e.g., [[Actual vs Expected Analysis|actual versus expected]], [[Roll Forward Analysis|roll forward analysis]]).
> 18. [[Reserve Communication|Communicate results and drivers of change]] to various [[Stakeholder Reporting|stakeholders]] ([[Stakeholder Reporting|internal management]], [[Stakeholder Reporting|investors]], [[Regulatory Reporting|regulators]]).
> 19. Define and apply [[Reinsurance|reinsurance]] concepts to calculate [[Net Losses|net]], [[Ceded Losses|ceded]], and [[Gross Losses|gross losses]].
> 20. Utilize [[External Information in Reserving|external information]] in a [[Claim Reserve|reserve]] analysis (e.g., [[Inflation|inflation]], [[Tort Reform|tort reform]], [[Tort Litigation|attorney involvement]]).
> 
> **Readings:**
> - Friedland
> - ASOP 43
