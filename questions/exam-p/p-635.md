Let $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ be mutually independent exponential random variables with means $\alpha$, $\beta$, and 4 respectively.

Assume the following:

i) $U = X + Y + Z$
ii) $V = X - Y$
iii) $E(U) = \text{Var}(V)$
iv) $E(U) - E(V) = \dfrac{\text{Var}(U) - \text{Var}(V)}{2}$

Calculate $\alpha$.

- A) $0.5$
- B) $1.0$
- C) $1.5$
- D) $2.0$
- E) $2.5$

## Explanation

An exponential with mean $\mu$ has variance $\mu^2$. Means add, and for independent variables variances add regardless of the sign of the coefficient, so from (i) and (ii):

$$
\begin{align*}
E(U) &= \alpha + \beta + 4 & \text{Var}(U) &= \alpha^2 + \beta^2 + 16 \\
E(V) &= \alpha - \beta     & \text{Var}(V) &= \alpha^2 + \beta^2
\end{align*}
$$

Note $\text{Var}(V)$ *adds* the two variances even though $V$ is a difference — that is what makes (iv) tractable.

Condition (iii) says

$$
\alpha + \beta + 4 = \alpha^2 + \beta^2
$$

Condition (iv) becomes

$$
\begin{align*}
(\alpha + \beta + 4) - (\alpha - \beta) &= \frac{(\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + 16) - (\alpha^2 + \beta^2)}{2} \\
2\beta + 4 &= 8 \\
\beta &= 2
\end{align*}
$$

Substituting into (iii):

$$
\begin{align*}
\alpha + 2 + 4 &= \alpha^2 + 4 \\
\alpha^2 - \alpha - 2 &= 0 \\
(\alpha - 2)(\alpha + 1) &= 0
\end{align*}
$$

A mean of an exponential must be positive, so $\alpha = \mathbf{2.0}$.

The root $\alpha = -1$ is the trap in miniature: the algebra is indifferent to it, but no exponential distribution has a negative mean. Checking $\alpha = \beta = 2$ back through the conditions confirms the answer — $E(U) = 8 = \text{Var}(V)$, and $E(U) - E(V) = 8 = (24 - 8)/2$.
