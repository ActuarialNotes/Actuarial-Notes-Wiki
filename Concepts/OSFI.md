The **Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI)** is Canada's federal prudential regulator. It supervises federally regulated insurers, banks and pension plans, sets capital requirements through the [[MCT]] guideline, and issues the guidelines governing the [[ORSA]], the [[FCT]], [[Corporate Governance|corporate governance]], reinsurance and the [[Appointed Actuary|appointed actuary]].

OSFI regulates solvency, not [[Rate Regulation|rates]] or [[Market Conduct Regulation|market conduct]]. It does not approve premium rates, adjudicate consumer complaints or licence agents, which are provincial matters.

OSFI shares information with provincial regulators through the [[Canadian Council of Insurance Regulators]], and with [[PACICC]], which handles the aftermath of a failure.

## Mandate

OSFI's statutory objectives are to protect the rights and interests of policyholders and creditors while allowing institutions to compete and take reasonable risks. Its mandate does not include preventing all failures, because a zero-failure regime would be achieved only by prohibiting risk-taking.

## Supervisory framework

OSFI rates each institution's inherent risk and quality of risk management by significant activity, arriving at a composite risk rating and an intervention stage. The stages run from 0 (normal) and 1 (early warning) through to 4 (non-viability). Escalation brings reporting requirements, then business restrictions, and eventually control of assets.

OSFI's tools are:

- Guidelines: the A series on capital, the B series on prudential matters and the E series on corporate governance.
- The [[Canadian Annual Return|Annual and Quarterly Returns]].
- On-site examinations and supervisory letters.
- Capital add-ons and increases to internal targets.
- The power to take control of an insurer.

OSFI has extended prudential supervision into [[Climate Risk|climate risk]] (Guideline B-15 and standardised climate scenario exercises), [[Concentration Risk|concentration risk]] and [[Model Risk|model risk]], on the view that these are solvency issues rather than social policy.

> [!example]- Would OSFI intervene with this insurer? {Example}
> A federally regulated insurer reports an [[MCT]] ratio of $142\%$. Its supervisory target is $150\%$ and its internal target is $180\%$. Its capital plan projects $138\%$ at the next quarter.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - The ratio is below the $150\%$ supervisory target but above the $100\%$ minimum, so the insurer is not at the point of non-viability.
> > - Below the supervisory target, it is in intervention territory, with heightened supervision and likely Stage 1 or 2.
> > - Expect OSFI to require a written capital restoration plan with dates, restrict dividends and other capital distributions, increase reporting frequency and challenge the assumptions in the projection.
> > - The projected decline to $138\%$ makes it worse: a ratio below target and falling is a different conversation from one below target and recovering.
> > - The insurer's own [[ORSA]] should already have set an internal target that gave early warning. An internal target that gave none is itself a supervisory finding.
> > - Yes, OSFI would intervene.
