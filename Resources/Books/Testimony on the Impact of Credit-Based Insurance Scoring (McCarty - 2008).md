Testimony given on May 21, 2008 before the Subcommittee on Oversight and Investigations of the House Committee on Financial Services by Kevin M. McCarty, Florida Insurance Commissioner, who also appeared as chair of the Property & Casualty Committee of the National Association of Insurance Commissioners. It covers the role of credit-based insurance scores in personal lines in Florida, his concerns about the 2007 FTC Report, and the actions of other states.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam 6U (CAS)|Exam 6U]] — objective A3; the testimony, excluding Appendices 1 and 2.

## Opening Statement
- Empirical studies, including the 2007 Federal Trade Commission Report, indicate that credit-based insurance scores, while accurate predictors of claims activity, disparately impact certain classes of people.
- The testimony shares the State of Florida's actions and the role of credit-based insurance scores in Florida today, gives the commissioner's concerns about the 2007 FTC Report, and reports on the actions of other states.

## The Use of Credit-Based Insurance Scores in Personal Insurance Lines
- Proponents argue that [[Credit-Based Insurance Scoring|credit-based insurance scores]] are predictive of an insured's future claims experience; critics argue that their use is another example of discrimination against lower income individuals and protected classes.
- Studies show that credit scores can be predictors of future claim activity, but also that their use disparately impacts certain classes of people.

## Other Rating Factors Considered to Be Inappropriate
- Examples are the historical use of race in rating life insurance and genetic testing in health insurance, which Congress outlawed through HIPAA in 1996; legislators and regulators must weigh the benefits of simplistic claims prediction against sound public policy.
- In February 2007 the commissioner held a public hearing on the use of occupation and education as underwriting or rating factors for private passenger auto insurance, to ask whether they act as a proxy for race.

## The Credit Reporting System
- A 2000 study by Consumer Reports found that 50% of credit reports contained errors, and credit reports disproportionately affect recent divorcees, recently naturalized citizens, the elderly, the disabled, those with certain religious convictions, and younger individuals.
- Empirical studies show no significant difference in the magnitude of claims, only in the frequency of claims filed.
- The methodology used to create credit-based insurance scores is opaque to consumers and varies from company to company.

## Disproportionate Impact of Credit-Based Insurance Scores
- The FTC Report's analysis clearly demonstrates statistically significant correlations between credit scoring and race/ethnicity; a 2004 Texas Insurance Department report showed African-Americans' average credit score 10–35% below that of Caucasians and Hispanics' roughly 5–25% worse.
- Credit-based insurance scores also have a negative impact on young people, the elderly and certain religious groups.

## Florida Actions Regarding Credit-Based Insurance Scores
- The 2003 Florida Legislature enacted legislation (626.9741, F.S.) limiting the use of credit-based scores in private automobile and personal residential insurance, modeled after the NCOIL Model Law but with stronger consumer protections.

## Conclusion and 2007 FTC Report
- The commissioner's opinion is that the negative impact on classes of people based on race, age and religion outweighs any suggested enhanced accuracy in pricing and underwriting, although the broader regulatory community has differing views.
- His concerns about the FTC Report include data supplied by a handful of firms, no premium data, a one-sided narrative, and a process that gave industry trade associations advance copies of the report.

## State Involvement
- Forty-eight states have taken some form of legislative or regulatory action limiting the use of credit scoring in insurance, and four states have effectively banned the use of credit history in underwriting or rating for automobile insurance.
- Federal action should not preempt or diminish consumer protection efforts already enacted by state legislatures; the testimony comments on H.R. 5633 and H.R. 6062.
