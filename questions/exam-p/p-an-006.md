A policyholder has an auto policy and a home policy with the same insurer. Let $X$ be the number of auto claims and $Y$ the number of home claims the policyholder files in a year. Their joint probability function is:

| | $Y = 0$ | $Y = 1$ | $Y = 2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $X = 0$ | 0.30 | 0.10 | 0.05 |
| $X = 1$ | 0.15 | 0.15 | 0.05 |
| $X = 2$ | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.05 |

Calculate the expected number of home claims given that the policyholder files at least one auto claim.

- A) 0.45
- B) 0.65
- C) 0.71
- D) 0.82
- E) 1.00

## Explanation

Condition on the event $X \ge 1$, the bottom two rows of the table:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(X \ge 1) &= 0.35 + 0.20 \\
           &= 0.55
\end{align*}
$$

Sum $y\,p(x, y)$ over those rows, then divide by the probability of the condition:

$$
\begin{align*}
\sum_{x \ge 1} \sum_y y\,p(x, y) &= 1(0.15) + 2(0.05) + 1(0.10) + 2(0.05) \\
                                &= 0.45 \\[4pt]
E[Y \mid X \ge 1] &= \frac{0.45}{0.55} \\
                  &= \mathbf{0.818}
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **D**. It is above the unconditional mean $E[Y] = 0.65$: in this table, auto and home claims tend to occur together.

**Why not the others**

- **A** (0.45) is the sum over the two rows without dividing by $P(X \ge 1)$, so the "conditional" probabilities don't add to 1.
- **B** (0.65) is the marginal mean $E[Y]$, which ignores the condition.
- **C** (0.71) conditions on $X = 1$ only, 0.25/0.35, dropping the $X = 2$ row.
- **E** (1.00) conditions on $X = 2$ only, 0.20/0.20.
