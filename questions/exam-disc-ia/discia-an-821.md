A customer is hurt by a falling shelf in a store. The store's CGL has a \$500,000 each occurrence limit and a \$10,000 Medical Expense limit, with ample aggregate. The insurer first pays \$10,000 of her medical expenses under Coverage C. She then sues and is awarded \$520,000 of damages under Coverage A. How much more does the insurer pay on the award?

- A) \$490,000
- B) \$500,000
- C) \$510,000
- D) \$520,000

## Explanation

The each occurrence limit is the most paid for the sum of Coverage A damages **and Coverage C medical expenses** because of all bodily injury arising out of one occurrence. The medical payment has already used \$10,000 of it.

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Paid}_A &= \min(520{,}000,\ 500{,}000 - 10{,}000) \\
&= 490{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

The answer is **A**.

**Why not the others**

- **B** treats the each occurrence limit as applying to Coverage A alone.
- **C** adds the medical expense limit on top of the each occurrence limit.
- **D** ignores the each occurrence limit.
