A 20-year bond priced to have an annual effective yield of 10% has a Macaulay duration of 11. Immediately after the bond is priced, the market yield rate increases by 0.25%. The bond's approximate percentage price change, using a first-order Macaulay approximation, is $X$.

Calculate $X$.

- A) $-2.22\%$
- B) $-2.47\%$
- C) $-2.50\%$
- D) $-2.62\%$
- E) $-2.75\%$

## Explanation

The first-order Macaulay approximation estimates the price at a new yield $i$ from the price and Macaulay duration at the current yield $i_0$:

$$P(i) \approx P(i_0)\left(\frac{1+i_0}{1+i}\right)^{D_{MAC}(i_0)}$$

Here $i_0 = 0.10$, $i = 0.10 + 0.0025 = 0.1025$ and $D_{MAC} = 11$:

$$P(0.1025) \approx P(0.10)\left(\frac{1.10}{1.1025}\right)^{11} = 0.97534\,P(0.10)$$

The approximate percentage price change is

$$X = 100(0.97534 - 1) = -2.47\%$$

The first-order *modified* approximation would give a different answer: with $D_{MOD} = 11/1.10 = 10$, $\Delta P/P \approx -10(0.0025) = -2.50\%$, which is option C. The question asks for the Macaulay approximation, so the answer is B.
