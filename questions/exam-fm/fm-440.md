David purchases a level perpetuity-due, which provides annual payments of 1 and is priced to yield an annual effective rate of 6.25%.

Immediately before the second payment, the annual effective yield rate is 5.75%, and the price is estimated to be $X$ using the first-order modified approximation. The actual price of the perpetuity at this time is $Y$.

Calculate $\dfrac{X-Y}{Y}$.

- A) $-0.76\%$
- B) $-0.64\%$
- C) $-0.61\%$
- D) $-0.32\%$
- E) $-0.17\%$

## Explanation

The present value of the perpetuity-due, at annual effective interest rate $i$, is given by

$$P(i) = \frac{1+i}{i}$$

Immediately before the second payment the remaining payments are again a perpetuity-due of 1 a year, so at the original rate $i_0 = 0.0625$ its price is

$$
\begin{align*}
P(i_0) &= \frac{1.0625}{0.0625} \\
       &= 17
\end{align*}
$$

The derivative is $P'(i) = -\dfrac{1}{i^{2}}$, so the modified duration is

$$
\begin{align*}
D_{MOD} &= \frac{-P'(i)}{P(i)} \\
        &= \frac{1/i^{2}}{(1+i)/i} \\
        &= \frac{1}{i(1+i)} \\
        &= \frac{1}{0.0625(1 + 0.0625)} \\
        &= 15.058824
\end{align*}
$$

The first-order modified approximation of the present value, at an interest rate $i$ near an initial interest rate $i_0$, is $P(i_0)\left[1 - (i - i_0)D_{MOD}(i_0)\right]$. Here $i - i_0 = 0.0575 - 0.0625 = -0.005$, so

$$
\begin{align*}
X &= 17\left[1 - (-0.005)(15.058824)\right] \\
  &= 17(1.075294) \\
  &= 18.28
\end{align*}
$$

The actual new price is

$$
\begin{align*}
Y &= \frac{1.0575}{0.0575} \\
  &= 18.391304
\end{align*}
$$

Then

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{X - Y}{Y} &= \frac{18.28 - 18.391304}{18.391304} \\
                &= -0.006052 \\
                &\approx -0.61\%
\end{align*}
$$
