Two independent random samples of sizes 10 and 12 are taken from two separate normal distributions with unknown variances $\sigma_1^2$ and $\sigma_2^2$, respectively.

- $S_1^2$ and $S_2^2$ are the unbiased sample variances
- $S_1^2 = 4$
- You want to perform the following hypothesis test:
  - $H_0\colon 2\sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$
  - $H_1\colon 2\sigma_1^2 < \sigma_2^2$
- The p-value of this test is 1.0%

Calculate $S_2^2$.

- A) Less than 10
- B) At least 10, but less than 20
- C) At least 20, but less than 30
- D) At least 30, but less than 40
- E) At least 40

## Explanation

Comparing two normal variances uses the F distribution. For independent samples of sizes 10 and 12,

$$
\frac{S_1^2/\sigma_1^2}{S_2^2/\sigma_2^2} \sim F(9, 11).
$$

Under $H_0$, $\sigma_2^2 = 2\sigma_1^2$, so the unknown variances cancel and the reciprocal statistic is

$$
\frac{S_2^2/\sigma_2^2}{S_1^2/\sigma_1^2} = \frac{S_2^2}{2S_1^2} \sim F(11, 9).
$$

The alternative $2\sigma_1^2 < \sigma_2^2$ makes $S_2^2$ large relative to $2S_1^2$, so large values of this statistic are evidence against $H_0$ and the p-value is an upper tail:

$$
\Pr\left(F_{11,9} \ge \frac{S_2^2}{2(4)}\right) = 0.01
$$

The 99th percentile of $F(11, 9)$ is 5.178, so

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{S_2^2}{8} &= 5.178 \\
S_2^2 &= 8(5.178) \\
&= 41.42
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[40, \infty)$, so the answer is **E**.
