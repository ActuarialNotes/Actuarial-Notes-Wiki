Joe wants to use a Linear Mixed Model to determine if the use of any fertilizer results in a significantly different apple weight. After recording his observations for ten trees within the orchard, he has a dataset with the following form:

| Tree ID | Treatment | Apple Count | Trunk Diameter (cm) | Apple ID | Apple Weight (g) | Red Apple | Green Apple |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------------------|----------|------------------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Control | 20 | 14 | 1 | 83 | Y | N |
| 1 | Control | 20 | 14 | 2 | 65 | Y | N |
| 1 | Control | 20 | 14 | 3 | 76 | Y | N |
| 1 | Control | 20 | 14 | 4 | 69 | N | Y |
| 1 | Control | 20 | 14 | 5 | 87 | N | Y |
| … | … | … | … | … | … | … | … |
| 10 | Brand K | 18 | 18 | 178 | 94 | Y | N |
| 10 | Brand K | 18 | 18 | 179 | 102 | Y | N |

He has provided some notes to accompany his dataset:

- *TREE ID* and *APPLE ID* are both identifiers of the observation and therefore not considered to be covariates.
- The trees were either left untreated or treated with one of two brands (J or K).
- *APPLE COUNT* is the number of apples on a given tree.
- The apple can be only one of two colors: red or green.

You are given:

I. The apple dataset is an example of a two-level clustered dataset.
II. Including the Control Treatment in the intercept estimate and modeling Brand K and Brand J effect as the difference between the Control and the effect for Brand K or the difference between the intercept and Brand J will result in intrinsic aliasing.
III. If $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ represent the coefficients of the fixed effects associated with treatment, *TREAT_J* and *TREAT_K*, respectively, then the null and alternative hypotheses are as follows:

    Null Hypothesis: $H_0: \beta_1 = 0$ and $\beta_2 = 0$

    Alternative Hypothesis: $H_1: \beta_1 \neq 0$ or $\beta_2 \neq 0$

Determine which of the preceding statements are true.

- A) I only
- B) II only
- C) III only
- D) I, II and III
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

**I is true.** Apples (level 1) are nested within trees (level 2), with the tree-level covariates — treatment, apple count, trunk diameter — constant within a cluster. That is the definition of a two-level clustered dataset.

**II is false.** Folding the Control level into the intercept and coding Brand J and Brand K as *differences from Control* is the reference-level parameterization that **avoids** intrinsic aliasing. Intrinsic aliasing arises when an intercept is fitted alongside a dummy for *every* level of the factor, so the columns of the design matrix are linearly dependent. Here one level is dropped, so the design matrix is full rank.

**III is true.** "Does any fertilizer change apple weight?" is the joint test that neither treatment coefficient differs from zero, against the alternative that at least one does — exactly the stated hypotheses.

Statements I and III are true, which is not one of options (A) through (D), so the answer is **E**.
