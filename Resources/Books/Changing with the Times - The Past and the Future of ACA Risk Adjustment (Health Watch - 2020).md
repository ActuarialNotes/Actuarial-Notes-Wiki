An article in the June 2020 issue of the Society of Actuaries Health Section's Health Watch on the evolution of ACA risk adjustment. It tracks the program over time, including an assessment of volatility, drivers of performance and trends, to give issuers techniques for understanding year-over-year changes and lessening uncertainty. Most uncertainty in risk adjustment outcomes comes from two sources: market shifts and model changes.

> [!info] On the syllabus
> - [[Exam GH-301 (SOA)|Exam GH-301]] — Risk Adjustment, outcome 3a; the whole article.

## Introduction
- Issuers moved from medical underwriting to a synthesis of guaranteed issue, modified community rating and risk adjustment, which promised to address anticipated marketwide instability.
- Market shifts are the interactions issuers face every day; model changes originate from the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) as it institutes enhancements.
- Actual risk transfers are not known for years after rates are set.

## A Diverse History
- Risk adjustment's objectives have not changed since the program began, but the model underlying the risk score calculation has.
- Notable Risk Adjustment Changes
    - Annual: coefficient recalibrations to reflect more recent data.
    - 2015: transition to a model assigning hierarchical condition categories (HCCs) through ICD-10 codes.
    - 2017: addition of duration factors reflecting the length of a member's enrollment with an issuer.
    - 2018: addition of prescription drug classes (RxCs); reduction of the statewide average premium by 14 percent to proxy issuer administrative costs and a paid claims basis for transfers; addition of a high-cost risk pool (HCRP) for members with annual paid claims over $1 million; first adjustments from prior year risk adjustment data validation (RADV) audits.
    - 2019: begin phase-in of External Data Gathering Environment (EDGE) data in coefficient calibration.
    - 2021: updated condition categories calibrated from data with ICD-10 codes.

## Model Impacts
- Risk scores change every year with migrations into and out of ACA-compliant plans, morbidity movements, coding practices and updates to the HHS hierarchical condition category (HHS-HCC) model; model changes are known with reasonable certainty in advance.
- Figure 1 (components of the risk score) shows the "condition" component, HCC plus RxC, as an increasing proportion of the total, and composite risk scores shrinking.
- Figure 2 shows the range of year-over-year issuer-level risk score changes due solely to model updates.
- Figure 3 shows annual coefficient changes against risk score contribution.

## Market Impacts
- Figure 4 shows the range of state risk transfers as a percentage of total market premium; risk adjustment remains a considerable portion of ACA premium.
- Figure 5 shows issuer-level risk adjustment directionality; as many as 30 percent of renewing issuers reverse position from the prior year.
- Figure 6 shows the relationship between risk adjustment and enrollment; transfers can represent a much larger portion of premium for smaller issuers.

## An Unknown Future
- Potential areas of risk adjustment model improvement expressed by HHS or others: further developing coefficients with EDGE data, changing HCC/RxC values and categorizations, refreshing the CSR-induced utilization factors, introducing a nonlinear model to the calibration, reflecting additional factors in the transfer calculation, incorporating other factors with predictive power such as social determinants of health, updating governance procedures, and enhancing risk adjustment data validation.
- Risk adjustment in 2021, as finalized in the 2021 HHS payment notice released May 7, 2020, will likely use solely EDGE data from 2016 through 2018 ACA-compliant plans.
