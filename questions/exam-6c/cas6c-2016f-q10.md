Each of the Facility Association risk-sharing pools operates in one specific jurisdiction and member companies operating in the jurisdiction can transfer risk into that pool.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Describe how the financial results of a particular pool are shared among member companies.

### Explanation
They are shared based on their voluntary share of exposure in the jurisdiction. The insurer is allocated premium and losses from the pool based on this proportion even if they did not cede risks to the pool.

Alternatively:

They are shared based on a participation ratio that is based on the voluntary PPA non-fleet non-pool direct exposure.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to provide a discussion regarding the participation ratio of the pool and how it is calculated.
A common mistake was incorrectly stating that the results of the pool were shared based on premium instead of exposures.

## Part b (1.25 points)

Identify five requirements that a risk must meet in order to be eligible for transfer to a pool.

### Explanation
- The risks must be a private passenger automobile
- The risk must not be a residual market risk
- The risk must have the minimum third party liability coverage
- The risk must be written based on the classification of the insurer
- The risk must be written based on appropriate premium of insurer without modification

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to identify the five requirements to be eligible to transfer risk to the pool.
A common mistake was providing fewer than five requirements.

## Part c (1 point)

Describe how the pool operates with respect to:

- i. Actual transfer of premiums from a member company to the pool
- ii. Premium reimbursement from the pool to a member company

### Explanation
**i. Actual transfer of premiums from a member company to the pool**

At the end of each month, the RSP produce reports that tell the insurance company how much premium was transferred to the pool. Also, the transfer is automatic for the insurer, so that at the end of each month the insurer must pay or receive the difference based on the result of the pool and what they have ceded.

**ii. Premium reimbursement from the pool to a member company**

The pool reimburses the insurer a premium commission, so that the insurer can pay for their acquisition expenses. The pool does not pay any of the expense so the pool transfers back a percentage of the insurer's premium.

Alternatively:

- i. The insured risk must meet the basic requirements for the pool but also meet applicable limit. Some pools limit the percentage of premium that can be ceded to the pool to avoid the pool being used as a marketing tool.
- ii. The pool gives an expense allowance to the insured based on their ceded premium to the pool to cover for the cost of acquisition and claims handling.

Or:

- i. Member transfers the premium excluding fees like commissions/agents, and are subject to a limit depending on the province. The premium ceded must be the approved premium.
- ii. The premium returned is based on the participation ratio calculated, and in addition the pool reimburses an expense fee for the cost of writing and handling the policy.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of the expense allowance provided by the RSP to cover the cost of acquisition, operations, loss adjustment costs, etc.

Criterion (i) could be answered based on the FA reading or based on the candidate's general knowledge of the risk sharing pool mechanism.

Common mistakes included:

- Overly brief description
- Repeating concepts from part (a) without additional explanation

Criterion (ii) was more challenging. The question was specifically looking for knowledge regarding premium reimbursements to the member company.

A common mistake was discussing how the participation ratio is used to calculate the net amount returned to the member company after accounting for their share of the pooled losses.

## Part d (0.25 points)

Briefly describe how the Ontario pool differs from the other pools.

### Explanation
The Ontario pool differs from the other pool in that the insurer needs to retain 15% of the risk (premium and loss) and only transfer 85% to the RSP.

Alternatively:

In determining the participation ratio, ceded exposures are also considered.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to briefly describe one difference.
A common mistake was stating that the pool has a limit to the amount that can be transferred to the pool, as this is commonly done in other pools.
