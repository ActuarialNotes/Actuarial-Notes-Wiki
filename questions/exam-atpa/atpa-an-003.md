An insurer's claims app records the phone's location and photos of the damage when a policyholder reports a claim. Policyholders agreed to this collection in order to have their claims handled. The pricing team now proposes to use the full location history from the app as a rating variable at renewal, and legal counsel confirms that the use is permitted by law.

Which course of action is most consistent with data ethics principles?

- A) Proceed, because counsel has confirmed the use is lawful and the law sets the standard.
- B) Proceed after removing every protected characteristic from the data set.
- C) Abandon the proposal, because claims data may never be used for any purpose but claims.
- D) Tell policyholders about the new use, seek their agreement, and keep only the location fields the variable needs.
- E) Proceed, because policyholders consented to the data collection when they filed their claims.

## Explanation

Consent is tied to a purpose. Agreement to collection for claims handling does not automatically cover pricing, so the insurer should tell policyholders what will be used and for what, and seek their agreement. The privacy principle also limits the data to what the stated purpose needs, so a full location history is cut down to the fields the rating variable actually uses. The answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** treats the law as the whole test. Law sets the minimum, and a use can be legal and still violate consent or transparency.
- **B** addresses fairness but not consent or data minimisation, and removing protected characteristics does not stop a variable such as location from acting as a proxy for them.
- **C** overstates the principle. Reuse is possible when people know about it and agree to it.
- **E** assumes consent given for one purpose carries over to another. Consent for claims handling does not automatically cover pricing.
