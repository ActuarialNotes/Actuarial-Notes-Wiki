An annual perpetuity pays 1 one year from now. Payments will then increase by 4% per year for 5 years and 8% per year thereafter.

Calculate the present value of this perpetuity at an annual effective interest rate of 12%.

- A) 21.13
- B) 23.51
- C) 25.95
- D) 28.87
- E) 31.23

## Explanation

The payment at time $t$ is $1.04^{t-1}$ for $t = 1, \dots, 6$ (five 4% increases), and from then on each payment is 8% larger than the one before: $1.04^5(1.08)$ at time 7, $1.04^5(1.08)^2$ at time 8, and so on.

Split the payments after time 5, as in SOA's solution.

**Payments 1–5** ($1, 1.04, \dots, 1.04^4$) form a 5-payment geometric annuity-immediate growing at 4%:

$$PV_1 = \frac{1-\left(\frac{1.04}{1.12}\right)^5}{0.12-0.04}$$

**Payments 6 onward** start at $1.04^5$ at time 6 and grow 8% a year forever. At time 5 they are a geometric perpetuity-immediate worth $\dfrac{1.04^5}{0.12-0.08}$; discounting 5 years at 12%:

$$PV_2 = \frac{1.04^5}{1.12^5}\cdot\frac{1}{0.12-0.08} = \left(\frac{1.04}{1.12}\right)^5\frac{1}{0.12-0.08}$$

With $(1.04/1.12)^5 = 0.690362$:

$$
\begin{align*}
PV_1 &= \frac{1-0.690362}{0.08} \\
     &= 3.87 \\
PV_2 &= \frac{0.690362}{0.04} \\
     &= 17.26 \\
PV &= 3.87 + 17.26 \\
   &= 21.13
\end{align*}
$$
