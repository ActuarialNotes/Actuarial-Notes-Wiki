The values in the table below were empirically estimated.

| Security | Expected Return | Beta |
|---|---|---|
| Stock A | 6% | 0.5 |
| Stock B | 10% | 1.5 |

- The expected market return is 8%.
- The risk-free rate is 2%.

## Part a (1.5 points)

On a single graph, draw and label:

- i. The Security Market Line (SML) implied by the expected market return.
- ii. The empirically estimated SML.
- iii. The risk-return point for both securities listed above.

### Explanation
**SML implied by the expected market return.** It passes through the risk-free rate at $\beta = 0$ and the market at $\beta = 1$:

$$E(r_i) = r_f + \beta_i\,[E(r_M) - r_f], \qquad \text{slope} = E(r_M) - r_f = .08 - .02 = .06$$

so $\text{SML} = .02 + .06\beta$. The CAPM returns for the two stocks lie on it:

$$E(r_A) = .02 + .5(.08 - .02) = .05 \qquad E(r_B) = .02 + 1.5(.08 - .02) = .11$$

**Empirically estimated SML.** It passes through the two estimated points, A at $(0.5,\ 6\%)$ and B at $(1.5,\ 10\%)$:

$$\text{slope} = \frac{.1 - .06}{1.5 - .05} = .04$$

(denominator as printed), so $\text{SML} = .04 + .04\beta$.

![Sample answer: E(r) in percent against β from 0 to 1.6. Solid line 1), the SML implied by the expected market return, rises from 2% at β = 0 through 8% at β = 1 to 12% at about β = 1.6. Dotted line 2), the empirically estimated SML, rises from 4% at β = 0 through A at (0.5, 6%), 8% at β = 1 and B at (1.5, 10%). The two lines cross at β = 1, with A above the implied SML and B below it.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas9-2014-q3-sml-sample-answer.png)

Alternatively: plot the expected risk premium $E[r_i] - r_f$ against beta. The SML from the expected market then has intercept 0 and slope .06, $E[R_i] = \beta(.06)$. For the empirical line, write $r_i - r_f = \gamma_0 + \beta\gamma_1$ through A $(.5,\ .04)$ and B $(1.5,\ .08)$:

$$.04 = \gamma_0 + .5\gamma_1 \qquad .08 = \gamma_0 + 1.5\gamma_1$$

which gives $.04 = 1\gamma_1$, so $\gamma_1 = .04$, and $.04 = \gamma_0 + .5(.04)$, so $\gamma_0 = .02$: $E[R_i] = .02 + \beta(.04)$ — intercept .02, slope .04.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates expected to know how to graph the SML implied by the expected market return (line with intercept at risk free rate and going through market rate); also be able to graph empirical SML going through points A and B;
- To receive full credit, candidate should have graphed and labelled both the implied SML and the empirical SML, graph and label points A & B, and label the axes
- Common errors – not graphing the empirical SML, not labelling something (lines, points or axes)
- For the labelling, we accepted both giving the point coordinates for A & B or labeling the numbers on the axis so that it was clear what the A & B coordinates were

## Part b (0.5 points)

Explain whether Stock A or Stock B is a better buy according to the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM).

### Explanation
Stock A has a positive $\alpha$ and Stock B a negative one:

$$\alpha_A = 6\% - [2\% + 0.5(8\% - 2\%)] = 1\%$$

$$\alpha_B = 10\% - [2\% + 1.5(8\% - 2\%)] = -1\%$$

Therefore, Stock A is a better buy based on CAPM.

Alternatively: Stock A is a better buy according to CAPM since it is above the SML (B is below the SML).

### Examiner Report
- Candidates expected to be able to assess how stocks are performing with respect to CAPM expectations
- To receive full credit, candidate was expected to identify which stock is a better buy and explain why
- Common error – using positive alpha as the explanation but not identifying what alpha is;
candidates could receive full credit without mentioning alpha (eg explaining that A is better because it lies above the implied SML), but if positive alpha was used as the only explanation, it needed to be defined.  We accepted both explicit calculation of alpha and also graphing and labelling it as definition.
