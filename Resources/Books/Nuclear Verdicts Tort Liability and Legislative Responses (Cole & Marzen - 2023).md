A Journal of Insurance Regulation article (2023) that brings together insurance and legal studies literature on tort reform and nuclear verdicts, large awards such as punitive damages in the hundreds of millions of dollars. It focuses on the effects of nuclear verdicts on the insurance industry and the actions being taken to mitigate them, including legal strategies and state legislative activity.

## Abstract
- Importance: the number and size of punitive damage awards has grown considerably since the 1980s; in 2021 there were 24 jury verdicts in excess of $100 million, totaling $309 billion, which can reduce funds for safety and mitigation, discourage innovation, raise insurance and claims costs, lead to bankruptcy and reduce the capacity of the global insurance market.
- Findings: reasons nuclear verdicts are becoming more common include the facts of the case, the venue where cases are heard and the plaintiff attorney's use of "reptile theory" tactics at trial; companies can mitigate losses with a focus on safety, and attorneys can use defense strategies and appeals.
- Conclusion: given the scale of the issue, state and federal government intervention is likely to be needed; some states have already acted to limit damage awards, raise the standard of proof required for awards and allow bifurcated trials.

## The Impact of Nuclear Verdicts
- Nuclear verdicts can directly affect the cost and availability of insurance: annual insurance payments in commercial trucking increased by 22.5% between 2019 and 2020, and a Swiss Re Institute report noted a significant increase in the cost of liability insurance in 2020.
- Nuclear verdicts can lead to larger settlement awards (the "shadow effect") and to reductions in insurance capacity; global casualty insurance capacity fell from $2.2 billion in 2018 to $1.4 billion in 2020.

## State and Federal Legislative Activity
- Legislatures in all states have passed some bills related to [[Tort Reform|tort reform]] that limit award amounts, increase the standard of proof required for awards or allow the use of bifurcated trials.
- In 17 states, punitive damages are limited to some dollar amount or a ratio of compensatory damages, whichever is greater; the most common dollar limit is $500,000 and the most common ratio is 3:1.
- At the federal level the recent focus has been on specific areas of growing concern such as the trucking industry; the Stop Underrides Act (S. 1605) would require trailers or semi-trailers to install rear, side and front guards.
