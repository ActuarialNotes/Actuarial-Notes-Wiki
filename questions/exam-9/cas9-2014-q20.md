## Part a (1.5 points)

Identify and briefly describe three types of underwriting profit.

### Explanation
- **Corporate target underwriting profit** – the UW profit used for corporate decisions of what rates to file to hit target returns.
- **Breakeven UW profit provision** – the U/W profit required to earn a risk-free return for investors (no compensation for risk).
- **Charged UW profit provision** – the UW profit charged after experience rating and schedule modifications are made (Robbin).

Alternatively, other sets of three that the report shows:

- Break-even profit – calculated so the insurer will just "break even" (profit set to the risk-free rate of return); manual profit – calculated using manual rates; accrual profit – accrual UW profit almost always differs from the priced profit provision due to catastrophes and other unexpected losses or market events during the policy period.
- Statutory – earned premium less incurred expenses and undiscounted loss; GAAP – earned premium less deferred expense and undiscounted loss; IASB fair value – earned premium less deferred expense and discounted loss.
- Underwriting profit due to risk selection (good UW control, risk control and selection); due to expense saving (improved company efficiency or expense spending); due to improving loss experience (good loss control and risk mitigation plans).

### Examiner Report
The most common error was describing three methods of calculating the UW Profit Provision, rather than three types of UW Profit.  Another common error was describing other types of profit, i.e., not UW Profit.

## Part b (1 point)

Formulate an argument for why the underwriting profit provision should include the investment income earned on stockholder supplied funds.

### Explanation
The split between policyholder supplied funds (premiums and reserves) and stockholder supplied funds (surplus) is somewhat arbitrary, because it depends on the insurer's reserving practices today and in the past, whether a risk margin is added to reserves, whether expenses are priced for, etc. So the split between "PHSF" and "surplus" isn't really a definite, actual split; it's just an arbitrary construct. It's fairer to just include investment income on all funds an insurer has.

Alternatively, other arguments the report accepted:

- A lower target profit, to include investment income on surplus, because: when the actual UW credit exceeds the target, the insurer and the investor benefit with higher returns but the insured does not, and including investment income on surplus helps offset this; it helps ensure capital is being used efficiently — an overcapitalized insurer will have more investment income on surplus and thus a lower profit provision, which encourages it not to be overcapitalized, and the extra capital can be deployed in other assets for economic efficiency; and it benefits consumers by lowering the price they are charged for coverage.
- Investment income earned on stockholder supplied funds (SHSF) is real earned profit of the insurer; the distinction between PHSF and SHSF is somewhat artificial, as it is not visible on the financial/annual statements to the regulators; and any claim is backed by the full amount of the insurer's surplus — if there is any deficiency in premium charged and investment income earned on PHSF, the SHSF will have to cover the shortage.
- Heavily capitalized insurers might be earning much more than their competitors, since they can charge the same rate as the others by showing low U/W income (excluding the investment income on S/H funds) and still have a much better return on surplus. Therefore, if rate regulation is based on return on surplus, it should be included.

### Examiner Report
This part was designed to have the candidate formulate an argument for something that is generally not done.  If the candidate argued the opposite (i.e., that Investment Income on Shareholder Supplied Funds should NOT be included in the UW Profit Provision calculation), they still received credit as long as they specifically stated what they were arguing.  There were a number of reasons that could be provided (for either argument).  The candidate only needed to have one reason, but the expectation was that they would flesh that reason out, showing pros and cons about that reason, rather than just writing a sentence stating the reason.  Partial credit was awarded, however for stating the reason.
Common errors were candidates referring to Policyholder supplied funds (rather than Stockholder supplied), stating an invalid reason in their argument, or a reason that was for the opposite argument (without specifically stating they were choosing the opposite argument).
