A random variable $X$ is Poisson distributed with mean parameter $\lambda$.

The posterior distribution of $\lambda$ is:

$$
\lambda = \begin{cases} 0.8; & \mathit{probability} = 0.55 \\ 1.2; & \mathit{probability} = 0.45 \end{cases}
$$

A sample of size 10 is drawn from the posterior distribution of $\lambda$. The 10 sampled values are:
0.8, 0.8, 0.8, 1.2, 0.8, 1.2, 1.2, 0.8, 0.8, 1.2

The 10 sampled $\lambda$'s are stored in a vector named lambdas in R as follows:

```
lambdas <- c(0.8, 0.8, 0.8, 1.2, 0.8, 1.2, 1.2, 0.8, 0.8, 1.2)
```

A random sample of size 10 is drawn for the random variable $X$ using the `lambdas` vector. The R function `rpois()` is used for sampling in the next line.

```
rpois(n = 10, lambda = lambdas)
```

The sampled values of $X$ are:
1, 1, 3, 0, 1, 3, 0, 1, 1, 3

Calculate the posterior predictive probability that $X = 1$ using the sampling distribution above.

- A) Less than or equal to 0.2
- B) Greater than 0.2, but less than or equal to 0.4
- C) Greater than 0.4, but less than or equal to 0.6
- D) Greater than 0.6, but less than or equal to 0.8
- E) Greater than 0.8

## Explanation

The posterior predictive distribution is estimated by simulation: draw $\lambda$ from its posterior, then draw $X$ from a Poisson with that mean. Each simulated $X$ is a draw from the posterior predictive distribution, so the probability that $X = 1$ is estimated by the proportion of simulated values equal to 1.

The sampled values are 1, 1, 3, 0, 1, 3, 0, 1, 1, 3. The value 1 appears in positions 1, 2, 5, 8 and 9:

$$
\Pr(X = 1) \approx \frac{5}{10} = 0.5
$$

The question asks for the estimate from this sampling distribution. The exact posterior predictive probability would be

$$
\begin{align*}
\Pr(X = 1) &= 0.55\left(0.8e^{-0.8}\right) + 0.45\left(1.2e^{-1.2}\right) \\
&= 0.55(0.3595) + 0.45(0.3614) \\
&= 0.360
\end{align*}
$$

and ten simulated draws are far too few to estimate it closely.

This falls in $(0.4, 0.6]$, so the answer is **C**.
