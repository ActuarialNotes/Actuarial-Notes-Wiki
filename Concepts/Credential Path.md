A **Credential Path** is the sequence of exams, courses and experience an actuarial society requires before it awards a designation. Three societies set the paths most actuaries in North America follow: the Society of Actuaries (SOA), the Casualty Actuarial Society (CAS) and the [[Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)]].

%%credential-map%%

## Choosing a society

The choice follows the kind of insurance you want to work in. The SOA's path leads to life and health insurance, pensions and investments. The CAS's leads to property and casualty insurance: auto, home, commercial liability, workers' compensation and the reinsurance behind them.

The SOA and the CAS share their first two exams, [[Exam P-1 (SOA)|Probability]] and [[Exam FM-2 (SOA)|Financial Mathematics]], and a pass counts toward either. Many candidates choose a society after they are hired, because the job decides it.

## Associate, then Fellow

Each society awards an associateship first and a fellowship on top of it:

- the SOA's [[Associate of the Society of Actuaries (ASA)|ASA]] and [[Fellow of the Society of Actuaries (FSA)|FSA]];
- the CAS's [[Associate of the Casualty Actuarial Society (ACAS)|ACAS]] and [[Fellow of the Casualty Actuarial Society (FCAS)|FCAS]];
- the CIA's [[Associate of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries (ACIA)|ACIA]] and [[Fellow of the Canadian Institute of Actuaries (FCIA)|FCIA]].

The SOA's fellowship is where a candidate picks a practice area, general insurance among them. The CAS's whole path is property and casualty, so its fellowship goes deeper into the same material.

## Practising in Canada

The CIA's own path starts from a CIA-accredited degree. An associate-level credential from the SOA or CAS carries credit toward the ACIA, and a fellowship toward the FCIA, so many Canadian actuaries hold two designations. Some roles in Canada, such as the [[Appointed Actuary]], require the FCIA.

## Keeping a credential

A credential has to be kept current once it is held. CAS members who provide actuarial services meet its continuing-education policy and attest each year, and SOA members attest each year to its CPD requirement. The CIA asks for 80 hours of continuing professional development over a rolling two years, at least 30 of them guided.
