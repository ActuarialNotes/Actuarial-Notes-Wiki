An insurer's loss severity random variable, $X$, has density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
c(x - 5), & 5 \le x \le 8 \\
c(11 - x), & 8 \le x \le 11 \\
0. & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

where $c$ is a constant.

Calculate the 30th percentile of the loss severity.

- A) $5.47$
- B) $6.14$
- C) $6.80$
- D) $7.06$
- E) $7.32$

## Explanation

First find $c$ by making the density integrate to 1. The two pieces are triangles of base 3 and height $3c$:

$$
\begin{align*}
c\left[\int_5^8 (x - 5)\,dx + \int_8^{11}(11 - x)\,dx\right] &= 1 \\
c\left[4.5 + 4.5\right] &= 1 \\
c &= \frac{1}{9}
\end{align*}
$$

On the left branch the cdf is

$$
F(x) = \int_5^x \frac{y - 5}{9}\,dy = \left.\frac{(y-5)^2}{18}\right|_5^x = \frac{(x - 5)^2}{18}
$$

Since $F(8) = 9/18 = 0.5$, the 30th percentile lies below 8 and this branch is the right one:

$$
\begin{align*}
0.3 &= \frac{(m - 5)^2}{18} \\
5.4 &= (m - 5)^2 \\
2.324 &= m - 5 \\
m &= \mathbf{7.324}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (C), 6.80, is $5 + 0.3(11 - 5)$ — 30% of the way along the support. That would be right for a uniform density, but this one is triangular and rises across $[5,8]$, so probability piles up toward the middle and the 30th percentile sits further right than a linear reading suggests.
