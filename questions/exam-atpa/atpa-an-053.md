An actuary is writing the report on a new commercial property loss-cost model for the head of underwriting, who will rely on its findings. The model was fit to policy and claim data extracted by the claims department.

Determine which of the following statements about the report are true under ASOP No. 41.

- I. Because the actuary checked the claims department's extract for reasonableness, the report need not disclose any reliance on that department for the data.
- II. The report should disclose the uncertainty in the findings, such as how stable the model's performance may be on new business.
- III. The report should state its information date, the date through which the data and information it reflects run.

- A) I only
- B) I and II only
- C) I and III only
- D) II and III only
- E) The answer is not given by (A), (B), (C), or (D)

## Explanation

A report is required when findings are meant to be relied on, and ASOP No. 41's required disclosures include uncertainty or risk, reliance on others for data, who is responsible for each material assumption and method, and the information date.

**I is false.** Checking the data is part of the work, and the report says what checks were made, but it still discloses that the data came from the claims department. Reasonableness checks do not remove the reliance.

**II is true.** Uncertainty or risk is a required disclosure.

**III is true.** The information date is a required disclosure.

II and III are true, so the answer is **D**.

**Why not the others**

- **A**, **B** and **C** accept I, treating the actuary's own checks as a substitute for disclosing the reliance.
- **E** would need a different combination, but exactly II and III are true.
