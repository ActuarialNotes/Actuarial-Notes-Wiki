Given the following information about an insurance company's workers compensation book of business as of December 31, 2016:

| Accident Year | Payroll (\$00) | Reported Claims (\$000) | Indicated Ultimate Claim Counts | Selected Ultimate Severity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 306,000 | 15,450 | 2,300 | 7,000 |
| 2014 | 313,000 | 17,000 | 2,400 | 7,500 |
| 2015 | 318,000 | 14,625 | 2,500 | not provided |
| 2016 | 325,000 | 11,000 | not provided | not provided |

- Annual inflation rate for payroll = 2%.
- Annual claim count trend = 1%.
- Annual severity trend = 8%.
- The cumulative reported claims development factor at 12 months = 1.8.

## Part a (1.75 points)

Select and briefly justify an ultimate frequency estimate for accident year 2016.

### Answer
0.0078 claims per \$100 of payroll

### Explanation
Frequency is claim counts per unit of exposure, and **both** the numerator and the denominator have to be brought to the 2016 level before the ratio is taken — payroll is an inflation-sensitive exposure base, so leaving it untrended would let wage inflation masquerade as falling frequency.

The trend period is measured from each accident year to 2016, so 2013 gets three years of trend, 2014 two, and 2015 one.

| AY | Payroll (\$00) | $\times 1.02^n$ | Trended payroll | Counts | $\times 1.01^n$ | Trended counts | Trended frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 306,000 | $1.02^3$ | 324,730 | 2,300 | $1.01^3$ | 2,369.7 | 0.007297 |
| 2014 | 313,000 | $1.02^2$ | 325,645 | 2,400 | $1.01^2$ | 2,448.2 | 0.007518 |
| 2015 | 318,000 | $1.02^1$ | 324,360 | 2,500 | $1.01^1$ | 2,525.0 | 0.007785 |

$$\text{Trended frequency}_{AY} = \frac{\text{Ultimate counts} \times (1.01)^{2016-AY}}{\text{Payroll} \times (1.02)^{2016-AY}}$$

The three trended frequencies rise steadily — 0.00730, 0.00752, 0.00778. Because the trend is still increasing after both series have been adjusted for their own trends, the most responsive selection is the most recent year:

$$\textbf{Selected frequency} = 0.0078 \text{ claims per } \$100 \text{ of payroll}$$

The justification is the point of the part: an increasing pattern argues for weighting recent experience, so the 2015 value (or a two-year average) is preferred to a three-year average that would be dragged down by the older years. A two-year average, $0.00765$, and a three-year average, $0.00753$, were also accepted with a stated rationale — for example that three years is too short a history to conclude the increase is real. What was **not** acceptable was a selection with no justification at all.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of the Frequency – Severity technique, which includes the correct calculation of frequency using trended counts and a trended exposure base.  Candidates were expected to know the correct trend periods, and then trend counts, exposures and severities to the correct time period. Lastly, the candidate needed to show they could correctly apply the B-F approach using actual reported, and the amount unreported using the F-S ultimate and the percentage unreported, which was calculated using the 12 month-to-Ultimate LDF.

Candidates were expected to trend payrolls and claim counts for each accident year to 2016 level using the inflation percentages provided in the question.  The candidates were also expected to calculate the frequencies for each year using the trended counts divided by trended payroll, and identify the increasing frequency trend.  Lastly, candidates were expected to select a frequency trend and provide some justification for the selection.

Common errors included:

- Not justifying the frequency selection
- Attempting to calculate/justify the given trend percentages vs. using the given information to calculate frequency
- Not trending the claim counts and/or payroll

## Part b (0.5 points)

Select an appropriate ultimate severity estimate for accident year 2016.

### Answer
\$8,783

### Explanation
Ultimate severity selections are given only for accident years 2013 and 2014; 2015 has no selected severity and there is not enough information to derive one, so the selection has to be built from those two years. Trend each to the 2016 level at 8%:

| AY | Selected ultimate severity | $\times 1.08^n$ | Trended to 2016 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 7,000 | $1.08^3 = 1.2597$ | \$8,818 |
| 2014 | 7,500 | $1.08^2 = 1.1664$ | \$8,748 |

$$\textbf{Selected severity} = \frac{8{,}818 + 8{,}748}{2} = \$8{,}783$$

The two trended values are close together, which supports a simple average of both rather than picking one.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to trend the 2013 and 2014 ultimate severity selections to 2016, and then take an average for the severity selection.

Common errors included:

- Only using one year for selection vs. both years

o Candidates were expected to use both years since one year is not sufficient for a severity selection
- Attempting to calculate severities for other years where ultimate severity selections were not given in the question.

o There was not enough information to calculate the 2015 ultimate severity
- Using the 12-to-ultimate LDF to calculate an estimate for the 2016 severity

o Using this LDF and estimated ultimate claims from part (a) was not accepted, as the severity selection should be done separate from and not using the selected frequency.

## Part c (0.75 points)

Calculate accident year 2016 ultimate claims using a Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique that blends the reported development technique with the frequency-severity technique.

### Answer
Approximately \$20,895,513

### Explanation
The Bornhuetter-Ferguson estimate takes the **actual reported claims** and adds an *expected* amount for the portion not yet reported. Here the expected claims come from the frequency-severity technique rather than from an expected loss ratio.

**Step 1 — frequency-severity ultimate for 2016.** Apply the part a frequency and the part b severity to the 2016 exposure. Watch the units: payroll is in hundreds of dollars, so the frequency is per \$100 of payroll and the product is a claim count.

$$\text{Expected counts} = 325{,}000 \times 0.0078 = 2{,}535$$
$$\text{F-S ultimate} = 2{,}535 \times \$8{,}783 = \$22{,}264{,}905$$

**Step 2 — percent unreported from the reported CDF.**

$$\% \text{ unreported} = 1 - \frac{1}{CDF_{12}} = 1 - \frac{1}{1.8} = 0.4444$$

**Step 3 — Bornhuetter-Ferguson.** Reported claims are \$11,000 thousand = \$11,000,000:

$$\text{Ultimate}_{2016} = \text{Reported} + \left(1 - \frac{1}{CDF}\right) \times \text{F-S ultimate}$$
$$= 11{,}000{,}000 + 0.4444 \times 22{,}264{,}905 = 11{,}000{,}000 + 9{,}895{,}513 = \$20{,}895{,}513$$

Answers were graded on consistency with the candidate's own part a and b selections — a two-year-average frequency of 0.00765 gives an F-S ultimate near \$21.84 million and a BF ultimate near \$20.71 million, for example.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use their answers from part a and b to calculate the F-S ultimate for accident year 2016, using the 2016 exposure base (payroll).  Candidates were then expected to apply the B-F method by multiplying the F-S ultimate by the % unreported using the LDF given in the question, and then add on the actual reported.

Common errors included:

- Incorrectly calculating the frequency severity ultimate, where the most common mistake was not using the correct units, e.g. converting reported in to thousands, or incorrectly converting payroll from the question
- Incorrect application of the B-F approach, e.g. incorrectly calculating the percent unreported using the LDF, or applying the percent reported to the F-S ultimate
