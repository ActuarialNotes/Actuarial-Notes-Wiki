Given the following information about insurance companies A and B:

Company A

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Investment gain or loss (after appropriate tax charge) | 5% |
| Premium-to-Surplus ratio | 0.5 |
| Underwriting profit or loss as a percentage of premium | 2.4% |
| Reserves-to-Surplus ratio | 0.2 |

Company B

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Investment gain or loss (after appropriate tax charge) | 5% |
| Underwriting profit or loss (after appropriate tax charge) | \$6 |
| Capital, surplus, and equity in unearned premium reserves | \$500 |
| Reserves and other liabilities (excluding equity in unearned premium reserves) | \$200 |

## Part a (1 point)

Calculate the return on owners' equity for each company.

### Explanation
**Company A.** With $T/S$ the total return on owners' equity, $I/A$ the investment return, $R/S$ the reserves-to-surplus ratio, $U/P$ the underwriting return on premium and $P/S$ the premium-to-surplus ratio:

$$\frac{T}{S} = \frac{I}{A}\left(1 + \frac{R}{S}\right) + \frac{U}{P}\,\frac{P}{S}$$

$$\frac{T}{S} = 5\%\,(1 + 0.2) + (2.4\%)(0.5) = 7.2\%$$

Or, in Ferrari's second form:

$$\frac{T}{S} = \frac{I}{A} + \left(\frac{U}{R} + \frac{I}{A}\right)\frac{R}{S} = \frac{I}{A} + \left(\frac{U}{P} \times \frac{P}{S} \times \frac{S}{R} + \frac{I}{A}\right)\frac{R}{S}$$

$$\frac{T}{S} = 5\% + \left(2.4\% \times \frac{0.5}{0.2} + 5\%\right) \times 0.2 = 7.2\%$$

**Company B.** Surplus is the \$500 of capital, surplus and equity in unearned premium reserves; reserves are the \$200:

$$\frac{T}{S} = \frac{I}{A} + \left(\frac{U}{R} + \frac{I}{A}\right)\frac{R}{S} = 5\% + \left(\frac{6}{200} + 5\%\right)\frac{200}{500} = 8.2\%$$

Equivalently:

$$\frac{T}{S} = \frac{I}{A}\left(1 + \frac{R}{S}\right) + \frac{U}{P}\,\frac{P}{S} = 5\% \times \left(1 + \frac{200}{500}\right) + \frac{6}{500} = 8.2\%$$

$$\frac{T}{S} = \frac{\frac{I}{A}(R + S) + U}{S} = \frac{5\% \times (200 + 500) + 6}{500} = 8.2\%$$

### Examiner Report
- The candidates were expected to know the components of return
- Candidates scored well with most receiving full credit
- Common errors were in identifying surplus in company
  - Using the Reserves (200)
  - Using the sum of Reserves and Surplus (200+500)

## Part b (0.5 points)

Briefly describe the "interest" that an insurer pays for the use of reserve capital and contrast this with the interest a financial institution pays for debt capital.

### Explanation
**The "interest" on reserve capital.** Either of these was accepted:

1. The "interest" that an insurer pays for the use of reserve capital is the underwriting return / losses (as a percent of reserves and other liabilities).
2. The "interest" that an insurer pays for the use of capital is the $U/R$ term in Ferrari's 2nd equation.

**The difference from interest on debt capital.** Either of these was accepted:

1. The interest paid for the use of debt capital is fixed (predetermined, set, specific, not variable), whereas the underwriting losses are variable (not fixed, volatile, not known — an expected value with variance).
2. An increase in the relative amount of debt capital generally entails demands by the creditors for a progressively higher interest rate to reflect the increased risk of larger fixed commitments, but the relative profitability of expanding an insurance portfolio is not as predictable. The ability to reduce the relative variance of underwriting results by sheer volume and logical diversification may offset the costs of taking additional and possibly poorer risks.

### Examiner Report
- The candidates were expected to understand what portion of the calculation in part (a) represent "interest" and how this interest varies from typical financial institutions
- Candidates generally scored well especially in the first half of this part
- Common errors included:
  - Interest stated to P or S instead of R
  - Stating that insurers can have negative interest but financial institutions cannot
  - Stating that insurers will always decrease volatility by taking on more risks
