Given the following information for a book of business as of December 31, 2016:

| Accident Year | Cumulative Reported Loss & ALAE (\$000) |
|---|---|
| 2014 | 5,615 |
| 2015 | 4,315 |
| 2016 | 2,745 |

| Calendar Year | Earned Premium (\$000) |
|---|---|
| 2014 | 10,800 |
| 2015 | 11,250 |
| 2016 | 12,375 |

| Selected Reported Loss & ALAE Age to Age Factors | 12-24 | 24-36 | 36-48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| | 2.089 | 1.368 | 1.070 |

- All policies are annual.
- Exposures are written evenly throughout each calendar year.
- Annual loss and ALAE trend = 5%.
- Annual premium trend = 4%.
- There has been one rate change in the past five years: +5%, effective July 1, 2015.
- Fixed expense ratio = 15%.
- Variable expense ratio = 25%.
- Profit and contingencies provision = 5%.
- ULAE provision = 6% of loss and ALAE.
- Rates are to be in effect for one year.
- There is no loss development beyond 48 months.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the ultimate losses and ALAE for each accident year using the loss development technique.

### Answer
2014: 6,008; 2015: 6,316; 2016: 8,394 (\$000)

### Explanation
Build the cumulative development factors by multiplying the age-to-age factors from each year's maturity out to 48 months, beyond which there is no development:

$$CDF_{2014} = 1.070, \qquad CDF_{2015} = 1.368 \times 1.070 = 1.4638, \qquad CDF_{2016} = 2.089 \times 1.368 \times 1.070 = 3.0578$$

| AY | Reported | CDF | Ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 5,615 | 1.0700 | 6,008 |
| 2015 | 4,315 | 1.4638 | 6,316 |
| 2016 | 2,745 | 3.0578 | 8,394 |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate and apply a cumulative development factor. Common errors included not cumulating the age-to-age factors before applying them, and trending the losses when the question did not ask for it.

## Part b (0.75 points)

Calculate the ultimate losses and ALAE for each accident year using the Bornhuetter-Ferguson technique using an expected loss and ALAE ratio of 56%.

### Answer
2014: 6,011; 2015: 6,311; 2016: 7,409 (\$000)

### Explanation
$$\text{Ultimate} = \text{Reported} + \left(ELR \times EP\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{CDF}\right)$$

The expected losses use the **unadjusted** earned premium — on-leveling and trending belong in the rate indication, not in the BF expected loss calculation.

| AY | EP | Expected Loss & ALAE | % Unreported | Unreported | Reported | Ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 10,800 | $0.56(10{,}800) = 6{,}048$ | $1 - 1/1.0700 = 6.5\%$ | 396 | 5,615 | **6,011** |
| 2015 | 11,250 | 6,300 | $1 - 1/1.4638 = 31.7\%$ | 1,996 | 4,315 | **6,311** |
| 2016 | 12,375 | 6,930 | $1 - 1/3.0578 = 67.3\%$ | 4,664 | 2,745 | **7,409** |

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to use the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method to estimate ultimate losses. A common mistake was on-leveling and/or trending the premium used in the expected loss calculation.

## Part c (0.5 points)

Briefly justify an appropriate ultimate loss and ALAE selection from parts a. and b. above for accident years 2014 through 2016.

### Explanation
**Selection: the development method for 2014 and 2015, and the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method for 2016.** Two justifications:

**1. For 2014 and 2015 the two methods agree, so the choice barely matters.** The development and BF estimates differ by 3 (6,008 vs 6,011) for 2014 and by 5 (6,316 vs 6,311) for 2015. Both years are mature — 6.5% and 31.7% unreported — so the reported losses already carry most of the information and either method, or an average, is defensible.

**2. For 2016 the development method is highly leveraged and should be avoided.** With a CDF of 3.058, roughly two-thirds of the ultimate is still unreported, so every dollar of reported loss is multiplied threefold and any random fluctuation in a thin, immature diagonal is magnified accordingly. That is exactly the circumstance the Bornhuetter-Ferguson method is designed for: it fixes the unreported portion at an expected amount rather than extrapolating from volatile actual experience, giving a far more stable estimate. The difference is material — 8,394 against 7,409, nearly a million dollars.

The opposite selection is also defensible if argued: because the loss ratios are running above the 56% *a priori*, the BF method may understate accident year 2016, so a candidate concluding that the expected loss ratio is stale and preferring the development method could also earn credit.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to select a method of loss development for each year and justify each selection. Common errors included not making a selection at all, and not providing two distinct justifications.

## Part d (3 points)

Calculate the indicated rate change for policies effective July 1, 2017 using the ultimate loss and ALAE selections from part c. above, assuming full credibility.

### Answer
+7.8% using the Bornhuetter-Ferguson selections (+11.9% using the development method)

### Explanation
**Step 1 — on-level factors (parallelogram method).** One rate change, +5% effective 7/1/2015, on annual policies written evenly. A July 1 change affects $\tfrac{1}{2}(0.5)^2 = 12.5\%$ of that calendar year's earned premium and $1 - 12.5\% = 87.5\%$ of the next year's. Current rate level is 1.05:

| CY | Average rate level | OLF |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | $1.0000$ | $1.05 / 1.0000 = 1.0500$ |
| 2015 | $0.875(1.00) + 0.125(1.05) = 1.0063$ | $1.05 / 1.0063 = 1.0435$ |
| 2016 | $0.125(1.00) + 0.875(1.05) = 1.0438$ | $1.05 / 1.0438 = 1.0060$ |

**Step 2 — trend periods.** Rates are effective 7/1/2017 for one year on annual policies, so policies are written 7/1/2017 – 6/30/2018 and the average earned (and average accident) date of the prospective period is **7/1/2018**. From each calendar year's 7/1 average date:

$$2014: 4 \text{ years}, \qquad 2015: 3 \text{ years}, \qquad 2016: 2 \text{ years}$$

**Step 3 — on-level trended earned premium** at the 4% premium trend:

| CY | EP | OLF | $1.04^{n}$ | On-level trended EP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 10,800 | 1.0500 | $1.04^{4}$ | 13,266 |
| 2015 | 11,250 | 1.0435 | $1.04^{3}$ | 13,205 |
| 2016 | 12,375 | 1.0060 | $1.04^{2}$ | 13,465 |
| **Total** | | | | **39,936** |

**Step 4 — trended ultimate losses** at the 5% loss trend, using the part c selections (BF):

| AY | Ultimate | $1.05^{n}$ | Trended Ultimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 6,011 | $1.05^{4}$ | 7,306 |
| 2015 | 6,311 | $1.05^{3}$ | 7,306 |
| 2016 | 7,409 | $1.05^{2}$ | 8,168 |
| **Total** | | | **22,780** |

**Step 5 — loss ratio, ULAE load, and the indication.**

$$\text{Loss \& ALAE ratio} = \frac{22{,}780}{39{,}936} = 57.0\%$$

Loading ULAE at 6% of loss and ALAE:

$$57.0\% \times 1.06 = 60.5\%$$

$$\text{Indicated change} = \frac{\text{Loss \& LAE ratio} + \text{Fixed expense}}{1 - \text{Variable expense} - \text{Profit}} - 1 = \frac{0.605 + 0.15}{1 - 0.25 - 0.05} - 1$$

$$= \frac{0.755}{0.70} - 1 = +7.8\%$$

Had the development method ultimates been selected throughout, the trended losses total 23,869, the loss and LAE ratio is 63.4%, and the indication is **+11.9%** — the difference driven almost entirely by accident year 2016.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to calculate on-level factors and trend factors over the appropriate trend periods, apply them to the premium and to the selected ultimate losses to develop loss ratios, and adjust a selected loss ratio for expenses to reach an indicated rate need.

Common errors included miscalculating the average rate level factors, determining the trend period incorrectly, and failing to apply trend or the ULAE load to the losses.
