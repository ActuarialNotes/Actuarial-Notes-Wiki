Suppose that $X_1, \ldots, X_{10}$ is a random sample from the standard normal distribution, N(0, 1). An actuary is using $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i - \bar{X})^2$ to estimate the population variance, where $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i$

Calculate the bias of this estimator.

- A) Less than -0.2
- B) At least -0.2, but less than 0.0
- C) At least 0.0, but less than 0.2
- D) At least 0.2, but less than 0.4
- E) At least 0.4

## Explanation

Dividing the sum of squared deviations by $n$ instead of $n-1$ makes the variance estimator biased low. For a random sample with variance $\sigma^2$,

$$
E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i - \bar{X})^2\right] = (n-1)\sigma^2,
$$

so with $n = 10$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i - \bar{X})^2\right] &= \frac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2 \\
&= \frac{9}{10}(1) \\
&= 0.9
\end{align*}
$$

The bias is the expected value minus the true value:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Bias} &= 0.9 - 1 \\
&= -0.1
\end{align*}
$$

This falls in $[-0.2, 0.0)$, so the answer is **B**.
