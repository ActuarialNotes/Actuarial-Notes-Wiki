Given the following:

**Coupon Bonds**

| Term | Yield | Duration (Years) |
|---|---|---|
| 4-year | 7% | 3 |
| 10-year | 9% | 7 |
| 20-year | 10% | 11 |
| 30-year | 11% | 20 |

**Common Stocks**

| Stocks | Annual Dividend | Duration (Years) |
|---|---|---|
| A | 5.00% | 20 |
| B | 9.30% | 11 |
| C | 10.00% | 10 |
| D | 14.30% | 7 |

**Zero Coupon Bonds**

| Term | Yield |
|---|---|
| 3-year | 1.50% |
| 5-year | 2.00% |
| 7-year | 2.50% |
| 10-year | 3.00% |
| 15-year | 3.50% |
| 20-year | 4.00% |

An insurance company has liabilities with the following payment pattern:

| Development Year | Payment |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | 0 |
| 3 | 2.8 million |
| 4 to 6 | 0 |
| 7 | 5.2 million |
| 8 to 19 | 0 |
| 20 | 7.0 million |

- The liability discount rate is 3.0%.
- Assume all payments occur at the end of the year.

## Part a (0.5 points)

Calculate the Macaulay Duration of the liability.

### Explanation
$$D = \frac{(3)2.8M(1.03)^{-3} + (7)(5.2M)(1.03)^{-7} + (20)(7M)(1.03)^{-20}}{2.8M(1.03)^{-3} + 5.2M(1.03)^{-7} + 7M(1.03)^{-20}} = \frac{114.80}{10.67} = 10.76$$

assuming the discount rate is compounded annually.

Alternatively, weight each payment by its share of the total payout — 18.67% at year 3, 34.67% at year 7 and 46.67% at year 20 — and discount continuously:

$$D = \frac{3(.1867)e^{-.03(3)} + 7(.3467)e^{-.03(7)} + 20(.4667)e^{-.03(20)}}{.1867e^{-.03(3)} + .3467e^{-.03(7)} + .4667e^{-.03(20)}} = \frac{.5119 + 1.9672 + 5.1226}{.1706 + .2810 + .2561} = \frac{7.6017}{.7077} = 10.74 \text{ years}$$

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know how to calculate Macaulay Duration for a series of liability payments.
- For full credit, candidates were expected to demonstrate knowledge of how Macaulay Duration is defined (the correct formula and applicable interest rate) and to calculate the correct result.
- Candidates generally scored well on this part.
- Common errors included applying incorrect interest rates and calculation errors.

## Part b (1.5 points)

The target rate of return on investments is 11.6%.

Using the securities listed above, design an investment portfolio that is immunized from small changes in interest rates and has an expected return greater than or equal to the target rate of return on investments.

### Explanation
To immunize, the portfolio needs the same PV as the liabilities (10.666M) and the same duration (10.763 years).

To get at least 11.6%, the portfolio will need stock D (the only security with a return above 11.6%), assuming no capital gains are expected, so only the dividend rate is used. Then, to get a duration equal to 10.763, stock D will need to be weighted with something of higher duration. Try the 30-year bond (yield 11%, duration 20):

$$E(r) = w_{Bond}(0.11) + (1 - w_{Bond})(0.143) \ge 0.116 \Rightarrow w_{Bond} \le 0.8182, \quad w_{stock\,D} \ge 0.1818$$

$$\text{Duration: } 10.763 = w_{Bond}(20) + (1 - w_{Bond})(7) \Rightarrow w_{Bond} = 0.2895, \quad w_{stock\,D} = 0.7105$$

- PV invested in the 20-yr bond (as printed — the 30-year bond, of duration 20) $= 0.2895(10.666m) = 3.088m$
- PV invested in stock D $= 7.578m$

Using these amounts, the duration and PV will be the same as the liabilities, so the portfolio is immunized against small changes.

Not every pairing works: stocks B and D give $11w + 7(1 - w) = 10.763$, $w = .941$, but the annual dividend $.941(.093) + .059(.143) < 11.6\%$.

Alternatively, other portfolios meet both conditions:

*Stock A and stock D.* Stock D is a must, and stock A (duration 20 years) adjusts the duration: $w_A + w_D = 1$ and $20w_A + 7w_D = 10.7628$ give $w_A = 0.2894$, $w_D = 0.7106$. The annual dividends of the portfolio are $10.6662 \times (0.2894 \times 5\% + 0.7106 \times 143\%) = 1.2381$ (143% as printed). With the stocks bought at present and sold at the end of year 20, IRR(PV = 10.6662, FV = 10.6662, PMT = 1.2381, n = 20) = 11.61% > 11.6%. Invest $0.2894 \times 10.6662 = 3.087$ in stock A and $0.7106 \times 10.6662 = 7.579$ in stock D.

*Three securities* — the 30-year coupon bond (weight $a$, duration 20, yield 11%), stock D (weight $b$, duration 7, yield 14.3%) and the 20-year zero-coupon bond (weight $1 - a - b$, duration 20, yield 4%):

$$10.763 = 20a + 7b + 20(1 - a - b) \qquad 11.6\% = 11\%a + 14.3\%b + 4\%(1 - a - b)$$

give $a = 3.99\%$, $b = 71.08\%$, $1 - a - b = 24.93\%$. With the PV of the liability from part a, \$10,666,203, invest \$425,581 in the 30-year coupon bond, \$7,581,537 in stock D and \$2,659,084 in the 20-year zero-coupon bond, for an asset portfolio duration of 10.763 years with an investment yield of 11.6%.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know that a duration matching asset-liability management strategy will immunize against small changes in interest rates and how to apply that knowledge to a selection of investments to design an appropriate portfolio.
- Candidates who received full credit demonstrated the knowledge of duration matching strategy by selecting appropriate assets for the portfolio that resulted in matching the duration of the liabilities and producing an adequate expected return. They also demonstrated their understanding of asset-liability management by giving sufficient explanation of their approach and correctly calculating the portfolio composition and expected return.
- Multiple portfolios were possible that met the criteria of matching duration and an adequate return. Successful candidates identified that any such portfolio must include Common Stock D along with other asset(s) with longer duration.
- Common errors that candidates made were:
  - Not using a duration-matching strategy (e.g., cash-flow matching)
  - Using assets that did not allow for achieving the target return and/or the appropriate duration
  - Solving for the return of the portfolio instead of the duration
  - Calculation errors

## Part c (0.5 points)

Explain which investments from those listed above should be included in the portfolio to be completely immunized from all interest rate changes.

### Explanation
Exact cash flow matching, with the zero-coupon bonds of the liabilities' own terms:

- 3-year zero for $\frac{2.8}{1.015^3}$m = 2.69m
- 7-year zero for $\frac{5.2}{1.025^7}$m = 4.37m
- 20-year zero for $\frac{7}{1.04^{20}}$m = 3.19m

Exact cash flow matching shelters the company from reinvestment risk and change in value of bonds, since there's no need to rebalance every year or reinvest coupons.

### Examiner Report
- Candidates were expected to know that a cash flow matching or dedication strategy would immunize the company against all interest rate changes and to apply that knowledge to design an appropriate portfolio.
- Candidates who received full credit identified the correct strategy as well as the correct investments
- Common errors included:
  - Using an incorrect strategy (e.g., duration matching)
  - Identifying either the correct strategy or investments but not both
- A number of candidates suggested using stocks as the investment strategy. While this reflects the overall recommendation of the Feldblum paper to provide inflation-sensitive assets to match inflation-sensitive P&C liabilities, it is not a strategy that **completely** immunizes against interest rate changes.
