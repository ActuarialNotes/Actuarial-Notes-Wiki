The government-supported tier of Canada's income security system provides pensions through a combination of three programs.

## Part a (1.5 points)

Identify and briefly describe each of the three programs.

### Explanation
Any three of the following:

- Canada Pension Plan (CPP/QPP) – provides a monthly income replacement after retirement to retirees which is funded by employees and employers
- Old Age Security (OAS) – provides monthly benefits to all who reach age 65 and meet certain residence requirements, but is subject to a special tax or "clawback."
- Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) – provides monthly benefits in addition to OAS benefit subject to an income test and residence requirements.
- Allowance and Survivor's Allowance – provides benefits paid out between ages 60 and 64
to the living or deceased pensioner's spouse or common-law partner

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to list three government supported tiers of Canadian income security and provide a brief description on each.
Common errors include:

- Listing Registered Retirement Savings Plans (RRSP), Employment Insurance (EI), or Workers Compensation (WC) as one of the three tiers
- Simply identifying the programs without describing the program

## Part b (0.5 points)

For any two of the programs, briefly describe how they are funded.

### Explanation
Any two of the following:

- Canada Pension Plan (CPP/QPP) – funded by contributions from employers and employees
- Old Age Security (OAS) – financed on a pay-as-you-go basis from government general tax revenue
- Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) – financed on a pay-as-you-go basis from government general tax revenue

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate an understanding of the funding behind the programs.

Common errors include:

- Canadian Pension Plan (CPP/QPP) – indicating this program was funded by government
- Old Age Security (OAS) – indicating this program was funded by employee/employer contributions, or government revenue

## Part c (0.5 points)

For any two of the programs, briefly describe how the benefits are taxed.

### Explanation
Any two of the following:

- Canada Pension Plan (CPP/QPP) – benefit taxed normally as part of income
- Old Age Security (OAS) – benefit taxed normally as part of income and subject to "clawback" tax
- Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) – Benefit is not taxed

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to demonstrate that they know how the benefits from these programs are taxed.

Common errors include:

- Canada Pension Plan (CPP/QPP) – indicating that the contribution was taxed
- Old Age Security (OAS) – indicating this was not taxed, or was not part of taxable income
- Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) – indicating that this was taxed
