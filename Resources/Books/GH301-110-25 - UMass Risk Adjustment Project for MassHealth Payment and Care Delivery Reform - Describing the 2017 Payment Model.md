A report dated October 11, 2016 on the Social Determinants of Health model that UMass developed for MassHealth to improve its approach to risk adjustment and to incorporate, to the extent feasible, key social determinants of health. The final model predicts a concurrent cost outcome that applies only to services MassHealth expects its 2017 managed care payments and alternative payment models to cover, and refines the State's earlier DxCG medical-risk-based predictions with predictors for unstable housing, disability, agency relationships, serious mental illness and substance use disorders, plus a measure of "neighborhood stress".

## Social Determinants of Health Modeling
- Risk adjustment models are designed to determine the relative resource needs of individual members, so that plans that enroll sicker members receive more money than plans that avoid them; they are not designed to determine the total amount of money allocated across all plans.
- The methods use concurrent models to predict costs for those enrolled for at least one-half year in the CY2013 PCC plan, with costs annualized and top-coded at $125K.
- The model relies on the DxCG v4.2 concurrent Medicaid risk score, selected medical conditions, social determinants of health (disability, housing, and medical/behavioral indicators) and neighborhood-based predictors of health.
