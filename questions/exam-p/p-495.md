The heights, in feet, of basketball players in a city league are modeled by a random variable, $X$, with probability density function

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{3(x-6)^2}{2}, & \text{for } 5 < x < 7 \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Calculate $\text{Var}(X)$.

- A) $0.33$
- B) $0.60$
- C) $2.00$
- D) $4.00$
- E) $6.00$

## Explanation

The density is symmetric about $x = 6$, since it depends on $x$ only through $(x-6)^2$. That gives the mean immediately:

$$
E[X] = 6
$$

For the second moment, expand $x f(x) \cdot x$:

$$
\begin{align*}
E[X^2] &= \frac{3}{2}\int_5^7 x^2(x-6)^2\,dx \\
       &= \frac{3}{2}\int_5^7 \left(x^4 - 12x^3 + 36x^2\right)dx \\
       &= \frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{x^5}{5} - 3x^4 + 12x^3\right)\Bigg|_5^7 \\
       &= 36.6
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(X) &= 36.6 - 6^2 \\
              &= \mathbf{0.60}
\end{align*}
$$

A cleaner route uses the symmetry directly: substituting $u = x - 6$ gives $\text{Var}(X) = E[U^2] = \tfrac{3}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}u^4\,du = \tfrac{3}{2}\cdot\tfrac{2}{5} = 0.6$, with no large cancellation to lose precision in. Choice (E), 6.00, is the mean rather than the variance; the density is concentrated at the ends of a two-foot interval, so a variance well below 1 is what the shape implies.
