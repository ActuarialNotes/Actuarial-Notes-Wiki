The NAIC's Credit for Reinsurance Model Law (Model #785), as published in NAIC Model Laws, Regulations, Guidelines and Other Resources, Summer 2019. Its purpose is to protect the interest of insureds, claimants, ceding insurers, assuming insurers and the public generally, and it sets out when a domestic ceding insurer is allowed credit for reinsurance ceded as an asset or a reduction from liability.

## 2 Credit Allowed a Domestic Ceding Insurer
- A. Credit is allowed when the reinsurance is ceded to an assuming insurer that is licensed to transact insurance or reinsurance in the state.
- B. Credit is allowed when the assuming insurer is accredited by the commissioner as a reinsurer in the state.
- C. Credit is allowed when the assuming insurer is domiciled in, or entered through, a state that employs standards regarding credit for reinsurance substantially similar to those of the statute.
- D. Credit is allowed when the assuming insurer maintains a trust fund in a qualified U.S. financial institution for the payment of the valid claims of its U.S. ceding insurers.
- E. Credit is allowed when the assuming insurer has been certified by the commissioner as a reinsurer in the state and secures its obligations as the subsection requires.
- F. Credit is allowed when the assuming insurer meets the conditions set out, including being domiciled and licensed in a Reciprocal Jurisdiction.
- G. Credit is allowed when the assuming insurer does not meet subsections A to F, but only as to the insurance of risks located in jurisdictions where the reinsurance is required by applicable law or regulation of that jurisdiction.
- H. Provisions that apply if the assuming insurer is not licensed, accredited or certified to transact insurance or reinsurance in the state.
