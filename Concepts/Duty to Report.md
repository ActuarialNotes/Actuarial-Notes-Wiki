![[Media/Figures/Duty_to_Report.svg|340]]

The **Duty to Report** is the [[Appointed Actuary]]'s statutory obligation under the [[Insurance Companies Act]] to escalate any matter that has material adverse effects on the financial condition of the insurer. The actuary reports in turn to:

1. **The CEO and CFO.**
2. **The directors**, if the matter is not suitably addressed.
3. **[[OSFI]]**, if it is still not addressed.

The escalation is sequential, and each step must be given a real opportunity. The actuary does not go straight to OSFI, because management is entitled to address the matter first, and the directors after them. What the actuary may not do is stop when an answer is unsatisfactory.

> [!example]- What does the Appointed Actuary do when the CFO refuses this correction? {Example}
> In November the Appointed Actuary concludes that a contract booked as reinsurance does not transfer significant [[Risk Transfer|insurance risk]] and must be accounted for as a deposit. Correcting it would reduce [[Capital Available]] by $\$45$ million and take the [[MCT]] ratio from $166\%$ to $141\%$, below the $150\%$ supervisory target. The CFO responds that the auditors are comfortable and no change will be made.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > - The matter was raised with the CFO, and the answer that no change will be made is a decision, not a resolution.
> > - A $25$-point overstatement of the capital ratio, taking it from above the supervisory target to below it, is a material adverse effect on financial condition.
> > - The actuary therefore reports to the board in writing, setting out the risk transfer analysis, the $\$45$ million effect and the resulting ratio.
> > - The auditors' comfort is not an answer, because the [[Risk Transfer|risk transfer]] assessment is an actuarial judgement the actuary must make, and the [[Standards of Practice]] do not permit deferring it to the auditor. Speaking to the auditor directly puts any disagreement on the record before the statements are signed.
> > - If the board declines to act, the actuary must report to OSFI.
> > - Independently of the escalation, if the financial statements reflect the contract improperly, the actuary cannot give an unqualified [[Statement of Actuarial Opinion]].

## The trigger

The trigger is a matter with material adverse effects on the financial condition of the company. It is not limited to reserve deficiencies. An improperly accounted reinsurance contract, a projected [[MCT]] breach, a data failure that prevents a reliable valuation, or a governance failure preventing the actuary from doing the work all qualify.

## Civil immunity

Section 361 protects the actuary from liability for statements made in good faith in carrying out these duties. Without it, the duty would expose the actuary to suit by the company they are required to report on. It does not protect an actuary who says nothing.

## Resignation and the successor actuary

Resigning without reporting leaves the matter undisclosed, which is the outcome the framework is designed to prevent. Both the company and the actuary must notify OSFI of a change in the appointment, and the actuary may state the reasons, which makes a dismissal over a valuation disagreement visible.

Under the [[Standards of Practice]], an actuary asked to accept an appointment must communicate with the predecessor before accepting, so an insurer cannot escape a finding by replacing the person who made it.

## Documentation

The actuary documents the dates, what was reported, to whom, and the response received. If the matter is examined later, the file is the record of whether the duty was discharged.

> [!trap] Resigning instead of reporting
> Resignation does not discharge the duty. The matter stays undisclosed, so the actuary must complete the escalation, up to OSFI if needed.
