The random variables $X_1$, $X_2$, $X_3$ represent the values of three independent investments. Each is normally distributed with $E(X_i) = 100$ and $\text{Var}(X_i) = 27i/2$, $i = 1, 2, 3$. Let $\Phi(x)$ denote the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal variable.

Determine the probability that the average value of the three investments exceeds 106.

- A) $\Phi(2)$
- B) $\Phi(2/3)$
- C) $1 - \Phi(2/3)$
- D) $1 - \Phi(2)$
- E) $1 - \Phi\left(2/\sqrt{3}\right)$

## Explanation

Note the variances are **not** equal: $\text{Var}(X_i) = 27i/2$ gives $13.5$, $27$ and $40.5$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$.

Let $Y = \tfrac{1}{3}(X_1 + X_2 + X_3)$. A linear combination of independent normal variables is normal, with

$$
E[Y] = \frac{100 + 100 + 100}{3} = 100
$$

For the variance, the factor $\tfrac{1}{3}$ enters squared and the three variances add:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(Y) &= \frac{1}{9}\left(\frac{27}{2}\right)(1 + 2 + 3) \\
              &= \frac{1}{9}\left(\frac{27}{2}\right)(6) \\
              &= 9
\end{align*}
$$

so $\text{SD}(Y) = 3$. Standardizing:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[Y > 106] &= P\left[Z > \frac{106 - 100}{3}\right] \\
           &= P[Z > 2] \\
           &= \mathbf{1 - \Phi(2)}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A) is the complement — the probability of falling *below* 106. Choice (E) comes from not squaring the $\tfrac{1}{3}$: dividing the variance of the sum, $13.5 + 27 + 40.5 = 81$, by 3 instead of 9 gives $\text{Var}(Y) = 27$, $\text{SD}(Y) = 3\sqrt{3}$ and $z = 6/(3\sqrt{3}) = 2/\sqrt{3}$.
