A company is implementing population density in its rating plan for a line of business. The company has three pieces of information.

- An analysis the company performed using a generalized linear model (GLM) on internal data only.
- The rating factors from a competitor's rate filing.
- The rating factors from an external industry benchmark.

| Population Density | Internal GLM | Competitor | Industry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 1.5 | 0.9 | 0.4 |
| Medium | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| High | 1.3 | 1.1 | 1.6 |

![Combined bar and line chart titled Population Density Relativities - Internal GLM Results, for three population density levels: Low, Medium and High. Grey bars give internal exposures against a right-hand axis: about 500 at Low, 2,000 at Medium and 5,000 at High. Three lines read against a left-hand relativity axis. The internal GLM relativity is 1.50 at Low, 1.00 at Medium and 1.30 at High. The upper standard error line is 2.50 at Low, 1.20 at Medium and 1.40 at High; the lower standard error line is 0.50 at Low, 0.80 at Medium and 1.20 at High, so the band is widest at Low, where exposures are fewest, and narrowest at High](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ActuarialNotes/Actuarial-Notes-Wiki/main/Media/Attachments/cas5-2018s-q13-population-density-glm.png)

Provide a recommendation for the low and high level rating factors given the chart above and the external rating factors.

Justify the recommendations considering:

- i. GLM diagnostics
- ii. Competitor
- iii. Industry benchmark

### Explanation
The chart plots the internal GLM relativity for each level with its upper and lower standard-error bounds, over bars of the internal exposures at each level. Read from it: the **low** level has very few exposures and a very wide band (roughly 0.5 to 2.5 around the 1.5 estimate), while the **high** level holds most of the exposures and has a narrow band (roughly 1.2 to 1.4 around 1.3).

**High density: recommend 1.3 (the internal GLM).**

- *i. GLM diagnostics:* the high level has the most exposures and tight standard errors, so the GLM estimate is credible and should be relied on.
- *ii. Competitor:* the competitor's 1.1 agrees that high density costs more than medium; the difference may reflect the competitor's different book and underwriting, and the company's own credible data should outweigh it.
- *iii. Industry benchmark:* the industry's 1.6 also agrees on direction. 1.3 sits between the competitor and the industry, which supports it as reasonable.

**Low density: recommend about 0.9 (the competitor's factor).**

- *i. GLM diagnostics:* the low level has few exposures and a very wide standard-error band that includes 1.0 and values well below it, so the GLM's 1.5 is not credible and should not be used on its own — a low-density surcharge is also counter to both external sources.
- *ii. Competitor:* 0.9 lies inside the GLM's standard-error band and comes from a company writing a similar line in the same market, so it is a reasonable source to rely on where the internal data is thin.
- *iii. Industry benchmark:* the industry's 0.4 supports a discount for low density but falls below the GLM band and reflects the whole industry's mix rather than this company's book, so it is given less weight (a blend of the competitor and the industry, such as 0.65, would also be a supportable selection).

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to understand how to analyze internal GLM model output for low and high rating variables, compare this output to industry and competitor values, make a recommendation using all of this information, and support the recommendation.

Common errors included:

- Not discussing the industry and competitor values.
- Not articulating reasons for selection, particularly for the low variable where there was less credibility and reliance on some combination of industry and competitor was necessary.
- Stating observations about the factors without any reasoning, for example, "the competitor factor is higher than the industry factor."
