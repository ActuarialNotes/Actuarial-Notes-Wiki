A dental patient anticipates needing $X$ fillings next year, where $X$ is Poisson distributed with mean 3. The patient has a choice of dental insurance policies that reimburse different numbers of fillings next year, as shown in the table below.

| Policy | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum number of fillings reimbursed next year | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |

Policies that reimburse a larger maximum number of fillings also charge higher premiums.

The patient wants at least a 75% probability of having all fillings reimbursed next year.

Determine the lowest premium policy that the patient should choose.

- A) Policy A
- B) Policy B
- C) Policy C
- D) Policy D
- E) Policy E

## Explanation

All fillings are reimbursed when $X$ does not exceed the policy maximum $n$, so the requirement is

$$
P[X \le n] = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \frac{e^{-3}3^i}{i!} \ge 0.75
$$

Since higher maxima cost more, the answer is the *smallest* $n$ that clears the bar. Accumulate the Poisson pmf:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[X \le 3] &= 0.6472 \\
P[X \le 4] &= 0.8153
\end{align*}
$$

Three fillings is not enough — 0.6472 falls short of 0.75 — while four clears it, so $n = 4$ and the patient should buy **Policy C**.

Choice (B), Policy B, matches the maximum to the *mean* of 3. But a Poisson at its mean has only about 65% of its mass at or below that point: the distribution is right-skewed, so covering a stated probability requires a limit above the mean, not at it.
