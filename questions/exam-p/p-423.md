A survey was conducted within the population of those who claim to have contributed to charity during the previous year. Results indicate that 70% of this population claimed to have contributed at least 1000, 50% overstated the value of their contributions, and 45% did both.

Assume that the survey accurately represents the population.

Calculate the probability that a randomly selected person overstated the value of his contribution, given that they claimed to have contributed less than 1000.

- A) $1/20$
- B) $1/10$
- C) $1/6$
- D) $3/4$
- E) $5/6$

## Explanation

A two-by-two table is the cleanest way to organize the three given percentages. Fill in the margins first, then the given overlap:

| | Overstated | Didn't overstate | Row total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claimed at least 1000 | 0.45 | 0.25 | 0.70 |
| Claimed less than 1000 | 0.05 | 0.25 | 0.30 |
| Column total | 0.50 | 0.50 | 1.00 |

Every interior cell follows by subtraction: the "claimed less than 1000" row total is $1 - 0.70 = 0.30$, and the overstated column must total 0.50, so the missing cell is $0.50 - 0.45 = 0.05$.

The conditional probability restricts attention to the second row:

$$
\begin{align*}
P[\text{overstated} \mid \text{claimed} < 1000] &= \frac{P[\text{overstated and claimed} < 1000]}{P[\text{claimed} < 1000]} \\
                                                &= \frac{0.05}{0.30} \\
                                                &= \mathbf{\frac{1}{6}}
\end{align*}
$$

Choice (A), $1/20$, is the joint probability 0.05 rather than the conditional one. Note how strongly the two are associated: 64% of those claiming at least 1000 overstated, against only 17% of the others.
