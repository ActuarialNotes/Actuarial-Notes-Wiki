A zero-coupon bond with a face amount of 1000 sells for a price of 640 and matures in $n$ years, where $n$ is a whole number.

A second bond has the same price, same time until maturity, and same annual effective yield. It pays annual coupons at an annual rate equal to 50% of the annual effective yield rate.

Calculate the face value of the second bond.

- A) 780
- B) 805
- C) 830
- D) 855
- E) 880

## Explanation

The zero-coupon bond's price is its face amount discounted at the yield rate $i$ for $n$ years:

$$
\begin{align*}
640(1+i)^{n} &= 1000 \\
(1+i)^{n} &= 1.5625 \\
v^{n} &= 0.64
\end{align*}
$$

The second bond has face value $F$, the same price, term and yield, and pays annual coupons of $F\,\dfrac{i}{2}$. Pricing it at the yield rate $i$:

$$
\begin{align*}
640 &= F\,\frac{i}{2}\,a_{\overline{n}|i} + F v^{n} \\
    &= F\left[\frac{i}{2}\cdot\frac{1-v^{n}}{i} + v^{n}\right] \\
    &= F\left[\frac{1}{2}(1-0.64) + 0.64\right] \\
    &= 0.82F
\end{align*}
$$

The $i$ cancels, so neither $i$ nor $n$ is needed:

$$
\begin{align*}
F &= \frac{640}{0.82} \\
  &= 780.49
\end{align*}
$$
