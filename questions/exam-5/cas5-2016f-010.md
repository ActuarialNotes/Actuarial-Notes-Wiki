A homeowners insurance company is considering utilizing number of vehicles in the household as an additional risk characteristic within its risk classification system.

Briefly discuss the appropriateness of adding this risk characteristic to the company's risk classification system using four considerations from the Actuarial Standard of Practice No. 12: Risk Classification (for All Practice Areas).

### Explanation
ASOP 12 lists several considerations for selecting risk characteristics; any four, each *applied to this variable* rather than merely named, earn the point. A model answer:

- **Relationship to expected outcomes (causality).** ASOP 12 requires a demonstrable relationship between the characteristic and expected outcomes, though causality is desirable rather than required. The number of vehicles in a household has no intuitive causal link to homeowners losses — it is at best a proxy for household size or affluence — so this consideration is the weakest for the proposal and would need statistical support before the variable is used.
- **Objectivity.** The characteristic must be defined so that two people measuring it get the same answer. "Number of vehicles in the household" is countable and unambiguous, so it satisfies objectivity, provided the company defines "household" and "vehicle" precisely in its manual.
- **Practicality.** The characteristic should be inexpensive to obtain and difficult for the insured to manipulate. Vehicle counts can be collected at application and confirmed against motor vehicle records or the insured's auto policy, so verification cost is low — though an insured could garage a vehicle elsewhere to move classes.
- **Applicable law.** The variable must be permitted in every jurisdiction where it is filed. No law currently prohibits using the number of household vehicles as a homeowners rating characteristic, so this consideration presents no obstacle, but it must be re-checked state by state.
- **Credibility.** The cells at the high end — households with five or more vehicles — will hold very little data. Those categories would need to be grouped together to produce a credible loss cost, or the indication credibility-weighted against the overall average.
- **Industry practices.** Number of vehicles is not a characteristic commonly used in homeowners rating, so there is no industry benchmark to lean on and the company would be acting alone in filing it.

Weighing them, the variable is objective, practical and legally permitted, but its weak relationship to homeowners losses and the thin data in the larger categories are the reasons to be cautious about adopting it.

### Examiner Report
Candidates were expected to know the considerations ASOP 12 sets out for selecting a risk characteristic and to apply four of them to the proposed variable. The question was open-ended — any four of the standard's considerations were accepted.

The common error was naming the considerations without the "briefly discuss" — listing four criteria but not saying how the number of vehicles in a household measures up against each one.
