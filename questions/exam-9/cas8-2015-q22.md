An insurance company is exposed to three independent catastrophic risks in three different regions in a given year. More than one event can occur in a year but each event can only occur once in a year. Events have the following size and probability:

| Event | Loss Amount | Annual Probability of Occurrence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$10,000,000 | 0.10 |
| 2 | \$15,000,000 | 0.05 |
| 3 | \$35,000,000 | 0.02 |

## Part a (2.25 points)

Calculate the Aggregate Exceedance Probabilities associated with the insurance company's exposure.

### Explanation
List every combination of events, its aggregate loss and its probability (each event either occurs or does not), then accumulate the exceedance probability from the largest aggregate loss down:

| Aggregate loss | Event combination | Probability of combination | Exceedance probability | F(x) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 M | 1,2,3 | (0.1)(0.05)(0.02) = 0.0001 | 0 | 1 |
| 50 M | 2,3 | (1-0.1)(0.05)(0.02) = 0.0009 | 0.0001 | 0.9999 |
| 45 M | 1,3 | (0.1)(1-0.05)(0.02) = 0.0019 | 0.0001 + 0.0009 = 0.001 | 0.999 |
| 35 M | 3 | (1-0.1)(1-0.05)(0.02) = 0.0171 | 0.001 + 0.0019 = 0.0029 | 0.9971 |
| 25 M | 1,2 | (0.1)(0.05)(1-0.02) = 0.0049 | 0.0029 + 0.0171 = 0.02 | 0.98 |
| 15 M | 2 | (1-0.1)(0.05)(1-0.02) = 0.0441 | 0.02 + 0.0049 = 0.0249 | 0.9751 |
| 10 M | 1 | (0.1)(1-0.05)(1-0.02) = 0.0931 | 0.0249 + 0.0441 = 0.069 | 0.931 |
| 0 M | - | (1-0.1)(1-0.05)(1-0.02) = 0.8379 | 0.069 + 0.0931 = 0.1621 | 0.8379 |

### Examiner Report
Most common errors

Some candidates did not identify all the possible discrete outcomes (only identified 10M, 15M and 35M as possible aggregate losses). Points were deducted for each missing outcome.

Other candidates did not multiply by (1 – probability that event occurs) for each total loss amount.

Some candidates mistakenly used $1 - \prod(1 - p_i)$ instead of $\sum(p_i)$.

## Part b (0.5 points)

Using a randomly generated number of 0.86, simulate the insured total loss.

### Explanation
0.86 lies between 0.8379 and 0.931 on the F(x) distribution → a 10 M loss occurs.

Alternatively, on the exceedance side: 1 - 0.86 = 0.14, which lies between 0.069 and 0.1621 on the exceedance distribution → a 10 M loss occurs.

### Examiner Report
Most common error

Some candidates adequately identified the probability range in which the simulation falls, but interpolated with the distribution to derive the aggregate loss rather than selecting the appropriate loss amount given the discrete distribution (\$10M)
