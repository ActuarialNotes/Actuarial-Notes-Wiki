A policyholder's homeowners policy has the following terms:

- a deductible of 1,000 per loss,
- the insurer pays 80% of the loss in excess of the deductible, and
- the insurer's payment on any one loss is capped at a maximum payment of 8,000.

The policyholder suffers a loss of 12,000 and submits a claim to the insurer.

Calculate the amount of this loss that the policyholder retains (does not recover from the insurer).

- A) 1,000
- B) 3,200
- C) 4,000
- D) 6,400
- E) 8,000

## Explanation

The insurer's payment is the coinsured excess over the deductible, capped at the maximum payment:

$$
\begin{align*}
Y &= \min\big(0.80\,(X - 1{,}000)^+,\ 8{,}000\big) \\
  &= \min\big(0.80 \times 11{,}000,\ 8{,}000\big) \\
  &= \min(8{,}800,\ 8{,}000) \\
  &= 8{,}000
\end{align*}
$$

Whatever the insurer does not pay, the policyholder keeps:

$$
\begin{align*}
X - Y &= 12{,}000 - 8{,}000 \\
      &= \mathbf{4{,}000}
\end{align*}
$$

That is the deductible (1,000), the policyholder's 20% coinsurance share of the excess (20% of 11,000, or 2,200), and the 800 by which the coinsured amount exceeds the maximum payment. The answer is **C**.

**Why not the others**

- **A** counts only the deductible, as if the insurer paid the whole excess; the policyholder also bears a coinsurance share and anything above the maximum payment.
- **B** ignores the maximum payment: 12,000 - 8,800 = 3,200. The 8,000 cap binds here, so the extra 800 stays with the policyholder.
- **D** caps the *loss* at 8,000 before applying the deductible and coinsurance, giving a payment of 80% of 7,000, or 5,600. The maximum is on the insurer's payment, not on the loss.
- **E** is the insurer's payment, not the amount the policyholder retains.
