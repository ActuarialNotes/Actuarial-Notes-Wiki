Consider the following time-series data for price of a stock on January 1 for the last 5 years:

| Date | Jan 1, 2013 | Jan 1, 2014 | Jan 1, 2015 | Jan 1, 2016 | Jan 1, 2017 |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Price | 63.18 | 81.89 | 103.43 | 123.90 | 133.53 |

Calculate the sample autocorrelation at lag 1 for this data.

- A) Less than 0.2
- B) At least 0.2, but less than 0.4
- C) At least 0.4, but less than 0.6
- D) At least 0.6, but less than 0.8
- E) At least 0.8

## Explanation

The sample autocorrelation at lag 1 is the lag-1 sample autocovariance divided by the sample variance, both taken about the overall mean:

$$
r_1 = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}(x_t - \bar{x})(x_{t+1} - \bar{x})}{\sum_{t=1}^{n}(x_t - \bar{x})^2}
$$

The mean of the five prices is $\bar{x} = 505.93/5 = 101.186$, so the deviations $x_t - \bar{x}$ are

$$
-38.006, \quad -19.296, \quad 2.244, \quad 22.714, \quad 32.344.
$$

The denominator is the sum of their squares, 3,383.887. The numerator multiplies neighbouring deviations:

$$
\begin{align*}
\sum_{t=1}^{4}(x_t - \bar{x})(x_{t+1} - \bar{x}) &= (-38.006)(-19.296) + (-19.296)(2.244) + (2.244)(22.714) + (22.714)(32.344) \\
&= 733.364 - 43.300 + 50.970 + 734.662 \\
&= 1{,}475.695
\end{align*}
$$

Then

$$
r_1 = \frac{1{,}475.695}{3{,}383.887} = 0.436.
$$

This falls in $[0.4, 0.6)$, so the answer is **C**.
